The Peak of DeGayo: how it looks, challenge by challenge

Build 0.3.104, luchador-ui, 2026-11-14. An outside eye, scored on looks only (not play), against the station at 9.5: a diorama, a space with painted backgrounds but real props in it and nice buildings. Frames come from the game itself (tests/peak_watch.tscn, 1280x720): start is the game's own camera at each challenge's start; wide and end are a free camera over the challenge's whole run and its far end (the climber stays at the start, so they show the place, not play). Click a frame for full size.

The whole mountain: The Peak opens on a true 9.5 diorama and has two other places that come close (the temple 7, the cableway 7). Most of the mountain is a 4 to 6: the props and machines are real and often charming, but they stand on huge single-colour slabs with no painted backdrop behind them, so the frame reads as a lit stage set rather than a place. The mine (4.5), the crystal cave (4) and the glacier (4.5) are the low ground; the station-to-mine drop from 9.5 to 4.5 is the steepest fall in the whole climb.

0. La Estación 9

The town is the 9.5 and earns it: real buildings with balconies, tile roofs and shutters, a crane, crates, barrels, cacti and papel picado in front of painted mesas and clouds, with the climb threaded through it. 0.1 and 0.2 are one continuous diorama. The cart descent (0.3) starts in the same town but its run leaves it: out past a fine painted desert, then into a grey tunnel of plain posts, flat strata and stand-in blocks that ends abruptly on bare sky at the mine mouth. The biome is a 9 only because of that last stretch.

0.1 El Primer Paso 9.5

Real buildings (red balcony house, blue-shuttered villa, green arcade house, arch gate), crane, crates with dog faces, barrels, cacti, papel picado, painted mesas + clouds.
Pulls it down: foreground dirt strip is a wide plain band (a third of the frame), wanted-posters and the wall are flat.

0.2 Súbete 9.5

Station shed with tiled roof, cart + lever on a timber platform, crane, cacti under the deck.
Pulls it down: same plain dirt foreground; under-deck mostly empty.

0.3 El Descenso 6

Start = the station (9). Leaving town: painted desert, hopper, fence, cacti (8). Then the tunnel: grey posts in a row, flat strata bands, a flat olive-khaki slab (ride2, top right), a see-through blue box (ride3), a white slab and purple panel (ride4); mine mouth ends abruptly on bare sky (end) - the rock is cut off square.

1. La Mina Vieja 4.5

The timber is good: galleries, ladders, a winding wheel, carts on a trestle, a waterfall curtain. But every challenge sits on the same muddy purple-brown wall of diagonal bands, with no painted depth, and the middle of the mine (1.3 to 1.8) is mostly empty wall with a few planks and small things floating free. It hangs together as one place, but a dark and empty one. Biggest single drop on the mountain after the station.

Held down by flat horizontal stripes on every wall, spray balls that hang in the air, things floating on the wall (red valve boxes, stair pieces), and a big untextured olive slab that fills a third of the frame at the aqueduct and the mill arm.

3. La Gruta de Cristal 4

The weakest biome. One indigo-violet over everything; the crystals are big plain prisms, so the whole cave reads as stand-in blocks; the same flat lilac hexagon card hangs in nearly every frame; very few props. The geode wheel (3.4) is the one memorable piece. The challenges hang together only because they are all the same colour.

4. El Jardín de Hongos 5

The first painted depth since the station: rows of teal mushroom silhouettes behind the climb, vines, caps, a spore curtain and the glowing eyes of Los Ojos. Let down by the spore columns, which are flat yellow slabs with no glow, by plain trapezoid caps, and by dark empty slabs and black voids around the edges. Hangs together in mood; uneven frame to frame.

5. El Templo del Primer Perro 7

The strongest climb after the station and the only biome with real buildings throughout: doorways, columns, carved friezes, torches, a giant dog mask, a tower of cogs, a hall of luchador champion statues, a feathered serpent, an altar. One warm sandstone family holds it together. What keeps it off an 8: big plain dark-brown slabs and black voids around the set pieces, glowing white bars with no material, and the ghost-block stair repeated.

5.1 La Puerta 6.5

temple doorways, a bone-scatter panel, a lit stone floor with a crenellated rail; stepped zigzag-glyph blocks and carved dog faces up the wall (c5_1_wide).
Pulls it down: right third is a black void; the floor's light spot blows out to white.

5.2 La Máscara 7.5

7. Los Riscos del Viento 6.5

The painted backdrop is back and it is lovely: snowy ranges, clouds, a valley with a river and fields. The set pieces are fun (a dead tree, rope ladders, donkeys in blankets, a rockfall of scaffolding, the lower cableway station). But the rock itself is giant plain brown swirl and grey contour-line slabs, often filling half the frame, with box-shaped niches and plain white tabs as holds.

7.1 El Balcón 6.5

painted mountains, clouds and valley behind a stone balcony with flags and grass tufts; wind streaks.
Pulls it down: the rock is a giant brown wood-grain swirl with nothing on it; a glowing cyan rectangle top left reads as a stand-in.

7.2 Nidos 6

Pulls it down: the niche is a bare box; swirl rock fills two thirds of the frame.

7.3 Árbol Seco 6.5

7.4 Puente Roto 6.5

7.5 La Cornisa 5.5

7.7 Los Burros 6.5

7.8 Viento a Favor 6

7.9 Chimenea de Roca 6.5

8. El Teleférico 7

The busiest real structures on the mountain: rust lattice towers, gondolas, bull wheels, a winding house, chairs on a bunting-hung cable, a broken gondola, all over the painted ranges and valley; every challenge reads as part of one machine. Two things hold it down: the sky shows a flat beige band between the cloud layers, and the fang (8.5, 8.6) is a big plain swirl slab.
