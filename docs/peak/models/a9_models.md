# El Glaciar: the models to make

The model list for biome 9, from its dress file `tools/peak/areas/a9_dress.py` (166 decos, 98 of them
skins). Every stem in the tables is NEW: not in `kit/models/` nor in `Top Dog Kit\models\`, nor on the
a0 to a8 lists: 67 placed by the dress file and 6 more made for a9.py's movers, fallers and
breakables (section 4). Then the re-makes (section 5). Make each at exactly its size (metres, w x h x
d; +z faces the camera). The game rescales a landed model that is more than a quarter off; a skin is
never rescaled, so a skin is made to its box exactly.

Reused (not listed): `frozen_climber_deep` (4, flat on the back walls' faces, moved where the pages
move them), `ice_axe` (4: the brink, the rope's anchor on the sill, the stem niche, and head down in
the frog's roof), `rope_coil_frozen` (3), `glove_frozen` (1), `ice_block` (1, on the cave's floor
against the far wall), `tally_marks_ice` (1, on the chimney's far wall), `lantern_hang` (3: the frog's,
the window's, the last cane's), `streamer_pole` (7, at 0.75: the route's canes with their paper flags),
`icicle_row` (1, under the high roof's eave), `snow_cornice` (1, the tower's top, in the foreground),
`sign_pug_arrow_east` (1, the corner ledge, note 85), `frog_blanket` (the frog), `camp_bedroll` (at
0.68), `tea_brazier` and `tea_kettle` (the cliffs' tea things, as page 9.0 asks). The serac keeps the
kit's `serac`. a9.py keeps the prayer pole for the storm ridge. NOTHING CLIMBER-SHAPED is on this list:
the seven frozen climbers you hold stay the game's statues on their bone grips until the new climber
model lands (MODELLING_RUN; Parker 11-13).

Makers: **A** ice walls, masses and every skin of the glacier's own body (here: the far side, the bed,
the floors' snow, the tongue, the west wall, the crack's, the cave's, the balcony's and the upper
level's back walls, the shelf, the roofs, the slide and its kicker, the brow, the tower, the flanks,
the niches, the snow hole, the window's reveal, the slab hole and the hollow, the ice at the climbers'
backs, and the holds, which belong to the wall: knuckles, nubs, plates, stones, tusks). **B** signs,
flags, lanterns, snow fringes, small dressing (here: the three wall arrow signs, the lantern on its
axe-shaft hook, the short torn paper, the three snow fringes). **C** the breakables and the movers'
ice, made the way the a3 crystal cave's crystals are (cut in flat facets, flat colour per facet, no
glow): the five ringed icicles that hold, the snow bridge piece, and section 4's falling icicle, bare
serac and stump, shards, slab and the chute's kicker board.

The glacier's look (the sheet `docs/peak/playtest/design/data/a9_biome.py` and Parker's third-round
answers): **ice is cut, never moulded, and its face is paint.** The body is blue-black and glossy, a
few big flat planes 2 to 4 m across and bigger meeting at hard angles; its bands, thin white crack
lines and stones are flat colour on those planes in three or four blues: long uneven wavy lines and
bands tilted about ten degrees down to the east (the way the ice flows); no ink inside a face, no step
of shape for a band; flush: nothing sticks out of it that is not a hold. **Nothing painted narrows to a
point** (no streak, no wedge stain, no seam that trails away). A few big breaks are real shape and only
those get an ink line (a brow with its painted shadow, the shelf's broken end, one fallen block, the
tower's twin). Designed outlines: away from holds and the tops you stand on an edge may move up to 0.8 m
(it leans, it is notched where a block has gone, no long ruled edge); the holds, the standing tops and
the faces of any gap you climb stay exactly where the course has them. **No wall is tiled**: one piece
made for its place. **No dark slot or recess cut into a face** (the slab hole and the snow hole are the
pages' own real breaks). The sheet names no plants: no outcrops, no plants. **What you hold:** pale blue
ice (frosted, matte, white frost along its top edge, plain blocks with square ends) for 2 s; grey-brown
stones, dry, no frost, for good; firm snow, white and matte, 0.4 to 0.7 m thick with a square front
edge and the ice plainly under it, never blank for more than 4 m (blue hollows, wind ridges, a line of
old boot prints going the way the route goes). **What you can't:** the blue-black glossy body with its
hard white glint; clear ice glazed over rock. **Scenery** inside: the crack's far wall, mid blue and
dull, two or three big planes, never pale blue, never snow on it, never a shape you could stand on;
outside, the glacier's far side as one card, greyer and hazed. **Light is colour, never glow**: each
room's way out is painted palest, its closed corners the deepest blue; the lanterns are the only warm
light (the game adds it). Every dark wet crack an icicle grows from, every ring of splinters under one,
every boot print, hollow and dent is PAINT on the skin it lies on, as its row says. Note 90: every point
of ice a hand can meet is DRAWN SHARP (the solid ends blunt; that is the game's), so the icicles', the
tusks' and the tooth's models go to the full point.

A skin's origin is the middle of its box's x span at its foot, the middle of its depth (as the dress
file places it); a turned skin (a knuckle, a plate) is made level and the game turns it about the middle
of its base. "Skin (lines)" are a9.py's line numbers; z ranges are where the piece stands.
