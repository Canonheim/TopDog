## 2. Why it fell short (so the plan fixes the cause, not only the pieces)

1. **Too many things made too fast.** 1,081 models in one night. Each maker judged its own work on
   its own bench, mostly from straight ahead, and nothing stopped a 3 from landing.
2. **Skins were made as the course's outlines, not as real objects.** A ledge became a slab, a
   needle a box with a roof, a cap a trapezoid, a landing a dark line. What you touch looks like
   a collision box with paint on it (notes 126, 129, 133, 140).
3. **Re-makes replaced better pieces.** 29 of 57 swaps were worse (the gondola 9 to 2, the chair,
   the trolley, the counterweight, the kite, the bell, the boiler, the snail, the caps).
4. **No painted backdrop.** The station is a diorama: painted mesas and sky behind, real houses and
   props in front. Most biomes have real things standing on huge single-colour slabs with nothing
   painted behind, so "the frame reads as a lit stage set rather than a place".
5. **Empty frames.** Long stretches show a wall, a ledge and one prop (mine 1.3 to 1.8, the cave,
   the glacier, the storm ridge's middle).
6. **Craft errors seen only in the game:** z-fighting between pieces (the makers' check is one model
   at a time: notes 109, 119); pieces at different depths that meet only straight ahead (115, 116,
   121, 134); drawings bigger or smaller than their solids (113, 124, 129, 133); polished colours
   that striped (fixed); ropes drawn as lines (137); blank white or flat cards (the hexagon card,
   the white void shapes, the beige sky band, the yellow spore columns, the olive slab).

## 3. The standard: what a 9 is

**A diorama frame has four layers, every frame:**

1. **The painted backdrop** (far, 12 m and more behind): one painted scene per stretch, never a
   flat colour: the biome's own deep space, painted in the house style (the station's mesas and
   clouds; the cliffs' ranges and valley are already this).
2. **The painted set** (the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind): walls that recede,
   with painted openings, arches, galleries, distant lights, stains and structure, so the wall is a
   place, not a slab. Painted faces in long wavy bands as Parker approved, but broken up by things.
3. **Real things** (in front of the set and around the climb): buildings, machines, furniture,
   props, plants, frogs. At least three good things in every frame, and one hero piece per
   challenge (the station's crane, the temple's mask, the geode). Things are what make it a diorama.
4. **The climb itself as things:** every ledge, hold, rope, pipe and mover is a real object (a
   timber beam, a carved step, a crystal, a rope with twist), its drawing the shape of its solid.

Plus a few foreground framing pieces where nobody climbs behind them, and light that picks out the
climb.

**One connected world, from the background through the play space to the foreground** (Parker,
11-14: "Make sure models can actually correctly come from background to playspace to foreground, so
the world looks like it is all a connected thing, like the mushrooms in the garden had tops that
were not connected to their stalks at all"). A thing that crosses the depths is ONE thing: a
mushroom's stalk grows out of the ground behind and its cap is the bounce you land on; a branch
grows from the trunk behind and its tip is the hold (note 134); a crystal spike roots in the big
crystal behind and juts out through the climb (note 121); a pipe and its joints are one run (note
115); a stair, its string and its treads are one stair (note 116). Nothing is made as a front piece
and a separate back piece that only line up from straight ahead. Every such thing is made, and
judged, from the game's own angled camera, high and low, and in the game with the climber in front
of it and behind it.

**A thing at 9:** a real silhouette that reads at 13.7 m from the game's camera; three levels of
detail (the form, the secondary parts, the trim and wear); the house's character (pug faces, lucha
masks, papel picado, honest wear); matte painted colour with ink; consistent with the plaza houses
and the frogs; and seen right from the game's angled camera, not only from the front.

## 4. How the models are made, and the gate

**One model at a time, each by its own session** (Parker, 11-14: "Each model will be made by an
independent session, one at a time, then sent to the reviewer session to get feedback and iterate
until it is at least rated an 8.5, instead of as batch models, since that produced much worse
quality"). No batches.

1. **A maker session per model.** It gets one model's brief (what it is, where it stands, its
   size, the solid it must be the shape of, the layers it crosses, the standard pieces to match,
   the old model it replaces if any, and the playtest notes on it), makes that one model, and
   nothing else.
2. **Its sheet** shows the model from the front, three-quarter, and from the game's camera (13.7 m,
   a little above and to one side, at true size and enlarged, as thread B did for the stop frog);
   where it is held, beside its solid's outline; where it crosses the depths, from high and low so
   its parts are seen to join.
3. **The reviewer session** scores the sheet against the standard pieces and says what is wrong.
   The maker changes it and sends it again, round after round, until the reviewer rates it **8.5
   or more**. Only then does it land.
4. **In the game:** synced and imported, then a frame of its place from the game's own camera,
   checked by the reviewer again (a model that drops under 8.5 in the game goes back).
5. **The scenes the models make** are scored as frames by the same reviewer, against the target of
   9 for every challenge and biome; Parker sees one sheet per biome and his word is final.
6. **The sweeps run on every sync** (built once, in phase 0): pairs of pieces sharing a plane
   (z-fighting), pieces meant to touch at different depths, drawings against solids, plain stand-in
   boxes, blank or flat cards. A sync with a hit is fixed before frames.

Fewer things, better made: reuse the pieces that already score 8.5 or more wherever they fit (the
station kit, the old models, the frogs), and make new ones only where nothing fits.

**Solids are free.** Parker: "all solids have 100% freedom to change shape, that is actually
encouraged." Every solid is re-cut to the real object's shape where that makes a better thing (a
needle one crystal, a cap a dome on its stalk, a ledge a carved stone), and the course is re-worked
to match; the play is checked after.

## 5. The phases

### Phase 0: the clean-up and the tools

- **The 29 better old models are NOT put back now** (Parker, 11-14: "We do not need to put the
  models back just yet since they will go back or be modified and go back once we do the full
  run"). Each is handled in its biome's pass: the old model is the starting point, brought to 8.5
  or more by its maker session and the reviewer, with any part a note needed added (the gondola's
  step and rack, the trolley's solid bar). The list is part 1 of THINGS_RATING.
- **Remove the blank and flat cards:** the lilac hexagon card through the crystal cave, the white
  void shapes and flat orange card of the storm ridge and summit (10.5 to 10.7, 11.1, 11.2, 11.5),
  the beige band in the cableway's sky, the glowing white bars and panels (temple 5.2, glacier 9.4,
  9.6, 9.7), the flat yellow spore columns (4.2 to 4.4: make them glowing curtains of spores), the
  olive slab (2.3, 2.4), the floating spray balls and squares.
- **The worst 30 things** (part 2 of THINGS_RATING): most are bars, hairlines and black panels.
  Each is either made into the real thing (the cableway's chalk holds as carved chalk knobs; its
  short cables as real cable with clamps; the black waters' landings as stone ledges with faces;
  the tunnel posts as timber sets with caps and braces) or removed if nothing needs it.
- **Build the five sweeps** (part 4) and run them over the whole mountain. Fix their hits:
  z-fighting (notes 109, 119), depth (115), drawn against solid (113, 124, 129, 133), invisible
  geometry (104), the ram that traps you (113).
- **Re-rate.** Expected: every biome up by about half a point to a point from this alone.

### Phase 1: the painted backdrops (the biggest single lift)

**The painted backdrops pass the reviewer too** (Parker, 11-14: "painted back drops must also pass
the reviewer score"): each backdrop and painted set is made by its own session, one at a time, with
the same sheets (including how it sits behind the climb from the game's camera) and the same loop
with the reviewer until it is rated at least 8.5, then checked in the game.

One painted backdrop and one painted set per biome stretch, made as flat painted cards and
painted wall pieces in the house style (the station's and cliffs' are the model). This is the
"painted background" of the diorama and is missing from biomes 1 to 6, 9, 10 and the summit's
back. What each paints:

- **1 The mine:** galleries receding into the dark, timber sets and ladders in depth, lantern
  pools far off, an ore chute, a distant winding wheel, the waterfall's glow.
- **2 The black waters:** vaulted cisterns and aqueduct arches going back in rows, channels
  catching light, pipe galleries, the dam's face, mist.
- **3 The crystal cave:** the geode's great chambers, a forest of crystal silhouettes going back in
  layers of colour (deep indigo far, lilac near), pools of glow, a mirror lake. One colour family
  but at least three values, never one indigo over everything.
- **4 The mushroom garden:** extend the teal mushroom silhouettes it already has (the rating's one
  bright spot) into a full painted grove, glowing spore curtains, a mushroom cottage village in the
  distance, the canopy.
- **5 The temple:** carved halls receding, colonnades, stairs going up into the dark, braziers far
  off, a giant carved dog face in the distance. Fill the black voids.
- **6 The forge:** furnace halls in red glow going back, chains and gantries in silhouette, sparks,
  a lit doorway to daylight at the top; break the brown wallpaper with value and colour.
- **9 The glacier:** a painted ice cathedral, seracs and crevasses in depth, the ranges beyond
  (already showing through gaps), blue light shafts.
- **10 The storm ridge:** a painted storm cloudscape with lightning-lit spires going back, the
  sea of cloud below; replace the white voids.
- **11 The summit:** the sea of clouds and the ring's ruin silhouetted against the sun.
- **0.3 the cart ride's tunnel and the mine mouth:** a painted tunnel receding, and the mouth
  opening onto the painted desert, not bare sky.

### The order (Parker, 11-14)

1. **Phase 0, the clean-up**, over the whole mountain.
2. **The mine first, as the test and the example** for the quality needed ("the first one done will
   be the mine, as it is our test and example one for the quality needed"). Before its pass:
   **move the mine and make the new barrel area** (note 146): the mine about 60 m west under the

3. Then the other biomes, one at a time (the list below gives the suggested order after the mine).

### Phase 3: from 8 to 9 and up

- One hero piece per challenge that does not yet have one.
- Foreground framing where it helps (at the edges, never over the climb).
- Light: a key light on each hero piece and on the climb; lanterns where a frame is too dark.
- The effects still on the fix thread's WORK_LOG list (steam, sparks, dust, splashes, glints).
- The final independent rating, frame by frame and thing by thing, until every challenge, biome and
  thing is 9 or more; then Parker's playtest.

## 6. Who does it

- **One maker session per model**, started for that model and ended when it lands (part 4). Many
  models can be in flight at once, each in its own session, but each session makes one model.
- **The reviewer session** (the independent rating session's method and eye), scoring every model
  sheet until 8.5, every in-game check, and every frame against the 9.
- **The fix thread:** the course changes (solids re-cut to real objects, the play re-checked), the
  sweeps' fixes, the notes' play changes.
- **Me:** the per-biome plans, one brief per model, the syncs and frames, and Parker's sheets.
- One biome at a time through to its re-rating, so the method is proven on the first before the
  next starts.

## 7. Decided, and still open

Decided by Parker, 11-14:

1. The old models are not put back now; they go back, or are modified and go back, in the full
   run, each through the maker and reviewer loop.
2. Solids have complete freedom to change shape, and it is encouraged.
3. The playtest notes are folded into the biome passes, so everything is done at once.
4. Each model is made by its own session, one at a time, and goes back and forth with the reviewer
   until it is rated at least 8.5. No batches.
5. Models cross from the background through the play space to the foreground as one connected
   thing.
6. Nothing starts yet: more playtesting first, and the planning goes on.
7. The painted backdrops pass the reviewer the same way as the models.
8. The order: the clean-up first, then the mine as the test and example biome, after moving it and
   making the new barrel area (barrels running right to left); then the other biomes.
