El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.4: Rebote Doble

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is the pocket at the head of the slime ramp, where it runs into the room's east wall under the island's corner. There is no floor here, only slime, so nothing can grow up from below except on a long stalk. One mushroom did: its cap stands out over the pit on 10 m of stalk. One could not, and grew sideways out of the east wall on a bent stalk, tied to the rock with a web of white mycelium. Between them a root from the frog's garden above reaches out over the pit and lets one vine down. Cap, vine, wall cap: three living things that happen to stand in a line across a hole.

What must not change

  • The first cap, where it is and leaning as it does. The vine and the knot it hangs from. The wall cap, 2.6 m, tipped 10 degrees.
  • The small shelf at 311 m that the wall cap throws you back to, 2.8 m wide with the snail wall at its back.
  • The pit: a miss goes down the ramp to Sombreros.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The pocket's back wall and a stand of bell mushrooms. Rounded mossy rock closes the pocket 6 m back, with slime running down it and boulders where it meets the ramp. Three bell mushrooms stand on the back of the ramp, thin and pale blue-green. They stand to one side of the vine and stop below its end, never behind it. (6.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The first cap's stalk. 10 m of cream stalk from the ramp, rooted 2 m back and leaning forward, with its skirt near the top. It dips and rocks when the cap throws you (note 36). (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 3The root from the frog's garden. As thick as your body where it leaves the east wall, under the garden's lip. It arches out 6 m over the pit, a little behind you, and its tip turns forward and ends in the knot. The vine hangs from a root you can follow back to the wall with your eye. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4The wall cap's bent stalk. A short thick stalk that comes out of the wall below the cap and bends up to it. The wet bracket you see today becomes its elbow. (1.2 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 5The two shelves, from behind. The shelf you step off stands on its tall stalk from the ramp. The shelf you are thrown to is a shelf fungus growing from the snail wall, and two smaller shelves grow under it in steps, further back. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6Three lights. A lantern pod on the root above the knot, one on the first cap's stalk, and glow moss where the ramp meets the east wall. The vine's top, the first cap's underside and the wall cap are the lit things. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The two caps, spotted. Both orange with pale spots, rolled rims and cream gills, flat on the face that throws (note 37). The wall cap is the garden's sideways cap: its rim rolls further on the low side. Both squash and spring (note 36).
  • 8The knot. The root's tip, a fist of wood with the vine grown out of it. Today it is a short bar in the air with nothing holding it.
  • 9Mycelium web on the east wall. A white web spread flat on the rock round the wall cap's stalk, 6 m tall. It ties the cap to the wall, and it is the pale patch that shows you from across the pit where the wall cap is.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
