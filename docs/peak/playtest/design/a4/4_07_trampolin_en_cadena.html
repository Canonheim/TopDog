El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.7: Trampolín en Cadena

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Over the giant's west shoulder the roof comes down in two masses of rock, both black with slime: a broad stalactite on the east, and on the west a spur that sticks out of the back wall. Between them is a slot 6.8 m wide with rock at its back. Nothing can root in slime, but mycelium can cling to it, and a white web has spread up the slot's back wall. Its fruit are four mushrooms that grow sideways out of the two walls, one above another, each on the wall the last one left in shade. A fifth sits low on the giant's shoulder. The web's thick cords run from cap to cap in the same zigzag you bounce.

What must not change

  • The five caps, each where it is and leaning as it does. Each keeps the sideways speed you bring.
  • The three root ends under the stalactite, and the drop from the last one onto the first cap.
  • The two slick walls, and the mossy ledge on top of the spur.

The three layers

Background

  • 1One far mushroom. On the terrace below the spur, dim and blue-green. Everything else behind this challenge is rock. (7.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The slot's back wall. Rounded wet rock that joins the stalactite to the spur 3 m behind you. It closes the slot, so there is no black gap behind the caps. (2.6 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The web. White mycelium spread flat on the back wall. Its thick cords run from cap to cap, so the zigzag of the route is drawn on the wall behind it. Thinner strands fill in between. Three knots in it glow green and light the caps from behind. (2.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4Four bent stalks. Each sideways cap stands on a short thick stalk that comes out of the wall below it and bends up, with its foot in the web. The first cap needs none you can see: it grows straight out of the giant's shoulder. (1.2 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 5The root down the stalactite. A root as thick as a leg comes down the stalactite's east face from the roof and goes in under its foot. The three root ends you hold are where it comes out again. (1.2 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 6Mossy stalactites under the spur. Two short thick ones, hanging over the giant's west rim. They make the spur's underside a roof and not a flat cut. (1.4 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 7A boulder and a lantern on the top ledge. A mossy boulder at the back of the ledge with a lantern pod on it. It is the light you bounce up toward. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8Five caps, spotted. All five orange with pale spots, rolled rims and cream gills, flat on the face that throws (note 37). Each squashes toward its wall and springs back, and its stalk rocks (note 36). The tilt of each is easy to read because the spots tilt with it.
  • 9Three root ends. Tan, dry and knobbed at the tip, each a little lower than the last. They come out of a crack along the stalactite's foot, which is drawn on.
  • 10Slime sheets on both walls. The two walls are blank today. They get glossy black sheets with pale runs and slow drips, and a wet ring round the foot of each cap's stalk.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
