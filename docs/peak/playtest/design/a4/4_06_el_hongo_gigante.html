El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.6: El Hongo Gigante

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The biggest mushroom in the garden stands on the island's mossy top. Its cap is 19 m across and reaches out past the terrace's edge, over the gap. It is the same kind as every other mushroom here: stalk, skirt, cap and gills, orange with pale spots. But it is very old, and an old cap here sweats slime: its stalk and its whole cap are wet and can't be held. Three things on it stay dry. The gills under its east half hang in the open air over the gap. The rim on that side has rolled up into a dry brim. And when the cap opened, the veil that covered its gills tore: most of it is the skirt on the stalk, and one shred still hangs from the east rim with a dried knob on its end. You go out along the gills, swing on the knob, and come up round the rim.

What must not change

  • The cap's outline exactly, and what is slick on it: everything but the gills, the brim, the knob and the little mushrooms.
  • The six gills, 1.4 m apart, the last one dry only at its foot. The knob 1 m outside the rim. The brim, 1.6 m.
  • The terrace's floor is open from end to end under the cap: the stalk stands behind you and is not solid.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The terrace's far side. One far mushroom on the terrace, the cap of another showing over the gap to the east, and mossy boulders along the back. A snail sleeps on one of them. The scenery mushrooms that stand on the terrace today move back here (note 37). (6.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The stalk. A real stalk, 5 m thick at the foot and 3 m at the top, standing behind the play space so the terrace stays open in front of it. It is wet and dark green, with runs of slime, so nobody reaches for it. Its skirt hangs 4 m up: a torn frill, the rest of the veil. (1.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 3The gill plates, running back. Under the cap, the gills are plates that run from the rim back to the stalk, 5 m deep, cream and dry on this side. Each gill you hold is the front end of one. The underside is lit from below, so the plates show as a pleated ceiling. (1.2 to 5.5 m behind you)
  • 4The wet gills of the west half. Under the west half the gills are slimed black and stuck together in sheets. You can see at a glance which half of the underside to use. (1.2 to 5.5 m behind you)
  • 5A lantern pod and glow moss. A lantern pod low on the stalk's east side and a patch of glow moss on the terrace. The cap is lit on its underside, which is where the route is. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6A real cap: orange, spotted, wet. Today it is drawn as brown faceted rock. It becomes the garden's mushroom at its biggest: a rounded orange cap with pale spots, made to the same outline. A wet green-yellow sheen lies over all of it and slow drips fall from the rim, so it reads as slime and not as rock. The spots are flat and under the wet. Only raised, dry, cream things can be held (note 42).
  • 7The brim. The east rim's edge, rolled up and dry, cream like the gills. It is the pale lip you are aiming for when you let go.
  • 8The veil's shred and its knob. A strip of the torn veil hanging from the rim, wet and dark, with its end dried into a firm cream knob as big as two fists. The knob is the one thing on it you can hold, and it looks like it.
  • 9Two little mushrooms on the shoulder. Two small cream mushrooms, dry, standing on the slick shoulder: the second and the fourth of the five that are there today. The other three go (note 44). That leaves three gaps of about 2.8 m, 3.0 m and 2.8 m, each a short lunge up the slime.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10Fern and one small cap. At the bottom left, in front of the island's rock and below the terrace's floor. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)
