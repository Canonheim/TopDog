El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.6: El Hongo Gigante

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The biggest mushroom in the garden stands on the island's mossy top. Its cap is 19 m across and reaches out past the terrace's edge, over the gap. It is the same kind as every other mushroom here: stalk, skirt, cap and gills, orange with pale spots. But it is very old, and an old cap here sweats slime: its stalk and its whole cap are wet and can't be held, and under the slime its orange has gone to a dark rust, nearly black. Three things on it stay dry. The gills under its east half hang in the open air over the gap. The rim on that side has rolled up into a dry brim. And when the cap opened, the veil that covered its gills tore: most of it is the skirt on the stalk, and one shred still hangs from the east rim with a dried knob on its end. You go out along the gills, swing on the knob, and come up round the rim.

What must not change

  • The cap's outline exactly, and what is slick on it: everything but the gills, the brim, the knob and the little mushrooms.
  • The six gills, 1.4 m apart, the last one dry only at its foot. The knob 1 m outside the rim. The brim, 1.6 m.
  • The terrace's floor is open from end to end under the cap: the stalk stands behind you and is not solid.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The terrace's far side. A stand of three young bell mushrooms on the terrace, thin and pale blue-green, and mossy boulders along the back. A snail sleeps on one of them. The orange scenery mushrooms that stand on the terrace today are gone (Parker, 11-13). (6.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The stalk. A real stalk, 5 m thick at the foot and 3 m at the top, standing behind the play space so the terrace stays open in front of it. It is wet and dark green, with runs of slime, so nobody reaches for it. Its skirt hangs 4 m up: a torn frill, the rest of the veil. (1.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 3The gill plates, running back. Under the cap, the gills are plates that run from the rim back to the stalk, 5 m deep, cream and dry on this side. Each gill you hold is the front end of one. The underside is lit from below, so the plates show as a pleated ceiling. (1.2 to 5.5 m behind you)
  • 4The wet gills of the west half. Under the west half the gills are slimed black and stuck together in sheets. You can see at a glance which half of the underside to use. (1.2 to 5.5 m behind you)
  • 5A lantern pod and glow moss. A lantern pod low on the stalk's east side and a patch of glow moss on the terrace. The cap is lit on its underside, which is where the route is. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6A real cap: dark, wet, and plainly a mushroom. Today it is drawn as brown faceted rock. It becomes a real mushroom cap, rounded, made to the same outline (note 42). It is not the bright orange of the caps that throw you. Under its slime the orange has gone to a dark rust, nearly black where the slime is thick, with a wet green-yellow sheen and slow drips from the rim. Its spots show only as faint ghosts under the wet. Dark, glossy and wet says it can't be held, and nothing about it says bounce (Parker, 11-13). Only raised, dry, cream things on it can be held.
  • 7The crown: dry and cream. The flat top of the cap, where the slime has run off and the skin has dried and cracked into pale scales. It is orange in the course today and it does not throw. It becomes cream, the same dry cream as the gills and the brim, so the three dry places on the giant are one colour and the crown reads as a place to stand.
  • 8The brim. The east rim's edge, rolled up and dry, cream like the gills. It is the pale lip you are aiming for when you let go.
  • 9The veil's shred and its knob. A strip of the torn veil hanging from the rim, wet and dark, with its end dried into a firm cream knob as big as two fists. The knob is the one thing on it you can hold, and it looks like it.
  • 10Two little mushrooms on the shoulder. Two small cream mushrooms, dry, standing on the slick shoulder: the second and the fourth of the five that are there today. The other three go (note 44). That leaves three gaps of about 2.8 m, 3.0 m and 2.8 m, each a short lunge up the slime.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11Fern and one small bell mushroom. At the bottom left, in front of the island's rock and below the terrace's floor: a fern clump and one small bell mushroom, both dark. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Slow drips of slime off the cap's rim onto the terrace.
  • A wet sheen that moves a little on the cap as the camera moves.
  • The knob's strand stretches and shakes when you swing on it.
