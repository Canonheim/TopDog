El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.1: Sombreros

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is the foot of the garden's west hall, a room 100 m tall beside the well. You come in out of the air, from the first crossing, into a curtain of roots at the well's rim. One great root comes down the well's wall at the door, lies along the floor, and rears up as a broken stump. Where a root that size lies under moss, mushrooms come up along it in a row, and that is the challenge: four mushrooms of one family, each older and taller than the last. The first three are young and full of spring. The fourth is old: its cap has turned up like a saucer and gone dry, and it is the shelf you rest on. One more, older still, fell off its stalk long ago and lies soft on the floor. A climber lay down on it and never got up.

What must not change

  • The stump you step off, and the three caps exactly where they are, each wider than the last: 3.2 m, 4.0 m, 4.4 m.
  • Each cap's throwing face, flat and at its angle. The first leans 13 degrees east, the other two are level.
  • The shelf at 276 m, the soft old cap on the floor with the frozen climber on it, and the moss floor that catches every fall in the west hall.
  • The root beam at the door and its three hanging roots, which catch the fling from the crystal cave.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The west hall, far back. Two stalks as thick as towers go up out of sight, 6 to 10 m behind you, dim and blue-green. Their caps are far overhead. Rounded mossy boulders stand along the foot of the back wall. The scenery mushrooms that stand 2 m behind you today move back here (note 37). (6.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2A snail, asleep. On a boulder at the back, shell 1.8 m across, drawn in. It is the first snail you see, long before the ones you climb. (6.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 3The great root. As thick as a barrel. It comes down the back of the door at the well, and lies along the floor 2 m behind the path, half under moss, humping up and down. The knee and the stump you climb are its near end, broken off. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The four stalks. One behind each cap and one behind the shelf. Each roots on the great root 2 m back and leans forward, so its cap hangs over the path and the floor under it stays clear. Cream, with a skirt part way up and gills under the cap. When a cap throws you, its stalk dips and rocks with it (note 36). (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5Glow moss and two spore lanterns. Moss glows green on the great root. Two lantern pods grow on the stalks, low down, so the caps are lit from underneath. Today a lantern floats in the air beside the shelf: it moves onto the shelf's stalk. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6Three caps, spotted. Orange with pale spots, like the scenery mushrooms. No checks (note 37). The face you land on stays as flat as it is today. The roundness is in the rim, which rolls down all round, and in the cream gills underneath. Each cap sways a little at rest, squashes when you land and springs back taller, with a puff of spores from under the rim (note 36).
  • 7The shelf: an old cap turned up. Cream and dry, with its gills on top as fine lines running out from the middle. It has no orange left, so nobody expects it to throw them.
  • 8The frozen climber, half under moss. He lies on the soft old cap where he is today. Moss has grown over his legs and one arm like a blanket, and three tiny mushrooms come up beside him. The cap under him is slumped and faded to tan with no spots left, so it does not look like a cap that throws.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 9Fern and one small cap. At the bottom right corner, below the floor's edge. Nothing else stands in front (note 40). (1.4 to 2.2 m in front)
