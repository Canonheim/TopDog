El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.5: La Baba

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is the snails' wall, and the slime is theirs. Water seeps out from under the moss at the top of the island and runs down its east face, and five snails the size of carts live on that face and turn the water to slime. Each keeps to its own patch. It climbs 2.2 m to a tuft of glowing moss, eats, rests, and goes back down to where the wet is. The wall leans out over the pit, and it curves round behind you into a cove where more snails sleep and where they keep their eggs. A snail's shell is the one dry thing on the wall.

What must not change

  • The wall: 21.9 m of slime leaning out 12 degrees. The five snails, each crawling 2.2 m up and 2.2 m down and resting at each end.
  • Their timing: odd and even snails half a round apart, so one rests at its top while the next rests at its bottom.
  • The shell is the only hold. The foot and the head are slime.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The forest, across the gap. One far mushroom rises out of the pit with its cap at the height of the middle snails, and a smaller one stands on the frog's garden beyond. Dim, blue-green, and well behind the snails. (7.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The cove. The island's east face does not stop at the play space. It curves round to the east behind you and joins the back wall, so there is slick wall behind the snails all the way up, running with the same slime. Its top edge is the same mossy lip. (1.5 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 3Two more snails, asleep. On the cove wall, 4 m back, drawn into their shells. They show that the five in front of you are part of a herd. (3.5 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 4The eggs. A clutch of snail eggs in a hollow of the cove wall, each the size of a football and glowing faintly green. They are the wall's lamp: the shells show dark and round against their light. (3.5 to 4.5 m behind you)
  • 5The shelf's lower steps. The shelf you start from is the top one of three shelf fungi growing from the wall in steps. The lower two are smaller and further back. (1.2 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6The root arm's root. Across the gap, on the lip of the frog's garden: the knee of root that the root arm grows up from. (1.2 to 2.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7Each snail's patch. A stripe of fresh slime on the wall, wetter and glossier than the rest, exactly as long as that snail's crawl. You can read where each snail goes, and where the gaps between them are, before any of them moves.
  • 8A tuft of glow moss above each patch. What each snail climbs for. It is small, green and glowing, not pale, and nobody would take it for a hold. The top snail has no tuft: it eats the moss of the lip.
  • 9The mossy lip. Thick moss hangs over the top edge of the wall, and the water comes out from under it. It is the dry edge you pull up onto.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
