El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.8: Puente de Micelio

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Between the spur's top and a loop of root that hangs from the low roof to the west, a curtain of mycelium hangs from the roof like a net left to dry. Nobody built the bridge. It is the curtain's bottom edge, grown thick: two cords as thick as your wrist, with small shelf fungi along them for treads. Just under the middle, in a pouch of the curtain, sits a nest of spore pods. Two of them are fat. One points east and one points up and west, and they blow in turn. The curtain bellies out a moment before each one blows, so you see a gust coming before it reaches you. Under all of it is 27 m of air and the terrace.

What must not change

  • The bridge: 14 m, sagging about 2 m, with its fifteen treads.
  • The two gusts and their rhythm. The east half shoves you back east. The west half lifts you and pushes west. Each warns for 0.8 seconds.
  • The ledge you leave and the root shelf you reach.

The three layers

Background

  • 1A great cap, far below. The top of a far mushroom that stands on the terrace, dim and blue-green. You look down on it, which tells you how high the bridge is. (8.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The curtain. A net of mycelium with holes as wide as your hand, hanging from the roof's stalactites to the bridge. It leans back, 3 m behind you at the top and touching the bridge at the bottom. It is dim grey-green and plainly a sheet, not a line. Before a gust it bellies the way the gust will blow. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3Roof stalactites. Four short mossy ones along the roof. The curtain is draped from their tips. The two long ones that hang here today are replaced by these. (1.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 4The pod nest. Two fat spore pods and a few small ones in a pouch of the curtain under the bridge's middle. The east one swells and blows east. Then the west one swells and blows up and west. They are what the gusts come from (note 38). (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 5The root loop. The shelf you reach is the bottom of a loop of root that comes down out of the low roof, as thick as a leg. It hangs from the roof by this root and is not on a stalk 27 m tall, as it is drawn today. (1.2 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6The bridge's east anchor. A mossy boulder at the back of the ledge, with the two cords wound round it and a lantern pod on top. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The bridge, grown and not built. Two white cords of mycelium, with fifteen small flat shelf fungi along them as treads, cream and dry. No planks and no rope. The sketch shows its sag, which the course's own line does not.
  • 8The gusts, made visible. Each gust is a burst of orange spores from its pod, thick enough to see its edges and which way it blows. A small puff comes first as the warning (note 38).
  • 9The two tied ends. At the west end the cords are grown round the root loop. At the east end they run back to the boulder.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A rushing sound for each gust, louder inside it.
