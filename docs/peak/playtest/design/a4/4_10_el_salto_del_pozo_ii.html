El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.10: El Salto del Pozo II

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The second crossing of the well, and the place where the garden stops. On the east rim is the garden's last door, a cave mouth of rounded mossy rock. A root arm stands in it like a diving board, and under the board's lip the garden's grandest mushroom has grown out over the drop. On the west rim, 12 m away, is cut stone: the doorstep of the temple of the First Top Dog. A stone beam sticks out 5 m over the well above it, and two bronze chains hang from the beam, each with a long banner of cut paper behind it. Between the rims the well goes down out of sight. The garden has started across: roots have crept round the back of the shaft, and there is moss and a bell sprout or two in the joints of the temple's first blocks. Green light on one side, torch gold on the other.

What must not change

  • The great cap's three throwing faces, exactly as they are: the middle 1 m, the east flank, and the west flank that hangs in the well.
  • The root sill, the post, and the diving board whose lip is the launch.
  • The two chains at 1.5 m apart, their ends 2 m above the temple's doorstep, and the doorstep's 2 m reach into the well. The beam above them grows; the chains do not move.
  • You see what a miss costs before you jump: both rims and the drop are lit.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The well's back wall. The shaft is round, so behind the crossing its wall curves from one rim to the other, 7 m back at the middle. Slick black rock with pale runs, lit green from the east and gold from the west so that it shows. Today there is only black here (note 46). (2.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 2Roots round the back of the shaft. A band of thick roots that leaves the garden's rim, follows a crack round the back of the well, and reaches the temple's doorstep. Three small bell mushrooms grow on it, pale blue-green. It is far back and below the chains' ends, and it is how the garden got across. (5.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 3The garden's last wall. Rounded mossy rock behind the cave mouth, with shelf fungus in three steps and a patch of glow moss at the floor. Nothing here is cut. (3.0 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 4The great cap's stalk. Short and very thick, 1.8 m. It grows out of a crack in the well's wall 4 m below the rim, behind the play space, and leans out and forward to hold the cap over the drop. Skirt, and gills fanning out under the cap's west lip. Today the cap has no stalk at all. (1.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 5The diving board's mother root. A root as thick as a barrel that comes down from the roof at the back of the cave mouth and spreads into the sill. The post and the board are one branch of it, turned toward the well. A lantern pod grows on it above the board. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The temple's first hall. Inside the door: a back wall of big pale blocks, 6 m back and in brown shadow, with the temple's carved band along it at two heights and one torch in a bronze bracket. It is drawn as the temple's own page for La Puerta has it, and shown here only as far as you can see from the garden. (6.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 7The door's side. The side of the doorway through the well's wall: pale fitted blocks, stepping out at the head in three steps under the banner beam. The steps are what carries the beam so far out over the well. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 8The two banners. A banner of cut paper hangs behind each chain, 1.1 m wide and 11 m long, whole and bright in the town's colours with the pug's paw on it. Today they hang inside the play space, 0.4 m behind the chains. They move back to just behind it, so that what is in the play space is the chain you catch and nothing else. With the well's wall lit behind them they are the brightest things across the drop: the target. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 9Corbels under the doorstep. Three stepped stone brackets that carry the doorstep out over the well, with moss in their joints. The doorstep is the front of a stone landing, not a block stuck on a wall. (1.2 to 2.2 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The great cap, as a mushroom. Today it is a cream wedge with an orange band. It becomes the brightest spotted cap in the garden, with a fat rolled rim that droops over the well and cream gills under it. It is 4 m across the screen, as now, and about 6 m from front to back, most of that behind you. The three faces you land on keep their angles exactly. It squashes deepest of all the caps and springs highest (notes 36, 37, 46).
  • 11The beam, made longer. The temple's stone beam is real stone in the play space, and today it stops at x -303.5, short of both chains. It is made 2.6 m longer, to x -300.9, so both chains hang from it (Parker, 11-13). Under it, at the head of each chain, is a carved pug boss with a bronze ring. The new length can be held like the rest. The throw off the great cap tops out under it and passes below its end: to be checked again when it is built.
  • 12The garden on the temple's doorstep. Drawn onto the stone: one root across the doorstep's face, moss in the joints of its first blocks, and three pale bell sprouts no bigger than a hand. Nothing orange.
  • 13The doorstep's carved edge. The well side of the doorstep gets the temple's carved band, deep and pale: pug faces, belts and bones. Deep pale carving is the temple's sign for stone you can hold.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 14Fern on the garden's rock. One tuft growing from a crack, in front of the solid rock under the garden's sill. Nothing stands in front of the well, the chains or either rim. (1.3 to 1.9 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The great cap's deep squash and spring, with the biggest puff of spores in the garden.
  • The banners stir, and swing when you catch a chain.
  • Spores drift out of the garden's door over the well and stop half way. Torch smoke drifts out of the temple's.
  • Drips falling down the well, lit as they pass each door.
