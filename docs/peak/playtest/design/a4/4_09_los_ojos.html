El Jardín de Hongos · Area and background design

Challenge 4.9: Los Ojos

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

West of the bridge the roof comes down low, and behind you it comes down lower still: a brow of rock that hangs over the top of the west hall like the brow over an eye. It is the dimmest place in the garden, because the brow shuts out the glow from both ends. The brow's face is full of round hollows, and in each hollow a puffball grows on a short neck, straight out toward you: pale, round and faintly lit from inside. Seven eyes in seven sockets. Roots as thick as legs come through the roof here and run down the brow between the sockets. Their thin ends are the roots you hang from and their knuckles are the knots you hold. Under you there is nothing for 100 m.

What must not change

  • The seven puffballs exactly where they are, each throwing you straight away from its middle, each growing back after 4 seconds.
  • The four root knots on the ceiling and the seven hanging roots.
  • It is dim, not black, and the puffballs are the brightest things in it (note 45).

The three layers

Background

  • 1A far ledge of bell mushrooms. Along the bottom of the screen: a ledge on the hall's far wall with three small stands of young bell mushrooms, pale blue-green and dim. It is the only thing under you. Below it the hall goes down 90 m. (8.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The brow. The back wall under the low roof: rounded dark rock that hangs down to about 357 m, with a ragged lower edge. It carries on up into the dome in the roof. With it there, the dark place has a back, and the puffballs have something to grow from. (2.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The way out. Past the brow the roof goes up again and the back wall is far and mossy, with the garden's green glow on it. One mossy stalactite hangs clear of the two roots you catch there. It is the light you travel toward. (3.0 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 4Seven sockets. A round hollow in the brow behind each puffball, 1.9 m across, with the puffball's neck coming out of its middle toward you. A puffball that seems to float in the air today sits in a socket. When one bursts, its neck stays, and the new one swells on it. (1.2 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 5Roots down the brow. Thick roots that come through the roof and run down the brow's face between the sockets, flat against the rock. One hangs free as a loop: its bottom is the shelf you start from. In the dome, a knot of root as big as a cart wheel is what the two roots there hang from. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The puffballs, as eyes. Each is round, pale and tight as a drum, 1.2 m across, breathing slowly, with one small dark pore on its face like a pupil. Each lights a pale pool round itself, and the light goes out when it bursts (note 45, done). All seven look alike: the two you need are not marked.
  • 7Root knots. The four knots on the ceiling are knuckles of root, tan and dry, each with a root's line drawn on the ceiling running back from it.
  • 8Where the dim place begins and ends. These two lines are the edges of the dim place in the course file. The light fades over the last 7 m on each side of them and never snaps (note 45, done).

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • Each puffball's pore turns slowly to face you, so the eyes seem to follow you.
  • A burst is a cloud of pale spores, and the socket is dark until the next one grows.
  • A few spores drifting up out of the hall below, lit as they pass a puffball.
