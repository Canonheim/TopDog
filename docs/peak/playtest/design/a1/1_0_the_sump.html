La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Rest 1.0: The Sump and the frog's cave

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is the bottom level of the mine, and it is under water. When the mine worked, a watchman and a line of buckets kept this level dry, and a timber staging crossed it on piles. Then the water won. What is left above the surface is the heads of the piles, the caps of the drowned timber sets along the back wall, and the foot of the great waterfall, which lands here white and loud. Everything that falls far, from anywhere on the mountain, ends in this pool. At its west end, past the foot of the fall where nobody would think to swim, a doorway in the rock leads to the watchman's little room. It is dry, a lantern is lit in it, and the mine's frog lives there now.

What must not change

  • The pool, its water level and its floor, and the piles, each where it is.
  • The falling water's push, and the 6 m of calm under it just above the pool.
  • The way out is still the long swim east to the landing stage. The cave leads nowhere.
  • The only new things you can touch are the doorstep and the cave's floor, both from note 71.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The frog's cave. A dry room cut in the rock behind the west wall: 4.2 m wide and 2.7 m tall, with a doorway 1.2 m deep and 2.1 m high. Its floor and doorway come forward through the play space so you can walk in, and its front is cut away. Further back in it: a little stove with a kettle, an upturned ore bucket with the frog sitting on it, a shelf of boots and masks that the water has brought in, and one lit lantern, the only warm light at the bottom of the mine. Scratched up the door post are the watchman's marks of the water rising. The last one is level with the doorstep. (1.2 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 2The rock the cave is cut in. The course's west wall is a skin of rock 4 m thick with nothing behind it. The room needs rock all round it, so the wall is carried on to the west and back. It is black and running with the fall's spray. (1.2 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 3The foot of the waterfall. The fall lands here after 113 m. From the pool it is a white column 6 m wide going up out of sight, and it is the brightest thing in the picture. Where it hits, the water is white and the mist hides the west wall, so you find the cave's doorway by its warm light. The sheet of water itself comes forward through the play space: you swim through its foot. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The drowned level. Along the back wall, 3 to 4 m behind you: the timber sets of the bottom level, under water to their caps, and a side tunnel boarded shut that you see through the water. A barrel from the works above is wedged against one cap. What goes over the fall ends up here. (3.0 to 4.5 m behind you)
  • 5Carts on the bottom. Lying on the floor of the pool behind you, seen through the water: three old ore carts, one on its side. The cart from El Carrito Loco sinks here every time it goes over, so it has company. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The staging's back row. Behind each pile you can hold stands its twin, with a stub of the deck beam that joined them. The staging was a walkway two piles wide. Its deck is gone. (2.0 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7The way out. The mine's pug arrow, nailed to a back pile and pointing east along the piles. It is the only thing down here that tells you which way to swim. The kit has it. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The doorstep. New, from note 71: a rock step at the doorway, 0.8 m out from the wall, its top half a metre above the water and worn hollow in the middle. It is pale dry rock, so it reads as a thing to hold, and it is how you pull yourself out. The cave's floor behind it is the same pale rock.
  • 9The piles. Each pile gets a dark stain up to the old high water mark and a rusted iron band round its head, so it reads as the leg of something built and not as a pole in the water.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10The near bank. A low lip of black wet rock along the bottom of the frame, below the pool's floor. It is the front card that is there today, kept, because it covers only rock. (1.3 to 3.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Warm light from the doorway lying on the water and in the mist. It is how the cave is found.
  • The frog's croak, heard from about a screen away.
  • Rings spreading on the water from the drips off the roof, 80 m up.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 4: the note's first idea of a towel on a line becomes a towel on a peg. A line would be one more thing that looks like a rope.
  • Note 6 (done): the cart falls for real and sinks here.
  • Note 5 (done): the wind streaks are inside the waterfall's column, where the wind is.
  • Parker, 11-13: the room is whatever width works. It stays 4.2 m wide, with a 1.2 m doorway.
  • Course change: a doorway is cut through the west wall at the water line, a rock doorstep 0.8 m wide is added in the water in front of it, and the cave's floor is 5.4 m of new ground to stand on. Nothing else you touch moves.
