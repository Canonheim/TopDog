La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.3: Los Tablones

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

A plank walk along the cavern's wall, from the head of the ladder shaft towards the bucket lift on the pillar. It was built the mine's way: a square timber let into a socket in the rock every couple of metres, a strut under it, and planks nailed on top. Half way along it served a side tunnel. Five lengths of the planking are still nailed down and sound. In four places a timber has dropped out of the wall and the plank over it went too, and somebody later bridged each hole with a loose grey board that rests on nothing but its neighbours. Those four are the ones that break. Under the walk there is only air: 20 m down to the beach at the east end, 34 m to the water at the west.

What must not change

  • All nine planks where they are, the sag in the middle, and which four break.
  • The gaps between planks, and the ledge at the east end.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cavern's wall. Hand-cut rock, flat and pick-marked, 1.7 m behind the walk. It is dry above the planks and dark with wet below them. It is the wall the walk is fixed to. Today there is nothing behind the walk for 5 m, and its braces stand in the air. (2.9 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 2The bearers. Under each end of each nailed length: a square timber coming out of the wall to the walk, seen end-on, with a short strut under it. Nine of them. They take the place of today's slanting braces, which are in hold-coloured timber and lean across the open air under the walk. (1.2 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 3Four empty sockets. Under the middle of each loose board: the square hole in the rock where its bearer was, empty, with a pale scar below where the strut tore off. Before you step on a grey board you can see there is nothing under it. (2.9 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4A boarded side tunnel. In the wall behind the walk's soundest length, with two planks nailed across it and the stub of a gangway at its foot. It is what the walk was built to reach. (2.9 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 5The lower walk that fell. Six metres down the same wall: a row of sockets, two bearers, and one 3 m length of an older walk still in place. It is out of reach and leads nowhere. It tells you this wall sheds walks, and it gives the eye something to measure the drop by. (1.9 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 6Two lanterns. On iron brackets in the wall, 3 m above the planks: one over the head of the ladder, one over the tunnel's cap. Today they hang inside the play space, from nothing. (2.7 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 7The miners' bunting. Torn, strung along the wall high over the middle of the walk, where it is today. It is 4.5 m above the planks, well out of reach. (2.8 to 2.9 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8Nail heads. Each nailed length shows two rows of iron nail heads, one over each bearer. The four grey boards show none. It is a second way to tell them apart, for anyone who can't trust the colour.
  • 9The four loose boards. Grey and rotten, already cracked across, each with a corner gone: the game's one breakable look. They lie a little crooked, because nothing holds them.
