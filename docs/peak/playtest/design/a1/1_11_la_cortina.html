La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.11: La Cortina

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The last climb in the mine: 17 m up the back wall of the throat, right beside the top of the waterfall, to the dry shaft that goes up through the roof into the works above. No timber lasts in this spray, so the miners went up it on iron. They hammered staples into the wet rock, each a U of iron as wide as two hands, in two rows 1.3 m apart: a rung for the left hand and a rung for the right, because one row in that wet was not enough to trust. They are the same staples as the rungs up the pillar at El Cubo. All 28 are still there, rust orange, a few bent. The spray wets them in turn, one slow drip down each row. Five were bound round with pale cord by somebody who came later, with a little board nailed over each to turn the water aside, and those five stay dry. Somebody hung a rope from the lip, and somebody carried a life ring all the way up here and left it at the foot of the slope below.

What must not change

  • All 28 holds where they are, the two slow drips, dry for longer than wet, and the five holds that never get wet, in another colour (note 13; Parker, 11-13).
  • The rope from the lip to half way down (note 14), and the life ring's throw back to the balcony (note 15).
  • The balcony you start from and the lip you finish on.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The throat's back wall. The wall the staples are hammered into: black rock, polished and running with water, straight behind you. The holds float in the dark today. With the wall drawn, they are plainly in something. Inside the dry shaft at the top it is lined with planks on three timber frames, the same as every shaft in the mine. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 2The top of the waterfall. You are level with its top now. It comes through a slot in the roof as one smooth dark sheet and turns white a few metres down. It is 2.5 m from the west row of staples at the bottom and 4 m at the top, and the drips that wet the staples are its spray. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3Drip boards. Over four of the five staples that never get wet: a short board nailed to the wall at a slant, grey, 0.8 m long. The drip runs down to it, along it and off its low end. The fifth dry staple is the top one, inside the dry shaft. The boards are why those staples stay dry, and you can see it happen. (1.2 to 1.3 m behind you)
  • 4The top drift. Behind the balcony: the same timber frames and hand winch as on El Derrumbe's page. The pug arrow sign in the drift stands just inside the play space today. It moves back 0.4 m, against the drift's back wall. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5An empty camp. Against the drift's back wall: a bedroll, a cold lantern and a pickaxe, and nobody. Whoever it was got this far, looked at the last wall, and is not here. It is the second and last camp in the mine. (1.4 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6A lantern by the east wall. Today it hangs inside the play space between the rope and the wall, from nothing, like a third thing to take. It moves 0.8 m east and back, onto an iron bracket in the back wall. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 7The wheel's roof, from above. Under the slope you slide down if you fall: the two posts that hold it up and the trough from the fall, as on La Polea Grande's page. (1.3 to 2.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The staples. Parker, 11-13: every hold is an iron staple, a rust-orange U of iron hammered into the rock, about 0.5 m wide and standing a hand's width out from the wall, in place of today's rock knob. A few are bent down at one end or pulled a little crooked. All of them still hold. Each has a rust streak on the rock under it. Wet, a staple is a shiny blue-grey and drips, as the holds do now, and it is dry for longer than it is wet.
  • 9The five that stay dry. The same staples, bound round and round with pale cord, the way a tool's handle is bound. Cord is pale where iron is orange, so they keep the other colour they have today, and cord is a thing you hold. They are ringed here. They never get wet, and they are the rests.
  • 10The rope and its bracket. There today (note 14): 8 m of rope from an iron bracket under the lip. The bracket gets a strap back to the shaft's last timber frame, so you can see what the rope hangs from.
  • 11The life ring. There today (note 15), propped at the foot of the wet slope. It is the one painted thing in the mine, red and white, from the town: the town follows you up.
  • 12The balcony. The drift's floor timbers, carried out 3.6 m into the throat. Their sawn ends show at its west edge.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A front post on the east wall. In four broken lengths, in front of the rock east of the throat. It is 2.5 m from the rope and never in front of the staples. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The two slow drips, as built (note 13). At each drip board the drip runs along the board and falls off its low end.
  • A wet staple shines and drips from its two ends. A dry one is matte.
  • Mist drifting east from the fall across the lower rows.
  • The lit lantern is the last amber light. Above the lip the light is cold teal.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 13 (done, first-try numbers): the wet is a slow drip you can watch, and five holds never get wet. Parker, 11-13: dry for longer than wet, and the always-dry holds in another colour, both stay.
  • Course change (note 13; Parker, 11-13): each of the 28 holds becomes an iron staple, a U of iron about 0.5 m wide, in place of a rock knob. Each staple's middle is where its knob's middle is today, and the drips and their timing do not change. A bar 0.5 m wide is a little wider to catch than a knob, so the reaches should be tried again in the game.
  • The always-dry staples are bound with pale cord. That is this design's answer for their look. It keeps the pale colour they have now.
  • Note 14 (done): the rope from the lip to half way down.
  • Note 15 (done, first-try throw): the life ring at the foot of the wet slope.
  • In the play space today and moved out of it: the lantern between the rope and the wall (onto a bracket in the back wall, item 6), and the pug arrow sign in the drift (back 0.4 m, item 4).
  • The timber set at the drift's mouth stays as it is. Its posts stand behind and in front of the play space, not in it, at a doorway you only walk through.
