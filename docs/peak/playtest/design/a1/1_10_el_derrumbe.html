La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.10: El Derrumbe

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This was the miners' way between two levels of the mine: a timber-lined shaft, 3.6 m wide and 15 m tall, with a hoist in the front half to haul ore up and a ladder way in the back half for the men. Then the roof came in. The falling rock went down the middle of the shaft and snapped every cross-beam on its way. What is left sticking out of the two walls are the broken ends of those beams, and they are your holds. The ladders behind are still mostly there, out of reach. The roof is still coming down.

What must not change

  • The eight timber stubs, each exactly where it is: two on the east wall, three on the west, two on the east, one on the west.
  • The shaft's width, its slick walls, the rubble slope and the fallen beam at its foot.
  • The two lines the rocks fall down, 20 cm apart in the middle of the shaft, and their rhythm (note 11).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The chimney. The hole in the roof the rocks fall out of, black inside, with snapped roof timbers and torn planking hanging round its mouth (note 12). (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 2The hoist's wheel. Knocked crooked at the head of the shaft. It says what this shaft was for. No cable hangs from it: a hanging line reads as something to hold. The cable is down at the bottom. (1.8 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3The shaft's back wall. Plank lining held by a timber frame every 1.3 m, some planks gone with dark rock showing. Two pale scars run down its middle where the rocks scrape it: they mark the two lines the rocks fall down, before you have seen a rock. (2.4 to 2.7 m behind you)
  • 4A lantern left on a landing. Today a lantern hangs in the very middle of the shaft, in the path of the rocks. It moves here, where it lights the back wall so a falling rock shows up dark against it. (1.6 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 5The ladder way. In the back half of the shaft: a half landing behind every stub, and short ladders between them, one snapped. You can see the easy way up the miners had, and you can't get to it. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The foot of the fall. The rubble heap carries on back into the scene behind the slope you walk up. Half buried in it: the hoist's crushed bucket and its cable in a heap. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7The lower level's back wall. Timber frames along the gallery, and a side tunnel boarded shut with two planks nailed across. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 8A warning sign. Beside the rubble: the mine's skull-pug diamond, for falling rock. The kit has it. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 9The upper level. Timber frames, and the hand winch that worked the hoist, standing at the shaft's edge with its handle in the air. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The rock between the two levels. The biggest thing on the screen, and today a blank brown face. It gets a slanting seam of gold ore and a few old drill holes, drawn on its face.
  • 11Sawn ends. Each stub's beam runs on into the rock, and its square sawn end shows on the rock's face beside it. You can read where the holds are from the row of little squares up each wall.
  • 12Splintered ends and empty sockets. The stubs get splintered ends, and the walls get an empty socket wherever a beam is gone altogether. The three long lunges across the shaft are at exactly those places.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13The shaft's two front posts. Thin, each broken in two or three places. They stand in front of the rock at the edges of the shaft, never in front of the open shaft, so they frame the climb and cover nothing you need to see. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)
  • 14A low lip of rubble. Along the very bottom of the frame, below the gallery's floor. It covers nothing you can stand on. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A puff of dust where a rock hits the fallen beam.
  • A few motes of dust drifting close to the camera.
