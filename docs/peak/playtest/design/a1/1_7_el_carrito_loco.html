La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.7: El Carrito Loco

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of El Diente was the mine's high road. Ore came down from the upper works to this track and was pushed east along it to the bucket lift. West of the pillar the track crossed 20 m of open cavern on a deck slung by chains from a long beam under the roof. That deck has fallen and taken the track with it. What is left is the cart on its siding, held by one wooden chock, a stub of track on two slanting struts with boards nailed across its end, and one last chain, hanging where the deck used to be, too far out to reach from the boards. The track points straight at the waterfall, and that is where the cart goes. The chain is the only way off it.

What must not change

  • The cart, its 0.7 s wait, its speed, and that it falls for real a cart's length past the boards (note 6).
  • One chain (Parker, 11-13), hung from the first roof beam, out of reach of the boards.
  • The 3.4 m of pillar top east of the cart, to rest on.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The waterfall. For the first time you see it side-on and whole: a white column 30 m to the west, from the roof down past the bottom of the picture. The track is aimed at its middle. The sheet itself comes forward through the play space, and the cart falls through it. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2Two slanting struts. From the pillar's west face up to the underside of the track's stub: they are what holds it out over the drop. Today the kit's 6 m trestle leg is placed here, inside the play space, and by the course's numbers its feet are in the air. The struts take its place, at the back. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 3The beam under the roof. What the chain hangs from. The first of Las Vigas has a hanger post behind it, up to the roof, and behind that runs a second long beam that never broke. No chain hangs from the back beam: a hanging chain in the background would look like one to take. It has empty iron eyes where the deck's other chains were. (1.2 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 4A second cart, off the rails. On the back of the pillar's top, tipped against the wall behind the siding, grey: a cart that never made its last run. With it, the back rail of the track, which is whole. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5Two lamp posts. The two lanterns here hang inside the play space today, 3.4 m over the track, from nothing. Each gets a thin timber post with an iron bracket at the back of the track to hang from. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 6The warning sign. The skull-pug diamond before the cart. Today it stands just inside the play space. It moves back 0.4 m, to the back edge of the track. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The one chain. Parker, 11-13: one chain, not two. It hangs 2.6 m past the end of the boards, where the far chain hangs today, which is 1.6 m further out than the near one. From the boards it is out of reach, so riding the cart is the only way to it. It is a real chain with links you can see, the kit's chain from higher up the mountain, in the rusty red the chains have now. It hangs from an iron eye bolted through the first beam, and its tail is at chest height over the cart's road. Catch it while the cart is falling and you keep the chain: the cart drops away under you.
  • 8The near chain goes. The chain that hangs 1 m from the end of the boards today is taken away (crossed out here). With the boards solid it could be taken without riding the cart. An empty iron eye is left in the beam where it hung.
  • 9Boards across the track's end. There today (note 6): the miners' own stop, three boards nailed across the last 1.6 m of rail, with the rails' ends bent down past them. A track that ends in the air always gets these. The rails themselves lie flat on the track you walk on and stay as part of it.
  • 10The chock. A wooden wedge under the cart's west wheel. Your weight in the cart rocks it off the wedge. It is why the cart waits, and why it goes. It is drawn on the track and is too small to be a thing to hold.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11A front strut's foot. The front twin of the back struts is gone. What is left is its lower end in two short lengths, in front of the pillar's rock and nowhere else. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The chock jumps out and skitters off the edge when the cart starts.
  • The chain sways a little and its links clink. Nothing else in the picture moves but the waterfall.
  • A white burst in the waterfall where the cart goes through it.
