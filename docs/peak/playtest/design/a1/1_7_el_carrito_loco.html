Challenge 1.7: El Carrito Loco

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of El Diente was the mine's high road. Ore came down from the upper works to this track, and at the pillar's west edge it went down the incline: a cart road on timber that tips over the edge, drops 12 m beside the pillar, runs through a dip and climbs a short rise to a landing by the cavern's wall. The rise was there to slow a loaded cart. A haul chain drew the empty cart back up. It ran down from the roof beams through three iron rings on pins in a rib of the wall. The landing has fallen and taken the top of the rise with it, so the rise now ends in the air, aimed at the waterfall. The haul chain broke under the roof, and its last 9 m hangs from the lowest ring, out where the landing was. The cart still stands on its siding, held by one wooden chock. Step in and it goes over the edge far faster than any cart was meant to, and the rise throws it. The chain is the only way off.

What must not change

  • The cart on the pillar's top, its chock, its 0.7 s wait, and the 3.4 m of pillar top east of it to rest on.
  • The cart's road (note 103): over the edge, 12 m down, through the dip, and off the rise, far faster than today.
  • One chain, on an iron ring in the wall, caught out of the air. Riding the cart is the only way to it.
  • Three rings in all, rising east to the first beam of Las Vigas.
  • The cart falls for real once it leaves the rise, and a rider who catches the chain keeps the chain (note 6).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The waterfall. You see it side-on and whole for the first time: a white column from the roof down past the bottom of the picture. The rise is aimed at its middle. The sheet itself comes forward through the play space. The cart flies into it, and so does a rider who misses the chain. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The chain rib. A rib of the cavern's back wall that comes forward to 1.6 m behind you and slants up from the fall's side towards the roof. The three rings are driven into it. It is painted rock, made as one piece for this place. Its beds are long wavy bands of flat colour in three grey-violets, slanting the way the rib slants, and one band of ore runs along it under the rings. There is no ink inside its face. Two breaks are real shape: an overhang along its lower edge with a painted shadow under it, and one small outcrop below the middle ring with a clump of flat painted ferns on it, kept green by the spray. It stands 0.3 m out of the rib, still behind the play space. There is no dark pocket in its face. Its outline leans and is notched. Behind it the back wall carries on, 3 m back and duller. (1.6 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The bench under the incline. A bench of the same back wall, 14 m under the track. The incline's timber stands on it. It is painted the same way as the rib, darker and wetter, with an uneven top. It is all behind you: where you are, there is only air down to the Sump. (1.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 4The incline's timber. Three timber posts stand on the bench, at the back. Each carries a cap that comes forward under the rails, so the road you ride is the near end of its own frame. A brace and a tie hold the posts together. The front posts are gone. West of the lip, two post stumps on the bench show where the landing stood. (1.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5Three slanting struts. From the pillar's west face: two up to the stub of level track, and one long one up to the head of the drop. Today the kit's 6 m trestle leg is placed here, inside the play space, and by the course's numbers its feet are in the air. The struts take its place, at the back. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 6The beam under the roof. The back beam of Las Vigas and its four hanger posts. The haul chain ran along it on its way down to the rings. Nothing hangs from it now, and nothing hangs from the first beam in front of it. (1.2 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 7A second cart, off the rails. On the back of the pillar's top, tipped against the wall behind the siding, grey: a cart that never made its last run. With it, the back rail of the track, which is whole. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 8Four lanterns. The two lanterns over the track hang inside the play space today, 3.4 m up, from nothing. Each gets a thin timber post with an iron bracket at the back of the track. Two more are new. One hangs under the cap at the lip, so you can see where the road ends. One is on a bracket on the rib above the chain's ring, so the chain is lit. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 9The warning sign. The skull-pug diamond before the cart. Today it stands just inside the play space. It moves back 0.4 m, to the back edge of the track. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The cart's new road. Note 103. The rails no longer stop at the end of the stub. They bend over its edge, drop 9 m at 72 degrees, sweep through a dip 12 m under the track, and climb 1.5 m to a lip that points up and west at 45 degrees. The road is rails on two timber stringers with sleepers. It is honey-coloured timber and you can hold it, like the stub. The cart reaches 12 m a second in the dip. Today it never passes 5.
  • 11The broken stop at the lip. A track that ends in the air gets boards nailed across its rails (note 6). The boards that were across the end of the stub move here, to the track's new end. They are snapped through in the middle, a cart's width, with a stump left either side and the rails' ends bent up past them. Carts have been through here before yours.
  • 12The throw. Dashed here: the cart's flight. It leaves the lip at 10 m a second with you standing in it. A little over a second later it passes the chain, near the top of its arc, with the chain's tail at your chest. Put a hand up and take it. The cart flies on into the waterfall and falls to the Sump.
  • 13The chain on the lowest ring. One chain, 9 m long: a real chain with links you can see, the kit's chain from higher up the mountain, in rusty red. It hangs from an iron ring on a pin, 7.4 m east of the waterfall. Its tail hangs 0.9 m above the cart's floor where the cart passes, which is how high today's chains hang over the cart. Catch it and you keep the chain: the cart drops away under you. Then climb it to its ring.
  • 14Two more rings. Each is a rust-orange iron ring 0.5 m across on an iron pin as thick as a wrist. The pin is driven into the rib and comes forward 1.6 m, so the ring hangs in the middle of the play space, as the forge's wall rings do. The three rings rise east, 4.4 m apart and 2 m higher each time. From the last one you fling up to the underside of the first beam of Las Vigas (dashed).
  • 15The two old chains go. Replaced by note 103. Both chains that hang from the first roof beam today are taken away (crossed out here). Nothing hangs from that beam.
  • 16The chock. A wooden wedge under the cart's west wheel. Your weight in the cart rocks it off the wedge. It is why the cart waits, and why it goes. It is drawn on the track and is too small to be a thing to hold.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 17A front strut's foot. The front twin of the back struts is gone. What is left is its lower end in two short lengths, in front of the pillar's rock and nowhere else. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The chock jumps out and skitters off the edge when the cart starts.
  • The wheels shriek down the drop and throw sparks through the dip.
  • The chain sways a little and its links clink. Nothing else in the picture moves but the waterfall.
  • A white burst in the waterfall where the cart goes through it.

  • Course change, the chain: one chain, 9.0 m long, from a ring at (-230.6, 44.2). Its tail is at 35.2 m. Parker's drawing has the ring about 6 m east of the waterfall. It is 7.4 m east here, so that the three flings up from it stay inside what the mine asks.
