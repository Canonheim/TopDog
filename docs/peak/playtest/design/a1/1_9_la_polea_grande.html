La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.9: La Polea Grande

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The mine's biggest machine, at the foot of the throat the waterfall comes down. It is a winding wheel 6 m across that hauled the big ore buckets up from the bottom level, 85 m below. It was never turned by hand. A trough catches the edge of the fall and tips it onto a water wheel on the same axle, just behind, and the water wheel turns the winding wheel. The water has not stopped, so neither has the wheel: one slow turn every 20 seconds, with nothing left on it to wind. The planks under it are the landing where the buckets were unhooked. The black sloping roof over it kept spray and falling rock off the machine. You take the rim at the bottom, and it lifts you up the side nearest the fall, over the top, and down to the porch of the upper gallery.

What must not change

  • The wheel: its size, its place, its speed and the way it turns.
  • The staging under it as one run of planks (note 8), the porch, and the gallery floor.
  • The slick roof over the wheel, and the life ring at its foot (note 15).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The waterfall. Right beside you. The wheel's rim passes within a metre of it, and for a quarter of the ride you are going up with the water coming down at your shoulder. It is white, loud, and the brightest thing here by far. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The water wheel. On the same axle, a metre and a half behind the wheel you ride: a wheel with buckets round its rim, 5 m across, running with water. It turns with the front wheel, so you can see that one drives the other. (1.7 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 3The trough from the fall. A timber trough from the edge of the waterfall to just past the top of the water wheel, under the sloping roof. Water runs along it and falls onto the wheel's east side. That is why the wheel turns the way it does. (1.5 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 4The frame that carries it. Two long bearers run out from the rock under the axle, on a slanting strut. The staging hangs under them on two posts, and the porch you step off onto is their near end. Today the staging's brace is a hold-coloured timber in the play space. It goes. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5The roof's posts. Two posts at the back, from the bearers up to the sloping roof. They show that the black slab over the wheel is a roof on a frame, not a loose rock. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 6The upper gallery. The same back wall as on El Derrumbe's page: timber frames along the gallery and a side tunnel boarded shut. The ore crate that stands in the play space today, where it looks like a step, moves to the back wall. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 7A lantern at the gallery's mouth. Today it hangs just outside the wheel's rim, where you come down. It moves onto the back post of the mouth's timber set. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The hub. An iron-banded hub with the square end of the axle showing in its middle. The axle is the one thing that goes from the wheel you hold back to the wheel that drives it.
  • 9The staging. One run of planks, as it is now (note 8), with two rows of nail heads where the hanging posts take it.
  • 10The sloping roof and the life ring. The roof is black wet boards with pale drips running down to its lower edge, which spills into the fall. Propped at its foot is the red and white life ring (note 15). It belongs to La Cortina's page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The mouth's front post. In two lengths, one below the gallery's floor and one above its roof, both in front of rock. The length between them, which would stand in front of the doorway, is gone. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)
