La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.8: Las Vigas

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Under the flat roof of the cavern, 78 m above the water, the miners hung one long beam on four posts bolted into the roof. It ran from above the incline to the landing under the winding wheel, and the incline's haul chain ran along it. When the incline's landing fell, the chain went tight and snapped the beam in three places. The four pieces are still there, each on its own post, each sagging to a different height, with 2.5 m of air between one splintered end and the next. A second beam at the back never broke, and shows the line the first one had. You come to the first piece from below, off the last of the three rings, and take it by its underside. The spray of the fall has turned the undersides of the other three black and slick. From there on you hold tops and ends only: swing from an end, let go, land on the next top. The waterfall is in front of you the whole way.

What must not change

  • The four beams: where each hangs, how long it is, how high.
  • The first beam can be held from underneath. The other three can be held by their tops and ends only (note 103).
  • The three rings that lead up to the first beam, and the staging you land on at the end.
  • Nothing between you and the water.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The waterfall. Straight ahead, 6 m past the last beam, and you fling towards it three times. From here it is a sheet 6 m wide, white, with the winding wheel turning slowly in its spray at the top left. It lights the beams from in front. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The chain rib. The same piece as on El Carrito Loco's page: a rib of the cavern's back wall, 1.6 m behind you, slanting up from the fall's side to just under the beams. It is painted rock: long wavy beds of flat colour, a few big real breaks, a leaning notched outline. The three rings are driven into it. (1.6 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The back beam. The twin of the beam you are crossing, close under the roof and all in one piece, 18.5 m long. It shows what the four pieces were. Along it are three iron rollers that the haul chain ran over. Nothing hangs from them. (1.5 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 4Four hanger posts. One behind the middle of each piece, from the piece up to the roof. Today a post in hold-coloured timber stands in the middle of the play space on top of each beam. It moves to the back and goes grey, so the top of a beam is clear to land on. (1.2 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 5Two lanterns. On iron brackets on the hanger posts of the first and third pieces, at the back. Today they hang inside the play space, just over the beams. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 6Stalactites, kept off the route. The roof's stalactites hang only east of the first beam. By the course's numbers a 6 m one is placed at the roof between the second and third beams today. That one goes: nothing may hang in the air you fling through. (1.5 to 1.9 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The three rings. Note 103. Three rust-orange iron rings 0.5 m across, each on an iron pin driven into the rib behind, rise east under the beams. The chain you caught off the cart hangs from the lowest. Climb it, then fling ring to ring, and from the last ring up to the underside of the first beam at its west end (dashed).
  • 8Black undersides. Note 103. The undersides of the second, third and fourth beams are black and glossy with wet from the fall's spray: the mine's look for a thing you can't hold. Their tops and their ends stay dry and honey-coloured. The first beam is out of the spray and dry all round. Parker's drawing marks the underside of the staging the same way.
  • 9The two old chains go. Replaced by note 103. Both chains that hang from the first beam today are taken away (crossed out here). Nothing hangs from the first beam.
  • 10Splintered ends. Every end that faces a gap is splintered. The two outer ends are sawn square. So the four read as one beam, broken in three places. On the second and third pieces the splintered west end is the hold you swing from.
  • 11Iron stirrups. A rust-orange iron stirrup round each piece where its hanger post takes it. It is what the piece hangs in, and it is the one spot on each beam that looks strong. Under the three wet pieces the strap is as black as the timber.
  • 12Bolt plates in the roof. Drawn on the roof above each post: an iron plate with four bolt heads.

Small effects, no models

  • A piece dips and creaks on its post when you land on it. It does not break.
  • Spray drifting in from the waterfall across the last gap, and a slow drip off the three wet undersides.

  • Course change, the undersides: the lowest 0.1 m of the second, third and fourth beams becomes slick for the whole 2.0 m of each. Their tops and their end faces stay as they are. The first beam does not change.
  • The staging: Parker's drawing has the same purple mark under the staging at the far end (x -237.2 to -232.2). His words name the beams only. This page takes the drawing: the staging's underside is slick too. Its own page, La Polea Grande, is not redone here.
  • Course change, the flings: the first is as today, from under the first beam's west end to the second beam's top. The second and third can no longer start from under a beam. They start from a beam's west end: from (-222.8, 51.8) to the third beam's top at (-226.3, 50.8), 3.5 m across and 1.0 m down, and from (-227.3, 50.6) to the fourth beam's top at (-230.8, 52.3), 3.5 m across and 1.7 m up, a fling of 4.0 m a second.
  • The start of Las Vigas stays on top of the first beam. You arrive hanging under its west end, and can pull up round the end to rest.
  • In the play space today in hold colours, and moved out of it: the post that stands on top of each of the four beams (to the back, grey, item 4), and the two lanterns over the beams (back onto brackets, item 5).
  • Note 4: nothing hangs from the beams now. The one chain hangs lower down, from the first ring.
