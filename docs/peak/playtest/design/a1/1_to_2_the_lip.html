La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Join 1 to 2: The lip: from the mine to the black waters

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the dry shaft, where the mine stops and the works begin. The miners' shaft came up through the roof here into a river cave. A generation later the company built its water works along that river and laid brick straight on top of the mine: a weir across the river's end, a pier to keep the water out of the old shaft, and a brick arch over the shaft's mouth, standing on the miners' last timber set because it was there. So within one screen everything changes hands. Posts and planks give way to brick and stone, rust-orange pipe appears overhead, the amber lantern is below your feet and the next one is greenish. And you find out what the waterfall is: the works' river, black and fast, going over the edge 6 m from where you stand.

What must not change

  • The lip ledge, the dry shaft, the pier with its peaked top, and the slot the river falls through.
  • The last rows of La Cortina and the rope's bracket below the lip. The first pipe of El Borde above it.
  • The river's level and its pull towards the edge.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mine's side: the throat's back wall. Below the roof: the wet black rock of La Cortina, and the plank lining inside the dry shaft. This is the last of the mine's way of building. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 2The river going over. At the back, a brick sill with a stone coping holds the river 1 m deep. The water slides over it, bends, and drops into the slot as one dark sheet. Nothing here is a block of water with a flat end (note 22). From the ledge you look straight across the top of the fall you have climbed beside since the mine's first room. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The works' side: a brick wall in bays. Above the roof: the back wall of the river tunnel, in slate-grey brick, divided by flat piers every 4 m, with a round arch bricked up in each bay. It is the first brick in the game, and round arches are only ever the works'. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 4The last timber set, and the first arch on top of it. In the mouth of the dry shaft stands the mine's last set: two grey posts and a cap. The works' first arch springs straight off that cap, brick on timber, with a cast iron pug in its middle. It is lower than every other arch on the wall, because the shaft was here first. This one object is the join. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5Two lanterns. The mine's last, amber, on the west post of the timber set, lighting the shaft from inside. The works' first, greenish, hung high over the ledge where the works have it today. Climbing out of the shaft you pass from one into the other. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The lip ledge. Two layers, and they should look like two. Below: the miners' rock ledge, pale and pick-marked. On top of it: a course of pale stone coping, the first of the copings you stand on all through the works. The ledge's west end, where your hand first lands, shows five courses of dry pale brick under the coping.
  • 7The pier. Between the slot and the shaft: a brick pier with a peaked stone cap, black and slimy with the river's spray. Wet brick is the works' way of saying you can't hold it. Its foot, inside the mine's roof, is rock.
  • 8The rope's bracket. The mine's last piece of iron (note 14), strapped back to the last timber set.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 9One post, timber below and brick above. In front of the pier's rock and nowhere else: a grey timber post up to the roof line, and on top of it a brick pier half a metre wide. It is the foreground's own version of the join. (1.2 to 1.6 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The light changes as you climb out: warm on your back from the shaft, cold teal on your face from the tunnel.
  • A pale shimmer off the river on the undersides of the arches. It moves but it does not pulse.
  • The fall's roar drops to a hiss the moment your head is above the roof.
