Area and background design

Biome 1: La Mina Vieja

The old mine, 30 m under the trailhead to 90 m above it. Timber, rails and mud, round the great waterfall.

Design only. This sheet comes first: every area in the biome is drawn from it.

What this place is

The town's grandparents cut this mine by hand for gold. It is one big cavern with a 113 m waterfall down its middle, and the workings climb round the fall: galleries, ladder ways, plank catwalks, a bucket lift, a cart track on a ledge, roof beams, the winding wheel, a hoist shaft. Then the water got in and the roof started to fall, and the miners walked out and left everything where it stood.

Since then the only people through here are the ones who came to climb the Peak. They left lanterns, bunting from town, and one of themselves, sitting by a cold lantern with tally marks scratched on the wall beside him.

So every place in the mine is two things at once: a piece of a working mine that made sense on the day it was left, and the same place eighty years on, wet, rotted and half fallen. What you climb is what is still standing.

How it is built

  • Square-set timbering. Posts 0.35 m square, a cap across the top, a sill at the foot, a set every 1.3 m in a shaft and every 2.6 m in a gallery. The same posts and caps everywhere: this is the mine's one way of building.
  • Plank lining between the sets, nailed on. Where the lining is gone, wet rock shows.
  • Iron only where the miners needed it: rails on sleepers, strap brackets, chain, bolts, the rims of wheels. All of it rust orange.
  • Rock cut by hand: flat pick-marked faces, old drill holes, and slanting seams of gold ore.
  • Rock is made as paint, not built of blocks (the walls and masses rule). A wall, a pillar or the roof is a few big planes with a leaning, notched outline. Its beds are long wavy bands of flat colour in three or four tones, slanting the way the ore seams slant, with the wet as darker bands and nothing that narrows to a point. Only a few big breaks are real shape: an overhang with a painted shadow under it, a ledge, a leaning slab or a fallen block. No dark slot or pocket is cut into a face. A few small outcrops stand out of each wall, and what grows on them is the mine's own: pale ferns and moss where the waterfall's spray or a drip keeps the rock wet, and little pale mushrooms on old timber, each one a flat painted card.
  • Timber lining is made the same way: its planks are long flat bands of colour on one face, and only the sets' posts, caps and sills are real shape. A bay where planks are gone is painted as the wet rock behind, not cut as a hole. The mine has no brick of its own. The one brick thing you see from it, the works' first arch at the lip, is made in flat courses of colour, not brick by brick.
  • Hoisting gear: the winding wheel, buckets, a hand winch, pulleys. Every one stands on a timber frame of the same posts.
  • The miners painted nothing, carved nothing and laid no brick. Paint and brick belong to the works above; carving belongs to the temple.

Colour and light

Almost dark. Warm amber pools round the lanterns, and between them you see little. The one cool thing in the room is the waterfall: pale blue-white, always somewhere in the picture, and brighter than anything else in the background. Far rock is a cold grey-violet, so the warm timber of the climb stands out in front of it.

The three layers in this biome

LayerWhat goes in it here
BackgroundThe rest of the working, carried on behind you: the back wall with its timber sets, ladder ways, boarded side tunnels, ore chutes, and further back the far wall of the cavern and the waterfall.
Play spaceThe beams, planks, rungs, rails and rock that are still standing. Each one is the near end of something that runs back into the background.
ForegroundThe front post of a timber set, in broken lengths, and a low lip of rubble along the bottom of the frame. Only in front of rock, never in front of the open space you climb through.

How you come in, and how you leave

Coming in: On the cart, down the tunnel from the station. The tunnel's timber sets have gone from whitewashed to grey and wet by the time the buffer tips you out, and you have already seen the waterfall cavern once, through a gap, on the way down.

Leaving: Up beside the top of the waterfall to its lip. The fall turns out to be the works' river going over a brick weir: the last timber set of the mine stands against the first brick arch of the works, and the amber lantern light gives way to cold teal.

Running through it

  • The waterfall is seen only where it really is: from the Sump, El Carrito Loco, Las Vigas, La Polea Grande, La Cortina and the lip. Everywhere else you hear it and see its cold light on the rock.
  • The ones who tried: one frozen climber by a cold lantern, with tally marks.
  • The town follows you up: the miners' bunting, torn.
  • The pug: on the danger signs and the way signs, as a skull and as an arrow.
  • The frog: in a little cave room at the west end of the Sump, the lowest place in the game.

Rules that hold on the whole mountain

  1. What you can hold is dry, pale and matte. What you can't is dark, glossy and wet. This is the mountain's first law and no design breaks it.
  2. Where a thing stands tells you more than its colour (Parker, 11-13). If it is in the play space you can generally hold it. Scenery stays out of the play space, behind or in front, and is greyer, darker and cooler than the things you climb.
  3. Everything you touch is the near end of something real that carries on behind it, like a ladder and its rungs.
  4. Nothing thin hangs in the background near the climb: it reads as a rope (notes 4 and 18). A hanging thing is a real rope, or thick, or far back.
  5. The foreground is sparse: thin posts and low lips, in front of solid rock or below the lowest floor, never over the open space you climb through (note 40).
  6. Every moving thing shows its machine: what drives it stands in the background (notes 19, 36, 57).
  7. Every danger shows where it comes from and warns before it acts (notes 12, 21, 23, 56).
  8. Every breakable thing wears the game's one breakable look: cracked already, with a corner gone (notes 16, 24, 31, 53, 64).
  9. The painted back wall never stops inside the frame, so there are no black holes (note 51). The insides of small pits can't be held (note 52).
  10. One frog is hidden in every biome (note 60), in a little room you can walk into. A room you can enter is cut away at the front: floor, back wall and roof, no front wall.
  11. Far backgrounds are flat facade pieces (Parker, 11-13): one cut-out card standing well back, painted in a few broad rows that haze toward the horizon, with a few bold silhouettes cut into its ridge. Never modelled terrain, and never an empty plane running to a ruled horizon. The station's dry hills are the worked example.
  12. Open ground is designed, never one flat colour (Parker, 11-13): one painted slab with no height that runs off every edge of the screen, laid out as a real place in bands from the camera back (tufts and stones, a worn road with ruts, a trodden yard, paving where people built, patched open country, paler far bands), with real plants and rocks standing on its darker patches. No more than about 4 m of one flat colour on screen. The station's ground is the worked example.
  13. Walls and rock are painted, not built of blocks (Parker, 11-13: "I like the painted look"). A wall, a cliff or any mass you climb is a few big planes with a designed, uneven outline. Its layers and courses are flat painted colour on those planes, as long wavy lines and bands, with no ink inside a face and nothing that tapers to a point (no streaks or wedges: "it does not need these random triangles ... just the painted lines look lovely"). Only a few big breaks are real shape: an overhang, a deep recess, a ledge, a leaning slab. No repeated square panel, no long ruled edge. The holds and the tops you stand on stay exactly where the course has them. The mass's own body carries on behind it, duller, as one big form. Worked example: the split tower top of the wind cliffs (design/walls_example_before.jpg and walls_example_after.png).
