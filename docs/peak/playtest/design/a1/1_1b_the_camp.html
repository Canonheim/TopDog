La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Rest 1.1b: The frozen climber's camp

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The west end of the gallery, between the mud pit and the mouth that opens on the cavern. The ladder shaft comes down through the roof here, so this was where the miners waited their turn to climb: there is a bench cut into the back wall for them. Long after they left, somebody who came to climb the Peak made camp on that bench, at the foot of the first hard climb. He tried the ladder, came back, scratched a mark, and tried again. He is still here, sitting against the post with his lantern gone cold.

What must not change

  • The frozen climber, seated exactly where he is.
  • The floor, the foot of the ladder, and the mouth onto the incline.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The gallery's back wall. The same plank lining and timber sets as the rest of the gallery, on good ground here, so the posts are 2 m apart or more and all of them stand. (1.2 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 2The miners' waiting room. A shallow room cut into the back wall, 2.1 m wide and 1.2 m deep, with a plank bench. The climber's bedroll is laid out on the bench. Above it is a rail of wooden pegs with one miner's lamp still on it. Today the bedroll lies in the play space at his feet. It moves back onto the bench. (1.3 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 3His things. Against the wall beside him: his lantern, cold, with no light on it. A coil of rope lying flat. A pickaxe leaning on the post. They are the kit's camp pieces, set a little further back than today so that nothing lies where you walk. (1.2 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 4The tally marks. Scratched into the lining above his shoulder, in fives: the count he kept. Pale scratches on grey planks, the brightest marks on the wall. The last row stops part way along. (1.3 to 1.3 m behind you)
  • 5The lantern that still burns. The miners' lantern above him is lit, as it is today, but it hangs on an iron bracket from the roof timber at the back wall and not in the play space. His own is not lit. The one pool of amber light at this end of the gallery falls on him and on the marks. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 6The bunting. The miners' bunting from town, torn, strung high between the mouth and the shaft. Today it runs 8 m along the gallery and passes behind the foot of the ladder. It is shortened to 3.5 m, so that no line crosses behind the rungs. (1.4 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 7The winch at the head of the incline. In a second cut by the mouth: the hand winch that hauled ore up the incline from the bottom level. Its drum is bare. It tells you that the slope outside was a track, and that the timbers you walk up it on were its sleepers. (1.3 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 8The cavern, first seen. Through the mouth: the cavern's far wall, cold grey-violet, and mist. You can't see the waterfall from here, because the pillar stands in the way. You hear it, and its blue-white light lies on the mouth's west post and along the floor. (6.0 to 6.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9A worn path. The floor between where he sits and the foot of the ladder is worn pale, a track one boot wide. It is drawn on the floor and changes nothing you stand on.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10A low lip of rubble. Along the bottom of the frame, below the floor, in front of rock only. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)
