La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.4: La Cuerda del Minero

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The plank walk used to go all the way to the pillar. Its last 5 m fell while the mine was still working, and the miners did not rebuild it. They tied a rope to the arm of the bucket lift, which sticks out over the gap, and swung across. That rope is still there. It hangs from an iron rod under the arm, greased so that its hook would turn, which is why you can't climb past the rope's top. On the far side is the pillar's shoulder, with the socket of the walk's last bearer still in its face. Below is 34 m of air and the water.

What must not change

  • The rope, its length and where it hangs. The greased rod above it.
  • The gap, the walk's end and the 4 m landing on the pillar's shoulder.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cavern's wall, and where the last span was. The same wall as behind Los Tablones, carried on to the pillar. Across the gap, at the walk's height: three empty sockets and one bearer snapped off short. The gap is plainly a piece of the walk that is missing. (2.9 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 2The foot of the hoistway. Flat against the wall: the ragged bottom of the dark boards that the lift's weight and bucket run in front of. It is there today (note 4) and belongs to El Cubo. From here it is a dark shape at the top right that says a machine is above you. (2.4 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 3The arm's back half. The arm you will walk along is one of a pair. Its twin is straight behind it. A slanting brace carries the pair from the pillar's face, and a cross timber ties them to the rock. Today the brace is in hold-coloured timber in the play space. It moves back and goes grey. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 4Two lanterns. One on an iron bracket in the wall over the pillar's shoulder. One standing on the arm's back twin. Today both hang inside the play space, from nothing. (1.3 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 5A warning sign. The skull-pug diamond on a post at the back edge of the walk's broken end. The miners' own notice that the walk stops. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The rod's eye and strap. The rope is knotted through an iron eye at the foot of the rod, and the rod hangs from an iron strap round the arm. So you can see the whole chain of what holds you: arm, strap, rod, eye, rope. The rod is black and glossy with grease, with a drip at its foot.
  • 7The two broken ends. The walk's last plank ends in splinters. In the face of the pillar's shoulder, level with it, is the socket and the stump of the bearer that carried the other end.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 8The lift frame's front post. One thin post in broken lengths, in front of the pillar's rock beside the iron rungs. It never crosses the open air. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)
