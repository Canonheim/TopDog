La Mina Vieja · Area and background design

Challenge 1.5: El Cubo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The bucket lift, built up the side of the pillar between the walk's level and the station 12 m above it. An ore bucket hangs on one side of a pulley and an iron weight on the other, so that a full bucket going down hauled the weight up, and the weight coming down hauled the empty bucket back. It is how the ore from the pillar's top got down to the plank walk. It was left as it stood, empty and in balance, and it still goes: the weight one way, the bucket the other, with a wait at each end. You ride the weight. The iron rungs up the pillar beside it were the lift men's ladder.

What must not change

  • The iron rungs, the arm, and the station deck with its 2.1 m gap from the wall.
  • The weight's road and its timing: 2.5 s at the foot, 7.5 s up, 2.5 s at the top. The bucket moves the other way.
  • No cables (note 4), and nothing added to work the lift (Parker, 11-13).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cavern's wall. The same hand-cut wall as behind the plank walk, carried on up. The lift is built against it. (2.9 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 2The hoistway. There today, and kept exactly: a dark frame on the station deck with an arm over the pulley, and the broad dark boards the weight and the bucket run in front of. It is dark, flat and broad, with no rungs and no thin lines, so it does not read as something to climb (note 4). (2.4 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 3The pulley. On the frame's arm, where it is today, but set back against the hoistway and grey. Today it hangs inside the play space. It turns one way, stops, and turns back. (2.0 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4The station's back half. The deck you land on carries on back to the wall on two bearers, seen end-on under it, with a slanting brace from the pillar. On its back half: three ore crates and a spare bucket on its side. This is where ore from the pillar's top was sent down. Today the brace is in hold-coloured timber in the play space. It moves back and goes grey. (1.3 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 5The lower station's back half. The arm's twin behind it, the brace that carries them both and the cross timber that ties them to the rock. The same pieces as on La Cuerda del Minero's page. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 6Two lanterns. One standing on the arm's twin, one standing on the top crate. Today both hang inside the play space, in the open air, with nothing above them. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The weight. A riveted iron box of scrap and rock with a lifting eye on top, rust orange. It is the thing you hold, so it is the brightest thing on the lift.
  • 8The bucket. It matches what you touch now (note 3): iron, rust orange, and you can hold it and stand in it. Its handle over the top stays drawn only, so that its mouth stays open.
  • 9The deck's west end. The deck stops 2.1 m short of the pillar. Its end is drawn as a finished edge with a trimmer across it, not a break: the gap was the hatch that the ore went down, and it is the gap you fall through from Vetas de Lodo.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10The lift frame's front post. One thin post in four broken lengths, in front of the pillar's rock beside the iron rungs. It stops below the station, so it is nowhere near the ribs of Vetas de Lodo. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)
