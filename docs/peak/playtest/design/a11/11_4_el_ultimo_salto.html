La Cumbre · Area and background design

Challenge 11.4: El Último Salto

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the ring's west post, at 995 m: 2.6 m of dry rock with the post's timber head at its west end. It is the highest place the ring's builders stood, and the end of everything they made except the bell. From here the bell is in plain view, 24 m to the west and 8 m up, across the open back of the dome, and its rope hangs down to below your feet. Between you and it there is one thing: an iron ring on the tip of a splinter of rock, 3.7 m west of the post and level with its top. The ring anchored the post's stay, the tie that held the corner against the pull of the ropes, and that is why it is level with the post's top. So the last leap is made the way a luchador makes it. Climb the post's timber head by its three turnbuckle eyes. Stand on the pad. Jump to the ring, swing, and let go. It is the worst fall in the game: a miss lands on the dome, and the dome ends at the prow.

What must not change

  • The post's top and its timber head, each where it is. The timber head is held all over.
  • The ring's distance from the post: 3.7 m. Only its height changes (note 99).
  • The bell rope where it hangs, and the last leap: 20 m across from the ring.
  • The camera pulls back for the leap, so the rope and the dome under it are both on screen before you jump.
  • Nothing breaks the fall. Under the ring there is 25 m of air, then the dome.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The peak's own back. Tan rock, 3 m behind you, standing 4 to 5 m above the dome's white back and ending at the very top, behind the foot of the bell frame. Everything above it and east of it is sky. This is the widest view of open sky on the summit, and nothing is put into it. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2The splinter that carries the ring. A splinter of the west horn's pillar, one layer back. It splits off the pillar's west flank at 977 m and stands 21 m, leaning 3 m to the west, into the wind. It comes to a point too narrow to stand on, 0.15 m under the ring. An iron eye bolt in the point comes forward to you, and the ring hangs from it. Today the ring floats in the air with nothing carrying it. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 3The flagstaff behind the timber head. A staff 6.3 m tall, lashed to the back of the timber head and raked back. From the side it is hidden by the head until it rises above the red pad. It also carries the west end of the jade rope's back run, with the paper from post to post. Its top is 2 m behind you and 3.3 m above the pad you stand on, so the lowest flag of the paper tied to it is 2.5 m above the pad, out of any hand's reach. (1.3 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 4The paper over the leap. The town's paper on thick old ring rope, whole and bright, from the flagstaff's top to the top of the bell staff: 30 m, rising 10 m, with flags 0.8 m tall like the tea shop's. It runs over the line of the leap and shows you where the leap goes. Its flags pass 3.6 m over the ring and 2.5 m over the beam of the bell frame. Today it is tied to the timber head's top, 1.2 m behind you, at the height of your hands when you stand on the post. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 5At the bell. On the far side of the gap: the bell staff rising 4.8 m above the belt's post, the frame's back strut, and the rib of rock that the perch is the top of. They are designed on La Campana's page. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The timber head, climbed. Square honey timber, 0.4 m thick and 3 m tall. Its three iron turnbuckle eyes stand out of its east face 0.9 m apart, like rungs, and its top is a red pad. You climb it and stand on the pad, 3 m above the horn's top.
  • 7The iron ring, moved up. Rust-orange iron, 0.52 m across. It moves up 1.9 m, from 996.3 m to 998.2 m, level with the post's top. It does not move sideways. The cross marks where it is today.
  • 8The plank that goes. The rotten plank west of the post, 2.2 m long, is taken away. Nothing stands between the post and the ring but 3.7 m of air.
  • 9The catch. The bell rope, with its wooden toll handle at 992 m. The leap is aimed 3.3 m above the handle. Above it hangs the bell, which is solid in the design and tolls when you land on the rope. Both are on La Campana's page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10The dome's snow cap (there today). The white cap on the dome's back and on the summit's east cliff, in front of solid ice. The leap passes high above it and ends at the rope, 3 m short of it. It stays. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The paper over the leap lifts toward the east, back the way you came.
  • Gold dust in the low sun. The ring creaks on its bolt when you swing.
  • As the camera pulls back, the bell's bronze catches the sun.
