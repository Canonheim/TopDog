Challenge 11.1: Sobre las Nubes: the tea shop under the prow

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The ledge where the storm ridge ends and the summit begins: 17 m of rock at 964 m, roofed by the prow of the summit's dome, 4 to 5 m overhead, and open to the east with the cloud at your feet. It is the last flat place on the mountain and the first one above the weather, so it is where anybody who gets this far stops. A frog saw that, and keeps a tea shop here. The shop is a recess in the ledge's back wall, 6 m wide, with a counter, a small stove, and the same pot, cups and kettle as the tea cave on the wind cliffs. The frog hangs the town's paper fresh across its mouth for an awning. A warm spout runs in the shop's wall, and nobody asks where the water comes from. The ledge is still not quite safe. The black mouth of the tunnel is at its west end, where whatever the summit drops comes sliding out across the floor. And the whole dome pours off the lip over your head.

What must not change

  • The floor: 17 m, with the slab's gentle rise to the west. The prow's underside and its lip.
  • The tunnel's mouth at the west end, and the 1 m drop from it onto the floor.
  • The storm ridge's way up: its last rungs, and the east end of the ledge that you pull up onto.
  • The nose and the iron ring off the east end, which start La Corona.
  • Nothing in the shop can be touched. All of it stands 1.3 m or more behind you.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ledge's back wall. The summit's own rock, closing the back of the alcove 1.3 m behind you, from the floor to the prow's underside. Today the nearest thing behind the ledge is the peak's back, 3 m away, with one pool of lamp light on it. The wall is tan, a step duller than the floor, in big rounded blocks. The prow's black ice comes down onto its top in a glazed edge. (1.3 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 2The tea shop's recess. Cut into the back wall under the paper: 6 m wide, 2.6 m tall and 2.5 m deep, with its front open, so you look straight in. A plank floor, plank walls, and warm amber on every surface from the lantern and the stove. A shelf of tea tins runs along its back wall. It is the one warm room above the storm, and it is the first thing you see as your head comes over the ledge's end. (1.3 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 3The counter, the frog and two stools. A plank counter 2.4 m long under a striped cloth, set 0.5 m inside the recess, with a row of cups along it and one cup set out at its east end, for you. The frog stands behind it on an upturned crate, in an apron, so that its head and hands show over the top. Now and then it pours a cup. Two stools stand at the counter's ends, just inside the recess. Nothing here is within reach: the nearest stool is 1.35 m behind you. (1.4 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4The stove, the kettle and the tin pipe. A small iron stove in the east corner, with the cliffs' kettle on it, steaming. Its tin pipe goes up through the recess's roof and comes out of the wall as a cowl, 3.4 m above the floor. The smoke runs east along the prow's underside and out past the lip. From the storm ridge's last rungs you see that thread of smoke before you see the ledge. (1.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 5The warm spout. In the west corner: a stone spout in the wall, running a thread of warm water into a stone basin, with steam on it. This is the frogs' spring. Its water has come 22 m through the mountain from the pool under the west shoulder, and the spout is level with that pool. It is a hint for a player who looks. The frog fills the kettle here. (3.0 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 6The lantern and the sign. A lit lantern on an iron hook under the lintel, in place of the cold lantern that stands on the floor today. Over the lintel hangs a board with a painted cup and a curl of steam. It has no words. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 7The awning. The town's paper, whole and bright, on thick old ring rope: 9 m long, tied to two iron eyes in the back wall, 2 m behind you, with its lowest flag 3 m above the floor. It hangs across the mouth of the shop like an awning. It is the line that is there today, moved 1 m further back and 0.9 m down, so that its flags hang clear of the prow. Today the prow cuts them in half. The second line, which hangs at the height of your head today, is taken down. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 8The tunnel's mouth. At the west end the floor ends against a black opening 4.4 m tall: the foot of the tunnel under the dome. Its back wall is dark blue-violet ice, 2 m behind, going up out of sight. Nothing is drawn in it. Whatever the summit drops comes out of here, 1 m above the floor, and slides across the slab. (2.0 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 9East of the ledge. The peak's own back carries on east behind the open air, tan and dull. In front of it stand the first and fourth teeth of the crown, each a needle of rock one layer back. The fourth tooth has a spur on its west flank, level with your head, and the iron ring hangs from the spur's tip. They are designed on La Corona's page. (1.4 to 3.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The floor: one rock. All 17 m of the floor is the summit's rock: warm cream, rounded and dry. Today its east 8 m is drawn in the storm ridge's grey slate, in square blocks, and butts against the cream slab at x -340. It becomes one floor. The slate ends below it, in a line at 962 m along the ledge's underside, as the storm ridge's last page has it. In front of the tunnel's mouth the rock is scored with long pale scrapes fanning east. What comes out of the tunnel has marked the floor.
  • 11The prow overhead. Black ice in flat planes with a hard white glint, as the sheet has it. It gets no icicles: on the glacier an icicle is a hold. The lantern's amber lies on its facets over the shop, and a little snow smokes off the lip.
  • 12The way on. Off the ledge's east end: the rime nose and the iron ring. Today they are a carved stone block and a ring in the air. They are designed on La Corona's page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13The dome's snow cap (there today). The white cap on the dome's back, 2 m in front of you. Its foot is set 1.5 m up from the prow's underside, so the ice shows below it as a dark rim. It stands in front of solid ice everywhere, and it stays. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Steam off the kettle and off the spout. Smoke from the cowl, running east under the prow.
  • Amber from the lantern and the stove on the recess's walls, on the floor in front of it and on the prow above. It is colour on the surfaces, not a glow.
  • The frog's croak and the kettle's lid ticking, heard from the storm ridge's last rungs.
  • The awning's paper lifts toward the east in a gust and hangs still between gusts.
  • Now and then a little snow comes down the tunnel and out across the floor.

  • The floor's east 8 m belongs to the storm ridge's file in the course and is drawn in its grey slate. It is drawn as the summit's cream rock, to match the sheet and the storm ridge's last page. Its shape does not change.
