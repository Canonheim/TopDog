La Cumbre · Area and background design

Challenge 11.5: La Campana

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The bell. The temple's people hung it on the very top to start and to end a bout: a bronze bell 2.2 m across, with the First Top Dog's face cast on it, in an iron yoke under the east end of a timber frame. Its rope hangs 10 m down the summit's east cliff with a wooden toll handle on its end, and that rope is what the last leap catches. The frame stands at the ringside, where a ring's timekeeper sits. Nobody has rung it for a very long time. The last of the ones who tried sits in the timekeeper's seat, a perch of rock 4 m under the bell, looking up at it, with marigolds laid beside him. There is nobody above him. When your weight comes onto the rope the bell tips toward you, the clapper strikes, and the whole summit hears that somebody has arrived. Then you climb the rope, into the sound.

What must not change

  • The rope: where it hangs, its 10 m, and the toll handle on its end.
  • The perch and the one who sits on it. The frame's post, beam and brace.
  • The way up: the rope, then the bell and its yoke, then the beam.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The peak's own back. The summit's rock behind its ice, 3 m back, ending in a blunt head at 1,000.8 m behind the foot of the frame's post. The post is footed in the mountain, and this shows it. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2The rib under the perch, and his marigolds. A rib of warm rock comes forward out of the peak's back, 1.3 m behind you, and the perch is its top. So the perch is a place on the mountain, and not a block stuck on a wall of ice. The rib's top carries on 1 m behind the perch. The marigolds lie there, beside the one who sits, on the side the rope is on. Today the pile lies in the play space, where it can't be held. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3The frame's back strut. A square timber from the back of the post down to the head of the peak's back, 3 m behind. It holds the frame against the pull of the bell, and it is why a frame with one post still stands. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The bell staff. A staff 8.2 m tall, lashed to the back of the frame's post and raked back, like the one behind the corner post. From the side it is hidden by the post until it rises above the belt. Its top is 4.8 m above the post's top. It carries the far ends of the two long lines of paper. (1.3 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 5The two lines of paper. From the bell staff's top one line runs east, down over the leap to the west horn, and one runs west, down to a staff on the peak's back above the west shoulder. Both are 2 m behind you. Over the beam their lowest flags are 2.5 m up or more, and over the post's top 4 m. Today one is tied to the beam's east end, at the height of your hands, and the other ends in the air 1.1 m under the beam's west end. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The bell, solid, and its clapper. Bronze, matte, 2.2 m across and 2.6 m tall, with the First Top Dog's face cast on the side that faces you. It hangs mouth down under the beam's east end, and the rope comes out of its mouth from the clapper. It is solid wherever it is drawn, and it can be held. When your weight comes onto the rope it tips toward you, the clapper shows under its lip and strikes, and it rocks back. The dashed lines show how far it swings. Bell, clapper and rope move as one thing.
  • 7The iron yoke. Rust-orange iron: a hanger from the beam, a pin, and a fork that holds the bell by its crown. The pin is the hinge the bell swings on, and you can see it turn. All of it can be held.
  • 8The bell rope and the toll handle. Hemp, 10 m long, with a wooden toll handle 0.5 m wide on its end. The leap arrives 3.3 m above the handle. Every hard swing or tug tolls the bell again. Hang still and it rocks to rest.
  • 9The perch and the last of the ones who tried. Warm dry rock, 2.1 m wide: the timekeeper's seat, and the last place on the mountain where you stand on stone. He sits at its back with his mask turned up to the bell. He is the game's climber model, posed sitting: nothing is modelled for him, and the new climber model takes his place when it comes. He stays exactly where he is.
  • 10The marigolds on the frame. A garland wound up the post and laid along the beam. Today it hangs 0.4 m in front of the timber, 0.5 m thick. It is pressed flat onto the post's face and the beam's front face, so it is part of the timber and is held with it.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The summit's snow cap (there today). The white cap on the summit block, in front of solid ice. Its east edge runs up the cliff 0.4 m west of the perch's back, and it covers nothing you stand on. It stays. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The catch at the end of the last leap is the loudest toll. Each toll after it is as loud as the tug that made it.
  • A little dust falls from the yoke's pin at the first toll: it has not turned for a long time.
  • Marigold petals come loose from the frame when the bell rings.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 100: the rope rings the bell, and the bell and its clapper swing with it. The bell tips when your weight comes onto the rope, tolls at every hard swing or tug, and rocks to rest when you hang still. The three tolls at the belt stay as the finish.
  • Course change: the bell becomes a solid thing that swings. Today the only solid there is its hanger, an iron bar 0.18 m wide from 1,002.2 to 1,005.3 m, and the bell's model does not move. The bell is 2.2 m across and 2.6 m tall. Its mouth is at 1,002.2 m, where the rope's top is today, and its crown is at 1,004.8 m, 0.5 m under the beam. It hangs from the yoke's pin under the beam and is drawn tipping 15 degrees either way, which moves the rope's top 0.8 m either side. How far it tips is settled when it is built.
  • So the way up changes a little. Today you climb from the rope straight up a bare iron bar to the beam. In the design you climb from the rope onto the bell's flank, up the bell, and onto the yoke and the beam.
  • In the course today the bell's model hangs from a point at 1,006.3 m, which is 0.6 m above the beam's top. By those numbers the bell hangs through the beam, and its mouth is 1.5 m above the top of the rope.
  • Scenery put right. The marigold pile moves from 0.5 m behind you to 1.5 m behind and 1.7 m east, onto the rib's top. The two garlands on the frame move from 0.4 m in front of the timber to flat on it. The two lines of paper move up to the bell staff's top, at 1,011.2 m, 2 m behind you: one from the beam's east end, and one from a loose end in the air 1.1 m under the beam's west end.
  • Confirmed by Parker: the bell is solid. It can be held and stood on.
  • Parker: a new climber model is coming very soon. The one who sits is the game's climber model, posed; nothing bespoke is made for him.
  • Nothing else you touch moves: the rope's place and length, the handle, the perch, the frame.
