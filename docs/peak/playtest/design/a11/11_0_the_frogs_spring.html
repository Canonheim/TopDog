La Cumbre · Area and background design

Rest 11.0: The frogs' hot spring

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

West of the bell the mountain is shut. A shoulder of black ice comes down from the west wall into a tunnel under the dome, and whatever flies too far over the bell slides down it and out onto the tea ledge. Under that slide the mountain is warm. A hot spring rises inside the shoulder, in a cave of ochre rock 19 m wide. Its breath goes up a vent to the tunnel's mouth and freezes there: a patch of hoarfrost in the floor, the one pale thing in the dark, with a wisp of steam coming through a crack in it. Take that patch as you slide over it, and it holds you for 3 seconds, then breaks under you and drops you down the vent into the pool. The frogs of the mountain are all there, in water to their eyes, by candle light. They have kept the place to themselves. There is one way out, at the far left: the spring's overflow, a water slide that carries you down inside the mountain to the long fall line, and so to the Sump. It is the reward for falling the wrong way off the very top, and it costs the whole climb.

What must not change

  • The shoulder's slide and the tunnel as they are. A body that does not take the frost slides over it and out onto the tea ledge, as today.
  • So a miss over the bell still costs only the area.
  • The tunnel's roof, the dome's west corner, and the tea ledge at the tunnel's foot.
  • Everything else on this page is new (note 102).

The three layers

Background

  • 1Warm rock under the ice. The shoulder's black ice is a skin. Under it is the mountain's own rock, and round the spring that rock is warm and bare: rounded, ochre and cream, crusted with pale mineral, with no ice on it. Where the cave is cut away to the camera you see the skin of ice outside and the band of warm rock inside it. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The spring's cave. A cave inside the west shoulder, under the tunnel: 19 m wide and about 6 m tall, with its back wall 3 m behind you and its front cut away. Above the steam its walls and roof are dark and wet, and can't be held. Along the back wall, 2 m above the water, runs a ledge 7 m long. In the east wall, level with the water, is the crack that a rill of warm water leaves by: it goes to the spout in the tea shop's wall. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The vent, which is the chute. A smooth wet tube of warm dark rock, 2 m across and 9.4 m long. It drops 2 m straight down from the hole in the tunnel's floor, then slants down and back to the west, under the shoulder, and opens in the cave's roof over the middle of the pool. Nothing in it can be held. It is cut away to the camera, so you see yourself go down it. When the frost above has broken, daylight comes down it onto the water. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 4The water slide. The way out, at the pool's west end: a smooth wet tube 2 m across, with the spring's warm water running down it. It drops from the lip, bends east under the pool, and goes down into the mountain. It leaves this biome's region through its floor, at 962 m, at about x -378. Where it comes out is for the storm ridge's and the glacier's designs. The sheet proposes a steaming spout over the gap at the top of the glacier, at about 886 m. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 5The pool's back, and the frogs. The pool runs back 3 m behind you, milky blue-green, under thick white steam. About thirty frogs are here, all the same little frog as every biome's: in the water to their eyes, in a row along the back rim, on the ledge of the back wall, and on the dry floor's back edge. A few have brought what their own biome's frog has: a teacup, a blanket, a ticket. They watch you fall in, and then they go back to sitting. Every one of them is in the back of the cave, 2.3 m or more behind you. (2.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6Three candle lanterns. Hung from the roof at the back, each with a candle in it. They and the daylight from the vent are the cave's light, and they show as amber on the steam and on the wall behind them. (2.4 to 2.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The frost wall. A plug of hoarfrost in the slide's floor at the tunnel's mouth, 2 m wide and 2 m deep, flush with the slope, just below the dome's west corner at about 980 m: the place of the arrow in note 102. It has the rime's look: grey-white, matte, cracked across, with a corner gone and steam coming through the crack. It is the one pale thing in the dark. A hand that takes it is held for 3 seconds. In the last second it ticks and its crack opens. Then it breaks away under you and you go down the vent. Twelve seconds later it has grown back, in plain view, and the way is shut.
  • 8The pool, the rim stones and the dry floor. The pool is 12 m wide and 1 m deep, and you land in the middle of it. At its east end are three rounded rim stones and 4.8 m of dry floor: pale, dry mineral crust, the only things in the cave that can be held, and the place to climb out and sit. Nothing in the cave can hurt you.
  • 9The slide's lip. At the pool's west end the water runs over a smooth rounded lip, 0.1 m under the surface, into the slide's mouth. The lip is dark and wet and can't be held. Swim over it and you are gone. A slow current in the pool's west half draws toward it, and the steam leans that way.
  • 10The tunnel's floor, as it is. Black ice above the frost and below it. A body that slides over the frost without taking it goes on down the tunnel to the tea ledge, as it does today.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The shoulder's snow cap, cut back. Today one white card covers the whole face of the shoulder from 963 m up, 2 m in front. It would hide the cave. Its lower 10.5 m goes, and so does the strip of it under the dome. What is left stands in front of solid ice, 2.2 m above the cave's roof and 2.5 m or more west of the vent. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A wisp of steam through the crack in the frost wall, seen from as far up the shoulder as you can see the wall.
  • Thick white steam over the pool, thinning toward the roof. The frogs' eyes show through it.
  • Many croaks at once, heard faintly in the tunnel as you slide past the frost.
  • A shaft of daylight down the vent onto the water for the 12 seconds the frost is gone, narrowing as it grows back.
  • A ring of ripples where you land. The nearest frogs bob on it.
