Rest 11.0: The frogs' hot spring: west of the belt

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The reward for the climb. West of the bell frame the summit falls away into a notch between its west face and the shoulder, and a column of steam stands up out of the notch, leaning east at its top, in plain view from the beam. Under the notch the mountain is warm. A hot spring rises in a cave of ochre rock inside the shoulder, and its breath comes up a round shaft, the sink, which opens in the notch's floor. Whoever has the belt can step off the west end of the bell frame's beam: the west face takes them down, its eave turns them off over the sink, and they drop into a warm pool full of frogs, by candle light. The frogs have kept the place to themselves. It is cozy and nothing in it can hurt you: there is a bank to sit on, a low table with a pot and cups, folded towels. There are two ways on. On the far left, over a low rim wreathed in marigolds, is the spring's overflow: a water slide that carries you down inside the mountain to the long fall line, and so all the way down to the Sump, the celebratory drop. Or climb the frogs' stair, a rope up the side of the sink, to a doorstep under the eave and a wall of frost. Break the frost, and behind it is the tunnel under the dome, the teahouse chute, which slides you east and puts you out at the tea shop.

What must not change

  • The tunnel under the dome as it is, from the frost wall east and down to the tea ledge.
  • The bell frame, its beam's west end and the summit's apex.
  • The shoulder's slope west of the sink: a body that lands on it slides east into the sink.
  • Everything else on this page is new (note 102).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The steam column. Thick white steam, 3 m wide, standing up out of the sink 2.2 m behind the climb, and leaning east at its top, where the summit's slow west air takes it. It rises 26 m, to above the bell frame's beam, so it is in the frame of the close camera at the post and at the bell. It is how you guess the place is there. It is always behind the climb, never in front of it. (2.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 2The peak's back above the shoulder, the staff and the paper. The peak's own back, 3 m behind, tan, one painted piece with long wavy bedding lines, ending in an uneven skyline. A little outcrop on it carries a cushion of pink-flowered moss and a tuft of gold grass. A flagstaff stands on it above the shoulder, and the town's paper runs to it from the bell staff, over the notch and the steam, 2 m behind the climb. The line that was there today ends in the air. Now it goes somewhere. (1.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The frog's sign on the back strut. A painted board 0.7 m square on the bell frame's back strut, under the beam's west end: a frog in a pool under a curl of steam, and no words. It is the tea shop's sign's fellow, and it faces you as you walk the beam to the belt. (1.5 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 4Warm rock round the cave and the sink. The shoulder's black ice is a skin. Under it, round the spring, the mountain's rock is warm and bare: ochre and cream, crusted with pale mineral, painted in long wavy bands like every mass up here. Where the cave and the sink are cut away to the camera you see the skin of ice outside and a band of warm rock about 1 m thick inside it. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The spring's cave. A cave inside the shoulder, under the notch: 15.6 m wide and 3 to 4 m tall, with its back wall 3 m behind you and its front cut away. Its walls and roof above the steam are dark and wet, and can't be held. Along the back wall, 2 m above the water, runs a ledge 6 m long for frogs. A painted board over the slide's mouth shows a frog on a slide. A rill leaves by a crack in the east wall: it goes down to the spout in the tea shop's wall. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The sink. A round hole 4.5 m across in the notch's floor, and the shaft under it, 3 m across, of smooth wet warm rock. It is 4.3 m deep on its east side, under the doorstep, and 8.7 m on its west. It opens in the cave's roof over the deep end of the pool. It is cut away to the camera, so you see yourself go down it. Its steam is the column above. (1.2 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 7The water slide. The spring's overflow, at the pool's far west end: a smooth wet tube 2 m across with warm water running down it. It drops from its mouth, bends east under the pool, and goes on down into the mountain. It leaves this biome's region through its floor, at 962 m, between x -379.2 and x -377. Where it comes out is for the storm ridge's and the glacier's designs: the sheet proposes a steaming spout over the gap at the top of the glacier, at about 886 m. (1.2 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 8The pool's back half, and the frogs. The pool runs back 3 m behind you, milky blue-green, under thick white steam. About thirty frogs are here, all the same little frog as every biome's: in the water to their eyes, along the back rim, on the back wall's ledge, and on the back of the bank. A few have brought what their own biome's frog has: a teacup, a blanket, a ticket. They watch you drop in, and then they go back to sitting. Now and then one goes over the rim and down the slide on a tea tray. Every one of them is 1.3 m or more behind you. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 9The lanterns, the low table, the towels. Three candle lanterns hung from the roof at the back. They and the daylight down the sink are the cave's light, shown as amber on the steam and the walls. At the back of the bank, 1.4 m behind you: a low table with the tea cave's pot and two cups, one of them set out for you, and a pile of folded towels. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The way down from the beam. After the TOP DOG card you are on the post's top (see the ceremony's page). Step down to the beam's west end and off it. You drop about 8 m onto the summit's west face, slide 17 m down it, are turned off the eave's lip over the sink, and fall 12 m down the sink into the deep end of the pool. There is nothing to catch and nothing to miss: from the beam's end every way down goes into the water.
  • 11The summit's west face and its eave. Black ice, one painted piece with the dome: a few big planes, long wavy bands of blue-black, white glints. Its last 4.4 m is turned out 3.1 m to the west as an eave, 42 degrees steep, whose lip is over the sink. It turns a sliding body off over the sink and clear of the doorstep, and it roofs the doorstep and the frost wall. It can't be held.
  • 12The frogs' stair. A real little climb in the summit's own kit. From the bank, take the wooden toll handle of a hemp rope hanging down the sink's east side: the bell rope's kind. Climb the rope 4.5 m to the iron ring it hangs from, the summit's kind, set in the shaft's wall. Then pull up 0.9 m onto the doorstep. From the bank to the doorstep is 7.3 m. A body falling down the sink can catch the rope too, and then it is already on the stair.
  • 13The doorstep. Warm dry summit rock, 1.9 m wide, cut level into the slope at 978.3 m, under the eave, with 4.3 m of headroom. You stand on it to take the frost wall. Two frog prints are worn into it.
  • 14The frost wall. Hoarfrost across the top of the tunnel, 0.9 m thick and about 4.3 m tall, under the dome's west corner: the place of the arrow in note 102. It has the rime's look: grey-white and matte, cracked across, with a corner gone at its foot, which is the tea frog's own door. From the spring's side: take it, it holds for 3 seconds, ticks, sheds frost dust and its crack opens, then it breaks away in pieces that go sliding down the tunnel ahead of you. Step through and slide. From the tunnel's side the pieces come sliding out onto the tea ledge, with a breath of steam. Twelve seconds later the wall has grown back from its edges, and the door is shut.
  • 15The pool, the bank and the rim stones. The pool is 8.8 m wide and 0.8 m deep, and you land in its deep end, under the sink. At its east end three rounded rim stones, and then 4.2 m of dry bank: pale mineral crust, the place to climb out and sit. Bank and stones can be held. Nothing in the cave can hurt you.
  • 16The slide's rim and its marigolds. At the pool's far west end a low rim of rounded stone stands 0.3 m out of the water, and the slide's mouth is behind it, 2.2 m wide, wreathed in the town's marigolds. You go over the rim on purpose: the slide is a choice, not a current that takes you. The rim can be held and stood on, and from its top the slide is a step down.
  • 17The teahouse chute. Behind the frost wall: the tunnel under the dome, as it is today. Black ice, 3.6 m across, 21 m long. It slides you east and puts you out at the west end of the tea ledge, 1 m above its floor, by the tea shop. It is drawn on the tea shop's page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 18The shoulder's snow cap, cut back. Today one white card covers the whole face of the shoulder from 963 m up, 2 m in front. It would hide the cave, the sink and the stair. What is left stands in front of solid ice only: set 1.3 m back from the slope, west of x -375.2 and above 976.4 m, and west of x -380.6 below that. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)
  • 19The dome's snow cap (there today). The white cap on the dome's back and the summit's west face, in front of solid ice. Its foot is 2 m above the frost wall and it covers nothing you touch. It stays. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The steam column, always: thick at the sink, thinning upward, leaning east at the top. It is there from the first time you look west from the bell.
  • Thick white steam over the pool, thinning toward the roof. The frogs' eyes show through it.
  • Many croaks at once, heard faintly from the beam's west end, and loudly as you fall.
  • A ring of ripples where you land. The nearest frogs bob on it.
  • When the frost wall breaks, the cold air from the tunnel makes the steam over the doorstep swirl for a moment.
