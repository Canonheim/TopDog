Challenge 11.2: La Corona

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The crown. East of the dome, five needles of rock stand up out of the cloud in a ring, and the people who built the temple pinned an eight-sided boss of gold to the tip of each one: a crown for the First Top Dog's mountain, set just under his ring. You go round it as the gold goes. Out under the prow's lip to the first tooth. Up the west side, over the dome, on the second and third. Back east under the horns to the fourth. Across to the fifth, which has a shelf. The gold is what you swing from. Under each boss the west wind has built a collar of rime on the needle, and that is what you land on: it holds for 3 seconds and then leaves you hanging from the gold in clear air. The needles stand one layer back, so a body that lets go passes in front of them and meets nothing until the dome. The first launch is an old iron ring under the prow's lip. It is also the summit's one last chance, for a body that comes off the prow slowly.

What must not change

  • The iron ring, the nose, the five gold bosses, the four collars, the shelf, its block and its iced belly: each exactly where it is, at its size.
  • Every rime step's timing: it holds for 3 seconds and is back 12 seconds later.
  • The five flings as they are. The fourth is thrown flat, 11 m across and 3.8 m down.
  • Nothing on the east side stops a fall. Every needle stands one layer back, and a body passes in front of it.

Background

  • 1The peak's own back. Warm tan rock, one painted card 3 m behind you, behind the dome and west of the teeth. East of it is open sky. Today its east edge is a straight cut at x -318 and its foot is a straight cut at 960 m, and the camera at the first tooth reaches down to 959 m. Its east edge becomes a broken buttress. Its foot meets the storm ridge's slate in a line at 962 m, as the storm ridge's last page has it, and east of the slate it goes down to 953 m with the teeth. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2The horns' roots. The two pillars the horns stand on: flat rock cards 1.8 m back, 26 m tall, going down into the cloud. From the crown they are the biggest things in the sky, and they show you where the climb goes next. Like the teeth, they end today in a straight cut at 958 m, in the air. The storm ridge's pages ask that each is carried down to 953 m, behind the top of the storm's cloud, so that the crown stands out of the storm. This design does the same. (1.8 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3Four marigold garlands. Four swags of the town's marigolds, 0.5 m thick, hung from tooth to tooth round the crown. Today each runs straight from gold to gold, 1.2 m behind you and 1.2 m under the line you fly, and it reads as a rope to the next hold (note 98). Each is now tied to the backs of two needles, 2 m behind you and 3 m or more under their gold, and hangs lower in between. None passes behind a boss or a collar. Where a garland crosses a needle it goes behind the needle. (2.0 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 4The five teeth. Five needles of the summit's rock, 1.4 m back and 2.5 to 3.4 m wide at the foot. Three stand in the open with their feet in the cloud. The second and third stand up from behind the dome. Each ends in a blunt tip just under its gold. The fifth is stouter than the rest, and at 979.2 m its body makes a step 4.2 m wide. The shelf you stand on is that step's front edge. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 5The fourth tooth's spur. On the west flank of the fourth tooth, 14 m under its gold: a knuckle of rock with an iron eye bolt in its tip. The first ring hangs from that bolt. Today the ring is in the air with nothing carrying it. So you launch from the foot of the tooth whose top you reach four flings later. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 6The fallen post and the second frog. On the fifth tooth's step, 1.5 m behind the shelf: the ring's third corner post, fallen, with a frog sitting on its end. They are designed on the next page. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7Rime, where there are carved stone blocks today. The nose, the four collars and the block on the shelf are all the summit's rime: grey-white frost feathers, pale and matte, cracked across, with the east corner gone, each grown toward the west. A collar wraps its needle and comes forward to you. The nose grows off the spur toward the ledge. Each is drawn at exactly the size that holds.
  • 8The five gold bosses. Eight-sided, 0.52 m across, brushed and matte. Each sits on a short bronze neck that comes forward from the tip of its tooth. Each one is in view from the one before it.
  • 9The first ring. Rust-orange iron, 0.52 m across, hanging from the eye bolt in the spur, 2.3 m under the prow's lip. It is the launch for the crown, and the one last chance for a body that comes off the prow slowly.
  • 10The shelf and its iced belly. The shelf's top is warm dry rock, 2.6 m wide. Its underside is glazed black, so a body thrown up at it from below meets ice and not an edge. No pale mark is drawn on the belly.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The dome's snow cap (there today). The white cap on the dome's back, set about 1 m in from the line a body slides on. It is 3 m or more from the nearest collar, and it covers only solid ice. It stays. (2.0 to 2.0 m in front)
