La Cumbre · Area and background design

Challenge 11.3: Las Cuerdas del Campeón

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The ring. Two horns of rock stand 6 m apart, and the temple's people cut them square to be the corner posts of the First Top Dog's ring: a shaft of dark glazed rock 2.2 m thick for each, with three turnbuckle blocks standing out of its inner face, one above the other, and a timber head with a red pad on its top. They strung the ring's three ropes round the posts, red, cream and jade, and laid its floor and canvas between them. The weather has taken the floor and most of the ropes. What it could not take is the corners. Along the sloping face of each block a rope is still strung tight between two iron eyes, with a fat cushion over the turnbuckle at each end. Red at the bottom, cream in the middle, jade at the top: each colour is a matched pair, one on each post, facing across the gap. They are still lively. Throw yourself into a corner and it throws you back out, harder than you came in, at the other corner. Six ropes up, you come over the top of the west post and into its timber head. Behind the gap the jade rope's back run still spans the top, carrying the town's paper. The red and cream back runs hang snapped down the backs of the posts. One beam of the floor spans between the pillars with the canvas hanging off it as an apron. You start from the shelf below, where the third post lies fallen, and look up through the place where the ring's floor was.

What must not change

  • The six bouncing lines exactly where they are, and what they do: each throws you up and across, a little harder than you came in.
  • The inner faces of both posts, the west post's top and the east post's ice tip at 999.9 m. No part of a post or a rope can be held.
  • The fifth gold boss, the rime block under it, and the fling from the boss into the west post's lowest rope: 6.8 m across and 3.8 m up.
  • The west post's top and its timber head, which stops the last rope's throw.
  • One move only, listed in the notes: the east post's outer face goes 1.6 m east.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The pillars under the posts. Each post stands on a pillar of tan rock 1.8 m back, going down into the cloud. Each pillar is one painted piece in two or three slanted planes, its bedding long wavy lines of three tans, with a leaning, notched outline. The builders cut the top 2 m of each square as a plinth for its post, and that cut edge is chipped. Behind the east post the pillar carries on up as a thin back blade. Two little outcrops on the west pillar and one on the east carry the summit's plants: a cushion of pink-flowered moss and a tuft of gold grass, each a flat painted card. (1.5 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 2The ring's last floor beam and its apron. One beam of the ring's floor still spans from pillar to pillar, 2.3 m behind you, at 984 m, with the broken stubs of five joists standing up off it. The canvas is folded over it and hangs down 1.7 m as a torn apron: cream, with a faded pug's face. The timber is weathered grey, so it can't be taken for a bar, and the fling from the fifth boss passes in front of it. From the shelf it is the first thing that says this was a ring: a ring seen from underneath, its floor gone and its apron left. It takes the place of the canvas strip of the first design. (2.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 3The fifth tooth's step, the fallen post and the frog. The shelf is the front edge of a step in the fifth tooth's body, and the step carries on 1 m further back. On it lies the ring's third corner post, broken off at 2.2 m, grey, with its red pad out over the drop. Today that post stands upright on the shelf itself, in the play space, where it can't be held. A frog sits on the post's end, looking up at the ropes. It shows the move. The first time your feet are on the shelf it croaks and jumps into the west post's red rope, and it is thrown from post to post up all six. After that it goes again about every 25 seconds, but only while you are standing on the shelf or its block, or hanging from the fifth boss: never while you are in the air. It is never solid. At the top it hops over the timber head and off the back of the post, and next it is sitting on the fallen post again. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 4The east post's timber head. Square timber 3 m tall with its three iron eyes and its red pad: the fellow of the west post's head. It stands on the back of the east shaft's top, 1.5 m behind the climb, in weathered grey. Today a post 3.4 m tall stands 0.4 m behind you inside the horn's ice tip, where it looks like a thing to catch. It moves 1.2 m east, 1.1 m back and 2 m up, onto the widened shaft. With the west head, it gives the ring its two red pads at first sight. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5The back runs of the ropes, the flagstaff and the paper. Behind the gap: what is left of the ropes that went round the back of the ring, faded, matte and 0.3 m thick, 2.2 m behind you. The jade one, the top rope, still spans from post to post. The frog has re-tied its west end to a flagstaff lashed to the back of the west head, so that it rises 3.3 m above the pad, and the town's paper hangs on it: 12.5 m, whole and bright, its lowest flag 2.5 m above the pad you stand on. The red and the cream back runs have snapped. Their frayed ends, 1.2 to 2 m long, hang down the backs of the two posts. None hangs across the gap you fly through, so each colour reads as one rope that went round the ring. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 6The splinter that carries the second ring. A splinter splits off the west pillar's flank and stands up west of the post, leaning into the wind. It carries the iron ring of the last leap, which anchored the post's stay. It is designed on the next page. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The two corner posts. Each horn is cut square as a corner post: a shaft of dark rock glazed with black ice, 2.2 m thick, with its three turnbuckle blocks standing out of its inner face. The face is painted: long wavy bands of blue-black in three tones, white glints, and an iron band painted round the shaft at each rope's eye (the dashed lines). Nothing pale is drawn on a post, and nothing on it can be held. The east shaft is 0.6 m thick today. Its outer face moves 1.6 m east, from x -320.2 to x -318.6, so the two posts match. Its inner face, its ropes and its ice tip stay. The west post's outer foot loses a corner, 0.6 m, where nobody goes.
  • 8The six ring ropes, three matched pairs, and the move shown. Each rope is a band 0.3 m thick and 4.4 m long, wound in glossy tape, lying exactly along its bouncing line and 0.2 m proud of the ice. On each post the lowest is red, the middle one cream and the top one jade, so the pairs face each other across the gap in the order of a ring's three ropes. A fat cushion covers the turnbuckle at each end, and the cushions throw you too. At rest each rope quivers a little. When you hit one it squashes in, springs back and hums. Between them the three ropes cover the whole inner face of a post. Today none of this is drawn (note 98). The dashed line is the frog's path and yours: six throws, 2 m higher each time, and the last one carries you west over the post's top.
  • 9The west post's top and its timber head. The top is 2.6 m of warm dry rock. At its west end stands the post's timber head: square honey timber 0.4 m thick and 3 m tall, with three iron turnbuckle eyes up its east face and a red pad on top. It is the only timber in the ring you can hold. The sixth rope throws you over the top and into it.
  • 10The fifth boss and its rime block. The launch. Stand on the block to reach the gold, and the block leaves. Both are as on La Corona's page.

Small effects, no models

  • A rope squashes where you hit it, springs back and hums. Its cushions puff a little dust.
  • The apron stirs toward the east. The paper on the jade rope lifts the same way, and the snapped ends sway.
  • The frog's croak on the fallen post, a moment before it jumps. It is how you know to look.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Parker, 11-13, late, on the summit's story: right, but the summit does not yet look like the ruin of a ring. What now makes it read as one at first sight: the horns are cut square as two matching corner posts with three turnbuckle blocks each and painted iron bands; each post has a timber head with a red pad; the six ropes are three matched pairs in a ring's order; the jade rope's back run spans the top and the red and cream ones hang snapped; one floor beam spans the pillars with the canvas apron hanging off it; the third post lies fallen below; and the bell stands at the ringside as the timekeeper's.
  • Move in the play space: the east post's outer face, from x -320.2 to x -318.6, from 985.6 m to 998.5 m, with its top cut level at 998.5 m from x -318.6 to x -320.2. Its inner face, its three bouncing lines and its ice tip at x -320.6, 999.9 m, do not move. The west post's outer foot loses a corner 0.6 m on a side, at x -332.2, 984.1 m. Nothing else you touch moves.
  • Note 98, the ropes are drawn. In the game the six ring ropes had no model at all. Each now has one, lying exactly along its bouncing line, and it squashes and springs when it throws you. No bouncing line moves.
  • Note 98, the view from the shelf. Today, standing on the shelf, the camera leans 1 m down, and the frame tops out at about 988 m, level with the top of the west post's lowest rope. In the design, while you stand on the shelf the camera leans 3 m up and 3 m west. The frame then runs from 974 to 992 m, and it holds both red ropes whole, the west cream rope, the apron and the frog.
  • Note 98, the move shown, confirmed by Parker: a frog bounces up the ropes now and then. It starts from the fallen post, behind the shelf; the first time when your feet are first on the shelf, then about every 25 seconds while you are on the shelf, its block or the fifth boss, never while you are in the air. It is never solid, it is always ahead of you, and it leaves off the back of the west post.
  • Scenery moved out of the play space. The east post's head: 1.2 m east, 1.1 m back and 2 m up, onto the widened shaft, 1.5 m behind you. The fallen post: from standing upright on the shelf, 0.9 m behind you, to lying on the tooth's step, 1.5 m behind. The paper from post to post: from 1 m behind you, with its flags 1.2 m above the timber head's top, to the jade back run 2 m behind, with its lowest flag 2.5 m above the pad.
