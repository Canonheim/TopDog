La Cumbre · Area and background design

Challenge 11.3: Las Cuerdas del Campeón

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The ring. Two horns of rock stand 6 m apart on their pillars, and the First Top Dog's ring was hung between them: its canvas at the level of the horns' feet, a corner post on each horn's top. The weather has taken the ring's floor and the ropes that crossed the gap. What it could not take is the two corners. The inner face of each horn is cut in three notches, one above the other, and across each notch a ring rope is still strung tight between two iron eyes, with a fat cushion over the turnbuckle at each end: three ropes to a side, as a ring has, red, cream and jade. They are still lively. Throw yourself into the corner and it throws you back out, harder than you came in, at the other corner. Six ropes up, you come over the top of the west horn and into its corner post. You start from the shelf below, where the third post lies fallen, looking up through the place where the ring's floor was.

What must not change

  • The six bouncing lines exactly where they are, and what they do: each throws you up and across, a little harder than you came in.
  • The two horns' shapes. No part of a horn or a rope can be held.
  • The fifth gold boss, the rime block under it, and the fling from the boss into the west horn's lowest rope: 6.8 m across and 3.8 m up.
  • The west horn's top and its corner post, which stops the last rope's throw.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The horns' roots. The two pillars, 1.8 m back. Each horn stands on its pillar's top like a dark cap. Behind the east horn the pillar carries on up as a thin back blade to a flat at 996.5 m, which the east post stands on. (1.5 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 2The canvas strip. What is left of the ring's canvas: one strip 6 m wide and 1.5 m tall, cream, torn along its foot, with a faded pug's face. It hangs like a banner from a sagging rope between the two pillars, 2 m behind you, under the lowest ropes. From the shelf it is the first thing that says this was a ring. It hangs upright and torn so that it can't be taken for a floor. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 3The fifth tooth's step, the fallen post and the second frog. The shelf is the front edge of a step in the fifth tooth's body, and the step carries on 1 m further back. On it lies the ring's third corner post, 2.2 m long, with its red pad out over the drop. Today that post stands upright on the shelf itself, in the play space, where it can't be held. A frog sits on the post's end, looking up at the ropes. The first time you stand on the shelf it jumps into the west horn's lowest rope and is thrown from horn to horn up all six. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 4The east horn's post. A corner post 3.4 m tall with its three iron eyes and its red pad, standing on the flat of the back blade, 1.5 m behind the climb. Today it stands 0.4 m behind you, inside the horn's ice tip, and looks like a thing to catch. It moves 1 m east and 1.1 m back. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5The flagstaff, and the paper from horn to horn. A flagstaff 6.3 m tall is lashed to the back of the corner post and rakes back, so that its top is 2 m behind you and 3.3 m above the post's top. The town's paper runs from it to the top of the east horn's post, 12.5 m, whole and bright. Its flags hang 0.8 m below their rope, so the lowest flag is 2.5 m above the pad you stand on. The sheet's staff is 5.5 m: it is 0.8 m longer here, so that it is the flags and not the rope that clear a hand. Today this line hangs 1 m behind you with its flags 1.2 m above the post's top, where it can be taken for a way across. (1.3 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 6The splinter that carries the second ring. A splinter splits off the west pillar's flank and stands up west of the horn, leaning into the wind. It carries the iron ring of the last leap. It is designed on the next page. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The two horns. Dark rock glazed with black ice from foot to tip: blue-black, glossy, in flat planes. Nothing pale is drawn on a horn. Each rope's two iron eyes are set in the ice at the ends of its notch, so you see what the rope is strung on.
  • 8The six ring ropes, and the move shown once. Each rope is a band 0.3 m thick and 4.4 m long, wound in glossy tape, lying exactly along its bouncing line and 0.2 m proud of the ice. On each horn the lowest is red, the middle one cream and the top one jade. A fat cushion covers the turnbuckle at each end, and the cushions throw you too. At rest each rope quivers a little. When you hit one it squashes in, springs back and hums. Between them the three ropes cover the whole inner face of a horn. Today none of this is drawn (note 98). The dashed line is the frog's path and yours: six throws, 2 m higher each time, and the last one carries you west over the horn's top. The frog ends sitting at the corner post's foot, and waits for you there.
  • 9The west horn's top and the corner post. The top is 2.6 m of warm dry rock. At its west end stands the corner post: square honey timber 0.4 m thick and 3 m tall, with three iron turnbuckle eyes up its east face and a red pad on top. It is the only post you can hold. The sixth rope throws you over the top and into it.
  • 10The fifth boss and its rime block. The launch. Stand on the block to reach the gold, and the block leaves. Both are as on La Corona's page.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A rope squashes where you hit it, springs back and hums. Its cushions puff a little dust.
  • The canvas strip stirs toward the east. The paper overhead lifts the same way.
  • The frog's croak on the shelf, before it jumps.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 98, the ropes are drawn. In the game the six ring ropes had no model at all. Each now has one, lying exactly along its bouncing line, and it squashes and springs when it throws you. No bouncing line moves.
  • Note 98, the view from the shelf. Today, standing on the shelf, the camera leans 1 m down, and the frame tops out at about 988 m, level with the top of the west horn's lowest rope. In the design, while you stand on the shelf the camera leans 3 m up and 3 m west. The frame then runs from 974 to 992 m, and it holds the lowest rope of each horn whole, and the west horn's second rope.
  • Note 98, the place says what it is: the canvas strip, the cushions, the east horn's post and the fallen post are all in the shelf's view.
  • Note 98, the move is shown once, by the second frog that the sheet proposes. The note's other idea, a frozen climber caught in the lowest rope, is not used.
  • Scenery moved out of the play space. The east horn's post: 1 m east and 1.1 m back, to 1.5 m behind you. The fallen post: from standing upright on the shelf, 0.9 m behind you, to lying on the tooth's step, 1.5 m behind. The paper from horn to horn: from 1 m behind you, with its flags 1.2 m above the corner post's top, to 2 m behind, with its lowest flag 2.5 m above it.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
