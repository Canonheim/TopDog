Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.2: Nidos

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Under the arch, between the tower and the face, there is a bay of still air 28 m wide, and the vultures have it. Two ledges face each other across it: the alcove at the top of the tower's crack, and a deeper ledge on the face, 10 m higher, at the head of a slick slab. There is a nest on each, and the two birds fly one round between them, low on the way out and high on the way back, half a lap apart. Nobody built anything here. The trail went another way. The only way across is the way the birds go: you take one by its pale legs as it passes over the alcove's lip and let go when your feet brush the far ledge. The frog found the far nest first, and sits in it among the sticks.

What must not change

  • The two vultures' round, their speed, and one passing every 8.4 seconds.
  • The alcove's lip, where a bird passes 2 m over it, and the nest ledge, where it passes 2.1 m over.
  • The slick slab under the nest ledge, which tips an early drop onto El Balcón.
  • Only the legs can be held. The dark body cannot.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mountain's shoulder. The kit's flat cut-out card of the same cliff, 12 m back, hazed to a mid blue-grey, with ledges and one small dead tree cut into its edge. It stands on the west of the frame only, so the face goes on behind you and never stops inside the frame. East of its edge there is nothing of ours: sky, and the far view that is given. The sketch shows it as a dashed outline. Here it stands behind the west third of the bay, so a bird flies from sky into cliff and you can read how far it has gone. (12.0 to 14.0 m behind you)
  • 2The colony's cliff. The face's body, grey-tan, showing 1.5 m proud of the slick slab all the way down it, and carrying the nest ledge on behind you as a deeper shelf. On it: three more nests on small ledges, each with a white streak under it, and a bone pile. The slab you can touch has none of this on it. It stays dark and bare. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The far nest, and the frog in it. The nest that is on the nest ledge today, 1.6 m across, moved back to the ledge's back half. The second of the cliffs' two frogs sits in it among the sticks, 0.3 m tall, and looks up as a bird goes over (notes 68 and 60). (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 4The tower's body. The same body as on El Balcón's page, carried on up. A long white streak runs down it from the alcove. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The near nest and the larder. The alcove goes back 1.4 m further than you can walk. In that back part sit the round's other nest and the bone pile that is in the alcove today. Today this nest hangs at the height of the alcove's roof, inside the rock. It comes down onto the floor. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6The flag in the alcove. The second flag of El Balcón's gust. From here it tells you when the gust below has stopped. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The whitewashed lips. Where a bird passes lowest over a ledge it has whitened the lip. There are two such marks: 1.1 m of the alcove's lip, which is where you stand to take the legs, and 1.5 m of the nest ledge's lip, which is where you let go. They are painted on ledges you can already hold.
  • 8The legs. A vulture flies with its legs hanging straight down, pale and bare, 0.5 m long: the only pale thing on it. Its body and wings are dark. The kit's vulture is drawn at the course's size.
  • 9The slab. Dark brown, rain-streaked and glossy from the nest ledge's lip to the bottom, with nothing pale on it. It reads as a slide, and it is one.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10A chalk lip with a grass tuft. At the bottom west corner, in front of the solid rock under the slab. Nothing stands in front of the bay. (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)
