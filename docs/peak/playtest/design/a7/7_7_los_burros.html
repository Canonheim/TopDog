Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.7: Los Burros

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The last three burros of the pack trail. Each was swung up by the drivers' hoist, in a sling, to the ledge it stands on: one at the back of the ledge at the gully's head, and one on the top of each of two rock needles that stand out of the arch, with nothing but air round them. Each had a load for the cableway beside it. Then the trail failed under them, and nobody came back. They are still there, and they are in a bad mood: ears back, a hind leg cocked. Take hold of one and it kicks you east, up and over the gully, to the next. The third one kicks you onto the balcony of the tower's upper window. Above them all, the first painted timber of the cableway's scaffold comes down to stand on the needles, so you know where their loads were going.

What must not change

  • The three burros, each where it stands, and each one's kick.
  • The two needle ledges and the tower's balcony. Each kick lands 1.7 m short of the next burro.
  • The first kick's low line, which passes under the roof over the gully's head.
  • A miss lands in the gully or on the counter-slope, and goes through El Ojo.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mountain's shoulder. The kit's flat cut-out card of the same cliff, 12 m back, hazed to a mid blue-grey, with ledges and one small dead tree cut into its edge. It stands on the west of the frame only, so the face goes on behind you and never stops inside the frame. East of its edge there is nothing of ours: sky, and the far view that is given. The sketch shows it as a dashed outline. (12.0 to 14.0 m behind you)
  • 2The top of the gully's far wall. The arch's body, along the bottom of the frame, as on the page for Desprendimiento. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The two needles. Each burro ledge is the near edge of the flat top of a needle of grey-tan chalk, 4 m wide, that stands on the arch's body. The top goes back 2 m behind the ledge you land on. Today each needle is a flat tan card. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The three loads. Behind each burro, on the back of its ledge, is the load it carried, and an iron hitching peg with a frayed end of halter. Behind the first: four paint tins, two in the cableway's red and two in its yellow ochre. Behind the second: a bundle of sawn posts, painted ochre. Behind the third: a coil of grey haul cable, 0.15 m thick. They are the first things on the climb that belong to the cableway. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The standing hoist. On the back of the tower's balcony, at its west end: the same hoist as the one that lies on El Balcón, still standing. Two lashed poles lean out west over the gap, toward the second needle, with a pulley block at the head and no rope. It is how a burro got onto a needle. (2.0 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 6The scaffold's feet. The low station's scaffold starts 9 m overhead. Its four legs come down to the rock here as raking struts, 0.3 m square, painted yellow ochre, each ending in an iron shoe: one into the side of the first needle, two into the sides of the second, one onto the back of the tower's balcony. The three on the needles meet the rock under the ledges, so nothing stands on a ledge you land on. Today the scaffold stops at its lowest walkway and stands on air. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7Smoke from the tea cave. A thread of smoke rising from a crack in the gully's top tread, just east of the first burro's ledge. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 8Where the boulders start. The rubble pile on the roof over the first burro, as on the page for Desprendimiento. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The burros. Each stands facing west with its ears flat back and its near hind leg cocked, so it reads as a kick waiting to happen. By the law of note 76, the burro that is drawn is the burro you can touch: head, neck, barrel with its striped blanket, four legs. Today only the barrel and two legs are solid.
  • 10Straw. On each ledge's west end, and on the balcony, a scatter of dry straw from the burro's feed lies exactly where a kick lands you. It is drawn on the ledge and changes nothing you stand on.

Small effects, no models

  • A burro flicks an ear and stamps when you come within 3 m of it.
  • The kick: a bray, a puff of dust and straw, and you are in the air.
  • The boulders go on coming down the gully underneath you the whole time.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • No note from the playtest is about this challenge. Note 68's tea cave is under the first burro's ledge and has its own page.
  • Note 76's law, what is drawn is what can be touched: the kit's burro is 1.8 m long and the solid shape the course gives it is 1.3 m. The two are made to agree, either way. If the head and neck become solid, that is a small course change: 0.5 m more burro to take hold of on its west side.
  • The burros are in the play space and can be held. So can everything else in it. The loads, the pegs and the hoist are all on the backs of the ledges, behind you.
  • This area has no foreground. Every kick flies through open air.
