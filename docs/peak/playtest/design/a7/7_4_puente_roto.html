Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.4: Puente Roto

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The pack trail crossed here. From the keyhole in the arch's hanging fin to the window in the tower there is a gap of 10 m, with the arch for a roof and El Balcón 46 m below, and the drivers hung a rope bridge across it: a plank deck 1 m wide between two hand ropes, made fast to a pair of posts at each end. It is out. It parted in the middle, and each half fell back against its own wall, where it hangs now like a ladder with boards missing. The stubs of the deck are still on their bearers at both ends, and the back post of each pair still stands with its hand rope frayed off short. You go down the west half, swing it, and let go for the east half.

What must not change

  • The keyhole's floor and sill, and the tower's window.
  • The two stubs of deck, each 1.5 m.
  • The two hanging halves: where each hangs, 5.3 m long, 7.1 m apart, and how they swing.
  • The fling: 7.1 m, from the west half to the east half.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The fin's body. The body of the hanging fin, as on the page for Árbol Seco. It closes the back of the keyhole. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The tower's body, and what is left in the window. The tower's body closes the back of the window, 1 m further back than you can walk. Against it: a stack of spare deck boards and a pack saddle on a peg. Somebody meant to mend the bridge. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The west anchor. The back post of the pair the bridge was made fast to: 1.6 m tall and 0.25 m thick, with the back hand rope still knotted round it and frayed off after 0.6 m, like a tassel. Under the stub of deck is its back bearer, a pole out of the rock with a strut under it. The front post of the pair is a stump: see the foreground. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 4The east anchor. The same, at the tower's end: back post, frayed hand rope, bearer and strut. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5Boards that fell. Two boards from the middle of the bridge, caught on the rock on their way down: one in a joint of the tower's body and one on a ledge of the fin's body. Both are behind you and out of reach. (1.4 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 6La Cornisa's first flag. At the back of the window's east mouth. You see it streaming east before you are across the bridge. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The west hanging half. Note 66. Half a rope bridge, hanging down from the end of the stub: two side ropes with a ladder of boards between them, three boards gone, and the lowest board hanging by one end. It hangs nearly edge on to the camera, so the two ropes show 0.3 m apart. All of it is hemp and honey planks, and all of it can be held. Its length and where it hangs are the course's.
  • 8The east hanging half. The same, down the tower's wall. The board on its end is the one you catch.
  • 9The stubs' broken ends. Each stub of deck is three boards on its two bearers. The last board is snapped across, with splinters. Frayed rope ends hang 0.3 m under it and sway.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10Two post stumps. The front post of each pair, snapped off 0.3 m above the floor when the bridge went. With the back posts they show you that you are walking between the two posts of a bridge's end. They are too low to cover anything. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The frayed rope ends sway a little, always. There is no wind under the arch, so they never stream.
  • A hanging half creaks on its knots when you put your weight on it.
  • A board you kick loose falls a long time and you hear it land on El Balcón.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 66 (not built): this place looks like a rope bridge that is out and broken. It plays the same.
  • Note 66 also says the swing should be judged again once the ropes swing properly (note 20). That still holds.
  • Course change, a small one: what you can hold on each side becomes the whole hanging half, 0.3 m wide as the camera sees it, where today it is one rope. That brings the two halves 0.3 m nearer each other. The board on each rope's end is the same 1.2 m board.
  • The two front posts are cut to stumps so that nothing stands in front of you at either end of the bridge.
