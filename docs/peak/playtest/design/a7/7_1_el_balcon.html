Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.1: El Balcón

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

You come out of the mountain here. The chimney's crack ends at a door in the east face, and 2 m under it is El Balcón: one hard bed of chalk standing out of the cliff as a shelf 22 m long. It is the first open air since the station, and the first sight of the town, tiny in the valley below. The pack trail's drivers used the shelf as their yard. Everything for the cableway was unloaded here, and then hauled up the west face of the rock tower that stands on the shelf's east end, by a hoist of two lashed poles. The burros went up in slings. The hoist has fallen and the trail below the shelf is gone under the scree. What is left to climb is the tower's own rock: an upright joint in its west face that frost has opened in three lengths, with two hard beds between them where it is still shut. One climber got this far, sat down against the back wall to look at the town, and is still there. The wind starts here: a gust off the tower, shown by two flags.

What must not change

  • The shelf, the door 2 m above its west end, and the walk east to the tower's foot.
  • The three lengths of crack and the two blank lengths between them: reaches of 1.6, 1.7 and 1.8 m.
  • The gust: it blows west, off the wall, for 1.5 seconds in every 5, from 9 m along the shelf to the tower.
  • The slick lip under the tower, and the one shrub on the east lip, over the throat.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mountain's shoulder. The kit's flat cut-out card of the same cliff, 12 m back, hazed to a mid blue-grey, with ledges and one small dead tree cut into its edge. It stands on the west of the frame only, so the face goes on behind you and never stops inside the frame. East of its edge there is nothing of ours: sky, and the far view that is given. The sketch shows it as a dashed outline. (12.0 to 14.0 m behind you)
  • 2The shelf's back and its back wall. The shelf carries on 3 m behind you as floor, to a back wall of grey-tan cliff. The wall is tall by the door and comes down in steps of one bed each to a low kerb, so the sky and the far view stay open behind most of the walk. The tops of its steps are the same two hard beds that close the tower's crack: you can follow them across the whole picture. (1.2 to 4.2 m behind you)
  • 3The tower's body. The tower you climb is the near edge of a bigger tower of grey-tan chalk that stands 1.5 m behind it and shows a metre proud of it on both sides. It replaces today's flat tan card. The two hard beds run round it as two darker bands that stand out as small ledges, with a nest's white streak under the upper one. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The one who stopped to look. The cliffs' frozen climber. He sits on the shelf's back floor with his back against the wall's lowest step, knees up, facing the valley and the town. He is 1.6 m behind the walk, so you pass in front of him. (1.6 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The pug arrow. The trail's arrow sign, pointing east along the shelf. It stands where it does today, moved back out of the play space. The kit has it. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 6The two flags. One at each edge of the gust. The first is a pole 3 m tall at the back of the shelf, 9 m along it. The second is 2.2 m tall and stands in the back corner of the alcove at the top of the crack. Each has a streamer 1.5 m long in the town's bunting colours. When the gust comes both stream west, off the wall, and between gusts both hang slack. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 7The fallen hoist. Lying at the tower's foot on the shelf's back floor: two poles 5.5 m long, still lashed together at the head, with the pulley block on the lashing and no rope. Beside them is a stack of split planks that never went up. It says how anything got up this wall, and that nothing does now. (2.2 to 3.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The crack's three lengths. Each length is an open joint with lips of fresh white chalk, broken and matte. The two lengths between them are the hard beds: shut tight, dark brown and rain-polished. The white tells you where a hand goes before you reach the wall.
  • 9The gust, drawn. Notes 89 and 70. Long wavy white lines with an inked edge, across the gust's whole reach and nowhere else, moving west. They start at the tower's face and stop at the first flag. A whistle comes one second before them.
  • 10The worn way. The shelf's top is dry white chalk, with a path trodden down its middle by the burros, from under the door to the foot of the crack. It is drawn on the shelf and changes nothing you stand on.
  • 11The shrub. The mercy. One dry shrub on the east lip, where it is today, with 0.5 m of root to catch. Below it is the whole mountain.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 12A chalk lip with a grass tuft. At the bottom west corner, in front of solid rock and below the walk. Its top is under the door's sill. (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)
  • 13One trail post. A lashed pole 3 m tall standing on the lip beside the door, with a rag of bunting on it that tells you the wind before you have reached the first flag. You pass behind it for one step as you come out of the door. It is the only thing on the cliffs that stands in front of you where you walk. (1.2 to 1.4 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The gust's wavy lines, and the whistle one second before them (notes 89, 70).
  • Grass tufts along the back wall's steps lean west in the gust and stand up again.
  • The camera looks a little down along the shelf, so the town sits low in the frame, as the course has it.
  • White dust blows out of the door behind you.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 89: the wind is drawn loudly. Wavy lines across the whole gust, and flags that stream.
  • Note 70: the lines stop where the gust stops. There is no wind you cannot see.
  • Scenery moved out of the play space, where it stands today: the first flag and the pug arrow go back behind the walk. Neither is something you touch.
  • The second flag stands today 0.6 m west of the alcove's lip, on nothing. It moves 1.1 m east, to the alcove's back corner.
  • Nothing you touch moves or is added.
