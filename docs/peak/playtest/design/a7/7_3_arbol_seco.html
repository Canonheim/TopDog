Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.3: Árbol Seco

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

One tree grew on this face. It rooted at the back of the vultures' ledge and leaned out from under the arch to get at the light, 24 m of trunk, until its crown was level with the keyhole in the arch's hanging fin. Then lightning ran down it. The strike killed it and left a black, glassy scar along the middle of the trunk, and the weather has bleached the rest silver white. The branches broke off short. Eight stubs are left across the scar, side to side, and they are the only way past it. At the top the last of the crown is still on: two long branches, split at the join, that reach almost to the keyhole's sill. They are the two that snap.

What must not change

  • The trunk's lean and its three lengths: 8 m you can hold, 11.5 m of scar you cannot, 4.5 m you can.
  • The eight stubs, each where it is, 1.72 m apart.
  • The two breaking branches: the same length, place and angle, giving way after 1.2 and 0.8 seconds. The keyhole's sill is 1.35 m from the tip.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mountain's shoulder. The kit's flat cut-out card of the same cliff, 12 m back, hazed to a mid blue-grey, with ledges and one small dead tree cut into its edge. It stands on the west of the frame only, so the face goes on behind you and never stops inside the frame. East of its edge there is nothing of ours: sky, and the far view that is given. The sketch shows it as a dashed outline. The white tree and its black scar both read against its mid blue-grey. (12.0 to 14.0 m behind you)
  • 2The fin's body. The hanging fin with the keyhole in it is the near edge of a thicker fin of grey-tan chalk. The body closes the back of the keyhole, so it reads as a doorway into rock and not as a window. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The tree's far boughs. A tree has branches all round it. Behind each stub you hold, a longer dead bough goes off into the scene from the far side of the trunk, grey-white and duller than the stubs. The stubs are the near ones, broken short. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4The crown. The kit's dead crown, 5 m wide, at the top of the trunk, moved back out of the play space. It is a fan of dead boughs. The two branches you hold are the two nearest of them, so they belong to the tree. (1.3 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 5The nest and its frog. At the tree's foot, as on the page for Nidos. The tree's roots go into the rock beside the nest. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The two breaking branches. Note 65. They are real branches of this tree: bleached white, tapering, with twigs, each joined to the wood below it. Each wears the cliffs' breakable look: split along the grain at the join, with a strip of bark hanging. The first is 2.9 m long and the second 2.3 m. A branch creaks and dips when you take it, and it goes in splinters and twigs.
  • 7The scar. Black and glassy, with a forked, jagged edge at both ends, so it is plainly lightning. A split runs up its middle.
  • 8The eight stubs. Each is the broken root of a branch, bleached white, with a ring of pale splintered wood at its end. They stand out of the scar on alternate sides.
  • 9The roots. At its foot the trunk spreads into three roots that grip the back of the ledge. They are drawn on the trunk's first metre.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A branch creaks and dips the moment you take it. Then it goes in splinters, with a puff of grey dust (note 65).
  • The broken branch grows back out from its join after 8 seconds, as the note allows.
  • A vulture passes under the trunk on its round, 4 m below the first stubs.
