Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.5: La Cornisa

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Out of the east mouth of the tower's window you are past the edge of El Balcón, and for the first time there is nothing under you but La Garganta. One hard bed of chalk juts 9 m east from the tower here, like the brim of a hat: La Cornisa. Its underside is a seam of flints, and the flints that hang lowest are the knobs you go along, hand over hand, while a crosswind blows out from the wall and swings your legs toward the valley. The trail's drivers did not go under it. They went over: they hammered a stair of iron pegs and plank treads up its sloping back, from its nose to the tunnel above. The treads are gone and the back row of pegs is bare. The front row is your way up. Just under the window's floor, where the tower's edge becomes the whole mountain's edge, a yellow sign sticks out over the drop.

What must not change

  • The first knob in the window's mouth, the six knobs under the roof and the knob on the nose, each where it is.
  • The six iron pegs up the back, and the slick slope between them.
  • The crosswind: it blows out, east, for 2.2 seconds in every 4.5, with 0.8 seconds of warning.
  • The drop: 47 m of air to the shrub, and then the throat.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The tower's body. The tower's body, showing a metre proud of its east face all the way down. Nothing else of ours stands on this side: east of it is the sky and the far view. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The cornice's body, and the flint seam. The cornice is the near edge of a thicker bed. Behind you its underside hangs 0.5 m lower than the roof you hold, and the seam of flints runs along it: a row of grey knobs with the six white ones in front of them. Above the sloping back it stands 2 m higher than the slope, as a step, and that step is where the trail's leavings are. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The stair's back row. Behind each iron peg is its twin, driven into the step, in dull brown rust. A plank tread lay across each pair. Two treads are still on, broken short, and both are behind you. So the six pegs read as the front row of a stair, and not as six pegs put there for a climber. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 4The warning sign. Note 67. A yellow diamond 0.9 m across with a black exclamation mark and no words, hung on a rust-orange iron bracket that sticks 1.2 m straight out from the tower's body, just under the window's floor. It is weathered and a little bent. It swings and creaks in the crosswind. It is scenery, behind you. (1.4 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 5The two flags. One at the back of the window's east mouth, 2 m tall. One on the body's step above the nose, 2.4 m tall. Both stream east, out from the wall, when the crosswind blows. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The knobs under the roof. Flints in their white rind, each the size of two fists: the same knobs as in the crack you came out of the mountain by. Dry, white and matte against the dark roof. The first one hangs in the window's mouth.
  • 7The nose. The cornice's tip is one great boss of white flint, 1.8 m across. It is how you get round from the underside to the back.
  • 8The six pegs. Rust-orange iron, 0.4 m long, each with a square hammered head and a ring of white chalk dust where it was driven in. The slope between them is dark brown and polished.
  • 9The crosswind, drawn. Notes 89 and 70. Long wavy white lines moving east, across the crosswind's whole reach: from the tower's face out to 4 m past the nose, under the cornice and over it. Where they curl and stop, the wind stops.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The crosswind's wavy lines, and its whistle 0.8 seconds before (notes 89, 70).
  • The sign creaks and swings on its bracket when the crosswind blows, and hangs still between (note 67).
  • Chalk dust and one dead leaf go past you with each gust, out and down.
  • At the window's mouth the camera pulls back and looks down, as the course has it, so the drop is on screen before you go out.
