Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.6: Desprendimiento

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the arch is a gully, and the gully is where the mountain throws its loose rock away. Boulders gather in a rubble pile at the gully's head, come down three slick treads in waves of three, and go through El Ojo, the hole in the arch's crown, to El Balcón 52 m below. The trail had to cross this. The drivers built a rock fence along the east slope: leaning timber posts, a top rail, and rope netting to stop the small stuff. The boulders went through it. The netting is torn out, and the top rail hangs in three lengths from the posts. You cross hand over hand along those three lengths, with your feet in the boulders' road over the hole. Then you climb the gully itself, on the flints that stand out of each tread. Under each of the two lips the soft bed is worn back into a pocket. The drivers sat out the rockfalls in them, and so do you.

What must not change

  • The three lengths of rail, each where it hangs, and the 1.5 m gaps between them.
  • The gully: three treads, two lips, the nine knobs, the two pockets with their steps and their lips' noses.
  • The boulders: waves of three every 9 seconds, a rumble 1.2 seconds before, down the roof, the treads and the hole.
  • El Ojo. Whatever slides goes through it onto El Balcón.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mountain's shoulder. The kit's flat cut-out card of the same cliff, 12 m back, hazed to a mid blue-grey, with ledges and one small dead tree cut into its edge. It stands on the west of the frame only, so the face goes on behind you and never stops inside the frame. East of its edge there is nothing of ours: sky, and the far view that is given. The sketch shows it as a dashed outline. Here it shows above the gully's far wall, in the west of the frame. (12.0 to 14.0 m behind you)
  • 2The gully's far wall. The arch's body. Behind you it stands higher than the gully's bed all the way along: by 15 m at El Ojo, and by 3 m at the gully's head. So the gully is a trough, and El Ojo reads as a well, not a window. It replaces today's flat tan card. A paler grey band is rubbed along it 0.8 m above each tread: the line the boulders take, drawn before a boulder comes. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The two needles. Two stalks of the cliff's body standing on the far wall. They carry the two burro ledges of Los Burros. Today each is a flat tan card. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The rock fence's posts and netting. Four timber posts stand on the slope behind you, leaning downhill, one at each end of each rail. Each rail hangs from its posts by rope lashings. What is left of the rope netting hangs in two torn pieces under the first rail and lies in a heap at the fence's foot. Over the hole there is none. The kit's fence frame is moved back to stand here. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 5Where the boulders start. The kit's rubble pile, 3 m wide, on the roof over the gully's head, moved back out of the play space, with the next three boulders sitting on it. Before each wave it shakes and a puff of dust comes off it. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6The driver's bench. The lower pocket goes back 0.7 m further than you can stand. At its back is a short plank on two iron pegs, where a driver sat out a rockfall. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 7The tea cave's door. At the back of the upper pocket: a doorway with a striped blanket tied back, and warm light on the pocket's floor. It is the drivers' shelter, and it has its own page. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 8The tower's body. At the east edge of the frame, closing the back of the stair tunnel you come out of. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The rails' lashings. Each rail is a round timber of the fence, honey coloured, 0.25 m thick, with a hemp lashing at each end where it hangs from its post. The ends that face a gap are splintered. The three gaps are where a post's top was knocked off.
  • 10The treads' knobs. Flints in their white rind, standing out of the slick treads, three to a tread. The treads themselves are dark brown and polished by the boulders, with dull grey scuffs and nothing white on them.
  • 11The two pockets. Each pocket has a floor and a step of dry white chalk, and the nose of the lip above it is a white knob. Their roofs and back walls stay dark.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 12A chalk lip with a grass tuft. At the bottom west corner, in front of the solid rock of the face. Nothing stands in front of the gully or the hole. (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Before each wave: a rumble, the rubble pile shakes, and a puff of dust comes off it. Then the boulders roll into view down the roof before they drop.
  • Dust hangs in the gully after a wave, as the course has it.
  • A boulder that goes through El Ojo is heard landing on El Balcón, a long time later.
