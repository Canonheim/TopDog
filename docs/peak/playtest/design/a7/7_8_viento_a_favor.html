Los Riscos del Viento · Area and background design

Challenge 7.8: Viento a Favor

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Everything here is east of the tower and over La Garganta. From the tower's upper window the trail's pegs once went on up the east face to a porch 10 m higher. They have all pulled out, and one plank is left sticking out of the window's mouth. 19 m out in the air stands El Cuerno, a lone horn of chalk on a stalk that comes up out of the throat, with 3 m of flat top and a dead snag on it. A third vulture has a nest on the horn and a nest in the window, and beats back and forth between them. So the way to the porch is there and back. Out on the bird. Up the snag: its stub, then a knot, then the end of its one bough. And back on the wind: every 7 seconds a gust comes off El Colmillo, the fang on the far side of the throat, and blows toward the tower. Let go of the bough as it starts and it carries you home. A wind sock on the porch shows you where home is.

What must not change

  • The perch plank, the vulture's road and its one second of hovering at each end.
  • The horn's flat top, and the snag's three holds: the stub, the knot, the bough's end 5.6 m over the top.
  • The porch: 17.9 m back to the west and 4.4 m higher than the bough.
  • The tail gust: toward the tower for 2.5 seconds in every 7, with a whistle one second before.
  • The cableway's two pylon legs overhead, which stop a throw that goes too high. They are biome 8's.
  • The puzzle, exactly as it is (note 69).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The tower's body. The tower's body, a metre proud of its east face. It closes the backs of the upper window and of the porch. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2Peg stubs up the east face. Up the body's east edge, from the plank to the porch: seven stubs of iron peg in dull brown rust, each snapped off short, 1.4 m apart. They are the trail's way to the porch, gone. They show that the porch is where you are meant to get to, and that there is no climbing to it. (1.4 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 3The window's nest. One end of the third vulture's round: a nest and a bone pile in the back of the upper window's east mouth, 1 m further back than you can walk. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The horn's stalk, and the horn's nest. The horn is the near edge of a stalk of grey-tan chalk that comes up from the throat, wider at the bottom. It is the one thing of ours that stands east of the tower. On the back of its top is the round's other nest, with a white streak down the stalk under it. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The snag's trunk. Above the stub the snag's trunk leans back, away from you, out of the play space, and goes on up to 6.4 m, grey-white. The knot is a branch stub that points at the camera out of it. The bough comes forward from it and ends at the camera, so what you hold is its end. Today the knot and the bough's end hang in the air with nothing drawn between them. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The wind sock on the porch. On a post at the back of the porch: a sock 1.5 m long in red and white bands. In the gust it fills and points west, into the porch. Between gusts it hangs. It is the brightest thing on the tower, so your eye goes to the porch from the horn. It stands where one of today's three flags is. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 7Two flags. One 2.4 m tall at the back of the window's mouth. One 1.6 m tall on the back of the horn's top, at its west end, clear of the snag and of the place where the vulture hovers. Both stream west in the gust. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The snag. The kit's snag, from the dead tree: bleached silver white, with a strip of bark on the stub. The knot is a round of pale end grain 0.4 m across. The bough's end is a bigger one, 0.5 m across, worn smooth.
  • 9The tail gust, drawn. Notes 70 and 89. Long wavy white lines moving west, across the whole gap: they peel off the face of El Colmillo and run to the tower. Today the wind stops in mid air 5 m east of the horn. Its east edge moves 4.8 m, to the fang's face, so there is no strip of still air. In the second of warning, a front of lines crosses from the fang first.
  • 10The perch plank. One honey plank, 1.1 m, sticking out of the window's mouth on a pole bearer, with a rope lashing. It is the first tread of the trail's way round the tower, and the only one left.
