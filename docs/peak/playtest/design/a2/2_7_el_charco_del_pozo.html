Las Aguas Negras · Area and background design

Challenge 2.7: El Charco del Pozo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The foot of El Pozo del Diablo. The well was here long before the company: a round shaft of black rock, 12 m across, that goes up through the whole mountain. The company built a brick hall round its foot and used the pool under it as a reservoir, and floated barrels across it from a store in the west wall. So the hall is brick on both sides and bare wet rock in the middle, where the well comes down. Stand under it and look up: the shaft goes up out of sight, and at the very end of it is one dot of light. You will cross this shaft three times higher up, and everything that falls from those crossings lands here. This is the place the mountain sends you back to.

What must not change

  • The pool, the two ledges, and the lip the pool goes over at its east end.
  • The barrels as note 21 has them: eight, unevenly spaced, out of the cove, drifting east, over the lip and down the fall. Two of the eight gaps are too wide to step across and have to be swum (Parker, 11-13).
  • The well's mouth in the roof: 12 m wide, from x -306 to -294, and the camera's lean upward here.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The hall's two brick flanks. Brick, 15 m tall, west and east of the well, each ending in a brick pier against the rock. A bricked-up round arch stands in the east flank. Each ledge sits on iron beams out of its flank: you see the beam ends under it. (5.5 to 6.5 m behind you)
  • 2The well's own rock. Between the two flanks there is no brick: black wet rock, the back of the well, from the water up through the roof and on up. Where the hall's roof meets it there is a ring of pale stone, the collar the company put round the well's mouth. The well keeps this width, 12 m, at every place you will meet it. (1.2 to 6.5 m behind you)
  • 3The throat. The look up the well, built the way a paper diorama builds a tunnel: five rings of black rock cut out of card, each smaller, further back and higher than the last. The first is 12 m across and touches the roof. The last is under 2 m. Each ring has a thin pale edge on its upper side, lit from above. Today this is one flat black shape. The rings make it a shaft. (2.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 4The dot of light. Behind the last ring: one small pale dot, the top of the well, 365 m up. It is the only thing in the biome that is daylight. It does not flicker. (7.9 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 5What has fallen. On a shelf of rock at the back of the pool, under the well, far behind the barrels: a broken crystal point, a rotted mushroom cap as big as a table, a carved stone block with a pug's face, and a coil of rope. One thing from each place above where the well is crossed. All of it grey-blue and dim, like the rest of the scenery. (3.4 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 6The barrels' cove. Note 21: a round brick arch at the waterline in the west flank, black inside, where the barrel store's slipway meets the pool. Each barrel floats forward out of the dark. It can't be touched until it is in your own plane. (1.3 to 4.4 m behind you)
  • 7The frog's door. At the east end of the ledge you start from: the round iron door of the valve chamber, left ajar, and the room behind it. It has its own page. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 8Two works lanterns. One on each flank, on wall brackets, greenish and steady. There is no lantern under the well. The middle of the hall is lit only by the pale light that comes down the shaft. (5.2 to 5.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The lip. A worn stone sill where the pool goes over into the fall, with white water breaking on it. A barrel tips on it before it goes.
  • 10Copings on the two ledges. Pale stone copings, as everywhere you stand in the works.
  • 11Hoops and brands. Each barrel gets two iron hoops and the company's pug branded on its end, drawn on. They are the mine's barrels: timber here is only ever a left-over from below.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • One slow drip down the middle of the well, landing in the pool with a ring. It has fallen a long way.
  • A few pale motes drifting down the shaft, the same ones the well has all the way up.
  • A faint pale light on the water under the well, and nothing brighter.
  • A barrel going over the lip tumbles down the fall and splashes in the river below.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 21: the barrels come out of a modelled cove, eight of them, unevenly spaced, and go over the fall.
  • Note 22: the pool bends over its lip into the fall. Foam where barrels and ledges meet the water.
  • Note 60: the frog is not on a barrel or in the cove. It is in the valve chamber at the ledge's east end.
  • Parker, 11-13: the three fallen things under the well stay: a crystal point, a mushroom cap, a carved temple block.
  • Note 2 (Parker, 11-13): the water keeps its own tint in each cave. Here it is black where it is deep.
