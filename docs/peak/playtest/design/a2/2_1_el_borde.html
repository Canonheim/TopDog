Las Aguas Negras · Area and background design

Challenge 2.1: El Borde

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is the tail of the works: the brick tunnel that carries the river its last stretch to the weir, where it goes over into the mine as the great waterfall. The company ran an iron water main under the tunnel's roof, from the wheel pit above to the dry shaft, to send wash water down to the mine. It is bracketed to the back wall and it is the only way across. Two lengths of it have rusted through. Just upstream of the weir hangs the chain that once held a boom across the river to catch driftwood: it is the last thing you can grab before the lip.

What must not change

  • The main exactly where it is: up from the ledge, then 19.5 m level at 94.5 m, with its two rusty lengths (note 16).
  • The one chain, its length, and how far its end hangs over the water.
  • The weir's slot, the pier with its peaked top, the lip ledge, the landing and the ladder up.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The tunnel's back wall. Slate-grey brick, 6 m tall, with a brick rib every 6 m. Upstream of the weir, round culvert mouths open at the waterline with black water going back under them. On the ledge there is one arch, bricked up. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The weir, carried on behind. The weir is as wide as the tunnel. Behind the slot you fall through, the same stone lip and the same white sheet of water run back into the scene, so the slot is the near end of a real weir. (1.2 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 3The main's brackets and its two ends. A cast bracket from the back wall under the main every few metres, and a bigger one where the chain hangs. At the west end the main turns up through the roof to the wheel pit. At the east end it turns straight back to the wall. From there on it is scenery: it runs flat along the back wall, 2.6 m behind you, and goes down the dry shaft. Every length of it that is in the play space is a length you can hold. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4What the landing hangs from. Three iron beams out of the back wall carry the brick landing over the river. You see their ends under it. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The head of the dry shaft. The last timber set of the mine, standing against the first brick of the works. It belongs to the join from the mine and is drawn here only so the two pages agree. (1.4 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The first works lantern. On a wall bracket over the ledge, greenish and steady. It is the first cold light after the mine's amber. (2.2 to 2.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The two rusted lengths. Already in the game (note 16): brown, cracked, a corner gone. They are drawn here so the page shows the place as it is.
  • 8Flanged joints on the main. A bolted flange every 3 m and at each end of a rusted length, drawn on the main. They say it is a pipe and not a girder.
  • 9The weir's stone lip. A pale stone coping on the edge the river goes over, with white water breaking on it. The pier's peaked top stays black and wet.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10A pipe at the frame's east edge. One riser pipe with an elbow, in front of the solid rock east of the ledge. It stands in front of the play space, so it is scenery, and it never crosses open space. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)
  • 11The near bank's brick lip. A low broken run of brick along the bottom of the frame, below the river's bed. It stops short of the weir, so nothing stands in front of the water or the fall. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)
