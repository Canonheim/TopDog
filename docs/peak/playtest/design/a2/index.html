Area and background design

Biome 2: Las Aguas Negras

The flooded works, 86 to 176 m. Brick, iron pipe and black water, along the river that feeds the great waterfall.

Design only. This sheet comes first: every area in the biome is drawn from it.

What this place is

When the mine was rich, the company built a water works above it: a dam, a mill race, a water wheel, an aqueduct, sluice gates, pumps and a boiler house, all to wash ore and drive machines. It is a generation newer than the timber mine and it was built to last: brick and cast iron, not posts and planks.

Then the river took it back. The lower floors are under black water, the current runs through the arches, and the whole works leaks, drips and steams. Some of it still runs by itself: the wheel turns, the gates rise and slam, the pipes are hot.

The river flows east and goes over the lip into the mine as the 113 m waterfall. Anything that goes in the water here is on its way to the Sump.

How it is built

  • Brick, in slate-grey courses, with round arches. Every opening is a round arch: doors, culverts, the aqueduct's spans, the windows of the boiler house. Round arches belong to this biome and to no other.
  • Buttresses and piers with a pale stone coping on top. The copings are what you stand on.
  • Cast iron: pipes on wall brackets, with flanged joints and valve wheels; riveted plate; grated catwalks on brackets; chain. Sound iron is rust orange. Pipes and fittings that only carry water are green with verdigris.
  • Every pipe goes from somewhere to somewhere: out of a boiler, along a wall, into a tank. No pipe just stops.
  • The company's pug is cast into the iron: on valve wheels, on gate frames, on the boiler door.
  • Timber only as left-overs from the mine below: barrels, a few planks. No timber sets.

Colour and light

Cold teal. Lanterns here are few and greenish, and most of the light comes up off the water as a pale moving shimmer on the undersides of arches. The far background is blue-black. Hot things are the only warm colour in the biome: the boiler's door, the hot pipe joints.

How you come in, and how you leave

Coming in: From the mine, at the lip of the great waterfall. The last timber set of the mine stands against the first brick of the works: the fall you have been climbing beside turns out to be the works' river going over a brick weir. Amber lantern light gives way to cold teal within one screen.

Leaving: At the dam's crest. The top courses of the dam are split by crystals growing down through the brick from above, the water behind the dam is clear, not black, and violet light comes down the last few metres. The grotto above is the first place on the climb that nobody built.

Running through it

  • The well: at the Charco you look up the Pozo and see one dot of light. You will cross that shaft three times.
  • The pug: cast into the iron.
  • The river is the one chance: everything that falls far ends up in this water.
  • The town follows you up: one string of bunting, at the boiler house.
  • The frog: in a dry valve chamber behind a round iron door left ajar.

Rules that hold on the whole mountain

  1. What you can hold is dry, pale and matte. What you can't is dark, glossy and wet. This is the mountain's first law and no design breaks it.
  2. Scenery must not look like either. Background pieces are greyer, darker and cooler than the things you climb, and never in a hold's own colour at full strength.
  3. Everything you touch is the near end of something real that carries on behind it, like a ladder and its rungs.
  4. Nothing thin hangs in the background near the climb: it reads as a rope (notes 4 and 18). A hanging thing is a real rope, or thick, or far back.
  5. The foreground is sparse: thin posts and low lips, in front of solid rock or below the lowest floor, never over the open space you climb through (note 40).
  6. Every moving thing shows its machine: what drives it stands in the background (notes 19, 36, 57).
  7. Every danger shows where it comes from and warns before it acts (notes 12, 21, 23, 56).
  8. Every breakable thing wears the game's one breakable look: cracked already, with a corner gone (notes 16, 24, 31, 53, 64).
  9. The painted back wall never stops inside the frame, so there are no black holes (note 51). The insides of small pits can't be held (note 52).
  10. One frog is hidden in every biome (note 60), in a little room you can walk into. A room you can enter is cut away at the front: floor, back wall and roof, no front wall.
