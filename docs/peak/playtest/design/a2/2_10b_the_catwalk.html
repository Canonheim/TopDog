Rest 2.10b: The catwalk over the upper reach

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Not a challenge: the walk between La Cascada Chica and the dam. The upper reach is a straight brick channel, and the keepers' catwalk runs along its back wall on iron brackets, 2 m over the water, from the weir to the dam's apron. It was a floor of iron grating in 2 m lengths with a hand rail. Most of the lengths have rusted through. Four are still sound: one at each end and two between. The river under it runs back to the weir.

What must not change

  • The catwalk as note 24 made it: four sound plates, eleven rusted lengths that give way, and the three 1.3 m gaps.
  • The river under it, running east to the top of La Cascada Chica.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The channel's back wall. Brick, with round culvert mouths at the waterline every 6 or 7 m and black water going back under them. (2.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2The catwalk's brackets. A cast bracket out of the back wall under every length: two under each sound plate, one, bent, under each rusted one. At each gap a bracket stands empty. (1.2 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3The back hand rail. On the wall side only, 1.1 m up, whole over the sound plates and bent or missing over the rest. There is no rail on the camera's side. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 4The dam's main. A fat green pipe along the wall under the roof, from the dam to the boiler house. Scenery, and out of reach. (1.8 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 5Two works lanterns. On wall brackets over the two rest plates, so the sound places are the lit places. (1.9 to 2.2 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The gaps' edges. Note 52: the three gaps are on the list of small pits whose insides should not be holdable. Drawn here as clean cut plate edges, 0.3 m deep. If Parker picks them from that list they take the slick look for their whole depth.
  • 7Sound plates. The four sound plates keep whole rust-orange grating with a riveted edge. The rusted lengths keep the breakable look they have today.
