Las Aguas Negras · Area and background design

Challenge 2.9: Tubería

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The boiler house. The boiler stands at the bottom, behind the stoker's grating, with its fire still in. Its steam pipe climbs the hall in three flights to the pump engine at the top, which lifted water back up to the works. The hall's floor is the tail pool, so everything stands on three brick piers, each a storey taller than the last, like a giant's stair. The steam pipe itself runs up the piers behind you, wrapped in lagging. At each joint a short bare stub comes forward off it and ends in a bolted flange, and those flanges are hot. Along the top of each pier runs a cold feed pipe, out in front where you can reach it: those are the three level runs where you can rest.

What must not change

  • The eleven hot joints and the two cool ones, each exactly where it is, and how long a hot joint holds you (note 23).
  • The three level runs, hand over hand.
  • The tail pool under the whole climb, and the grating you start from.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The boiler house's back wall. Brick, 27 m tall, sooty above the boiler and wet at the west end, where the pool above spills down it. (5.2 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 2The three piers. Brick, standing in the tail pool, with a pale stone coping on each and a round arch through each at the waterline. Their tops are at 124, 129.5 and 135 m. The level runs are bracketed along their tops and the steam flights climb past their corners. (1.3 to 5.2 m behind you)
  • 3The boiler. End on, 3 m across, on a brick setting behind the grating. Its fire door glows orange with the pug cast on it: the warmest thing in the biome. The first cool joint is its stop valve, and the first hot joints are on the pipe leaving its top. A flue goes up the east wall. (1.4 to 4.4 m behind you)
  • 4The steam pipe, lagged. The steam pipe stands half a metre and more behind the play space, on the piers' faces, wrapped in lagging with wire bands. It is scenery because of where it stands. At each joint a bare stub comes straight forward off it to the flange you hold, hidden from the side behind its own flange. Today the pipe between the joints is drawn inside the play space and can't be held. By Parker's rule of 11-13 that is wrong, so it moves back. (1.7 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 5The pump engine. On the top pier, where the steam pipe ends: a bed, a cylinder and a flywheel 3.2 m across, stopped. It is where the pipe was going. (2.0 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 6The feed tank. A riveted iron tank on the middle pier, 2.5 m across, with the cold feed pipe going into its foot. (2.4 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 7The works' bunting. One string of the town's paper flags, rotted, high under the roof and flat against the back wall. It is the only bunting in the biome. It is far from anything you hold. (5.0 to 5.2 m behind you)
  • 8Three lanterns and a warning plate. A lantern on the face of each pier, under its level run. Beside the first hot joint, a cast plate: a hand and a flame (note 23). (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9Straps on the level runs. Each level run is held by two iron straps on arms that go straight back to its pier. From the side an arm is hidden behind the run, so the run looks fixed and nothing new shows that you could take for a hold. A strap is part of its run: you hold it like the rest.
  • 10The two cool joints. Valve wheels with the pug cast in the hub, on the first joint and the last. A wheel is cool and a bare flange is hot: the shape tells you, and the glow. The eleven hot joints keep the glow, shimmer and flare the game already draws.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11A riser at the frame's east edge. One riser pipe in front of the solid rock above the flume. It stands in front of the play space, so it is scenery, and it never crosses the hall or the pool. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)

Small effects, no models
