Las Aguas Negras · Area and background design

Challenge 2.11: La Presa

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The dam: a wall of brick 15 m tall with the works' lake behind it. Its downstream side comes down in great steps, and those steps are its spillway: when the lake is high, water runs down them to the upper reach. The keeper did not climb the spillway. He walked into the dam. A low brick gallery goes in through the dam's foot to a lift shaft, and the shaft goes straight up inside the dam to its crest. In the shaft is one small iron cage with a hand winch on board. You stand in it and wind, and the winch's pinion climbs a toothed rack on the shaft's back wall. Stop winding and the cage sinks. The dam is cut open at the front, so you see the gallery, the shaft and the cage in section.

What must not change

  • Note 25: one lift in place of the five platforms and five cranks. It travels the whole height they cover today, from the apron's level at 152.2 m to the crest's at 167 m, and it rises only while you wind.
  • Parker, 11-13: the lift goes straight up in a shaft, and it always sinks when you stop winding. There is no catch.
  • The apron you start from, the crest you step off on to, the lake behind it and the rungs out through the roof, all where they are today.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The dam hall's back wall. Brick, far back, blue-black. The hall is wide: the dam runs a long way back into the scene. (6.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 2The spillway, carried on behind. The same steps run back into the scene for the dam's whole width. A round spill arch stands at their head, and white water comes down them behind you: a thin fall on each riser and a sheet on each tread. On the lowest riser the fall comes down beside the gallery's mouth, not across it. (1.4 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 3The inspection gallery. Cut through brick that is solid today: a round barrel vault 7.8 m long and 2.6 m tall, from the apron to the foot of the shaft. Its back wall is brick streaked white where the dam seeps. Floor, back wall and roof, no front wall. Its mouth on the apron is a round arch with the pug cast on its keystone. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The lift shaft. Brick, 2.6 m wide, straight up from the gallery to the hall's roof: 18 m. The lower 12 m of it is cut in brick that is solid today. Its back wall is dry and pale near the top and black and wet at the foot. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5Guide rails and the rack. On the shaft's back wall, just behind the play space: two iron guide rails the cage runs on, and between them a toothed rack that the winch's pinion climbs. They are scenery because of where they stand: flat on the back wall, behind the cage, where no hand reaches. The shaft's two side walls are bare. (1.2 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 6The shaft's head and foot. At the top, a buffer beam across the shaft under the roof. At the foot, a spring buffer in a shallow pit. They say where the cage's travel ends. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7Three works lanterns. On wall brackets: one over the apron, one in the gallery, one over the crest. The one in the gallery is what you see first: a pool of teal light inside the dam. They are the last of the works' lanterns but one. (2.1 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The gallery's floor. New floor: the apron's stone coping runs on into the dam, level, 7.8 m to the shaft's foot. The gallery's roof is 2.6 m up, black and wet.
  • 9The cage. Note 25: a level deck 2 m wide with an open iron cage 2.2 m tall, no front and no sides at deck height, so you walk in from the gallery and out on to the crest. The winch stands on the cage's back frame with its crank facing you, 1.5 m above the deck. Its shaft goes straight back to the pinion on the rack. Drawn at the shaft's foot, and dashed at the crest where you step off. The crank, the deck and the cage's top bar can be held, as they are in the play space.
  • 10The shaft's east wall, carried up. New wall: today the shaft's east side would be open above the third step. A brick wall 0.6 m thick closes it from that step to the roof, black and wet. The cage stays in a shaft the whole way up.
  • 11The spillway steps. The steps that are in the play space today stay where they are, without their platforms and cranks: black wet stone with a film of water, each riser 2.9 m. They are the spillway's near edge. Nobody climbs them.
  • 12The cast pug. On the dam's cut face, between the lake and the shaft, where it is today: the First Top Dog, 3 m across, cast in iron and let into the brick. The brick round it gets drawn courses.

Small effects, no models

  • A tick from the pinion for every tooth you gain, and a slow whirr from the winch's governor while the cage sinks.
  • A boom from the buffer beam when the cage reaches the top, and a soft thump from the spring buffer if you let it sink to the foot.
  • Water on the spillway behind, and its sound through the gallery's wall.
