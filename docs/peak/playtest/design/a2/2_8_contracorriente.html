Las Aguas Negras · Area and background design

Challenge 2.8: Contracorriente

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The flume: the brick tunnel that carries the river from the boiler house's tail pool down to the pool under the well. It is 25 m long and 8 m tall, with a row of brick pillars along its back holding up the vault. Beside each pillar a side culvert opens in the back wall, and in the mouth of each one the water lies almost still. The river comes hard down the middle. You swim it the wrong way.

What must not change

  • The flume's length, its hard current east, and the three patches of still water.
  • The ledge you start from, the iron grating at the far end, and the short chain that is the only way out.
  • The three pillars stay behind the water. Nothing stands in the swim.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The flume's back wall and its side culverts. Brick, black and wet to a tide line 1 m above the water. Three round culvert mouths at the waterline, one beside each pillar. The still water you rest in lies in front of each mouth. (2.2 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 2The three pillars. Brick, 0.9 m wide, from the bed to the vault, each with a pale stone band at the waterline and a pointed stone nose facing the current. They mark where the still water is, from the far end of the flume. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3Through the culverts. Black water going back to a second row of pillars, lit by the shimmer off the river. (4.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 4A depth gauge. A painted board on the middle pillar, marked in half metres, with the water well over the company's red line. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 5What the ledge and the grating hang from. Iron beams out of the back wall under each. You see their ends. (1.2 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6Two works lanterns. On brackets on the first and third pillars, high, so the water under them is lit and nothing hangs over the swim. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The grating and the ledge. The far landing is drawn as an iron grating with water showing through it. The near ledge gets a stone coping.
  • 8The chain's shackle. The way out hangs from a shackle on the grating's edge beam, drawn at its top.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 9The flume's near wall, cut down. One broken course of brick along the bottom of the frame, a full 3 m below the water's bed. Nothing at all stands in front of the water. (1.3 to 1.8 m in front)
