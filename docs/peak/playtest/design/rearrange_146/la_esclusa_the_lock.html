Challenge 1.13: La Esclusa, the lock

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The lock that let the barrels down from the river to the flume. It is a stone chamber 16 m long and 13 m deep under the end of the black waters' river, where the river used to pour into the mine. The black waters' slot still brings it down, now onto a flap gate in the chamber's roof at its west end. The old frog keeper still works it from his booth on the quay: he hauls his lever, the flap drops, the river comes down the back wall in a white sheet, and the chamber fills 11 m in six seconds. It holds for three, the sluices in the lower gate open, and it drains in five; then straight away it fills again. You step in through the wicket as the water drains under its sill, stand on the sill, catch the rise as it comes, and swim across as it lifts you, to reach out of the old dry shaft onto the lip ledge where El Borde starts.

What must not change about the play

  • A fall from El Borde lands in this lock (it used to go on down the slot to the Sump): gentler, and kept (Parker, 11-14 15:30).
  • El Borde exactly as today: its lip ledge (x -232 to -226, y 90), the slot, the pier with its peaked top, the dry shaft, the river, its one chain.
  • The way into El Borde is still up the old dry shaft onto the lip ledge, as from the mine today.

The cycle: 14 s, as pitched

Kept at 14 s (Parker, 11-14): 6 s rising, 3 s held, 5 s draining, no pause at the bottom. It still works. The wicket is open while the water is under its sill, 2.1 s of every cycle, enough to step in. On the sill you catch the rise as it comes (at 1.2 s); it lifts you, and in the 8.1 s until the lip ledge goes out of reach you swim the 10.3 m to the dry shaft's line at 1.3 m a second. To make that work without a pause, the chamber is now 16 m (the slot at its west end, by the gate), open to y 89 all along so nothing pins you under a roof, and the river comes down the back wall instead of pushing down through the middle. The lip ledge is in reach for 3.6 s of each cycle: the end of the rise and the hold.

The four layers

1. The painted backdrop

far, 12 m and more behind: one painted scene, never a flat colour

  • 1The river's culverts, through a grating. High in the chamber's back wall, a barred opening onto the culverts the river runs through behind the lock: painted going back in rows, lit teal from far off, with the sound of the water. The one look out of the chamber. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)

2. The painted set

the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind

  • 2The chamber's back wall. Cut stone in long courses, stained teal up to the highest water and green-black below the lowest, with a pale scum line painted where the water stands at the top of each fill. Rust tracks run down from the ledges' brackets. Under the slot, the river comes down this wall as a white sheet while it fills, behind you (looks only: it does not push you). (1.9 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3The quay's back wall and the keeper's booth. Brick, with the keeper's booth set into it beside the lower gate: a little stone room with a round-headed opening, a lamp, a stool and a window onto the chamber. Cut away at the front, so you see in. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The slot's foot and the flap gate. Where the black waters' slot comes down through the chamber's roof, at its west end: an iron frame and a heavy flap gate on a hinge, shut across it. When it drops, the river comes down the back wall into the chamber. When it is shut, the river slides off it into the bypass culvert's mouth at the back of the slot, which takes it down to the flume (and the barrels with it). (1.2 to 2.0 m behind you)

3. Real things

the hero piece and the things round the climb

  • 5The frog keeper and his lever. The hero piece. A frog in an oilskin cap in the booth, one hand on a tall lever with a brass knob. One second before each fill he hauls it over: that is the warning, as every danger on the mountain warns before it acts. He sits in the house frogs' style, matte, and never leaves his booth. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 6The gate wheel. A big iron hand wheel on the quay's wall by the gate, its hub a pug's face. It spins as the flap gate drops and spins back as it shuts, worked by shafts in the wall: the machine you can see (rule 6). (1.5 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 7The gauge board. A tall painted board on the back wall from the floor to the roof, marked every metre with a pug at the top, and a float on a guide that rides the water. Watching it tells you where the water is in its cycle. (1.5 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 8Lanterns. Three works lanterns, teal and steady: one in the booth, one on the back wall by L1, one on the east wall over L2. (1.9 to 2.1 m behind you)

4. The climb as things

every ledge, hold, rope and mover a real object, its drawing the shape of its solid

  • 9The wicket and its sill. A small iron door in the foot of the lower gate, 2.5 m tall. It is open while the water is under its sill and shuts with a clang as the water reaches it: 2.1 s of every cycle (from 13.0 s, as the drain ends, to 1.2 s, as the next rise comes). Its stone sill runs into the chamber as the lowest ledge, where you stand to catch the rise. (in the play space)
  • 10The lower gate. The chamber's west end: a riveted iron gate from the floor to the roof, black with water and slick. At the top of a fill it holds the whole chamber back from the quay; the drain's sluices are at its foot, under the quay, and let the water out into the flume's head. (in the play space)
  • 11The two ledges. Stone corbels with pale copings on iron brackets out of the back wall: L1 under the slot (x -239.6 to -238.2, top 83.0), L2 on the east wall (x -229.6 to -228.0, top 81.0). Each is out of reach from the water at the bottom: you take it as the water brings you level, and it keeps you up through a drain. (in the play space)
  • 12The rungs. Three iron staples up the east wall under the lip ledge (y 84.8, 85.8, 86.8): holds to wait on near the top. The top one is 3.2 m under the lip ledge and you are under its slab there: out of reach, so only the highest water gets you out. (in the play space)
  • 13The top of the dry shaft. The old dry shaft's head, as today: the mine's last timber set against the works' first brick, now the lock's way out. From the water at its highest you reach the lip ledge's edge, 1.5 m up, and climb out. El Borde starts there. (in the play space)

Foreground (sparse)

framing only, where nobody climbs behind it

  • 14The gate's front post. One thin timber post in front of the lower gate's edge, over solid gate, so it frames the way in and covers nothing you need (the doorway exception to the foreground rule). (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The river's white sheet down the back wall under the slot while it fills, foam where it meets the water, spray.
  • Bubbles and a swirl as the sluices drain it.
  • The clang of the wicket and the flap, the wheel's rattle.
  • Drips from the roof all the time (every rest has its unease).
