Challenge 0.3: El Descenso, the longer ride

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The cart ride goes on past where it stops today. The tunnel runs on 59 m further west and drops 17 m more to meet the mine at its new place. The extra length is the old haulage incline: a straight run down at 20 degrees, the steepest of the ride and the last, with the winding house at its head that used to let the carts down on a rope. The rope is snapped and lying on the sleepers, the runaway catch is rusted open, and the cart goes over the brow and down it on its own. At the foot are the ore bins, then the end of the line as you know it, the buffer and the door into the mine.

At the other end nothing changes: the tunnel's mouth (x -36) opens east onto the station's yard with the painted desert behind it (the far hills card, 95 m back), never onto bare sky, and the ride's first stretches are as the 0.3 pages have them. The quality plan's line for 0.3 is a painted tunnel receding, and the mouth opening onto the painted desert: the side galleries here carry the first part into the new stretch, and the mouth is the station's own.

The four layers

1. The painted backdrop

far, 12 m and more behind: one painted scene, never a flat colour

  • 1Side galleries going back. Two openings in the back wall at the incline's head and foot, with the old side workings painted going back into the dark behind them: timber sets in rows getting smaller, a lantern speck at the far end of each. The painted tunnel receding, as the quality plan asks. (10.0 to 14.0 m behind you)

2. The painted set

the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind

  • 2The back wall. Plank lining on rock, as today's tunnel: grey and wet, planks missing, smashed in the last 16 m, the same back wall the mine's galleries have. (1.7 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 3The timber sets. One every 2.6 m, as in today's tunnel, grey and rotten, standing plumb on the incline so their caps step down it. One cap cracked and propped. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)

3. Real things

the hero piece and the things round the climb

  • 4The winding house. The hero piece. At the incline's head, a chamber cut into the rock over and behind the track: a big iron winding drum 2.6 m across on a timber frame, its brake band and long brake lever, a seat for the brakeman, and the haulage rope's snapped end hanging off the drum. Carts used to be let down the incline on that rope. Now they free-run, and you are in one. (2.2 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 5The haulage rope, lying. The rope that ran down the incline, snapped, lying along the sleepers between the rails all the way down. Lying, never hanging (rule 4). (in the play space)
  • 6The runaway catch. An iron catch hinged beside the far rail near the top, meant to throw a runaway cart off the track. It is sprung open and rusted that way. A warning board on the back wall above it: the mine's skull-pug diamond. (in the play space)
  • 7The ore bins. At the incline's foot, timber ore bins built against the back wall with their chutes over the track, where carts were loaded on the way up. One chute's door hangs open; a little ore has spilled on the sleepers. (1.8 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 8The end of the line. Today's end of the line, moved with it: the board of miners' pegs with one hat and a dead lamp, the trough's end, the buffer's two braces raking back to the floor. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)

4. The climb as things

every ledge, hold, rope and mover a real object, its drawing the shape of its solid

  • 9The rail bed and the rails. The bed grips (dry, pale, matte) all the way down, the incline too: fall out of the cart anywhere and you can walk on. The kit's rails and sleepers, as today. (in the play space)
  • 10The buffer. The timber baulk across the track at the end, as today (0.6 by 0.8 m), 1.7 m short of the mine's door. (in the play space)

Foreground (sparse)

framing only, where nobody climbs behind it

  • 11Two broken front posts. A front post of a set at the incline's head and one at its foot, broken off short, standing over the rock below the bed so they cover nothing you need. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)
