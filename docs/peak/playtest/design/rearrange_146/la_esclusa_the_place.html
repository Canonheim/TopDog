La Esclusa: the new section under the black waters

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The company's lower works, between the mine and the black waters. When the mine was worked, barrels came down the black waters' river, down a lock 12 m deep at its end, and along a stone flume 32 m long to a crane at the head of the great falls, which swung them down into the mine. The crane broke, the mine was abandoned, and nobody turned the water off. The great falls pour down through the west end. The barrels still drift out of the dark and float to the lip and over it, one after another. An old frog still keeps the lock, hauling his lever and filling and emptying the chamber every eighteen seconds for nobody. It is cut stone, brick vaults and riveted iron, the black waters' teal light and their dark water, and the roar of the falls at one end.

The route through it

  1. La Cortina, as today, up its dry shaft to the top of the mine.
  2. Off the top row west onto the pier's coping (1.9 m).
  3. Up the crane's chain beside the lip, after a barrel has gone over, onto the crane's platform (Los Barriles starts).
  4. East along the flume, barrel to barrel, against the current and the barrels coming at you, onto the east landing.
  5. East along the quay past the keeper's booth (a rest, 13.5 m).
  6. In through the lock's wicket at the bottom of its cycle (La Esclusa starts).
  7. Cross while it is low, float up as it fills, take a ledge or a rung, and at the top of a fill reach out of the dry shaft onto the lip ledge.
  8. El Borde (2.1), exactly as today.

Where the water comes from and goes

The black waters' lower river still runs east along its tunnel to the slot at x -244 and pours down it, as today. It no longer falls into the mine: it falls onto the lock's flap gate. When the flap drops, the river fills the lock. When it is shut, the river slides off it into a bypass culvert behind the quay, which brings it down to the flume's head, and it comes out of the barrels' tunnel with the barrels. The lock drains through sluices in its lower gate into the flume's head too. The flume runs west and goes over its lip into the great falls, which come down from the well, through the Charco (2.7) and a shaft through the black waters' floor, past the lip, into the mine's throat and on to the Sump. All of the new section's water ends in the great falls.

What did not fit, and what changed

  • The mine drops 17 m as well as moving 59 m west (as Parker's drawing shows). Moved only west, its throat's roof would sit in the black waters' rock (110 square metres of overlap) and there would be no room for a flume and a 12 m lock between its top and El Borde. With the drop there are 17 m, which fit the lock exactly.
  • No passage east from the mine's top: it comes out under the flume and cannot reach the flume's west end. The mine lets out beside the lip instead and you climb the crane's chain (see Los Barriles).
  • The lock is under the black waters' slot and the old dry shaft, because those are where El Borde's water and its lip ledge are. That puts it 27 m east of the flume's head, so a short quay joins them.
  • The lock's cycle gets a 4 s pause at the bottom (18 s in all), for the wicket and the crossing.
  • The strip of rock from x -252 to -223 between y 86 and 89.4, which was the mine's roof, is now the new section's (the lock's roof and the support under the lip ledge).

What must not change about the play

  • La Cortina and every challenge of the mine exactly as today (moved); El Borde and every challenge of the black waters exactly as today.
  • The barrels' speed, spacing, count and hidden birth exactly as today; only their direction changes (note 146).
  • The great falls in one line, the well's middle, x -300, from under the forge to the Sump (notes 142, 143, 146).
