La Esclusa: the new section under the black waters

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The company's lower works, between the mine and the black waters. When the mine was worked, barrels came down the black waters' river, down a lock 12 m deep at its end, and along a stone flume to a crane at the head of the great falls, which swung them down into the mine. The crane broke, the mine was abandoned, and nobody turned the water off. The great falls pour down past the west end. The barrels still drift out of the dark and float to the end of the flume, where the barrel men's old tipping ramp flings them into the waste chute, one after another. An old frog still keeps the lock, hauling his lever and filling and emptying the chamber every eighteen seconds for nobody. It is cut stone, brick vaults and riveted iron, the black waters' teal light and their dark water, and the roar of the falls at one end.

The route through it

  1. La Cortina, as today, up its dry shaft to the top of the mine.
  2. Off the top row west onto the pier's coping (1.9 m): 1.12 Los Barriles starts.
  3. Up the keepers' ladder, 6.4 m, straight up beside the falls in their spray, onto the crane's platform.
  4. Down onto a barrel and east along the flume, barrel to barrel, against the current and the barrels coming at you, onto the east landing. Fall in, and the whirlpool takes you down its pipe to the alcove at La Cortina's top, to try again.
  5. East along the quay past the keeper's booth (a rest, 13.5 m).
  6. In through the lock's wicket while the water is under its sill: 1.13 La Esclusa starts.
  7. Catch the rise on the sill, swim across as it lifts you, and at the top of the fill reach out of the dry shaft onto the lip ledge; or rest on a ledge or a rung for the next fill.
  8. El Borde (2.1), exactly as today.

Where the water comes from and goes

The black waters' lower river still runs east along its tunnel to the slot at x -244 and pours down it, as today. It no longer falls into the mine: it falls onto the lock's flap gate. When the flap drops, the river fills the lock. When it is shut, the river slides off it into a bypass culvert behind the quay, which brings it down to the flume's head, and it comes out of the barrels' tunnel with the barrels. The lock drains through sluices in its lower gate into the flume's head too. The flume runs west; at its end its water goes down a grating under the tipping ramp and by a culvert behind the climb into the great falls (the barrels are flung into the waste chute and never reach them). The great falls come down from the well, through the Charco (2.7) and a 6 m shaft through the black waters' floor, past the ladder and the platform, into the mine's throat and on to the Sump.

What did not fit, and what changed

  • The mine drops 17 m as well as moving 59 m west (agreed by Parker). Moved only west, its throat's roof would sit in the black waters' rock (86 square metres of overlap) and there would be no room for a flume and a 12 m lock between its top and El Borde. With the drop there are 17 m, which fit the lock exactly.
  • The barrels are flung back into the waste chute at the flume's west end, never into the falls, so the way up from the mine is a plain ladder straight up beside the falls (Parker's answer). The first pages had a passage east that could not work and then a chain timed between barrels; both are gone.
  • The lock is under the black waters' slot and the old dry shaft, because those are where El Borde's water and its lip ledge are. The quay between the flume's head and the lock is 13.5 m.
  • The lock keeps the pitched 14 s (Parker). To work with no pause at the bottom its chamber is 16 m, the slot at its west end, open to y 89 all along, and the river comes down its back wall instead of pushing down through the middle.
  • A fall into the flume: a whirlpool by its west end takes you down a pipe to the alcove at the top of La Cortina, to try again (Parker).
  • The strip of rock from x -252 to -223 between y 86 and 89.4, which was the mine's roof, is now the new section's (the lock's roof and the support under the lip ledge).

What must not change about the play

  • La Cortina and every challenge of the mine exactly as today (moved); El Borde and every challenge of the black waters exactly as today.
  • The barrels' speed, spacing, count and hidden birth exactly as today; they run right to left (note 146).
  • The great falls one 6 m shaft on the well's middle line, x -300, all the way down to the Sump (notes 142, 143, 146; Parker, 11-14 15:30).

The four layers

1. The painted backdrop

far, 12 m and more behind: one painted scene, never a flat colour

  • 1The view down the great falls. Behind the falls' shaft: one tall painted card of the whole drop, from the black waters' floor down past the mine to the Sump's mist, the mine's lanterns warm and tiny far below. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)
  • 2The vaults going back. Through the flume's arcade: rows of brick vaults going back into the dark with the water running away under them. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)
  • 3The river's culverts. Through a barred opening high in the lock's back wall: the culverts behind the lock, painted going back, lit teal far off. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)

2. The painted set

the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind

  • 4The flume's arcade and its cracked vault. Brick piers every 6.5 m with round arches, mooring rings at the waterline, a cracked brick vault overhead. (2.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The barrels' tunnel and the waste chute. A brick arch at the waterline at the flume's head, a sluice gate half raised, black inside: where the barrels come from. Over the flume's west end, the waste chute's black mouth: where they go. (1.3 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 6The quay's wall and the keeper's booth. Brick, with the lock-keeper's booth set into it beside the lower gate. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7The lock chamber's wall. Cut stone stained teal to the highest water, the scum line painted at the top. (1.9 to 2.2 m behind you)

3. Real things

  • 8The broken crane. Los Barriles' hero: the barrel crane on the platform at the head of the falls, its jib snapped over the drop. (2.4 to 3.4 m behind you)

  • 10The gate wheel and the gauge board. The machine you can see: the wheel spins as the flap drops; the board's float rides the water. (1.5 to 1.9 m behind you)
