Challenge 1.12: Los Barriles

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The company's barrel flume. A stone channel 32 m long under the black waters' east end, built to carry barrels west from the lock to a crane at the head of the great falls, which swung them down to the mine. The crane broke and was never mended. The barrels still come: they drift out of the dark tunnel at the flume's head, float west along it, and at its end run up the barrel men's old tipping ramp and are flung back into the waste chute, booming away into the dark. You come up out of the mine beside the falls, climb the keepers' ladder in their spray to the crane's platform, and cross the flume east, barrel to barrel, against them.

What changed after Parker's answers (11-14 15:30)

  • The barrels no longer go over a lip into the great falls. At the flume's west end a kicker throws each one back into the background, into the waste chute, like the glacier's slabs (note 91).
  • So the flume's west end no longer has to reach the falls, and the way up is a plain climb straight up beside the falls: the keepers' ladder, 6.4 m, in a 2 m strip between the falls and the platform. No barrel passes it: the crane's chain and its barrel timing are gone.
  • The flume's water now runs from x -290.5 to -258.5; its barrels' road is as today (26.8 m), from the tunnel at x -261.8 to the kicker at x -288.6. The quay beyond it is 7.5 m (it was 13.5).
  • A fall into the flume no longer goes down the falls: the current holds you against the end wall and you climb out onto the platform. A fall off the ladder goes into the falls and rides them to the Sump (note 147).

What must not change about the play

  • The barrels exactly as today: eight, 0.55 m a second, the written gaps 3.0, 3.4, 2.9, 5.8, 3.1, 3.5, 2.9 and 6.2 m round a 30.8 m loop, 26.8 m of it on the water, 7.3 s unseen each loop. They run right to left, east to west, against you (note 146).
  • Two of the eight gaps are swum, as today.
  • Their birth hidden deep in a dark tunnel (note 114); and their end seen: they are flung into the waste chute, they never vanish on screen (note 21).
  • The current: 1.0 m a second, as today's pool, now running west.
  • A landing at each end, 2.2 m over the water, as today's two ledges.

The barrels' spacing, as written today

The water between each barrel and the next, round the loop. Yellow: a plain stretch (about 1.3 m of water is a stretch: the work log's measurement). Orange: a lunge. Red: a swim against the current. A barrel takes 49 s to float the flume, so from the platform you can watch several coming and pick your moment.

The four layers

1. The painted backdrop

far, 12 m and more behind: one painted scene, never a flat colour

  • 1The view down the great falls. A painted card 12 m behind the falls, the height of the whole drop: the falls' shaft going down past the mine's lit curtain into mist and dark, the mine's lanterns as warm dots far below, and at the top the falls coming out of the black waters' floor. You climb the ladder with it at your shoulder. The same card carries on down behind the mine's throat. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)
  • 2The vaults going back. Seen through the arcade's arches: rows of brick vaults going back into the dark, the flume's water running away under them as a channel that catches the lanterns, each row darker and smaller, a lantern speck at the far end. A flat painted card, the house style. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)

2. The painted set

the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind

  • 3The flume's arcade. The flume's back wall is a brick arcade: piers 1 m wide every 6.5 m, round arches between them, cut stone in long courses below the springing, iron mooring rings at the waterline, and the brick vault of the roof overhead, cracked along its crown with a dark seep line. (2.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The barrels' tunnel. A brick arch at the waterline in the back wall at the flume's head, 5.5 m wide, a sluice gate jammed half raised in its mouth, black inside. Behind it a tunnel runs back and up to the bypass that brings the river down past the lock. Each barrel is born 7 m back in it, where no light reaches, and drifts forward out of the dark into your plane over its first 2.4 m. You never see one appear (note 114). (1.3 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 5The waste chute. Over the flume's west end, a brick-lined mouth in the back wall, black inside, where the barrel men tipped broken barrels away down a chute into the dark. Every barrel that reaches the end of the flume now goes up the tipping ramp and is flung back into it, tumbling out of sight with a hollow boom. None reaches the great falls (Parker, 11-14). (1.3 to 8.0 m behind you)

3. Real things

the hero piece and the things round the climb

  • 6The broken crane. The hero piece. The company's barrel crane, still on the platform at the head of the falls: a riveted iron post, its jib snapped and hanging out over the falls at a slant with the pulley still on it, its hook long gone. Behind the climb, so it covers nothing, and lit by the lantern on its post. It says what this place was for: barrels came along the flume and were swung down to the mine. Now they go into the chute instead. (2.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 7Lanterns. Works lanterns on brackets, teal and steady (note 17): one on the crane's post so the ladder is lit, one on each of two piers, one over the east landing. (2.1 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 8The barrel men's things. On the quay's back wall by the east landing: a rack of boat hooks for fishing barrels out, a chalk tally board of barrels counted (the last column never finished), a coil of rope on a peg. Small, dry and pale: the place people stood. (1.6 to 2.4 m behind you)

4. The climb as things

every ledge, hold, rope and mover a real object, its drawing the shape of its solid

  • 9The keepers' ladder. An iron ladder standing on the pier's top and bolted up the platform's west face, 1.6 m right of the falls, in their spray: 6.4 m from the pier to the platform, rungs every 0.75 m, two of them rusted away (1.5 m reaches). A real ladder: rails drawn, only the rungs held. It is how the keepers came down to the mine, and it is the way up: no barrels pass it (Parker, 11-14). (in the play space)
  • 10The curtain's top and the pier's coping. La Cortina lets out at the top of its dry shaft, as today, into a low room under the platform (x -295 to -284.5, 2.2 m tall, open to the falls on its west side). Off the top row, 1.9 m west onto the top of the pier between the dry shaft and the falls' slot, which gets a pale stone coping (it is the mine's slick roof today). The ladder's foot is there; Los Barriles starts there. The room's east end, the old lip's grip, is a rest. (in the play space)
  • 11The platform. The flume's west end: a cut stone platform 4.5 m long on the end wall, its pale coping at y 79.4, where the crane stands. You step off its east edge down onto a barrel as one comes up to the kicker, 1.35 m down. Two iron rungs on its east face, out of the water, for anyone the current brings back to the end. (in the play space)
  • 12The kicker. The barrel men's tipping ramp: a worn stone ramp rising out of the water against the end wall, iron-shod, with white water on it. A barrel drifting onto it is let loose and thrown up 3.6 m and back into the waste chute (first guess), as the glacier's ice slabs are kicked into their cave (note 91). It throws barrels only; a climber the current brings to it is held against the end wall by the water and climbs out by the rungs. (in the play space)
  • 13The barrels. As today: eight, two iron hoops each and the company's pug branded on their ends, drawn on. Same speed, same spacing, now running west, right to left, against you. (in the play space)
  • 14The east landing. A cut stone slab on beams over the flume's head, 1.4 m east of where the barrels come into your plane, as today's west ledge was. It runs on east as the quay. (in the play space)

Foreground (sparse)

framing only, where nobody climbs behind it

  • 15A low lip of wet rock. Along the very bottom of the frame, below the lowest floor: a dark ragged lip of the rock the flume is cut in, with a few wet ferns. Nothing in front of the ladder, the open water or the falls. (1.6 to 2.6 m in front)
