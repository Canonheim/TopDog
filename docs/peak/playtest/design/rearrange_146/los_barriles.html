Challenge 1.12 (working number): Los Barriles

Design only. Nothing here is built. Numbers marked first guess are to be tuned by playing it.

The place, in one paragraph

The company's barrel flume. A stone channel 32 m long under the black waters' east end, built to carry barrels west from the lock to a crane at the head of the great falls, which swung them down to the mine. The crane broke and was never mended. The barrels still come: they drift out of the dark tunnel at the flume's east end, float west along it, and at the lip go over into the great falls one after another. You come up out of the mine beside that lip, climb the crane's old chain to its platform, and cross the flume east, barrel to barrel, against them. The water runs west into the falls; fall in and it takes you over the lip and down the falls to the Sump.

What did not fit, and what changed

The pitch had the mine let out along a short passage east into the new section. That cannot reach the flume's west end. La Cortina's dry shaft is 2 m east of the falls' slot (as in the mine today), so it comes up under the flume's last metres, below its water. A passage east from there runs under the flume, and every way up from below to the flume's west end crosses the flume's water or the line the barrels fall down. So the mine lets out where it always did, at the top of its dry shaft, into a low room under the flume's lip, and the way on is up the crane's chain beside the lip, between barrels. The chain is also the great falls' one last chance, as law 7 wants. If a climb timed against the barrels is wrong for the start, the other way is for the barrels to sink back into the dark at the lip instead of tipping over, which goes against note 21 and note 146: see the questions.

What must not change about the play

  • The barrels exactly as today: eight, 0.55 m a second, the written gaps 3.0, 3.4, 2.9, 5.8, 3.1, 3.5, 2.9 and 6.2 m round a 30.8 m loop, 26.8 m of it on the water, 7.3 s unseen each loop. Only their direction changes: east to west (note 146).
  • Two of the eight gaps are swum, as today.
  • Their birth hidden deep in a dark tunnel (note 114), and their end: they go over the lip and fall. They never vanish on screen (note 21).
  • The current: 1.0 m a second, as today's pool, now running west.
  • A hanging landing at each end, 2.2 m over the water, as today's two ledges; the barrels pass under the west one as they passed under the old east one.
  • Fall in and you go over the lip, down the great falls, to the Sump.

The barrels' spacing, as written today

The water between each barrel and the next, round the loop. Yellow: a plain stretch (about 1.3 m of water is a stretch: the work log's measurement). Orange: a lunge. Red: a swim against the current. A barrel takes 49 s to float the flume, so from the pier you can watch several coming and pick your moment.

The four layers

1. The painted backdrop

far, 12 m and more behind: one painted scene, never a flat colour

  • 1The view down the great falls. A painted card 12 m behind the falls, the height of the whole drop: the falls' column going down past the mine's lit curtain into mist and dark, the mine's lanterns as warm dots far below on its east side, and at the top the falls coming out of the black waters' floor. The one place where you see the whole of the great falls at once. The same card carries on down behind the mine's throat. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)
  • 2The vaults going back. Seen through the arcade's arches: rows of brick vaults going back into the dark, the flume's water running away under them as a channel that catches the lanterns, each row darker and smaller, and a lantern speck at the far end. A flat painted card, the house style. (12.0 to 16.0 m behind you)

2. The painted set

the near back wall and its depth, 2 to 10 m behind

  • 3The flume's arcade. The flume's back wall is a brick arcade: piers 1 m wide every 6.5 m, round arches between them, cut stone in long courses below the springing, iron mooring rings let into the piers at the waterline, and the brick vault of the roof overhead, cracked along its crown with a dark seep line. So the wall is a place with depth, not a slab. (2.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The barrels' tunnel. A brick arch at the waterline in the back wall, 5.5 m wide, a sluice gate jammed half raised in its mouth, black inside. Behind it a tunnel runs back and up into the rock to the bypass that brings the river down past the lock. Each barrel is born 7 m back in it, where no light reaches, and drifts forward out of the dark into your plane over its first 2.4 m. You never see one appear (note 114). (1.3 to 7.0 m behind you)

3. Real things

the hero piece and the things round the climb

  • 5The broken crane. The hero piece. The company's barrel crane, still standing on its platform at the head of the falls: a riveted iron post, its jib snapped and hanging out over the falls at a slant with the pulley still on it, its hook long gone down the falls. Behind the climb, so it covers nothing, and lit by the lantern on its post. It says what this place was for: barrels came along the flume and were swung down to the mine. (2.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 6Lanterns. Works lanterns on brackets: one on the crane's post so the lip and its chain are lit, one on each of two piers, one over the east landing. Teal and steady (note 17: nothing in this biome's light pulses). (2.1 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 7The barrel men's things. On the east landing's back wall: a rack of boat hooks for fishing barrels out, a chalk tally board of barrels counted (the last column never finished), and a coil of rope on a peg. Small, dry and pale, so the landing reads as the place people stood. (1.6 to 2.4 m behind you)

4. The climb as things

every ledge, hold, rope and mover a real object, its drawing the shape of its solid

  • 8The curtain's top and the pier's coping. La Cortina lets out at the top of its dry shaft, as today. Off the top row there are now two ways: east onto the old lip's grip (a rest under the flume, x -291 to -284.5), or 1.9 m west onto the top of the pier between the dry shaft and the falls' slot, which gets a pale stone coping (it is the mine's slick roof today). Los Barriles starts on the pier. (in the play space)
  • 9The lip chain. The crane's hoist chain, still shackled to an iron bracket under the platform's west edge: 4.4 m of real chain, its tail 1.3 m over the pier's top and 0.6 m out over the falls. It is the way up from the mine, and it is the great falls' one last chance for anyone the water takes over the lip (law 7). Barrels go past it: climb it after one has gone over. (in the play space)
  • 10The west landing: the crane's platform. A cut stone slab on two iron beams out of the arcade's pier, 5.6 m long, top 2.2 m over the water, its west end 0.6 m out over the lip. The pale coping is the part you stand on, as everywhere in the works. The barrels pass under it with 0.65 m to spare. (in the play space)
  • 11The lip and the race. A worn stone sill at the flume's west end with white water breaking on it. Over its last 2 m the flume quickens (2.4 m a second, first guess) and is painted as white water, so a barrel leaves the lip moving and arcs out into the falls instead of sliding down the face, and anyone in the water can see where it is taking them. (in the play space)
  • 12The barrels. As today: eight, each with two iron hoops and the company's pug branded on its ends, drawn on. Same speed, same spacing, now running west. At the lip each tips over and goes down the great falls, tumbling past La Cortina into the Sump, where it sinks. (in the play space)
  • 13The east landing. The same slab on beams over the flume's head, 6.5 m long, 1.4 m east of where the barrels come into your plane, as today's west ledge was. It runs on east as the quay. (in the play space)

Foreground (sparse)

framing only, where nobody climbs behind it

  • 14A low lip of wet rock. Along the very bottom of the frame, below the lowest floor: a dark ragged lip of the rock the flume is cut in, with a few wet ferns. Nothing in front of the open water or the falls. (1.6 to 2.6 m in front)
