El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.9: Bloques Fantasma

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The stair to the altar. It was built as a ramp of polished black stone, 10 m long, that nobody could walk up. Five blocks slide out of the ramp's face to make steps, and slide back in, to the beat of a drum. The drum stands on a shelf over the serpent's head, and a stone mallet on an axle beats it: four counts to the bar. Each block is out for three counts and gone for one, and the gone count walks up the stair. Nothing on the walls tells you which block goes when. You learn it by watching the stair and listening to the drum. At the top is the altar's threshold.

What must not change

  • The five blocks: where each comes out, its size, and the count it is gone on. Four counts of two beats, 96 beats a minute.
  • The ramp under them can't be held. A block that goes while you are on it leaves you on the ramp.
  • The serpent's head ledge at the foot and the threshold at the top.
  • Parker, 11-13: no carving gives the count away.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The stair hall's back wall. Wall bays behind the ledge, the stair and the threshold, and below them the gallery's back wall. Unbroken. The temple's carved band of pug faces, belts and bones runs along it high over the stair, and tells you nothing about the blocks. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The drum and its mallet. The temple drum, 1.6 m across, on a stone shelf on the back wall above the serpent's head. A stone arm with a bone mallet swings on an axle between two cheeks and strikes it on every count, hardest on the first. You see the mallet lift before each stroke. Today the drum hangs in the air in front of the serpent's head. It moves up onto the shelf. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 3The serpent's head. On the ledge where you start, against the back wall, under the drum (see La Serpiente). (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The altar's apse and two columns. At the head of the stair: two square columns, and between them the stepped niche with the altar in it (see El Altar). It is what you are climbing toward, and it is lit. (1.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5Torches. One by the drum, one over the middle of the stair, one at the threshold. (2.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6Five sockets in the ramp's face. Each block lives in a socket cut into the ramp: a carved pale frame round a dark mouth, drawn on the ramp's face the same way as the piston sockets. When a block is out you see 1.9 m of empty socket behind it. When it is in, its end sits just inside the frame and can't be held.
  • 7Where each block stands when it is out. The course draws the blocks drawn in. The dashed boxes are the steps as you climb them. Each block has a jade band along its top edge and a bronze shoe on its outer end, and it shakes before it goes.
  • 8The ramp's polish. Polished black, with pale scrapes down it. The big face of stone under the stair is the same black, in fitted blocks with tight joints.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The drum skin shakes and throws a little dust on each stroke.

  • Each socket breathes out a puff of dust as its block goes in.
