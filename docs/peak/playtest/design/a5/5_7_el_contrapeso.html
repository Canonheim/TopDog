El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.7: El Contrapeso

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

From the keeper's balcony a chain takes you up to the gallery: a walk 40 m long on the roof of the great hall, between a row of square columns, with two slots in the floor that look down on the champions. The gallery ends at the hall's open west end, and there the temple weighs you. On the back wall is a stone scale: one beam on an axle, and a stone pan hanging from each end. Step on the near pan and it sinks 3 m while the far pan rises 3 m. The way on is from the low pan to the high one, and then to a ledge on the west wall, before the scale settles back.

What must not change

  • The two slabs: where each is, how far and how fast the first sinks, and that the second does the opposite.
  • The fling from the sunk slab to the risen one, and the ledge that can only be reached while the second is still up.
  • The gallery's two slots, lined so their insides can't be held (note 52). The floor's top can be held right up to each edge.
  • The open drop to the west balcony, 23 m down.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The back wall. Wall bays behind the gallery and behind the open west end, without a break. Under the gallery's floor it is the great hall's far wall, further back. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The scale. A stone beam 6 m long on an axle between two carved cheeks, with a pug boss on the axle's end. It is drawn as it rests: east end up, west end down. When you stand on the east pan the beam tips the other way. It is thick stone, set against the wall, and it is browner than the pans. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3Two hanger slots. Each pan hangs from its end of the beam by a stone hanger that runs in a slot in the back wall. The slot is as long as the pan's travel, so the empty part of each slot shows how far that pan will go. Today each slab has a thin chain that goes straight up through the serpent's back. Those go. (1.2 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 4The carved panel. Under the scale: a small figure standing on the low pan and another leaping for the high one. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 5The serpent's bed. Overhead: the long carved beam the serpent's back rises out of (see La Serpiente). Its east end stands on the gallery's first column. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6The gallery's columns. Square carved columns with pug capitals along the back of the gallery. Each has a base to match it in front of the floor (item 13), so you walk between a column and a column's base. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 7Three doorways and a plaque. Stepped arch doorways between the columns, dark inside, and one stone pug plaque. The temple goes on behind the gallery. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 8Torches. One on each column, and one on the west wall over the ledge. The pans and the ledge are the brightest things at the west end. (2.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 9The side shrine's doorway. A small stepped doorway in the west wall at the ledge. It is the frog's room: see its own page. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10A jade band and bronze shoes on each pan. Both slabs move, so both wear the jade band along the top edge and bronze at their ends. Each is carved underneath with a shallow dish, so it reads as a pan of the scale.
  • 11The slots' black lining. The inside faces of the two slots in the gallery floor are polished black from just under the lip to the bottom (note 52). The floor's top is pale and carved right up to the edge.
  • 12The gallery's last edge. The west end of the gallery floor is carved deep and pale on its end face. It is the last sure hold before the scale.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13Column bases. The base of a column, 1.5 m wide, in front of the gallery floor opposite each column behind. Their tops are level with the floor, so they cover no part of you. None stands in front of a slot. (1.3 to 1.9 m in front)
