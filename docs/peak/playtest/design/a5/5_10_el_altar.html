El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.10: El Altar

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The altar's chamber is the temple's highest room and its holiest. The altar stands in a stepped niche at the top of the stair, with room in front of it to stand. Past it the floor stops. The chamber is a pit 11 m wide, 16 m deep to the gallery, crossed by one thin bridge of six stone slabs laid on brackets, and every slab is cracked through. Over the bridge a stone cross 10 m across turns on an axle in front of a great carved sun stone. On the far side a column of stone pegs goes up the east wall to the well's sill. The top five pegs are cracked. Three climbers got that far. They hung from the sill, hand to ankle, to reach past the bad pegs, and they are still there.

What must not change

  • The beam's six pieces, each breaking 3.5 seconds after it is touched, and the cross turning at 6.4 turns a minute (note 53).
  • The cross's arms pass half a metre over the beam and brush the east wall. Its outer 2 m knocks you out.
  • The three sound pegs, the five cracked ones, and the three frozen climbers, each exactly where it is.
  • The altar stays behind you, on the threshold, with clear floor in front of it (note 54).

The three layers

Background

  • 1The chamber's back wall. Wall bays from the bottom of the pit to the roof, 3.4 m back. It carries on down the pit, so the drop is a lit wall going down and not a black hole (note 51). (3.4 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 2The sun stone. A carved disc 9.4 m across on the back wall, right behind the cross: rings of small pugs, belts and bones round a centre. The carving is shallow and brown. The cross is the near end of its axle. (2.6 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The cross's cheeks. Two carved cheeks stand out of the sun stone's centre, one either side of the hub, and the stone axle runs between them. You see them between the turning arms. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The altar in its niche. A stepped niche 4.4 m wide in the back wall, and in it the altar: 3 m wide, with the First Top Dog's face on its front and a bronze bowl of coals on top. It is 1.3 m behind you. Two square columns stand either side. (1.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5Seven brackets. A stepped stone bracket sticks out of the back wall under each joint of the bridge. The six slabs lie on them, which is why the bridge is in six pieces. When a slab falls its brackets stay, out of reach behind you, and the slab comes back onto them. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The carved band. High on the back wall, level with the sill: a line of small figures hanging one from another, hand to ankle, down a wall. The three who tried were following the carving. (3.4 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 7A banner. One, whole and bright, high in the west corner, as far from the pegs as the chamber allows. (3.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 8Torches. Two on the back wall beside the pegs, one by each column, and one far down the pit so you see how deep it is. (3.1 to 3.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9Six cracked slabs. Each piece of the bridge is pale stone cracked right through, with a corner gone: the game's one look for a thing that will break (note 53). The joints between pieces are plain to see.
  • 10Dark bronze on the cross. The outer 2 m of each arm is edged in dark bronze, the temple's mark for what hurts. That is the part that knocks you out. The inner arms are plain stone. The hub wears a jade ring and a pug boss.
  • 11Eight pegs. Each peg is a carved pug's nose on a stone stem set into the wall. The lower three are sound: pale and whole. The upper five are cracked through with a corner gone. The wall between them is polished black.
  • 12The three who tried. Three frozen climbers hang down the wall beside the cracked pegs. The first holds the sill's edge with both hands. The second holds the first one's ankles and the third holds the second's. They are bone pale and dusty, in the pose they died in, and you climb them like carved stone. Their chain stops 4.6 m short of the bridge.
  • 13The place to stand. A ring of jade paw prints 1.6 m across, inlaid in the threshold right in front of the altar. It marks where a climber stands to pay respects (note 54). The figure in the sketch is standing on it.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The altar's bowl gives a small puff of smoke when respects are paid (note 54).
  • Each slab cracks further and sheds grit for its 3.5 seconds before it drops.
  • A low rush of air each time an arm of the cross goes by.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 53 (done): the whole beam is six breaking pieces and the cross turns four times as fast. Both are drawn as built.
  • Note 53 also says the climb up the east wall has not been tried at the new speed. If pegs have to move, they stay a column of pug noses, sound below and cracked above.
  • Note 54 (not built): 'Pay Your Respects'. The altar gets a marked place to stand in front of it, and the bowl answers. The second altar is on the sill above: see El Sacrificio.
  • Note 52: nothing in the pit can be held.
