El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.2: La Máscara

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The west end of the entry hall is one carved face: the First Top Dog in his mask, 16 m wide and 21 m tall, standing on top of the stepped platform. It was made to be climbed. The face itself is a wall, set back behind you and painted in terracotta and jade. The things you hold stand out from it a full metre: the jaw, the fangs, the lip and its teeth, the nostrils, the cheek studs, the lower lids of the eyes and the brow. They are bone white with gold tips, and nothing else on the face is. The jaw is a block that rides up and down in two slots. Behind the open mouth is the gullet, which leads down to the chute.

What must not change

  • Every hold where it is today, until the mask's features are measured and the route is laid again on the carving (note 47).
  • The jaw's ride: 4.5 m up, the wait at the bottom and the top, and the 2.1 m of room it leaves when it shuts.
  • The flat face stays behind you everywhere. You are never behind any part of the mask (note 47).
  • Nothing stands in front of the mask.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The hall's end wall. The same wall bays as the rest of the hall, rising out of the frame. Beside the mask the carved band shows small figures riding a jaw and climbing from tooth to nose to eye. (6.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 2The mask's face. The carved face, kept as it looks today: terracotta, jade and cream, with the ears and the crown above the brow. It is scenery. Its flat cheeks and forehead are out of reach behind you, so they are never mistaken for holds. Its colours are a shade browner than the bone white of the holds. (1.3 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 3The gullet. The open mouth, black inside, with a stepped throat going back and down to the chute. Far back in it, the jaw's counterweight, a 1 m stone block on a thick bronze chain, goes down as the jaw comes up. (2.0 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 4The jaw's two slots. One either side of the mouth, cut into the face and lined with bronze, 6.5 m tall. The jaw's ends ride in them. You can see how high the jaw will go before it moves. (1.2 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 5The eye sockets. Two deep sockets, each with an eye of inlaid jade lit from inside. The ledge you stand on under each is the eye's lower lid. (1.3 to 3.5 m behind you)
  • 6The nostril pits. Two round black pits in the nose. The pale ring round each pit is what you hold. (1.3 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 7Torches. Two on the wall east of the mask and one west of it. They light the face from the sides, so every hold throws a shadow onto the face behind it. (5.7 to 6.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8Bone white and gold on every hold. The rule for the mask: if it is bone white with a gold tip or a gold rim, you can hold it. The three teeth and the two fangs have gold tips. The nostril rings and the cheek studs have gold rims. The lower lids and the brow have a gold line along their front edge. Each hold stands out from the face on a stem you can see, with a gap of shadow behind it.
  • 9A jade band on the jaw. The jaw moves, so it wears the jade band along its lip, between the two fangs, and bronze shoes at its two ends where it meets the slots. It shakes before it rises.
  • 10The brow. The brow ridge is the mask's own brow. It carries on west past the edge of the face as a cornice, and that is your walk to the pistons.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A slow drip from the upper teeth into the gullet.
  • The jade eyes dim and brighten a little, slowly.
  • Dust falls from the two slots just before the jaw starts up.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 47, first half (done): the face is set back so you are always in front of it. This design keeps the whole flat face in the background and brings only the holds forward.
  • Note 47, second half (not built): what you can hold has to be plain. Here the holds are the carved features themselves, they are the only bone white and gold on the face, and each stands out on a stem.
  • When the mask's features are measured, the holds move to where the carving is. If a gap is then too far, one more carved stud goes on the face, in the same bone white and gold.
