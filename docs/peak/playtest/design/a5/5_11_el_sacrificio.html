El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.11: El Sacrificio

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the Pozo del Diablo. The temple roofed the well's mouth with a stepped vault and hung two great censers in it, and from the sill on the west side it gave things to the well: the stone on the sill is the sacrifice stone, the temple's second altar. This is the third crossing and the last. You go from the temple's sill to the first censer's tassel, from censer to censer in the middle, and off the second onto the east lip, with the whole well under you. The east side is not the temple any more. It is the foot of the chimney, where the temple's smiths had their forge: the same fitted blocks and the same stepped doorway, black with soot, one last torch at the door, and then iron, and red light coming up through the first grate in the floor.

What must not change

  • The two censers: where they hang, their 9.8 m chains, their swings of 5 and 6 seconds, and the tassel under each.
  • The 14 m between the temple's sill and the forge's lip, and the heights of both.
  • The well is open under the whole crossing and lit, so you see the drop.
  • On the east side: the lip, the doorway, the floor and the first grate, as the chimney's course has them.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The well's far wall. The round back of the well, 6 m behind you. Down to the level of the sills it is the temple's fitted blocks. Below that it is the well's own rough rock, stepping back into the dark, with a few glints of wet far down (note 51). The dashed line is the far rim, which is dark and far back and does not read as a floor. (6.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The stepped vault and its keystone. The roof over the well steps in from both sides to a flat cap. In the middle of the back wall, under the cap, is a stone pug's face 3.4 m across. It is between the two chains and well above the bowls. Today a string of cut paper crosses behind the two chains, and a banner hangs in each top corner. All three go. Nothing hangs in the well room but the two censers. (1.3 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 3Two chain bosses. A carved pug with a bronze ring under the cap for each censer, set just behind the play space. The same boss as every other chain in the temple, at twice the size. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 4The temple's side. The altar chamber's back wall, and the side of the doorway through the well's wall: pale blocks, the carved band, and a torch on the sill. (1.3 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 5The sacrifice stone. The second altar: a low stone 2.2 m wide in a stepped niche on the sill, with a bronze bowl on it. All of it is at least 1.3 m behind you, and you can stand in front of it. It counts for 'Pay Your Respects' just as the first altar does (Parker, 11-13), and its bowl answers in the same way. (1.3 to 2.7 m behind you)
  • 6The forge's back wall. East of the well the wall is the same fitted blocks in the same bays, with the carved band at the temple's own heights. Here it is black with soot and the carving is worn nearly flat. The chimney's first page has all of it. (3.6 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 7The forge's door, a small iron pug, and the last torch. The same stepped doorway as the temple's, seen from the well, with a small pug's face in black iron over it and one torch beside it. That torch is the last of the temple's. East of it the light is red and comes from below. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 8The work place by the door. A small anvil, a rack of tongs on the wall over it, and a stone quenching trough. They are the first made things of the forge, and all of them stand behind the play space. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 9The great iron mask and its breath pipe. Across the first room of the forge: an iron pug's face 8 m wide and 11 m tall on the back wall, and a fat ribbed pipe that comes up out of the floor behind the grate and goes into its chin. It is the first thing you see from the temple's sill, lit red from underneath. It belongs to the chimney's first challenge and is designed on that page. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The two great censers. The same censer as the hall's, half as big again: a pierced bronze bowl with a gold lid, a jade band and coals inside, on a chain of thick bronze links that can be held all the way up.
  • 11The tassels. Under each bowl hangs a tassel 1.5 m long: a string of five bronze bells with a jade bead at the end. The bead is what you reach for from the sill, and what brings you down onto the far lip. It is the lowest bright thing over the well.
  • 12The temple's sill. Pale carved stone with a worn notch at its very end, where the first tassel comes to hand. In front of the sacrifice stone the same ring of jade paw prints as at the altar marks the place to stand. It is drawn onto the sill and adds nothing to stand on.
  • 13The forge's lip. The ledge the second censer drops you on is temple stone: soot black on its face, and worn pale and dry on top and along its edge, where it is held. Its edge carries the temple's carved band one last time. The wall above it and below it is wet black, which you can't hold.
  • 14The first grate. An iron grate of square bars in a raised rim, 3 m long, rust orange where feet have worn it, with red light and a wisp of steam coming up through it. It is the chimney's first challenge and is designed on its own page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 15A column base, west. At the bottom edge of the frame on the temple's side, in front of solid rock. (1.3 to 1.9 m in front)
  • 16The forge floor's kerb, east. A low lip of soot-black blocks along the bottom of the frame on the forge's side, below the floor. It is the same kerb that runs the whole length of the forge floor. The two sides of the well get two different front pieces, pale stone and black. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Both censers trail smoke, so their two swings are drawn in the air and you can see them coming together.
  • Embers rise from the well room and drift east, toward the forge.
  • The light changes across the well: torch gold on the west sill, red from below on the east lip.
  • A wisp of steam and a gurgle at the grate before it blows.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 54, Parker, 11-13: both altars count for 'Pay Your Respects', and F is the keyboard key. The sacrifice stone has its own place to stand.
  • Note 51 (done): the well steps back into depth with far glints and is not a black hole.
  • The join follows the two sheets and the chimney's first page: the temple leaves by the censers over the well's mouth, and the chimney begins on soot-black temple stone, with one last torch at its door, beside the first grate.
  • Scenery moved out of the play space: the sacrifice stone's front reaches to 0.45 m behind you today. It moves back into a niche, so that all of it is at least 1.3 m behind.
  • Scenery that goes: the string of cut paper that crosses the well room behind the two chains, and the two banners in its top corners. Nothing hangs in the well room but the two censers.
  • Nothing you touch moves or is added on either side of the well.
