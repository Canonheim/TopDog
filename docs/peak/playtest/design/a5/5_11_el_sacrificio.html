El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.11: El Sacrificio

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the Pozo del Diablo. The temple roofed the well's mouth with a stepped vault and hung two great censers in it, and from the sill on the west side it gave things to the well: the stone on the sill is the sacrifice stone. This is the third crossing and the last. You go from the temple's sill to the first censer's tassel, from censer to censer in the middle, and off the second onto the east lip, with the whole well under you. The east side is not the temple any more. It is the foot of the chimney, where the temple's smiths had their forge: the same fitted blocks and the same stepped doorway, grey with soot, then iron, and red light coming up through the first grate in the floor.

What must not change

  • The two censers: where they hang, their 9.8 m chains, their swings of 5 and 6 seconds, and the tassel under each.
  • The 14 m between the temple's sill and the forge's lip, and the heights of both.
  • The well is open under the whole crossing and lit, so you see the drop.
  • On the east side: the lip, the doorway, the floor and the first grate, as the chimney's course has them.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The well's far wall. The round back of the well, 6 m behind you. Down to the level of the sills it is the temple's fitted blocks. Below that it is the well's own rough rock, stepping back into the dark, with a few glints of wet far down (note 51). The dashed line is the far rim, which is dark and far back and does not read as a floor. (6.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The stepped vault and its keystone. The roof over the well steps in from both sides to a flat cap. In the middle of the back wall, under the cap, is a stone pug's face 3.4 m across. It is between the two chains and well above the bowls. (1.3 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 3Two chain bosses. A carved pug with a bronze ring under the cap for each censer. The same boss as every other chain in the temple, at twice the size. (1.2 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 4The temple's side. The altar chamber's back wall, and the side of the doorway through the well's wall: pale blocks, a carved band, and the last torch of the temple. (1.3 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 5The sacrifice stone. The second altar: a low stone 2.2 m wide in a stepped niche on the sill, with a bronze bowl on it. It is 1.3 m behind you and you can stand in front of it. Whether it counts for 'Pay Your Respects' is the question note 54 already asks. (1.3 to 2.7 m behind you)
  • 6The forge's side. East of the well the wall is the same fitted blocks in the same bays, but dull grey with soot, with the carving worn nearly flat. The doorway through the well's east wall is the same stepped shape as the temple's. There is no torch on this side. The light is red and comes from below. (1.3 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 7A furnace mouth under a hood. The first of the forge's furnaces: a stepped arch in the back wall with fire inside it, and a ribbed iron hood over it. The small anvil that stands in the course today is in front of it. (1.3 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 8The vent pipe. A ribbed iron pipe, 0.6 m across, runs along the foot of the wall from the furnace and turns down into the floor beside the grate. It is what feeds the grate. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 9An iron pug. Over the forge's doorway, on the back wall: the pug's face again, 1.5 m, in black iron this time. (3.0 to 3.2 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The two great censers. The same censer as the hall's, half as big again: a pierced bronze bowl with a gold lid, a jade band and coals inside, on a chain of thick bronze links that can be held all the way up.
  • 11The tassels. Under each bowl hangs a tassel 1.5 m long: a string of five bronze bells with a jade bead at the end. The bead is what you reach for from the sill, and what brings you down onto the far lip. It is the lowest bright thing over the well.
  • 12The temple's sill. Pale carved stone with a worn notch at its very end, where the first tassel comes to hand. In front of the sacrifice stone the same ring of jade paw prints as at the altar marks the place to stand.
  • 13The forge's lip. The east lip is the same blocks, dry and matte, grey with soot and rubbed pale along its edge and top by hands and feet. It has to read as stone you can hold. The wall above it and below it is wet black, which you can't.
  • 14The first grate. An iron grate of square bars set in the floor, 3 m long, rust orange where feet have worn it, with red light and a wisp of steam coming up through it. It is the chimney's first challenge and is designed on its own page.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 15A column base, west. At the bottom edge of the frame on the temple's side, in front of solid rock. (1.3 to 1.9 m in front)
  • 16A quenching trough's rim, east. On the forge's side, low in front of the floor's thickness: the iron rim of a trough. Its top is below the floor you walk on. The two sides of the well get two different front pieces, stone and iron. (1.3 to 1.9 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Both censers trail smoke, so their two swings are drawn in the air and you can see them coming together.
  • Embers rise from the well room and drift east, toward the forge.
  • The light changes across the well: torch gold on the west sill, red from below on the east lip.
  • A wisp of steam and a gurgle at the grate before it blows.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 51 (done): the well steps back into depth with far glints and is not a black hole.
