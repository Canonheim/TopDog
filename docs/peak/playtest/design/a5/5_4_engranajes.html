El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.4: Engranajes

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Above the piston shaft is the gear room: the place where the temple's turning comes from, as far as anyone can tell. Four stone wheels, each 6 m across with its cogs, stand one above another in a zigzag and turn against each other. They are built the way a mill's wheels are built: the wheel is one greased disc, and each of its twelve cogs is a separate block set into a socket in the rim and pinned with bronze. The wheels hang on two tall stone posts on the back wall, two wheels to a post. Nobody knows what turns them. The room is 14 m wide and 22 m tall, and it opens at its top corner onto the balcony of the great hall.

What must not change

  • The four gears: where each is, its size, its twelve cogs, and that each turns against the one below.
  • The disc can't be held. Only cogs can.
  • Parker has still to choose how the gears get harder (note 49). Nothing here depends on which way he chooses.
  • Open air under the second and fourth gears, down to the piston shaft.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The gear room's back wall. Wall bays, 2.8 m behind you, browner than the cogs. At the top right the room is open to the great hall, and the hall's own back wall carries on behind the balcony. (2.8 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2Two posts. Two square carved posts stand against the back wall from floor to roof, each with a pug capital. The east post carries the first and third wheels and the west post the second and fourth. Each wheel's axle runs back into its post between two carved cheeks. You see the posts above, below and between the wheels, so each wheel is plainly hung on something. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 3The carved band. At three heights on the back wall: small figures riding cogs round, stepping from one wheel to the next, and one figure caught where two wheels meet. It is the only warning the room gives about the mesh. (2.8 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 4The grease shelf. Low in the west corner: an offering niche, and beside it a stone trough of black grease with a long ladle. Black runs of grease go down both posts from every axle. It says why the discs can't be held. (2.8 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 5Torches. One on each side of the room, in the gaps the wheels leave, and one by the way out. The cogs catch the light and the discs do not. (2.7 to 3.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6Cogs set into the rim. Each cog is its own block in its own socket, with a bronze pin through it. A cog you can hold is pale dry stone. A cog you can't is black and greased like the disc. Today all twelve on each wheel are pale. If Parker picks fewer (note 49), the others are swapped for black ones and nothing else changes. If the mesh is made to bite, every cog's two side faces get a dark bronze shoe, and its outer end stays pale.
  • 7Running dogs round each disc. A ring of small carved dogs runs round each disc's face, nose to tail, in shallow relief under the grease. They show which way a wheel turns and how fast, at any speed. If the wheels are made to stop and turn back, the dogs are seen to stop first.
  • 8A pug boss at each hub. The end of each axle shows at the wheel's centre as a carved pug's face in a jade ring. It is the same boss that chains hang from elsewhere in the temple.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • Stone dust falls from each of the three meshes all the time. If the mesh bites, a few sparks fall with it.
  • The whole train shakes and drops dust from its axles for a second before any change of direction, if Parker picks that.
  • A drip of grease from the lowest wheel onto the ledge's west end.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 49 (Parker to pick): fewer cogs that hold, a faster turn, a mesh that bites, a train that turns back. The room is designed so each of these is a small swap: cogs change colour, the dogs show speed and direction, bronze shoes mark a mesh that bites, and the shake before a move is the temple's own rule.
