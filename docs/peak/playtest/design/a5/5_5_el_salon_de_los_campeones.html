El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.5: El Salón de los Campeones

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The great hall is where the temple kept its champions: everyone who ever finished the climb, carved 9 m tall, standing in two ranks down the length of the hall. The far rank stands in niches in the far wall with its arms down. The near rank, the three you climb, stands out in the hall on great pilasters, each with both arms raised in victory and one fist gilded. The hall has no floor you can use. Its floor is a polished black ramp that falls away to the east, so each champion's pilaster is taller than the last: the second stands 4.5 m above the ramp and the third 16 m. The ramp is the hall's way back down. It ends at the drain.

What must not change

  • The three champions: where each stands, its pose, and every part of it you can hold.
  • The two flings, fist to raised arm: 7 m, then 9.5 m.
  • The east forearm of each champion can't be held today, and the gilded fist hangs free at its end. See the question below.
  • A miss lands on the ramp and slides to the drain. Nothing stands in front of any of it.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The hall's far wall. Wall bays from the ramp to the roof, 9 m back and brown with shadow. The carved band crosses it at three heights. The middle band, near the height of the fists, shows small figures swinging from fist to arm down a line of statues. (9.0 to 10.0 m behind you)
  • 2The far rank. Two more champions, the same size and the same stone, in tall stepped niches in the far wall. Their arms are down. That is the temple's rule: a statue with its arms down is scenery, and a statue with an arm raised is climbed. They are 8 m back and browner, so they are never taken for the near rank. (7.5 to 9.0 m behind you)
  • 3Two great pilasters. The second and third champions each stand on a plinth, and each plinth is the top of a great pilaster that rises from the ramp behind you, with a stepped head that reaches forward to carry the plinth. The third pilaster is 16 m tall and goes out of the bottom of the frame. The first champion stands on the balcony and needs none. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4Banners. One over each niche, whole and bright, hung high on the far wall. None hangs near a fist or an arm, and none hangs in the hall's open air. (8.9 to 9.0 m behind you)
  • 5Torches. One either side of each niche and one on the balcony. They sit low, below the line of the flings, so a climber in the air is seen against plain wall. (8.7 to 9.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6A black bracer on each launch arm. Each champion's east forearm, from the elbow to the fist, can't be held. Today it looks like the rest of the arm. Here it wears a bracer of polished black stone, the temple's look for what you can't hold, so the arm reads as it plays: climb to the elbow, then take the fist.
  • 7The gilded fist. Gold, with a jade band at the wrist. It is the only gold on each statue, and it is where you fling from.
  • 8The catching arm. The raised west arm of each champion is carved deep and pale along its whole length, with a belt of small pug faces wound round it. It is 3 m of forearm and a fist: the biggest pale thing in front of you as you fly.
  • 9The ramp's polish. Polished black, with long pale scrapes running down it where others have slid.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models
