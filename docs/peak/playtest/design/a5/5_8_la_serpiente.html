El Templo del Primer Perro · Area and background design

Challenge 5.8: La Serpiente

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Over the scale, under the roof, a stone serpent 20 m long lies along the back wall. Its tail is a bronze chain that hangs down to the ledge. Its head rests on a ledge at the far end. Its body is a long carved bed fixed to the wall, and its spine is nine stone blocks that stand out of the bed toward you, each on its own ram, each in its own slot. The rams rise and fall 3 m, one after another, so a wave runs along the spine from tail to head. Three smaller serpents look down out of the wall above, mouths open, and each has one long black fang that hangs into your way.

What must not change

  • The nine vertebrae: where each is, its 3 m rise and fall on a 4 second beat, each half a second behind the one before.
  • The three fangs, where they hang, and that they can't be held.
  • The tail chain up from the ledge, and the head's ledge at the east end.
  • Open air under the serpent, down to the scale and the gallery.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The back wall. Wall bays from below the scale up to the roof. Behind the serpent the blocks are carved in shallow relief with its coils and scales, browner than the spine. (2.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The serpent's bed. A carved stone beam 19 m long and 1.2 m tall, fixed to the back wall just under the spine. It is the serpent's body. The nine rams rise out of its top. At its west end a roller's axle turns in two carved cheeks: that is what works the rams. A row of small figures is carved along its face, walking the spine and ducking under each fang. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 3Nine slots. Behind each vertebra, a slot up the back wall, lined with bronze, as tall as that vertebra's rise. The ram runs in it. The empty slot above a vertebra shows how high it will come, so you can see whether there is room under a fang before you step. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4Three small serpent heads. One over each fang, 2.6 m wide, looking down out of the wall under the roof with its mouth open. Each fang is that head's one great tooth. They are the same carving as the serpent's own head, smaller. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The serpent's head. On the ledge at the east end, against the back wall, 3 m long, looking back down its own spine with jade eyes. You step off the last vertebra and walk past it. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6The tail's boss. A carved pug with a bronze ring, on the back wall at the spine's west end. The tail chain hangs from it. (1.3 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 7The scale, below. The beam of El Contrapeso's scale is on the same wall, just under the bed. Its hangers stop below the bed. Nothing of it crosses the spine. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 8Torches. Three under the roof, between the small heads, so each fang is lit from both sides and its black shows against the lit wall. (2.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9A jade band on every vertebra. Each vertebra is pale stone carved with scales on its top, with a jade band along its top edge and a bronze shoe underneath where it meets its ram.
  • 10The fangs. Polished black, wet, with a pale run down each. They are the only black things hanging in the room.
  • 11The tail. The chain you climb is the serpent's tail: thick bronze links, getting smaller toward the bottom, ending in a carved stone tip.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
