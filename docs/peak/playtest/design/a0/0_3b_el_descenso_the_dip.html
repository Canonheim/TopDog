Challenge 0.3b: El Descenso, part two: the dip, the crest and the banked drop

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The miners drove this tunnel along a soft seam of rock, and the seam wandered. So the track runs down to a low point 42 m in, climbs 2 m over a rib of hard rock that was not worth cutting through, and then drops down the far side at 17 degrees, the steepest grade of the ride. Water gathers at the low point, so a hand pump stands there. The rib needs no timber, so for two sets' length the roof is bare rock. Carts always went light over that crest, and in the end the rails past it tore out. So the cart leaves the track at the top, flies, and comes down 7.6 m further on, in a burst of dust. This stretch is also where the town lets go. The whitewash has stopped, and a small shrine in the wall at the bottom of the dip is the last thing in the town's paint.

What must not change

  • The track's line: down at 4 degrees to the low point at x -78, up at 9 degrees to the crest at x -90, then down at 17 degrees to x -104.
  • The cart's speed, and its path everywhere but the hop.
  • The lantern lights at x -78, -90 and -104.
  • The bed grips all the way: dry and pale, even at the low point.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The tunnel's back wall. Bare plank lining, the limewash gone. Behind the landing the planks are pale with old dust. Further down the drop the first planks are missing and wet rock shows in the gaps: two dark patches, the first wet you see on the ride. (1.7 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 2The hard rib. A slanting band of harder, bluer rock, 3 m wide, crossing the back wall at the crest. It has old drill holes in it and no timber on it: two sets are left out here, because this rock holds itself up. It is why the track climbs. (1.7 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 3The timber sets, bare. Bare wood, dull and dry, greyer than anything you can hold. At the low point the feet of three posts are stained dark to knee height. Past the crest the wood starts to go grey. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 4The shrine. A small arched niche in the back wall at the bottom of the dip, whitewashed, with candle stubs in it and a scrap of papel picado over it. The miners touched it on the way in. From here down, nothing is in the town's paint. (1.7 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 5The hand pump and its trough. At the low point, against the back wall: an iron hand pump, 1.4 m tall, with its handle up. A wooden trough runs from it along the foot of the back wall, up over the crest and on down the tunnel. It is why the dip is not a pond. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 6Three lanterns. Where the course has its lights: at the low point, at the crest and at the foot of the drop. The one on the crest hangs from an iron spike driven into the rib, since there is no cap there. It lights the place where the rails stop, so you see the break coming. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 7The skull-pug sign. The mine's own danger sign, on a post against the back wall half way up the climb to the crest. It is the warning before the hop, and the first of these signs you see. The kit has it. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The broken rail. From the crest at x -90 to x -94 both rails are gone and only the sleepers are left. The near rail's stub is bent up at the crest like the lip of a ramp. The far rail lies in the ballast beside the sleepers. The kit has the broken piece.
  • 9The hop. The cart leaves the rails at the crest, where the track turns from 9 degrees up to 17 degrees down. Thrown at its own 9 m a second, it flies 7.6 m in 0.9 seconds, is 0.9 m clear of the sleepers at its highest, noses over in the air, and comes down at x -97.6. The line drawn is the cart's floor. These are the numbers of a real throw, as a place to start: the feel is for the thread to tune. You are light in the cart while it flies, so hold on.
  • 10The landing. Where the cart comes down, from x -97 to x -100, the rails are scraped bright and the sleepers are doubled, two side by side, because this is where they always broke. Pale dust lies thick between them. It is the dust that jumps off the wheels. The bed under it is unchanged and still grips.
  • 11Banked sleepers. Below the landing the track is laid banked: the far rail 0.2 m higher than the near one, so the tops of the sleepers show. It reads as a track built to be run fast. The bed under your feet is unchanged.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 12Four front posts. The front post of every fourth set, so one every 10.4 m. Each is 0.3 m wide and plain. At the cart's speed one sweeps past about every 1.2 seconds, and there are never more than two on screen. The back post of the same set stands behind it, so you pass between the two. All the other sets have a back post and a cap only. Here: four, in bare wood, and none on the rib. The fourth is moved one set down the hill, to x -101, so that nothing crosses in front of the cart as it lands. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The candle in the shrine is the one small warm light between two lanterns.
  • At the crest: a clank as the wheels leave the rail, and then the wheel noise stops dead. The silence is how you know you are in the air.
  • At the landing (Parker, 11-13): a bang, a burst of pale dust off all four wheels that hangs and blows back up the slope behind the cart, and a shake of the camera.
  • The first drips, falling past the dark patches on the drop.
