La Estación · Area and background design

Challenge 0.1-0.2: El Primer Paso and Súbete

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is where the town stops and the mine's ground starts. The road ends at the station house, a small plastered building with a ticket window and a tiled roof. West of it stands the ore dock: a timber platform 2.2 m high and 31 m long that runs from the road to the tunnel mouth, with a siding along it. Ore came out of the mountain in carts, was swung down off the dock's end by a hand crane, and went into town by wagon. The last load is still sitting where the crane put it down, under the crane's arm. Its crates are your first steps. One cart is left on the siding with its brake on, and somebody still oils it.

What must not change

  • The three crates as steps: one, then two stacked against the dock's end. Each step is 1.1 m.
  • The dock: its deck 2.2 m up, its east end at x -4.6, its legs where they are.
  • The cart on the siding at x -9, with its low sides, and that it rolls once you have been in it a moment.
  • The red lever beside the cart, at x -7.6.
  • The start: on the road at x 6, 8 m east of the crates, facing the mountain.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The Peak and the town, far off. Far cut-outs, the plaza's own. The Peak stands whole in the west half of the sky with the tiny bell on its top: the only time you see all of it until the ending. Low in the east, the town's roofs. Both are flat and sun-bleached, so nothing near is mistaken for them. (12.0 to 40.0 m behind you)
  • 2The station house. Cream plaster, a terracotta roof, a painted door, a ticket window, and a small bell on a bracket at its corner. It stands on the road right behind where you start. On its wall, between the door and the window, hangs the board of the ones who tried: rows of small painted masks, most crossed out. The biome's sheet asks whether you want the board. (2.2 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 3The frog. Asleep on the sill of the ticket window, 1.1 m above the road, in plain sight. It is the first frog, so it is not hidden: it teaches you there are frogs to find (note 60). (2.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4The rest of the load. A barrel and a heap of ore sacks on the road beside the crates, 1.4 m further back. They say the crates are a load that was set down, not a staircase somebody built. Greyer than the crates, and too low to look like another step. (1.4 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 5The hand crane. On the dock's back corner, 3 m tall, its arm swung out over the crates. The hook block is wound right up under the arm's tip, so nothing hangs from it. It is what put the crates there. (2.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The dock's back half. The dock is 5 m deep and you climb only its front edge. Behind it runs the rest: a second row of posts, set between the front legs so both rows show, and a rail along the back edge. (1.3 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 7The second cart. On blocks at the back of the dock, its wheels gone. The cart you ride has been kept running with parts from this one. Its own brake lever stands beside it, rusted, with the same rod and chock: the dead one shows you how the red one works. (2.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 8The bunting. Torn papel picado from the station's roof to the crane's head, then from pole to pole along the dock's back rail, all the way to the tunnel. It points the way before the sign does. It is high, it is 2.3 m behind you, and its flags are 0.5 m deep, so it can't be taken for a rope. (2.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 9The pug arrow sign. Where it stands today, moved 0.4 m further back so it is clear of the play space. (1.4 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10Stencils on the crates. Each crate gets the mine's pug stencilled on its face and iron straps at its corners. The top edges are scuffed pale where hands and boots go.
  • 11Sawn ends along the deck's edge. The deck's cross-beams show as a row of square sawn ends along its face, one every 2 m. They say the deck carries on back. The mine uses the same mark on its rock.
  • 12The brake rod. The lever is red, and so is the band round the cart: here, red means it does something. A short iron rod runs along the deck from the lever's foot to a wooden chock under the cart's wheel, so you can see what the lever holds.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A fence post with a tail of bunting. The last post of the town's fence, at the right edge of the first screen, with a torn tail of bunting tied to it. It stands on the low ground in front of the road, and its top comes only 0.3 m above the road, so it never covers you. (1.8 to 2.0 m in front)
  • 14An agave clump. On the low ground at the bottom of the frame, in front of the road's face. It is below your feet wherever you stand. (1.6 to 2.4 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The frog croaks now and then, loud enough to hear from the start. It does not wake.
  • The bunting stirs. Nothing else moves until you do.
  • Short hard shadows under the dock and the crates: the noon sun is straight overhead.
