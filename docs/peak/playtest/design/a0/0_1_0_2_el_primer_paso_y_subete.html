La Estación · Area and background design

Challenge 0.1-0.2: El Primer Paso and Súbete

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

This is where the town stops and the mine's ground starts. You come out through the town's gate into a walled yard, and the Peak fills the sky ahead. On the yard wall behind you are the Missing posters: the masked faces of the ones who went up and did not come back. Across the yard stands the ore dock, a timber platform 2.2 m high and 31 m long that runs to the tunnel mouth, with a siding along it and the little station house built on its back. Ore came out of the mountain in carts and was swung down off the dock's end by a hand crane. The last load is still where the crane put it down, and it is your first steps: a barrel, then two crates. On the siding one cart is left, held by a tall red lever, and somebody still oils it. The frog is asleep in the station's ticket window.

What must not change

  • The three steps where they are: one box 1.1 m high, then two stacked against the dock's end. The first is now drawn as a barrel of the same size.
  • The dock: its deck 2.2 m up, its east end at x -4.6, its legs where they are.
  • The cart on the siding at x -9, with its low sides.
  • The start: on the road at x 6, 8 m east of the steps, facing the mountain.
  • The lever changes: see the notes at the foot of this page.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The Peak and the town, far off. Far cut-outs, the plaza's own. The Peak stands whole in the west half of the sky with the tiny bell on its top: the only time you see all of it until the ending. The town's roofs show over the yard wall. Both are flat and sun-bleached, so nothing near is mistaken for them. (12.0 to 40.0 m behind you)
  • 2The yard wall, the town gate and the arrow sign. A plastered wall 2.4 m tall with a tiled top closes the yard on the town's side, 3.6 m behind you. In it is the gate you came out of: an arch 2.4 m wide with the town's street going away behind it. It is why you can't walk back east: the way to town turns away from you, through the wall. The pug arrow sign stands by the wall's end where it does today, moved 0.4 m further back so it is out of the play space. (3.6 to 4.2 m behind you)
  • 3The station house. Built on the back of the dock, behind the cart: cream plaster, a terracotta roof, a narrow porch on two slim posts, a shut door, a ticket window, and a small bell on a bracket at its west corner. Its wall is 1.5 m behind you, so you walk and ride along its front. On the wall, straight above the lever's handle, a red arrow is painted, pointing down. (1.3 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 4The Missing posters. Four here (Parker, 11-13). Two fresh ones side by side on the yard wall, right behind where you start: the stripe and the fangs. An old copy of the stripe further along the same wall, by the arrow sign: sun-bleached, creased, one corner gone. And the star, on the station house wall beside the ticket window. Each is 0.5 m by 0.7 m and hangs at face height. The fifth is on the tunnel mouth's post, on the next page. (3.5 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 5The posters, drawn large. A key, not a thing in the scene: the three posters at 6 times their size, square by square. They are made the way the lock-in money is made. Each is 16 squares by 22, a square 3.1 cm across, in four inks of one of the money's six colours (lavender, green, rose): the colour, a lighter one, a darker one and a darkest. Round the edge runs the money's two-line border. Across the top, the money's woven band. In the middle, where the money has its sun, is a masked face: an oval with a dark rim, two diamond eyes and a slot of a mouth. Each mask has its own mark: a stripe down the middle, two fangs up the cheeks, or a star on the brow with a dot on the chin. Under the face, the money's dotted trim stands for the lines of print. No words and no numbers. The paper curls and creases the way the bills do. In this sketch the four inks are drawn pale blue, yellow, slate and black. (3.5 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 6The frog. Asleep on the sill of the ticket window, 1.1 m above the deck and 1 m west of the cart, in plain sight (Parker, 11-13). The cart carries you past it at arm's length as it pulls out. It is the first frog, so it is not hidden: it teaches you there are frogs to find (note 60). (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 7The hand crane and the rest of its load. On the dock's back corner beside the house, 3 m tall, its arm swung out over the steps. The hook block is wound right up under the arm's tip, so nothing hangs from it. On the road behind the steps lies the rest of the load: a second barrel on its side and a heap of ore sacks, too low to look like another step. (1.4 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 8The dock's back half and the second cart. The dock is 5 m deep and you climb only its front edge. Behind runs the rest: a second row of posts, set between the front legs so both rows show, and a rail along the back edge west of the house. Against that rail a second cart stands on blocks, its wheels gone. The cart you ride has been kept running with parts from this one. (1.3 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 9The bunting. Torn papel picado from the gate's head to a pole on the wall's end, on to the crane's head, and from the house's roof west from pole to pole along the dock, all the way to the tunnel. It points the way before the sign does. It is high, it is 2.3 m or more behind you, and its flags are 0.5 m deep, so it can't be taken for a rope. (2.3 to 3.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The barrel and the two crates. The first step is drawn as a standing barrel, 1.1 m tall and 1.2 m across, banded with iron. It is the same box to stand on. The two crates above it get the mine's pug stencilled on their faces. All three are scuffed pale on top, where hands and boots go.
  • 11Sawn ends along the deck's edge. The deck's cross-beams show as a row of square sawn ends along its face, one every 2 m. They say the deck carries on back. The mine uses the same mark on its rock.
  • 12The lever. As tall as you are: 1.7 m, standing in an iron quadrant bolted to the deck 0.2 m east of the cart. At rest it leans towards the cart, so its handle hangs right over the cart's east end, 1.65 m above the deck and 1.1 m above the cart's floor: at shoulder height from the deck, at chest height from inside the cart, and within 0.7 m of your shoulder either way. The shaft is red. The top 0.4 m is a pale wooden grip, because that is the part your hand takes. Take it as you take the chair, with the hand whose button you are holding, and draw it east: after 0.7 m it drops into its second notch and the cart is off (the dashed line). A rod along the deck ties the lever's foot to a chock under the cart's wheel, so you can see what it lets go. Pull it from the deck and the cart leaves without you unless you are over its end within a second. It comes back to the siding 25 seconds later, as it does today.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A fence post with a tail of bunting. The last post of the town's fence, with a torn tail of bunting tied to it. It stands on the low ground in front of the road, and its top comes only 0.3 m above the road, so it never covers you. (1.8 to 2.0 m in front)
  • 14An agave clump. On the low ground at the bottom of the frame, in front of the road's face. It is below your feet wherever you stand. (1.6 to 2.4 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The lever goes over with a heavy clunk, the chock is kicked out from under the wheel, and the cart starts to roll.
  • The frog croaks now and then, loud enough to hear from the steps. It does not wake, even when the cart goes by.
  • The bunting stirs, and one corner of the old poster lifts and settles.
  • Short hard shadows under the dock and the steps: the noon sun is straight overhead.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Parker, 11-13: the station may be completely reworked. This is the second pass. The station house moves up onto the back of the dock, behind the cart. The road gets a yard wall and the town's gate. The first step is drawn as a barrel, as the challenge list has it.
  • Parker, 11-13: Missing posters, made the way the lock-in money is made, take the place of the board of crossed-out masks. Four hang here and one at the tunnel mouth.
  • Parker, 11-13: the frog sits in plain sight. It is on the ticket window's sill, beside the cart (note 60).
  • Parker, 11-13: the lever works. COURSE CHANGE, the play: pulling the lever starts the cart whether you are in the cart or not, and getting into the cart no longer starts it. The lever becomes a thing your hand takes and keeps while you hold that limb's button, like the chair.
  • COURSE CHANGE, a thing you touch: the lever's foot stays at x -7.6. The lever grows from 1 m to 1.7 m, comes forward from 0.9 m behind you to 0.45 m behind you, and leans so that its handle is at x -7.95, 3.86 m up, over the cart's east end. Pulled, the handle is at x -7.25.
  • Nothing you stand on moves: not the steps, the deck, the legs or the rails. The first crate keeps its size and is only drawn as a barrel.
  • Scenery that is in the play space today and moves out of it: the pug arrow sign, 0.4 m further back.
