La Estación · Area and background design

Join 0 to 1: The station to the mine: the end of the line

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The end of the line. The tunnel levels out for its last 16 m and stops at a buffer, 2 m short of the door into the mine. By here everything the town sent in has dropped away. The bunting stopped 10 m in, the whitewash at 26 m, the shrine in the dip was the last paint, and the daylight is long gone. What is left is the mine's: grey wet timber, smashed lining, amber light. The door is one timber set that belongs to both places. The first thing beyond it is honey-coloured timber you can hold, the first since the dock, and under that timber is the first black mud you can touch.

What must not change

  • The buffer where it is, at x -150.3, and its size: 0.6 m by 0.8 m.
  • Where the cart stops: with its front end 0.4 m short of the buffer.
  • The door at x -152, and the gallery beyond it exactly as the mine's own page has it.
  • The mine's first lantern at x -154.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The tunnel's back wall, the last 16 m. The lining is smashed here and the rock behind it is wet. This is the same back wall the mine's galleries have. (1.7 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 2The mine's first gallery. Drawn here only so the two back walls meet at the door. What is in the gallery is on the mine's own page, 1.1 La Galería: the mud pit, the posts over it, the cart the mud took. (1.7 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 3The last five sets. Grey, wet and rotten, 2.6 m apart. One cap is cracked and held by a prop. After the fifth comes one wide bay, 4.2 m, with the buffer in it. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 4The door set. The timber set at the door, which the course already has. It is the tunnel's last set and the mine's first: the same posts and cap, no whitewash, nothing painted. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5The buffer's braces. The buffer is a timber baulk laid right across the track. Its far end is held by two braces that rake back to the floor against the back wall. What you see in the play space is the near end of it. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 6The miners' pegs. A board of six pegs on the back wall, where the men hung their lamps and coats when they got out of the carts. Five pegs are empty. One still has a hat on it, and a dead lamp. (1.6 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 7The trough's end. The trough along the wall's foot ends at the door, and its water runs over the sill and on towards the gallery's mud pit. The tunnel's water is what made the mine's first mud. (1.4 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 8The mine's first lantern. Where the course has it, just inside the door. The tunnel's last lantern, 18 m back up the track, is the same amber and just as dim, so the light does not change at the door. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The buffer's iron face. The iron block becomes the near end of the timber baulk, faced with an iron plate that is dented where carts have hit it. It keeps its size.
  • 10The sill at the door. The tunnel's floor is 0.6 m higher than the gallery's, and today it ends in a bare step. The step gets a worn timber sill along its edge, so it reads as the end of a loading floor.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The last front post, and the door's broken one. One whole front post, at x -140. The door set's front post is broken in the middle: a length hanging from the cap and a stump on the floor. The gap between them is where the cart throws you, so nothing covers you as you land. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)
