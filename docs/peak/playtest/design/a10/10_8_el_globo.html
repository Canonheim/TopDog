La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.8: El Globo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The station's weather balloon, still up. The keeper flew it from the buttress to measure the air above the storm: a skin of dark rubber as big as a room, a timber basket for the instruments under it, and a drag rope of hemp hanging from the basket with a bag of sand on its end. He wound it in and out with a hand winch on the buttress's top step, on a black greased cable. He left it out. It has ridden at the end of its cable ever since, bobbing 2 m, with its rope's foot inside the cloud and its skin in the sun. You take the rope from the last knob of La Nube and climb it. Part way up you come out of the top of the storm. The rain stops and the light turns gold. The feet of the summit's crown stand out of the cloud beside you. Then you swing the rope, let go to the west, and land beside the winch.

What must not change

  • The balloon, its basket, and its bob of 2 m.
  • The climb of 12 m up the rope, and the fling from it: about 9 m west and 2 m down to the buttress's top step.
  • The skin is solid and slick. Whatever falls on it from the summit slides off its side.
  • The headwall's wind, above 953 m, which blows east, against the fling.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cloud. La Nube's bank, from its own page. It fills the frame below 951 m, 1.5 m back. The rope's foot hangs inside it, in rain and grey. (1.5 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 2The summit's rock, standing out of the cloud. Above the cloud's top, what stands behind you is the summit's: three of its rock teeth 1.4 m back, the two pillars that its horns stand on 1.8 m back, and its tan back 3 m back. They are designed on the summit's pages, which carry them down below the summit's own frames. Today every one of them stops square at 958 m, in the air behind the rope. Seen from the ridge they have to end somewhere. This design asks that the teeth stop at 953 m, behind our cloud's top. The pillars and the back can go on down, because our cloud bank hides them. So the ridge's slate ends inside the cloud, and you come up out of the storm with warm rock rising round you. The west pillar is the back wall of the step you land on. The dark skin and the pale rope both show against it. (1.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The cloud's top. A strip of round heads, 1.3 m back, with their tops between 953 and 954.6 m, from La Nube's page. It stands in front of the summit's rock: the feet of the teeth and the pillars go down behind it. You climb out through this line. Above it nothing of the storm's stands behind you. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 4The rib and the last knob. La Nube's rib, from its own page. Its east edge is cut back so that the rope hangs against cloud and not against rock. (1.2 to 1.3 m behind you)
  • 5The winch and the arrow. At the back of the buttress's top step, where the fling lands: a hand winch on a timber frame, 1.2 m wide and only 0.45 m deep, with a crank and a full drum, bolted down through iron pug plates. It is shallow so that it stands in front of the summit's pillar. A guide post 2.2 m tall stands at its east end with an iron block at its head. The kit's pug arrow board is nailed to the post, pointing up at the rungs. This is the machine that the balloon bobs on. It is grey and wet, a step duller than the step you land on. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 6The cable. Note 96 and the sheet. One cable of black greased steel, 0.15 m thick, from the block on the guide post up to an iron ring under the back of the skin. About 9.5 m of it is in the air, and the rest of its 24 m is on the drum. It slants at 30 degrees, it is black and glossy like the line pole and the vane's arms, and it is 1.3 m behind you. So it is never taken for the rope, which is pale and hangs straight. It also points from the balloon to the place you land. The thin tether that hangs straight down behind the ropes today goes. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 7The landing's wind sock. Note 89. The headwall's wind has its flag at the place you are flinging to: a grey canvas sock under the block on the guide post. It streams east, toward you on the rope, when the wind is against the fling, and hangs slack when it is safe to go. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The one rope. Note 96. One hemp rope, 12 m long, hung from the middle of the basket's underside, in place of the two that hang 0.6 m apart today. It is pale, dry and matte, and as thick as a wrist. On its end is a small canvas bag of sand, the weight that the note proposes, so the wind does not carry the rope's foot away from La Nube's last knob. The bag is to be tested.
  • 9The basket. A crate of honey timber, 1.6 m wide and 0.6 m tall, pegged, with an iron pug plate on its side. It is drawn at exactly the size that is held. Nothing sticks out of it: the instruments are inside.
  • 10The neck. Between the basket and the skin the course has 1 m of nothing. Something has to be drawn there, and what is drawn must be what can be touched. So the basket hangs from a short neck of the same wet rubber, 0.5 m wide and 1 m tall, and the design asks for the neck to be solid and slick like the skin. It would be the one new solid on this page, and it is put to Parker below.
  • 11The skin. Dark red-brown rubber, shining wet, 4.2 m across and 4.8 m tall, with two paler patches glued on and a seam round its middle. It is drawn at exactly the size that is solid. Nothing on it is matte or dry. It reads as the one thing above the cloud that you cannot hold.
  • 12The step you land on. The buttress's top step: pale dry slate in square steps, 3.5 m long, the rest at the foot of the headwall. Below it the buttress's cap is blue-black and glossy and slopes at 37 degrees to its brink. A fling that falls short lands on the cap.
  • 13The winds, drawn. Note 89. Below 953 m: La Nube's wind, long wavy white lines moving east. Above it: the headwall's wind, also moving east, in shorter and quicker gusts. Its lines cross the rope, the balloon and the landing. The fling goes against them, so you go when the stripe is empty.

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The moment your head comes out of the cloud's top, the rain stops, the sound opens out, and the light on you turns from grey to gold.
  • The skin creaks. Rain runs off it below the cloud's top, and above it the skin steams a little in the sun.
  • The cable goes tight and slack with each bob, and the drum's pawl clicks.
  • The sandbag swings east in a gust, a moment before the rope does.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 96: the balloon has one rope, hung from the middle of the basket. The fling is measured again from that one rope: its line moves 0.3 m. The last reach of La Nube is aimed at it.
  • Note 96 also asks whether the light rope wants a weight on its end. The sandbag is that weight, shown here and still to be tested.
  • The sheet found that the balloon's thin tether hangs straight down behind the rope and reads as more rope. It becomes one thick black cable, slanting to a winch.
  • The sheet puts the cable 2 m back. The summit's teeth stand 1.4 m back in the same place, and its pillars 1.8 m back, and they would hide it. So the cable, the winch and its post stand 1.3 m back, in front of them.
  • The join with the summit: its teeth, pillars and back stand behind everything above the cloud's top. The summit's pages carry them down. This page asks only that the teeth stop at 953 m, behind our cloud's top, so that none comes down across La Nube. The ridge's own flank ends inside the cloud.
  • Notes 76 and 77, what is drawn is what can be touched: the skin and the basket are drawn at the size that is solid, and the neck is asked for so that it can be too.
  • Note 89: both winds are drawn, and the headwall's wind has a flag at the place you fling to.
  • Course changes: one rope at x -321.5 in place of two at x -321.8 and x -321.2, with a sandbag on its end. Asked below: one new slick solid, the neck, 0.5 m wide and 1 m tall, between the basket and the skin. Scenery: the thin tether is replaced by the slanting cable, a winch is added behind the top step, and the arrow sign's board moves onto the winch's guide post.
