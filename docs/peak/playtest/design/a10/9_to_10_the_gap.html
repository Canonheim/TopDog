La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Join 9 to 10: The gap: from the glacier to the storm ridge

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The glacier does not reach the ridge. Its ice has pulled away from the rock and left a gap 6 m wide, and the gap drains down the chute onto the whole of the glacier's long slide. On the glacier's side stands the last of the ones who tried, frozen on the very top, leaning out with one mitten raised. On the ridge's side there is one ledge, under a prow of wet slate that roofs it, and on that ledge the weather keeper built his cabin, out of the rain. Its stove is still lit. So what you fling at is a small amber room in a grey picture, with smoke streaming east from its pipe. Within one screen the ice ends, the slate begins, and the first wind of the ridge is blowing in your face.

What must not change

  • The fling of challenge 9.10: from the mitten, 7 m west and 3.4 m up, onto the ledge past its glazed first metre.
  • The ledge, the prow over it, and the gap under the flight, each exactly where it is.
  • The ridge's lowest wind. It blows east only, in gusts of 2 seconds with 3.5 seconds between, and the last of the flight is in it.
  • The torn line where it hangs, and El Filo's point 2.8 m from the mitten.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. From the glacier's top you see their lowest ends east of El Filo. It is the first sight of what the ridge is made of. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2Cloud in the gap. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It fills the open air between the prow and El Filo. Under it, at the foot of the picture, the valley shows as one last strip. After this ledge there is only cloud until the summit. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3The gap's far wall. The ridge's own rock, bared where the ice pulled away: the ridge's flank, wet slate in leaning slabs, dull blue-grey, 2.4 m back, with long pale scrapes low down where the ice has dragged across it. It is the same piece as on the glacier's page for El Rimaye. It is the first slate you see, and it is behind you and in shadow. (2.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The glacier's side. From the glacier's own page for El Rimaye, drawn here so that the two pages agree: a horn of ice at the last climber's back, the route's last cane on the tower's top with a lit lantern on it, and, in the ice under the ledge, the dark hole that the snow slabs come out of (note 93). None of it is the ridge's. (1.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 5The nook under El Alero. The back wall of the space under the prow: the ridge's flank in leaning ribs of slate, dark and running with water. It carries on east to under the prow's tip, so the ledge reads as the floor of a real hollow in a mountain. (3.4 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 6The keeper's cabin. On the ledge, against the nook's back wall, cut away at the front: plank walls, a slate roof, a lit stove and one lamp, and the frog at the table. It is designed on its own page. From the glacier's top its amber room is the only warm colour you can see, and it is exactly where you have to land (note 94). (1.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 7The prayer pole. The ridge's first prayer pole, 4 m tall, with the town's cut paper tied along its top. It stands at the cabin's west corner. Its paper streams east in a gust and hangs slack between gusts. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 8El Filo's body, and the cloud at its foot. Behind El Filo's blade stands its body: the same slate, 3 m wide and 3 m back, duller and greyer. A collar of painted cloud, 11 m wide and 5 m tall, lies across the body's lower end, 2 m back. The blade's point hangs in front of it. This collar takes the place of the flat grey card that stood in front of the last climber's mitten (note 92). (1.9 to 3.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The mitten. The launch: the raised mitten of the last climber on the glacier. He belongs to the glacier's pages. What this page asks of him is only that nothing stands in front of him, and that he is leaning at the cabin's light.
  • 10The porch. The ledge keeps its firm snow, white and matte, 5 m long. The wind has combed the snow into low ridges that all run east. The glazed first metre is blue-black and glossy, the last piece of the glacier's ice, so it reads as the one part of the porch that will not hold you.
  • 11The torn line and its spar. The way on, seen as you land. A square timber spar is let into the underside of the prow, and the torn half of a prayer line hangs from it, 4.3 m of hemp rope with paper along all of it. Only the spar's end shows, flush in the rock round the knot. It adds nothing to hold.
  • 12The first wind, drawn. Note 89. The lowest wind fills the band from 884 to 893 m: long wavy white lines with an inked edge, moving east, across the porch and the gap. They are thin and see-through, and they pass in front of you. In the 0.8 seconds before a gust a front of lines comes out from under the prow and a whistle rises. Between gusts there are no lines.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A lip of drifted snow. Along the front of the first ledge, under 0.3 m tall, in front of the ledge's own snow. It is the only thing on the whole ridge that stands in front of the climb, and it covers nothing above your ankles. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The cabin's smoke streams east in a gust and stands straight up between gusts. It is the first flag of the first wind, and you can read it from the mitten.
  • Amber light from the cabin lies on the porch's snow and on the underside of the prow, as colour and not as glow.
  • Rain runs down the prow's cap and falls off its brink in a thin broken line, 2 m east of the ledge's end. Under the prow it is dry.
  • Snow blowing off the glacier's top, as on the glacier's side today.
