La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.9: Escalera al Cielo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The last of the ridge, and the last thing the town built on the mountain. From the buttress's top step an iron ladder went up the headwall to the summit's ledge: a row of thick iron rungs driven into the slate, each one sticking straight out from the face, with a rail joining their outer ends. It was made by the same hands as the cableway, of the same rust-orange iron as every rung since the mine. The storm has had it for years. The rail is gone and most of the rungs with it. Four are left. Two are sound. Two are eaten through and hold for a moment. Up the face between them runs a line of empty bolt holes, each with a rust streak under it, so you can see where every missing rung was. You are above the storm here. The cloud's top is under your feet, no rain falls, and gold light comes down the wall from the summit. But the ridge's last wind is its hardest. It blows straight off the face, and a fall from these rungs is the whole mountain. At the top you go out round the end of the summit's ledge and onto it.

What must not change

  • The four rungs, each where it is: sound, rusted, a gap of 1.7 m, sound, rusted. A rusted rung holds for 1.2 seconds.
  • The last two reaches: to the east end of the summit's ledge at 962.4 m, and up onto its top at 964 m.
  • The wind above 953 m, which blows east, off the wall, in gusts of 1.8 seconds with 3 seconds between.
  • The buttress's top step, where you start. The face itself cannot be held.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The summit's west pillar. The back wall of the headwall's air is not the ridge's. It is the pillar that the summit's west horn stands on: warm tan rock, 8 m wide, 1.8 m back, with one of the crown's teeth standing in front of it and the summit's tan back behind. They are the summit's pieces and are on its pages. Our side of it: the ridge's slate flank ends below you, inside the cloud, and from the top step up the only slate left is the face under your hands. That change, from blue-grey rock behind you to warm rock behind you, is the join. (1.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 2The top of the storm. East of the buttress and below you: the round heads of the cloud's top, at 953 to 954.6 m, 1.3 m back. The foot of the pillar goes down behind it. It is the last cloud of ours that you see. Past it and far below is the storm's own deck. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 3The winch, the arrow and the sock. At the back of the top step: the balloon's winch, from El Globo's page, with its cable going off to the east. The kit's pug arrow board is nailed to the winch's guide post and points up at the rungs. Today the board stands on a post of its own directly behind the first rung, inside the play space. The sock under the post's block is the headwall's flag as well as the balloon's. It streams east, off the wall, and you can see it from every rung. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 4Two fallen rungs. Lying at the back of the step, east of the winch: two iron rungs that came out whole, rusted dark, one bent. They are the two that are missing from the long gap. They lie flat and dull, behind you, and are not holds. (1.3 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 5The ledge above. The ledge at 964 m is designed on the summit's pages, as a tea shop. This page gives it nothing. What you see of it from the rungs is its underside, a thread of steam from the shop's pipe, and the summit's line of whole, bright, dry paper under the prow. The ridge's own paper line there, which hangs only 1.4 m over the floor, is taken down on the summit's page. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6Two sound rungs. Each is a straight iron bar 0.9 m long and 0.16 m thick, standing out from the face, rust-orange, dry and matte. A square iron plate with two bolts holds it to the slate, and its outer end has a round eye where the rail was bolted. A sound rung is thick, straight and bolted at both ends.
  • 7Two rusted rungs. The mountain's one breakable look: cracked already, with a corner gone. A rusted rung is eaten to half its thickness, pale and scabbed, cracked through beside its plate, with one of the plate's two bolts missing, so its outer end has dropped by a hand's width. It sheds flakes of rust all the time. It is drawn inside the shape that is held. It holds for 1.2 seconds, then its inner end tears out and it swings down on its last bolt and hangs flat against the wet face, dark, inside its own rust streak. Eight seconds later a gust lifts it, and it knocks back into its socket with a clank and a puff of rust. Only then can it be held again.
  • 8Where the ladder was. Painted on the face, up the line the ladder took: a pair of dark bolt holes at each place a rung is gone, six places, with a long rust streak running down the slate from each. In the long gap, where two rungs came out together, each pair of holes keeps a black nub of iron no longer than a thumb. The stub that is there today is 0.35 m long, in a hold's own colour, and cannot be held. It is cut down to one of these nubs.
  • 9The top step. Pale dry slate in square steps, 3.5 m long. It is the last slate you stand on.
  • 10The ledge's end. The last two reaches are on the east end of the ledge, which overhangs the face by 2 m. This end of the ledge is the ridge's last slab of slate: dry, pale warm grey, in square steps, the same as every tower's top, and the only pale rock on the wall. Two old bolt holes with rust under them show where the ladder's top bracket was. The summit's page has this slab leaning on the summit's cream rock 8 m to the west. The top of the ledge is the summit's to design.
  • 11The last wind, drawn. Note 89. One stripe of long wavy white lines from 953 m to above the ledge, moving east, starting at the face itself. The lines come off the wall where your hands are. Its gusts are shorter and come more often than any other on the ridge. The flakes of rust from the two bad rungs fly east in it, and so does the paper over the ledge.

Small effects, no models

  • Gold light from above on everything that faces up. Your shadow falls down the wall.
  • Water runs down the face in thin bright lines from the snow above. The face is wet, though no rain falls here.
  • A rusted rung groans when a hand takes it, and flakes fall from it faster.
  • Rust flakes and the sock on the winch lift 0.8 seconds before each gust.
  • Far below, to the east, the storm flickers. You are looking down on it.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • The sheet's way out: the slate and the rain end below you, the top of the storm is under your feet, and the light is gold from above. Here the slate behind you ends inside the cloud, and the slate under your hands ends at the ledge. Note 97, the tea shop on the ledge, is on the summit's pages.
  • Note 89: the last wind is drawn from the face out, with three flags: the winch's sock, the rust flakes and the paper over the ledge.
  • The second playtest's law: what is drawn is what can be touched, and a thing that comes is seen coming. A rusted rung is drawn inside the shape that is held, and it comes back where you can watch it.
  • The sheet's rusted rung hangs by one bolt with its other end dropped. Here the end drops by a hand's width only, so the drawing stays on the solid.
  • The join with the summit: the back of the headwall is the summit's west pillar, on the summit's pages. The exit ledge's east 8 m is the ridge's last slab of slate, as the summit's page for the ledge has it.
  • Course changes, scenery only: the arrow sign's board moves 1.5 m east and 0.3 m back, onto the winch's guide post, out of the play space and out from behind the first rung. The rung stub in the play space is cut down to a nub, and a second nub is added. The ridge's paper line over the exit ledge is taken down, on the summit's page.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
