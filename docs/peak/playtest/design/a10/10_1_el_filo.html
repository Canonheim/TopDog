La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.1: El Filo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The first of the ridge's towers, and the thinnest. El Filo is one slab of slate standing on end, 13 m tall, and you climb its cut edge, which is 1 m thick. The rest of the slab runs away behind you and down into cloud. A vein of white quartz crosses the slab from side to side as it rises, and wherever it comes out at the front it leaves a knob: one on the east side, then one on the west. The keeper came this way every day. His line from the cabin's spar ended at a pale scar on the blade's west side, where a flake had come away, and he went up the knobs to the slab's flat dry head, where his line pole stands. The line is torn now, so you swing on what is left of it and let go. Three winds lie across the blade, one above the other, and they take turns: east at its foot, west in its middle, east at its top.

What must not change

  • The torn line, the fling to the scar, the eight knobs and the top, each exactly where it is.
  • The blade's slick faces, and its point, which stands clear of the glacier's top.
  • The three winds and their turns: east below 893 m, west from 893 to 898 m, east from 898 to 904 m.
  • The fall line: the face, El Alero's cap and its brink.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. Two of them show east of the blade. They are blades too, so El Filo reads as the nearest of many. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2A cloud bank. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It stands between the prow and the blade and behind the blade's lower half, so the knobs' side of the blade is seen against one plain grey. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3The nook, and the cabin in it. The start of the climb is the cabin's porch. The nook's slate wall, the cabin, its smoke and the prayer pole are on the cabin's own page. (1.2 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 4El Filo's body. The slab the blade is the edge of: the same slate, 3.6 m wide, 3 m back, duller and greyer, in two leaning slabs. It stops just under the blade's top and goes down into cloud. On it, in dull white paint, the quartz vein: a thin line that runs out from behind each knob and across to the next, so you can see that the knobs are one thing. Nothing else on the body is pale, so nothing on it can be taken for a second landing. (2.2 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 5The cloud at its foot. A collar of painted cloud 11 m wide and 5 m tall, 2 m back, across the lower end of the body. The body sinks into it and the blade's point hangs in front of it. It takes the place of the flat grey card that stood in front of the climb here (note 92). (1.9 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 6Two wind socks. Note 89. The middle wind has no rest in it, so its flag is a canvas sock on a timber arm from the body's west side, at 895 m, 2.2 m back and more than a metre clear of the knobs. It streams west. The top wind's flag is a sock on a short stub at the back of the blade's top. It streams east. The socks are plain grey canvas. They hang slack between gusts and lift 0.8 seconds before one. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 7The line pole's foot. The black pole on the top stands in a timber cradle bolted through an iron pug plate, and a square timber brace rakes back from it to the body. The pole belongs to Banderas, above. From the blade you see that it is built, and that it is not rock. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The torn line and its spar. Hemp rope with the town's paper along all 4.3 m of it, hung from the spar under the prow's tip. The paper is the lowest wind's flag at the very place you swing.
  • 9The landing scar. The 2.5 m of the blade's west side that holds: pale warm grey slate, dry and matte, broken in small square steps, with a crisp edge all round where the flake came away. Everything else on the blade is blue-black and glossy with rain running down it in thin white lines.
  • 10The quartz knobs. Eight knots of white quartz, each 0.5 m across and 0.3 m proud of the slate, dry and matte. Round each one the slate is dry for a hand's width, so a knob never looks like a wet glint.
  • 11The top. The slab's flat head, 2.8 m wide: dry pale slate in square steps, the rest at the end of the climb. The top wind blows across it.
  • 12Three winds, drawn. Note 89. Each wind is a stripe of long wavy white lines with an inked edge, exactly as tall as its band and as wide as the screen, moving the way it blows. Here that is three stripes, one above the other, going east, west and east. The rain leans the same way in each. Where the lines stop, the wind stops.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A lip of drifted snow. Along the front of the first ledge, under 0.3 m tall, in front of the ledge's own snow. It is the only thing on the whole ridge that stands in front of the climb, and it covers nothing above your ankles. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Wind lines in three stripes, each with its front and its whistle 0.8 seconds before the gust.
  • Rain in three stripes: leaning east, leaning west, leaning east, and falling straight in a band that is resting.
  • Rain running down the blade in thin white lines. It runs round each knob and leaves it dry.
  • The torn line's paper streams east in a gust. You swing with it or against it.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 89: three winds cross this climb, and each is drawn across its whole band with its own flag: the torn line's paper and the cabin's smoke, a sock on the body, a sock on the top.
  • Note 92: the flat grey cloud card in front of the blade's foot goes. A painted collar stands 2 m behind it.
  • The tower's root card, which ends in the air at 880 m today, becomes the body, and the body ends inside the collar.
  • The small slick patches that once stood on the rest ledges were taken out on 11-12. This design puts nothing on a rest that you can touch.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
