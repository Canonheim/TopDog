La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.4: Veleta

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The station's wind vane, as big as a house. The cableway runs on wind, so the town built its biggest wind engine here to count it: three arms of greased black steel, 4.7 m long, with a cup on the end of each, painted the cableway's red with a cream band. It turns on a banded mast that stands on the shoulder of the station tower's body, and a box under its hub still counts every turn on a dial. It has not stopped since the keeper left: once round in 5 seconds. The top of the lightning mast, the nest, was his stage for it, and a cup comes up past the nest's east end every 1.7 seconds. East of the vane there is 15 m of air, and then La Torre, the tower where he flew his kites. The line he crossed on is gone. So you take a cup as it rises and let it throw you. The wind in this band blows west, against the throw, so you go when it rests. The camera pulls back here to show the whole throw and the fall under it.

What must not change

  • The vane: its hub, its three arms, its three cups and its speed. Only the cups are held.
  • The nest you leave from, and La Torre's scar and shoulder that catch you.
  • The wind from 926 to 934 m, which blows west, against the throw.
  • The needle's slick base above the shoulder, and the open air under the throw.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. Two stand in the gap you are thrown across, far back, and give the gap its size. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2A cloud bank. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It lies under the throw, between the vane and La Torre. The fall is seen against it, and the face and El Alero show to its west. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3The vane's mast and its counting box. The kit's banded mast, 13.5 m tall, 2.5 m back, standing on the shoulder of the station tower's body. It is banded like the cableway's masts, but dulled and greyed, because it is scenery. An axle 2.5 m long runs back from the hub to the mast's head, so the wheel turns out in front of its mast, as the cableway's wind engines do. Under the head is a timber box with a white dial on it, 0.6 m across, and a hand that goes round with the vane. Two socks lower on the mast belong to the wind below. (2.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 4The head sock. Note 89. This wind's flag at the place you leave from: a grey canvas sock on a short staff on the mast's head, behind the hub. It streams west, toward you on the nest, when the wind is against the throw, and hangs slack when it is safe to go. (2.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5The nest's stage. Behind the nest: the plank stage the keeper stood on to tend the vane, 1.2 m deep, with a burnt hole in it. It is on El Rayo's page. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6La Torre's body, and the cloud at its foot. La Torre is the near edge of a body of slate 9 m wide, 3 m back. The body rises behind the shoulder as its back wall, and it goes down past the block's slick point into a collar of cloud 14 m wide. Nothing on the body is pale: the scar is the only pale thing on the tower's west side, so the place to land cannot be mistaken. (1.9 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 7The kite stand. On the wall behind the shoulder: the lowest of the kites' three reels, and this wind's flag at the place you land, a sock on a stub at the back of the shoulder. It streams west. The reels are on El Papalote's page. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The cups. Three deep cups, each drawn at exactly the size that is held: 0.8 m by 0.7 m. They are red with a cream band, dry and matte. That is the cableway's mark for a hold on a thing that moves, and you have known it since the cableway.
  • 9The arms and the hub. The three arms are black greased steel, glossy, 0.3 m thick, like the line pole on El Filo. They cannot be held, and they are exactly as long and as thick as what is solid. The hub is an iron boss 1.2 m across with the pug's face on it, greased black as well. The kit's vane has four arms and the course has three: the model follows the course.
  • 10The scar and the shoulder. What catches you: 3.9 m of La Torre's west side where a flake came away, pale warm grey, dry and stepped, and above it the shoulder's flat dry top, 3.5 m wide. Below the scar and above the shoulder the tower is blue-black and glossy.
  • 11The winds, drawn. Note 89. The vane's wind is one stripe from 926 to 934 m, as wide as the pulled-back screen, in long wavy white lines moving west. You are thrown into those lines. Between gusts the stripe is empty, and that is when to go. The mast's wind shows below it going east, and the kites' wind above it going east.

Small effects, no models

  • The arms hum as they pass, and each cup whistles. You hear the rhythm before you see it.
  • The dial's hand goes round once for each turn of the vane.
  • Rain is thrown off each cup in a short arc as it comes over the top.
  • The vane does not change speed in a gust. The sock and the lines tell you about the wind. The vane tells you only when a cup is coming.
