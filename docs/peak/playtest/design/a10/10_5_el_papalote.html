La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.5: El Papalote

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

La Torre's shoulder was the keeper's kite stand. To measure the air higher than his mast he flew three kites from here, one above the other, each on its own reel, and the reels are still bolted to the rock wall behind the shoulder. He cut the kites from the town's paper, with the pug's face in each, and gave each one a tail of the same cut paper, 6 m long. A tail is a prayer line that flies. The storm has kept all three in the air ever since. Each flies the same figure of eight, over and over, beside the needle that stands on the tower's east half. You go up them tail to tail. The top one swings its tail to within a reach of a pale scar on the needle, and two quartz knobs lead from the scar to the needle's flat top. The wind here blows east, toward the needle.

What must not change

  • The three kites, their figures of eight, their speed, and their 6 m tails.
  • The needle's scar, its two knobs and its top, and the post on the top.
  • The wind from 934 to 943.2 m, which blows east.
  • The shoulder you start from. The first kite's tail sweeps it.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. One shows past the needle, to the east. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2A cloud bank. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It stands behind the west half of the kites' flight. The kites are the brightest things in the picture, against grey. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3La Torre's body. The body behind the shoulder and the needle, 9 m wide, 3 m back. It is the shoulder's back wall. It stops in a saw-toothed top at about 941 m, 2.5 m below the needle's top, with one sharp spike on its west corner, so it cannot be taken for a way up. The quartz vein of the needle's two knobs is painted on it in dull white. The kites fly half in front of this wall and half in front of cloud. (2.2 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 4The three reels. Three timber reels, each 0.8 m across, bolted to the west edge of the body on iron pug plates, one above the other. Each reel is directly behind the middle of its kite's figure of eight. A kite's string is a dark thread no thicker than a finger. It runs from the kite's back to its reel, 2.4 m behind, so from the front it is a short dark stroke behind the kite, and nothing hangs under a kite but its tail. Each reel turns a little, back and forth, as its kite flies. There is no post or pole here: nothing upright stands behind a hanging tail. (2.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The shoulder's sock. The vane's wind, below 934 m, blows west across the shoulder. Its flag is a grey canvas sock on a stub at the back of the shoulder. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 6The first lone tower's body, and its cloud. At the west edge of the frame: the body of the first of the two lone towers, and the collar of cloud across its lower end. The west turns of the kites pass in front of the collar's east end. (1.9 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 7Two torn ends. On the top of the needle's post and on the first lone tower's post: the torn end of the keeper's high line, 1 m of hemp and paper. Both stream west. They belong to the wind above, and they show the way on. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The kites. Three paper diamonds, each drawn at exactly the size that is held: 1.2 m by 1.44 m. They are cut paper in the town's colours on a split cane cross, with the pug's face cut out of the middle, dry and matte. The kit's kite is 2.5 m across and is re-made at this size.
  • 9The tails. Each tail is 6 m of hemp cord with cut paper along all of it, the same as a prayer line, and it is a hold from end to end. It hangs from the kite's lowest point. The tails are this wind's flags: in a gust all three lean east together, toward the needle.
  • 10The needle's scar and knobs. On the needle's west side: 3 m of pale dry slate where a flake came away, and above it two knobs of white quartz. The rest of the needle is blue-black and glossy from the shoulder to the top.
  • 11The needle's top and its post. The needle's flat dry top, 2.5 m wide, with a line post on its west corner: a square honey timber 2.5 m tall and 0.3 m thick, pegged, on an iron pug plate. The post is the first of the keeper's high line and the start of Gendarmes.
  • 12The winds, drawn. Note 89. The kites' wind is one stripe from 934 to 943.2 m, in long wavy white lines moving east, across the kites and on to the needle. The vane's wind shows below it going west, and the wind of the first flights above it going west.

Small effects, no models

  • Each kite's paper rattles, and louder as it turns at the ends of its figure of eight.
  • The tails lift 0.8 seconds before a gust and lean east in it.
  • Rain leaning east in the kites' band.

  • Note 89: the kites' wind is drawn across its whole band, and the three tails are its flags.
