La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.6: Gendarmes

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The keeper's high line. From the needle on La Torre he strung one long line west, to the buttress that leans on the ridge, where the balloon's winch and the foot of the summit ladder are. It crossed 40 m of air in three spans, and to carry it he stood a square timber post on the needle and on each of the two lone towers between. The towers are thin: the top of each is 1.6 m wide. All three spans have snapped. What is left is the posts, each with a torn end of line streaming from its top, and the pale scars on the towers where flakes have come away. So you go the way the line went, through the air: from a post's top to the next tower, 10 m, then 12 m, then 14 m. Two winds meet here. East of the gap between the lone towers the wind blows west, and west of it the wind blows east. The camera pulls far back, to about 43 m across, to show a whole flight and the fall under it.

What must not change

  • The three posts, and the three flights from their tops: 10 m, 12 m and 14 m.
  • The landings: each lone tower's top and the scar on its east side, and the buttress's 1.5 m scar over its 1 m foothold.
  • The two winds from 943.2 to 953 m: west on the east side, east on the west side, meeting at x -305.
  • The towers' slick shafts and points. A body that misses passes under and behind every one of them.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. With the camera pulled back this is where the card shows best: a row of blades and needles in every gap, the same shape as the three you are on, with a few torn lines painted between them. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2Three cloud banks. Painted banks 7 m back, one in each gap, with their heads just under the line of the flights. Each flight is seen against open storm, and the fall under it against cloud. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3The ridge's flank. Under the buttress, the mountain behind the open air east of the face: leaning ribs of slate, 3 m back. The buttress is the only tower that leans on it. (3.0 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 4La Nube's cloud and rib. East of the buttress, the cloud that sits on its shoulder: one bank 1.5 m back, with the rib of dark rock and its five knobs standing in front of it. It is designed on La Nube's page. The third flight ends against it, so the buttress's pale scar is seen against plain cloud. (1.2 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 5The towers' bodies. Behind each lone tower, its body: the same slate, 3.6 m wide, 3 m back, duller and greyer, stopping just under the tower's top. La Torre's body stands behind the needle at the east edge. (2.2 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 6Two cloud collars. Across the lower end of each lone tower's body, 2 m back: a collar of painted cloud, 9.5 m and 10 m wide. Each tower's slick point hangs in front of its collar. They take the place of the two flat grey cards that stood in front of the towers' feet (note 92). (1.9 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 7Three torn ends. Note 89. The three whole paper lines that hang behind the flights today become what the story says they are: a torn end on each post's top, 1 m of hemp and paper. They are the two winds' flags. The needle's and the first tower's stream west. The second tower's streams east. So the two on the lone towers point at each other across the gap where the winds meet. (1.3 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 8Braces. Each post is held by a square timber brace that rakes back from it to the tower's body, and by an iron pug plate at its foot. A post that carried 40 m of line needed both. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The three posts. Square honey timber, 2.5 m tall and 0.3 m thick, pegged, with a split top. The top is where you launch from. The torn end streams from just behind it.
  • 10The landings. Each lone tower has a flat dry top and a pale scar on its east side: 2 m tall on the first, 1.5 m on the second. The buttress has one scar, 1.5 m tall, over a foothold 1 m wide. Each is pale warm grey, dry and stepped, with a crisp edge, on a tower that is otherwise blue-black and glossy. Seen from the post before, each landing is the only pale thing on its tower.
  • 11Two winds that meet, drawn. Note 89. From 943.2 to 953 m there are two stripes side by side. East of x -305 the long wavy white lines move west. West of it they move east. Where they meet, between the two lone towers, the lines of both curl upward and die. You can see the join from the first post. The first flight goes with its wind. The second starts with one and ends against the other. The third is against its wind all the way. The kites' wind shows below, going east.

Small effects, no models

  • The torn ends lift 0.8 seconds before a gust. The two on the lone towers lift at different times, because the two winds are out of step.
  • Rain leaning west on the east side and east on the west side, with a seam of straight rain where they meet.
  • The far cloud flickers pale now and then, small and silent.
  • The lightning's bolt, when it comes, is behind these towers and behind you. It does not cross the flights.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 89: both winds are drawn across their whole reach, their meeting is drawn, and each post carries a flag.
  • Note 92: the two flat grey cloud cards in front of the lone towers' feet go. Painted collars stand 2 m behind them, and the towers' root cards, which end in the air today, become bodies that end inside the collars.
  • Notes 4 and 18: the three whole paper lines behind the flights go. A whole prayer line is always a hold, and these were not. They become torn ends on the posts, as the sheet has it.
  • Course changes, scenery only: three paper lines removed. The three torn ends move back 1 m, from 0.3 m behind you to 1.3 m behind.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
