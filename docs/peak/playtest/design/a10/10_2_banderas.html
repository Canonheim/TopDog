La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Challenge 10.2: Banderas

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The keeper's rope way from El Filo to his station. The station's deck is 11 m higher than El Filo's top and 12 m to the west, too steep for one rope. So he strung it in three laps, like a path up a hillside: from a pole on El Filo out to the spire called La Aguja, back to the pole higher up, and out again over the spire to the corner of his deck. The ropes' upper ends ride on iron rings that he hauled up the pole after every storm, and he kept the pole black with grease so the rings would run and ice would not hold on it. That is why the pole cannot be climbed. The town tied its cut paper along all three ropes so he could find them in cloud, and it has gone on doing so: each paper now is a prayer for somebody who went up. One wind blows along the whole rope way, toward the station. It is with you on the first lap and the third, and against you on the second.

What must not change

  • The three lines, their ends and their slack, and the two reaches between them: 1.6 m at the spire and 1.7 m at the pole.
  • The pole and the spire. Neither can be held.
  • The wind from 904 to 914 m, which blows west.
  • The station deck's east corner, where the third line ends.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The ridge's far towers. The kit's one painted card, 14 m back: the ridge's other towers going away along the crest, with their feet in painted cloud and the back ones hazed to the storm's grey. Two show east of the pole, with a torn line painted between them. (14.0 to 14.2 m behind you)
  • 2A cloud bank. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It lies behind the lower two lines, between El Filo and the spire, so the brightest paper on the mountain hangs against plain grey. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 3The ridge's flank. The mountain behind the open air just east of the face: leaning ribs and gullies of slate, dull blue-grey, 3 m back. The face is its near edge. (3.0 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 4La Aguja's body, and the cloud at its foot. The spire's body, 3.6 m wide and 3 m back, sinks into a collar of cloud 8 m wide. The spire's point hangs in front of the collar. (1.9 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 5The station tower's body, and the cloud at its foot. The station's tower is the near edge of a bigger body of rock, 12 m wide at the top, 3 m back. Its top is a shoulder that reaches 6 m east of the deck, and the vane's mast stands on that shoulder. The shoulder's top is a row of leaning slab ends, saw-toothed, wet and dark. It is never level for more than half a metre, so it cannot be taken for a floor beside the deck. The body's lower end sinks into a collar of cloud 12.5 m wide, and La Aguja's spire stands up in front of that collar. (1.9 to 3.8 m behind you)
  • 6The foot of the vane's mast. The vane's banded mast stands in a timber shoe in a notch of the shoulder, bolted through iron pug plates. Today it stands on nothing. It is 2.5 m back, and the third line passes in front of it. (2.3 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 7The station. The burnt hut behind the deck, where the third line ends. It is on El Rayo's page. (1.4 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 8The pole's cradle, brace and torn end. The pole's foot is in a timber cradle on El Filo's top, and a square timber brace rakes back from the pole to El Filo's body. From the pole's top a torn end of an older line streams west, 1 m long. It is the flag at the top of the rope way. It moves from 0.3 m behind you to 1.3 m behind, out of the play space. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9Six iron rings. Each line ends in an iron ring on a square plate with the pug's face. There are three on the pole, one above the other, two on the spire, and one on a bollard at the corner of the station's deck. They are small and dark. They show that the lines are made fast, and where one lap ends and the next begins.
  • 10The paper. Cut paper in the town's colours at their loudest, hung along the whole of each rope, 0.9 m deep. A whole prayer line is always a hold. The papers are the wind's flags here: they hang slack between gusts, lift 0.8 seconds before one, and stream west in it, toward the station.
  • 11The pole. Black greased steel, 7.7 m tall and as thick as a wrist, glossy, with rain beading on it. It is the first black greased thing on the ridge. Everything black and greased from here up cannot be held.
  • 12The wind, drawn. Note 89. One stripe of long wavy white lines from 904 to 914 m, moving west along the rope way. The top wind of El Filo shows under it, going east, and the mast's wind shows over it, going east.

Small effects, no models

  • The papers snap in a gust, and you hear them.
  • Each rope sags and swings west in a gust. The paper shows it before you feel it.
  • Rain leaning west across the whole band, and straight when the band rests.
