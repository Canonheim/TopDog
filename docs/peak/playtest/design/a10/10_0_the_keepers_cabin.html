La Cresta de la Tormenta · Area and background design

Rest 10.0: The keeper's cabin

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The one sheltered place on the ridge. The weather station's keeper had to live somewhere, and the only dry spot above the glacier was the ledge under El Alero, where the prow keeps the rain off. He built a cabin there the cableway's way: square honey posts, thick planks, a roof of slate slabs pitched the way the prow pitches, with its back against the rock. He left the front open to the ledge, which was his porch, and he went to work from the porch's end along a line to El Filo. Nobody came to relieve him. The line has snapped and hangs from its spar. The stove is still lit, the kettle is on it, his coat is on its peg and his weather book is open on the table. The ridge's frog lives here now. It sits on the table under the lamp, in a small yellow rain hat, with a cup of tea, and watches the storm. The porch is still not a safe place to stand: the lowest wind blows straight along it and off its end.

What must not change

  • The ledge: its length, its height, and its glazed first metre.
  • The lowest wind, which blows east only, along the ledge and off its end.
  • The torn line, a hand past the ledge's east end, and the prow overhead.
  • The cabin is scenery. Nothing in it can be touched, and nothing is added to stand on or hold.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The nook's back wall. The ridge's flank where it passes under the prow: leaning ribs of wet slate, 3.4 m back. It fills the 4 m and more between the cabin's roof and the prow's underside, and it is the darkest thing in the picture, so the cabin's light sits in the middle of it. (3.4 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 2The cabin's shell. A plank floor level with the ledge, a plank back wall, two end walls and a roof, with no front wall. It is 4.3 m wide and 2.2 m deep. The roof is slate slabs on honey rafters, 2.5 m up at the west end and 2.1 m at the east, the same pitch as the prow above. The two front corner posts stand at the back edge of the ledge, with an iron pug plate at each foot. The walls are amber inside, from the stove and the lamp. That is colour on the planks, not a glow. (1.2 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The stove, the kettle and the pipe. A small iron stove at the east end with the keeper's kettle on it: the same kettle as in the drivers' cave on the cliffs and in the glacier's snow hole. Its pipe goes up through the roof and ends in a cowl 1.5 m above it. The smoke is the first wind's flag: it streams east under the prow in a gust and stands up straight between gusts. (1.6 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The table, the weather book, the lamp and the frog. A plank table in the middle of the room with the weather book open on it. The one lamp hangs over it. The frog sits on the table's west end, 0.3 m tall, in a small yellow rain hat, with a cup beside it, facing east toward the open end of the porch and the storm. It turns its head to follow you along the ledge. (1.6 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The bunk and the coat. A bunk along the west end with a folded blanket, and the keeper's oilskin coat on a peg at its foot. The coat is the size of a luchador. The frog's hat is cut from the same yellow cloth. (2.2 to 3.3 m behind you)
  • 6The wind slate. A roofing slate hung on the back wall over the bunk, with the keeper's chalk on it: nine rows of arrows, one above the other, pointing east, west, east, all the way up. In the eighth row there are two arrows that point at each other. It is his table of the ridge's winds, and it is true. A player who stops here can read the whole ridge off it. (3.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 7The prayer pole. At the cabin's west corner, 4 m tall, with the town's cut paper tied along its top. It is the first wind's second flag. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 8Cloud in the gap. A painted bank of cloud, 7 m back, with round heads and a scalloped underside, dark below and paler on top. It stands east of the prow's tip and hides the valley. It is on the join's page. (7.0 to 7.2 m behind you)
  • 9El Filo's cloud, across the gap. The collar of painted cloud that El Filo's body stands in, 2 m back. From the porch, the cloud and the torn line are all you see of the way on. (1.9 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The porch. The ledge's firm snow, white and matte, combed into low ridges that run east. A path one boot wide is trodden flat in it, from the cabin's east post to the ledge's end, where the keeper stepped off onto his line. The glazed first metre stays blue-black and glossy.
  • 11The torn line and its spar. A square honey spar is let into the underside of the prow and runs back to the nook's wall. Its end shows flush in the rock, 0.35 m square, with an iron pug plate, and the torn line is knotted round it. The line is hemp with the town's paper along all 4.3 m of it, wet and faded. Today the paper covers only 2.6 m of the rope.
  • 12The first wind, drawn. Note 89. Long wavy white lines with an inked edge, moving east, across the whole band from 884 to 893 m. They cross the porch at the height of your chest and carry on past its end, so you can see that the wind will take you the way the ledge ends. A front of lines and a whistle come 0.8 seconds before each gust.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13A lip of drifted snow. Along the front of the first ledge, under 0.3 m tall, in front of the ledge's own snow. It is the only thing on the whole ridge that stands in front of the climb, and it covers nothing above your ankles. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The kettle steams. The lamp swings a little in each gust. The frog's cup steams too.
  • The frog croaks when you land on the porch, and you can hear it from the glacier's top (note 60).
  • The paper on the pole and on the torn line lifts 0.8 seconds before each gust and streams east in it.
  • Rain falls off the prow's brink in a thin broken line, 2 m east of the ledge's end. It marks the brink that every fall on the ridge goes over.
  • When lightning takes the mast far above, the whole picture goes pale for an instant, and the room's amber is the only colour that does not change.

  • Note 40: nothing stands in front of the porch but the snow lip, which is under 0.3 m.
  • The note's plan tied the prayer line to the cabin's porch post. Here it stays where the course hangs it, under the prow's tip, on a spar, as the sheet has it.
  • Course changes, scenery only: the prayer pole stands 1.4 m further east, at the cabin's west corner, clear of the face. The paper on the torn line runs its whole 4.3 m.
