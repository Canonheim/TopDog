El Teleférico · Area and background design

Challenge 8.11: La Estación Alta

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The high station, at 786 m: the top of the cableway and the last made floor on the mountain. It is the same station as the one below, a board house under a striped tin roof, with the pug's sign, a ticket window and a deck of honey planks 14 m long. Then one winter the glacier came down over it. The ice lies on its roof and has buried its west half. It has filled the docks under the deck, where the two lower express lines came in, and it has put out a tongue across the deck itself. The wind engine on its roof is frozen still and white with frost. The fourth express car still stops at the deck's east end for a second and a half. You step off onto ice: the first ice on the climb, which holds you for two seconds and then lets go.

What must not change

  • The deck: 14 m at 786.0 m, against the face. Three sheets of ice, 3.0 m, 3.8 m and 3.0 m long, and three dry patches, 1.6 m, 1.2 m and 1.4 m.
  • Ice holds for 2 seconds, lets go, and takes you again 0.6 seconds later.
  • The fourth car stops for 1.5 seconds with the tip of its step at the deck's east edge, 0.15 m above it.
  • Black ice under the deck's east end and up its east edge, so the deck is only taken from above, off the car.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The swallowed docks. Under the deck the station had two more docks, for the lower pair of express lines. The glacier has filled them: a mass of blue-black ice, glossy, from the face out to the deck's east end, 1.3 m and more behind you. The two lower cables run straight into it through frost-rimmed holes they have sawn for themselves. The black ice under the deck's east end in the play space is the near edge of this. (1.3 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 2The deck's brackets. Two lattice brackets from under the deck back to the face, half sunk in the ice. The deck's strut is a card 0.7 m back today, inside the play space. It goes to 2.5 m. (2.5 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 3The station house, half buried. The same house as at the low station, 14 m wide and 6 m tall, standing 2.5 m back, with no roof and no post over the deck (note 77). Board walls, red below cream. Dark windows, the pug sign on the wall, a cold lantern by the ticket window. Snow is drifted against its west half up to the eaves, and the glacier's lip lies on its roof. Today its peaked roof is a card 1 m back, in the play space and inside the glacier's lip. It goes. (2.5 to 5.5 m behind you)
  • 4The express cables coming in. The four cables, 2 m behind you. The upper pair comes in over the deck to the bull wheel on the front of the house. The lower pair goes under the deck into the ice. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 5The upper pair's bull wheel. Lines 3 and 4 are one loop of cable, and it turns back here over a greyed bull wheel 4.4 m across on the front of the house, at its east end, 2 m back. It still turns, one way and then the other, as the cars come and go, and a little frost falls from it each time. (2.0 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 6The frozen wind engine. On the roof's east end, 3 m back: the same cup wheel as on the towers below, stopped, white with frost, with snow in its cups. It is the only engine on the cableway that does not turn. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 7The car's hanger arm. Scenery: greyed, behind you, no longer solid. (1.2 to 2.1 m behind you)
  • 8The last pennants, frozen stiff. A last line of pennants, 10 m long, from the end of the express lines' pennants to the frozen engine's mast. It is white with frost and does not move. It still points east. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The tongue of ice. Pale blue ice, frosted and matte, lying over the planks in three sheets with a white frost edge. It is the glacier's own ice and it looks the way every hold that lets go will look in the next biome. Between the sheets the planks show.
  • 10Three dry patches. Bare honey planks with boot scuffs: the only places on the deck that hold you for good. The course gives two of them the rock's colour today. All three are plank.
  • 11The fourth car's step. 0.6 m of iron with a cream band, drawn at the length it is (note 77). It stops 0.15 m above the deck's east edge.
  • 12A frozen glove. On the ice near the deck's west end: one glove, frozen to the planks. Somebody held on here for two seconds. It is 0.6 m back today and stays there.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13One platform post. A square station post in the cableway's red, 0.3 m wide, in front of the deck's west end, where the planks meet the rock. It matches the scaffold post in front of the low deck's west end. It covers a strip 0.3 m wide, at a place where nothing has to be read. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)
  • 14A lip of drifted snow. Along the deck's front edge, under 0.3 m tall, so it hides no more than the soles of your boots. It stops 3.5 m short of the east end, where you come off the car. (1.2 to 1.7 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The first snowflakes, falling straight. The course has them today.
  • The deck is in the mountain's blue shadow. Behind you the towers are still in gold light.
  • The mark on the limb that holds the ice shivers harder and harder until it lets go: the glacier's tell, first seen here.
  • Frost falls from the bull wheel each time it turns back.
