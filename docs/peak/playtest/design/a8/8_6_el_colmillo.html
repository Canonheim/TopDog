Challenge 8.6: El Colmillo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

El Colmillo is a fang of the cliffs' own rock standing by itself in the open air, 14 m across at this height, dark and polished by rain. The cableway never built on it. It only tied itself to it: the chair line is anchored in its west side and the zip line to its top. The linesmen who kept those anchors went up it the way you do, on the white chalk knobs that stick out of the dark rock: up the overhang, round the nose at 715 m, and up the slab to the iron plate on top. From the foot ledge to the plate it is 22 m, with no place to stand on the way.

What must not change

  • The rock's outline: the overhang, the nose at (-176, 715), the slab, the top.
  • The thirteen knobs, each where it is, about 1.8 m apart, and the rock slick everywhere else.
  • The foot ledge and the rib at the bottom. The anchor plate and the post on top.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The far line's last tower. 20 m back and hazed, in the open air east of the fang: the third tower of the dead second line, with its slack cable and one stopped car high up on the west. (20.0 to 20.5 m behind you)
  • 2The fang's far horn. The fang is thicker than the slice of it you climb. Its far horn stands 1.5 to 4 m back: the same dark rock, a step duller and greyer, rising 2 to 3 m above the slab and up to 2 m wider on the east. It never shows west of the overhang, so the overhang keeps a clean edge against the sky. It is why the fang reads as a rock and not as a card. (1.5 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 3The chair line's anchor. On the fang's west side, 2 m back: the iron arm and guide wheel where the chair line's cable is made fast, with the fourth chair's pole hanging from the cable beside it. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 4The top's fittings. Behind the plate: the post's back stay, a thick iron rod down to an eye bolt in the horn; a linesman's box and a cold lantern; and a streamer pole on the horn's tip, pointing east. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The zip line, leaving. The zip line goes out west from the back of the post, 2 m behind you. It belongs to challenge 8.7. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The chalk knobs. Thirteen white chalk knobs, each 0.4 m across and modelled at the size of its grip, standing 0.1 m out of the dark rock. White and matte against black and glossy: they are the only holds on the fang, and nothing else on it is pale. Four up the overhang, one on the nose, one over it, seven up the slab.
  • 7The foot ledge and the rib. White chalk: the ledge is the lip of a level bed of chalk that runs through the fang, and the rib is the same bed's broken edge, turned up. The fourth chair sets you down here.
  • 8Rain lines on the dark rock. The rock is rain-polished and glossy, with thin white drip lines running down the overhang and the east side. No ledge, no crack and no pale patch that is not a knob.
  • 9The anchor plate and the post. An iron plate 2.6 m long on the fang's top, and the zip line's post, 2.7 m tall, at its east end. Rust-orange iron, dry: both are held.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The fang's top catches the last sun: the plate and the post are in gold light while the overhang is in blue shadow. The course has this light today.
  • A little chalk dust from a knob as a hand takes it.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • No playtest note changes this challenge. It plays exactly as the course has it.
  • Notes 76 and 77, the rule they made: each knob is modelled at the size of its grip, 0.4 m, so nothing can be held that is not drawn.
  • Scenery that moves out of the play space: the chair line's wire, which ends on the fang 0.5 m back, becomes a cable 2 m back on an anchor arm.
  • This is the plainest area of the biome: one rock, its knobs and two anchors. The sheet asks for nothing more on the fang.
