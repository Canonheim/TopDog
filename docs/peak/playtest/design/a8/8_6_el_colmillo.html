Challenge 8.6: El Colmillo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

El Colmillo is a fang of the cliffs' own rock standing by itself in the open air, 14 m across at this height, dark and polished by rain and wind. It is chalk in level beds, like the cliffs below: where a bed ends at the face it stands out white and dry, and everywhere else the face is dark brown and glossy. The cableway never built on it. It only tied itself to it: the chair line is anchored in its west side and the zip line to its top. The linesmen who kept those anchors went up it the way you do, on the white ends of its beds: up the overhang, round the nose at 715 m, and up the slab to the iron plate on top. From the foot ledge to the plate it is 22 m, with no place to stand on the way.

What must not change

  • The rock's outline on the climb: the overhang, the nose at (-176, 715), the slab, the top. The east side, which holds nothing, may lean by up to 0.8 m.
  • The thirteen chalk holds, each where it is, about 1.8 m apart, and the rock slick everywhere else.
  • The foot ledge and the rib at the bottom. The anchor plate and the post on top.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The far line's last tower. 20 m back and hazed, in the open air east of the fang: the third tower of the dead second line, with its slack cable and one stopped car high up on the west. (20.0 to 20.5 m behind you)
  • 2The fang's far horn. The fang's own body, carrying on behind the slice of it you climb: one big form in two or three slanted planes, 1.5 to 4 m back, the same rock a step duller, greyer and cooler. Its beds are painted on it as thin wavy lines that carry on from the face's, and its skyline is uneven, rising 2 to 3 m above the slab and up to 2 m wider on the east. It never shows west of the overhang, so the overhang keeps a clean edge against the sky. It is why the fang reads as a rock and not as a card. (1.5 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 3The chair line's anchor. On the fang's west side, 2 m back: the iron arm and guide wheel where the chair line's cable is made fast, with the fourth chair's pole hanging from the cable beside it. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 4The top's fittings. Behind the plate: the post's back stay, a thick iron rod down to an eye bolt in the horn; a linesman's box and a cold lantern; and a streamer pole on the horn's tip, pointing east. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The zip line, leaving. The zip line goes out west from the back of the post, 2 m behind you. It belongs to challenge 8.7. (2.0 to 2.1 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The chalk holds. Thirteen white chalk blocks, each 0.4 m across and modelled at the size of its grip, standing 0.1 m proud of the face. Each is a plain block with square ends: the end of one of the rock's beds, where the bed meets the face. White and matte against dark and glossy, they are the only holds on the fang, and nothing else on it is white. Four up the overhang, one on the nose, one over it, seven up the slab.
  • 7The foot ledge and the rib. White chalk, as plain bands with square ends: the ledge is the lip of one bed, standing out 1.3 m, and the rib is the same bed's broken end, turned up. The ledge is the face's one real shelf. The fourth chair sets you down here.
  • 8The beds, painted. The face is a few large slanted planes of dark brown, and the rock's beds are paint on them: long, a little wavy lines and bands in three or four browns, near level, running from each hold east across the whole face. Each hold is where its bed ends at the face, so the holds and the beds read as one rock. There is no ink inside the face, no step of shape for a bed, and nothing painted narrows to a point: no rain streaks and no stains.
  • 9The east side's outline, and its three breaks. Away from the holds the outline is designed. The east side leans out and back by up to 0.8 m and is notched once, at 706 m, where a block has broken away. The fang's only real shapes are the overhang, with its own shadow painted a band darker under the nose, the foot ledge, and that notch. Nothing is cut into the face.
  • 10The anchor plate and the post. An iron plate 2.6 m long on the fang's top, and the zip line's post, 2.7 m tall, at its east end. Rust-orange iron, dry: both are held.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The fang's top catches the last sun: the plate and the post are in gold light while the overhang is in blue shadow. The course has this light today.
  • A little chalk dust from a hold as a hand takes it.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • No playtest note changes this challenge. It plays exactly as the course has it.
  • The walls rule (Parker, 11-13: the painted look, with lines and bands only): the fang is one painted piece made for its place, in place of the course's plain rock. Its beds are long wavy lines and bands. The rain drip lines an earlier pass drew down the face are taken out: nothing painted tapers to a point. The holds are plain blocks with square ends.
  • What the rule changes in the course: nothing you hold or stand on moves. The east side, which carries no hold, may lean out or back by up to 0.8 m, and is notched once, 0.9 m deep and 1.7 m tall, from 705.6 to 707.3 m. Its four corners today, (-158, 684), (-159, 698), (-161.5, 712), (-164.4, 725), become the uneven line drawn.
  • Notes 76 and 77, the rule they made: each chalk hold is modelled at the size of its grip, 0.4 m, so nothing can be held that is not drawn.
  • Scenery that moves out of the play space: the chair line's wire, which ends on the fang 0.5 m back, becomes a cable 2 m back on an anchor arm.
  • This is the plainest area of the biome: one rock, its holds and two anchors. The sheet asks for nothing more on the fang.
