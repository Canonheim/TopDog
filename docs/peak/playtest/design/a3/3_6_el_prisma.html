La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.6: El Prisma

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The Prism is the long clear crystal that lies under this stair: six-sided, 10 m long, on its side at La Rampa's own angle. A bright crystal behind it shines through it, and the light comes out split into colours, the way glass splits light. Above it the crack in the back wall climbs steeply, and out of the crack have grown eight thin leaves of crystal, like slates sticking out of a wall, in two rows. They are your steps. They are old and full of cracks, and each one snaps off 1.3 seconds after you take it and rings as it goes: eight leaves, eight notes, one scale (note 31). If you fall, the Prism's top face slides you west into a crystal sled. The sled is temple work. It sinks away under your weight along a slot in the wall and leaves you below the first needle, with the needles and the leaves to do again.

What must not change

  • Eight leaves up a 12 m diagonal, each where it is, each gone 1.3 seconds after it is taken and back 8 seconds later, each ringing its own note in order.
  • The glass face under them that pours a faller west, and the sled that catches him and sinks 24 m west to its berth, then comes home empty.
  • The rest at each end. The upper rest's east lip is glass.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack, and the leaves' far halves. The crack climbs the wall here at 58 degrees with its rib of dark rock. Each leaf you step on is the last half metre of a plate 3 m long and 0.26 m thick that grew out of the crack edge-on. The plates stand in two rows, one each side of the crack, which is why the steps go left, right, left, right. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The Prism. One clear six-sided crystal, 10 m long and 2.6 m thick, lying along the wall on its side, with a point at its upper end. The glass plate that catches a faller today is its top face. It is rooted in the wall along its whole back. (1.2 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 3The colours on the wall. Light goes through the Prism and comes out as a fan of faint colours up the back wall behind the leaves, red lowest and violet highest, roughly one band behind each leaf. They are the only colours in the cave that are not violet or white. They are dim, they are on the wall and never on a hold, and they do not move. (3.0 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 4The bright crystal behind the Prism. The one bright white cluster of this area, on the wall under the Prism's lower side and behind it. You see it through the Prism as a blur of white. It is what throws the colours. (4.0 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 5The sled's slot, its wheel and its weight. The groove the sled rides in starts here and runs west out of sight, shod with bronze. At its head is a stone wheel on a carved boss. A bronze chain runs from the sled over the wheel to a stone weight, and chain and weight both sit in a second groove cut straight down the wall, so nothing hangs free. The weight is what brings the sled home when it is empty. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 6An offering niche by the sled. A carved hollow with a small crystal and a jade ring, in the wall above the sled's home. One stands beside every piece of temple work in the cave. (2.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 7The roots of the two rests. The rest at each end is the end of a fat pale crystal with its fan of points on the rib, as everywhere on the route. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 8A mirror face, and a giant behind it. East of the leaves, a clear face on the back wall that shows the stair from the side: you can watch the leaves break behind you in it. Below it, the point of one far giant. Both are there today. (4.3 to 8.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The eight leaves. As the game has them since note 31: thin plates of pale crystal, a little see-through, crazed all over with fine white cracks, each with a corner already gone. The missing corner swaps sides from leaf to leaf. They are the cave's one picture of a thing that will break.
  • 10The Prism's top face. Clear indigo glass with a white glint, and long pale scratches down it from everything that has slid here. The scratches all run west, towards the sled, so the face tells you where it will send you.
  • 11The sled. A tray cut from one flat crystal, frosted on top, with a tall back of rough crystal bound by two bronze bands and a low kerb at its open end. A stone arm at its back reaches into the slot. It hangs level, an arm's length under the Prism's low end.
  • 12The two wet lips. A drip from the roof falls on the lower rest beside the last needle, and the east lip of the upper rest is glass. Neither rest is quite safe.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
