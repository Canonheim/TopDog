La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.5: Agujas

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The widest gap in the cave. Past the geode the back wall falls away into a bay so deep that it is dark, and the only things between you and the far side are four needles of crystal: 7, 9 and 11 m apart, each a little higher than the last. The first stands on the rest you are on and the last on the rest you are going to. The two in the middle have nothing under them. Each of those is one crystal crossed with another: the upright one you see, pointed at both ends, and a second that runs straight back from its waist into the wall and holds it up. Only a needle's point can be held: the top 1.3 m, rough and pale (note 30). Under the needles there is nothing but La Rampa, 30 to 40 m down, and it carries you back to the foot of the first stair.

What must not change

  • The four needle tips where they are: 7, 9 and 11 m apart, each a little higher than the last.
  • Only the point of each needle can be held, the top 1.3 m. The shafts are glass. The first needle's two sides and the last needle's east side stay frosted, as today.
  • The two rests, and the nub and frosted face under the first rest that are the way back up from the sled's berth.
  • Nothing under the needles but La Rampa.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack, and the rib of rock along it. The same crack as the stair's, higher up the wall now and climbing east under the roof. The rib of dark rock along it is the only part of the back wall that is near you here. Both rests and both middle needles root in it. (1.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The two cross arms. Behind the waist of each middle needle: a second crystal as thick as the needle, running 3 m straight back into the rib, with a small fan of points where it enters the rock. From the front you see its six-sided end as a knot on the needle's waist. It is what holds a needle up in the air. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The roots of the two rests. The rest at each end is the end of a fat pale crystal with its fan of points, as on the stair. The first and last needles are points that have grown up out of those two crystals. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4Three giants in the bay. Far crystals standing on La Rampa, well back, with their points coming up level with your feet between the needles. They are big, blue and dim, and their points lean away from you, so none of them reads as a place to land. Each has a faint light inside. They are there today. (5.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 5The sled's slot. Far below your feet, a straight groove cut in the back wall and shod with bronze, running 24 m west and 4 m down to a stone stop under the first rest. It is temple work: the crystal sled of El Prisma rides in it. From up here it is a thin bright line that tells you people worked this wall. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 6A mirror face between the middle needles. A clear face 5 m wide on the back wall of the bay, below the line of your second fling. It shows you crossing, very small. It is there today. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 7Two bright crystals, one at each end. A bright white cluster on the wall above each rest. The two ends of the crossing are lit and the middle is the dimmest stretch of the route, so the pale points of the needles are the brightest things in it. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The four points. Each needle's point becomes rough pale lilac crystal that looks like rock: frosted, six-sided, a little chipped, lighter towards the tip (note 30). It is the same crystal as the steps of the stair. Same size, same place.
  • 9The shafts. Below its point each needle is clear indigo glass, mirror-smooth, with one white glint down its length. No black: the shaft is plainly the same crystal as the point, but polished where the point is rough. Glass can't be held.
  • 10The frosted sides, and the way back up. The first needle's two sides, the last needle's east side, and the nub and face under the first rest are rough pale lilac like the points. They are the places a hand can stop, and they are the only way back up from the sled's berth.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • La Rampa stays lit far below, so the drop under each fling can be read.
  • A faint ring from a needle when you land on its point, each needle a different note.
  • Slow glitter falling through the bay, to show how much air there is.
