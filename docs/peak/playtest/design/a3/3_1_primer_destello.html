La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.1: Primer Destello

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The foot of the great hall. You come up out of the hole onto a floor of frosted crystal and walk east along it, under a stair of six crystals, to where the floor ends against La Rampa: one face of glass, 35 degrees steep and 60 m high, the side of the biggest crystal in the cave. Everything that falls anywhere in the cave slides down that face and stops here. The stair climbs back west over your head. It follows a crack in the back wall where water once came through. Along the crack six pointed shafts of crystal have grown out of the wall towards you, all leaning the same way, up and to the west, each one higher than the last. You walk up the sloping top of a shaft to its point, hang from the point, and fling to the next. At the west end of the floor one climber stands frozen with an arm up, under the mirror he fell from.

What must not change

  • The stair's line: six crystals climbing west from La Rampa's foot to the rest before the mirror, over the floor you walked in on.
  • The first is taken from the floor. The sixth is the rest before the mirror: the west end of its top stays exactly where it is today, so El Espejo's first reach does not change, and its west end still can't be held.
  • The under-face of every shaft is glass and can't be held.
  • A slip costs the same as today: the floor under the stair.
  • The floor, La Rampa's foot, and the frozen climber where he stands.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack, and the rib of rock along it. A dark split in the back wall, rising west at the stair's own angle, with a ridge of rough dark rock standing 1.5 m out of the wall along it. Water came through here, and every shaft on the stair is rooted in it. Tiny points fringe the crack all the way up. You can read where the stair goes from the crack before you see the shafts. (1.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The shafts' lower halves, and their roots. Each shaft is 4 m long. Its lower half runs back and down from the play space to its root on the rib, 1.4 m east of the part you stand on and 1.4 m lower. Round each root is a fan of three or four smaller points on a collar of rock. The lower halves and the fans are bluer and dimmer than the part you touch, and they are behind the play space, out of reach. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The foot of the mirror crystal. A clear crystal 6 m thick stands on the hall floor behind the top of the stair and leans west and out towards you as it rises. Its top is El Espejo, the next challenge. Down here it is far back, dim and blue. (4.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 4A second giant, further back. Leaning the other way, with a faint light inside it. With the mirror crystal it makes the far wall of the hall. Both are there today. (5.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 5The bright cluster under the stair. The one bright white crystal of this area: a cluster 4 m tall on the back edge of the floor, under the third and fourth shafts. It lights the stair from below, so the glass under-faces glint and the rough tops stay pale. One small dim cluster stands further along the floor's back edge. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 6A mirror face behind the frozen climber. A clear face 4.5 m wide on the back wall behind him. It shows his back, and it shows you, small, as you walk past him. It is the cave's first close look at your own reflection. (3.3 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 7His rope, and the broken glass. A coil of old rope on the floor behind his feet: the only rope in the cave. Round it lie long shards of clear glass, the sheared-off foot of the mirror 14 m overhead, and one stubby pale point: the tip of the sixth shaft, which the falling glass knocked off. He came down with it. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The six shafts. New shapes in the old places (note 26; Parker, 11-13: truly slanted). The dark six-sided pods in the sketch are today's steps, which go. Each of the first five is a six-sided shaft 1.1 m thick, leaning 30 degrees, with its point high and to the west. The part in the play space is 3 m long: a top face 2 m long, then a point 0.9 m long. The top face and the whole point are rough pale lilac. The under-face is clear indigo glass.
  • 9Where you stand and where you hang. You stand on a shaft's sloping top and walk up it. At the top you take the point, which juts out into clear air, drop to hang from it, swing, and let go. The lines are the tops and the small circles are the points. From each point the low end of the next top is 2.2 m across and 1.3 m up. A harder fling goes straight to the next point: 4.9 m across and 2.2 m up. The first top starts 1 m above the floor, at La Rampa's foot.
  • 10The sixth shaft, snapped. The rest before the mirror. It is thicker, 1.4 m, and nearly level: its top rises only 0.8 m in 3.4 m. Its point is gone. The west end is a flat glassy break that can't be held, where the mirror's falling foot took the point off. From the fifth point its low end is 1.5 m across and 1.9 m up.
  • 11The one who tried. The frozen climber, where the course has him: standing on the floor straight under the mirror's foot, one arm reaching up at it. Clear crystal has grown up round him in a shell 3.6 m tall, and you see him through it. The shell is glass: it can't be held.
  • 12A drift of chips at La Rampa's foot. Drawn on the floor where the glass slope meets it, under the first shaft: a low drift of crystal chips and glitter, swept down by everything that has ever fallen in the cave. Nothing to trip on. It marks the place a fall leaves you.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13Two dark points in the bottom corner. Rising from the bottom left corner of the frame, in front of the floor's own thickness, with their tips below the top of the floor. They cover nothing you walk on. They are the cave's only kind of foreground, and most areas above this one have none (note 40). (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A slow glint that travels along the glass under-faces of the shafts as you walk beneath them.
  • A drip now and then from the crack onto the floor, with a small bright ring where it lands.
  • Your reflection, and the frozen climber's back, in the mirror face behind him.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 26, and Parker, 11-13: the six hexagon steps become truly slanted pointed shafts jutting out of the back wall, and the flings are re-laid. Shown here as they will be.
  • Course change: the five flat tops, each 2.2 m wide, become sloping faces 2 m long. The high end of each is at today's west corner, 0.35 m lower. The low end is 1.35 m below today's top. Each shaft adds a point that reaches 1 m further west.
  • Course change: the five flings were 2.7 m across and 2.2 m up, corner to corner. They become 2.2 m across and 1.3 m up, from a point to the low end of the next top, with the rest of each rise walked up the slope. The last, onto the sixth, is 1.5 m across and 1.9 m up.
  • Course change: the sixth step keeps the west end of its top where it is. Its top slopes down 0.8 m to the east, and its body becomes a thick shaft with a broken west end in place of a six-sided pod.
  • Note 27 (done): the floor's flicker was the climber's own lamp, and it is steadied. Nothing here puts two faces in the same place.
  • Note 40 (done): the big dark slabs that stood in front of this floor are behind the climb now. The only foreground kept is the two low points in the corner.
