La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.1: Primer Destello

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The foot of the great hall. You come up out of the hole onto a floor of frosted crystal and walk east along it, under a stair of six crystals, to where the floor ends against La Rampa: one face of glass, 35 degrees steep and 60 m high, the side of the biggest crystal in the cave. Everything that falls anywhere in the cave slides down that face and stops here. The stair climbs back west over your head. It follows a crack in the back wall where water once came through. Along the crack six fat crystals have grown straight out of the wall towards you, each a little higher than the last, and you stand on their ends. At the west end of the floor one climber stands frozen with an arm up, under the mirror he fell from.

What must not change

  • The six places to stand, each exactly where it is and the size it is: five tops 2.2 m wide and the sixth 3 m wide, each with its two sloping shoulders. Each fling stays 2.7 m across and 2.2 m up.
  • The tops and shoulders can be held. The three under-faces of each step can't, and neither can the sixth step's west shoulder.
  • The floor, La Rampa's foot, and the frozen climber where he stands.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack, and the rib of rock along it. A dark split in the back wall, rising west at the stair's own angle, with a ridge of rough dark rock standing 1.5 m out of the wall along it. Water came through here, and every crystal on the stair grew out of it. Tiny points fringe the crack all the way up. You can read where the stair goes from the crack before you see the steps. (1.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The six roots. Behind each step, where it breaks out of the rib: three or four smaller points fanned out round its root, up, down and to the sides, on a collar of rock. They are what makes a step read as a crystal and not a pod (note 26). They are 1.5 m and more behind you, bluer and dimmer than the step, and can't be reached. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The foot of the mirror crystal. A clear crystal 6 m thick stands on the hall floor behind the top of the stair and leans west and out towards you as it rises. Its top is El Espejo, the next challenge. Down here it is far back, dim and blue. (4.0 to 7.0 m behind you)
  • 4A second giant, further back. Leaning the other way, with a faint light inside it. With the mirror crystal it makes the far wall of the hall. Both are there today. (5.0 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 5The bright cluster under the stair. The one bright white crystal of this area: a cluster 5 m tall on the back edge of the floor, under the third and fourth steps. It lights the stair from below, so the glass under-faces of the steps glint and the frosted tops stay pale. Two small dim clusters stand further along the floor's back edge. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 6A mirror face behind the frozen climber. A clear face 4.5 m wide on the back wall behind him. It shows his back, and it shows you, small, as you walk past him. It is the cave's first close look at your own reflection. (3.3 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 7His rope, and the broken glass. A coil of old rope on the floor behind his feet: the only rope in the cave. Round it lie long shards of clear glass, the sheared-off foot of the mirror 14 m overhead. He came down with it. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The six ends. Each step becomes the end of a fat pale crystal that comes out of the wall at you and stops in a low blunt point, seen end-on (note 26). The top face and the two upper faces are rough pale lilac, frosted, and look like rock: they are where you stand and hold. The three under-faces are clear indigo glass. Same places, same sizes.
  • 9The one who tried. The frozen climber, where the course has him: standing on the floor straight under the mirror's foot, one arm reaching up at it. Clear crystal has grown up round him in a shell 3.6 m tall, and you see him through it. The shell is glass: it can't be held.
  • 10A drift of chips at La Rampa's foot. Drawn on the floor where the glass slope meets it: a low drift of crystal chips and glitter, swept down by everything that has ever fallen in the cave. Nothing to trip on. It marks the place a fall leaves you.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11Two dark points in the bottom corner. Rising from the bottom left corner of the frame, in front of the floor's own thickness, with their tips below the top of the floor. They cover nothing you walk on. They are the cave's only kind of foreground, and most areas above this one have none (note 40). (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A slow glint that travels across the under-faces of the steps as you walk beneath them.
  • A drip now and then from the crack onto the floor, with a small bright ring where it lands.
  • Your reflection, and the frozen climber's back, in the mirror face behind him.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 27 (done): the floor's flicker was the climber's own lamp, and it is steadied. Nothing here puts two faces in the same place.
