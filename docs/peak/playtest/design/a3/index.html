Area and background design

Biome 3: La Gruta de Cristal

The crystal cave, 176 to 276 m. Glass and light. The first place on the climb that nobody built.

Design only. This sheet comes first: every area in the biome is drawn from it.

What this place is

Above the dam the mountain is hollow and full of crystal: points the size of houses, growing out of the walls, the roof and the floor. Water did this, over more time than the town has existed. The mine company never got up here.

Somebody did, long before them. The people who built the temple higher up came down to this cave for its crystal. They left very little, and all of it is theirs: chains with bronze fittings let into the roof, a geode set turning on a stone axle, a great quartz hung as a pendulum, a column with three crystal wheels turning on its face. It is a shrine, or a workshop, or both.

So the cave has two voices. Nearly everything is natural crystal. A few things are temple work in pale stone, bronze and jade, and each of those is something that moves. They are your first sight of the people whose temple you will climb two biomes from now.

How it is built

  • Crystal grows in points. Six-sided in section, ending in a point, always leaning the way it grew. Crystals jut out of the back wall towards you (note 26), out of the floor and down from the roof. Nothing in the cave is a flat-topped block or a plain hexagon post.
  • Crystals come in clusters: one big point with smaller ones round its root, on a collar of rough dark rock.
  • Two kinds of crystal. Rough crystal is pale lilac, frosted, and looks like rock: you can hold it (note 30). Clear crystal is deep indigo glass, mirror-smooth: you can't.
  • Temple work is rare and always the same: a pale stone boss or bracket carved with a pug's face, a bronze pin or chain, a small jade inlay. Wherever something in the cave turns, swings or hangs, one of these holds it up.
  • No timber, no brick, no iron. One coil of old rope and one frozen climber are the only other made things.

Colour and light

Violet and ice-white. The light comes from inside the crystals: big far ones glow dimly, and each area has one or two bright white ones that light it. No lanterns, no torches. The background is deep indigo, and the things you hold are the palest things on screen.

How you come in, and how you leave

Coming in: From the dam's crest. Crystals have grown down through the top of the brickwork and split it. Two steps up, the brick is gone and the floor is crystal. Teal light turns violet.

Leaving: By the first crossing of the well, east from a crystal horn on the west rim. On the far rim the garden has already started: roots hang into the shaft and there is moss in the cracks. You leave pale violet and land in green.

Running through it

  • Your own reflection, small, in the mirror faces behind you.
  • The temple's people were here first: every moving thing hangs from their stone and bronze.
  • The ones who tried: one frozen climber.
  • The well: the first of the three crossings.
  • The frog: inside a split geode, sitting among the crystals.

  • Pale lilac only for crystal you can hold, deep indigo glass for crystal you can't: is that the split you meant by notes 26, 30 and 33?

Rules that hold on the whole mountain

  1. What you can hold is dry, pale and matte. What you can't is dark, glossy and wet. This is the mountain's first law and no design breaks it.
  2. Scenery must not look like either. Background pieces are greyer, darker and cooler than the things you climb, and never in a hold's own colour at full strength.
  3. Everything you touch is the near end of something real that carries on behind it, like a ladder and its rungs.
  4. Nothing thin hangs in the background near the climb: it reads as a rope (notes 4 and 18). A hanging thing is a real rope, or thick, or far back.
  5. The foreground is sparse: thin posts and low lips, in front of solid rock or below the lowest floor, never over the open space you climb through (note 40).
  6. Every moving thing shows its machine: what drives it stands in the background (notes 19, 36, 57).
  7. Every danger shows where it comes from and warns before it acts (notes 12, 21, 23, 56).
  8. Every breakable thing wears the game's one breakable look: cracked already, with a corner gone (notes 16, 24, 31, 53, 64).
  9. The painted back wall never stops inside the frame, so there are no black holes (note 51). The insides of small pits can't be held (note 52).
  10. One frog is hidden in every biome (note 60), in a little room you can walk into. A room you can enter is cut away at the front: floor, back wall and roof, no front wall.
