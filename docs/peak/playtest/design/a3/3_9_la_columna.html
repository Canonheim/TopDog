La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.9: La Columna

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

A proposal. The cave's tallest single crystal is a clear six-sided column that stands on La Rampa and goes up 44 m to the roof. Three collars of crystal have grown right round it, like bracelets on an arm, and come loose (Parker, 11-13). Each is a flat ring wider than the column, so it hangs askew: the first with its high side to the west, the second to the east, the third to the west again. From the side they make a zigzag up the column. Each collar creeps round the column, the middle one the other way, and the column is worn bright where they ride. The collars are glass and stay behind you. What you hold are their spikes. A spike is a long pale point that slides out of a collar's rim as that part of the collar comes round to the front, crosses in front of the column from the collar's low end up to its high end, and slides back in as it goes away round the far side. So each collar carries you up and across. At each corner of the zigzag you leave a spike that is about to go in for one that has just come out, on the next collar up. Below the collars is a landing of the hall's own rock, and from its east end five nubs lead up the corner beside the well's wall to the shelf where the climb starts.

What must not change

  • The idea: collar to collar to the top, timing each change. Three of them, and the middle one goes the other way.
  • The spikes are the only holds. Each one slides in and out with a shake first, and a spike can go in under your hand (note 34; Parker, 11-13: harder).
  • The landing, the five nubs up to the lower shelf, and the two shelves stay where they are.
  • A fall lands on the glass ceiling's top face and slides back to the pendulum's far rest, as today.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The column. One clear six-sided crystal 2.6 m across, floor to roof, with a dim light running up inside it. It does not turn. It stands 5 m behind you: further back than today, so that the collars can go right round it without coming into the play space. (3.7 to 6.3 m behind you)
  • 2The three collars. Each is a flat ring of clear indigo glass 7.6 m across and 0.5 m thick, with a hole 3.2 m across, hanging on the column at 32 degrees. You see it edge-on, as a slanted bar across the column. Its nearest edge is 1.2 m behind you, so no part of a ring is ever in the play space. The three dashed circles in the sketch are today's rings, which go. (1.2 to 8.8 m behind you)
  • 3Three worn waists. Where each collar rides, the column is worn into a shallow waist and polished mirror-bright. It is the cave's sign for a thing that moves: crystal rubbing on crystal. Nothing else drives the collars that you can see. They creep, one turn in about 50 seconds. (3.7 to 3.9 m behind you)
  • 4The roots of the two shelves. Each shelf is one wide flat crystal that grew out of the wall like a balcony, frosted on top and clear below. Under each, where it leaves the wall, there is a fan of smaller points. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The chimney's crack. The crack again, climbing the corner beside the well's wall. Each of the five nubs is the tip of a thin rough crystal growing out of it, and the crack's zigzag is why the nubs go left and right. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The bright cluster on the landing. A cluster 3 m tall on the back edge of the landing, bright white. It lights the landing and the foot of the chimney, and from below it lights the first collar. It is there today. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 7A mirror face east of the column. A clear face 5 m wide on the back wall between the two shelves. It shows the collars from their other side: in it you can see a pair of spikes coming round the back before it reaches the front. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The spikes. Each collar carries four pairs of spikes. A spike is a rough pale lilac point 2.3 m long and 0.4 m thick that lies inside the ring and slides out through its rim. When it is out, its last half metre reaches the play space, and that is what you hold. The two spikes of a pair are 1.3 m apart. When a spike is in, nothing of it shows but a pale spot on the rim, behind you, and there is nothing to hold. The small circles in the sketch are three pairs, part way across.
  • 9The three slanted crossings. The lines are where spike tips travel in the play space. A pair slides out at a collar's low end, takes 10 seconds to cross to its high end, 6 m across and 3.8 m up, and slides in. The next pair follows 12.7 seconds after. First collar: from beside the lower shelf up to the west. Second: from the west up to the east. Third: from the east up to the west.
  • 10The way up, step by step. One. From the west end of the lower shelf, reach 1.3 m up and 0.7 m out to a spike as its pair slides out. Two. Ride up and west. The two spikes of the pair take turns to tremble for half a second and slide in for a second and a half: when yours trembles, move to the other. Three. At the top west corner the second collar's pair slides out 1 m above you, a second and a half before yours goes in for good. Take it. Four. Ride up and east, and change the same way to the third collar at the east corner. Five. Ride up and west, and at the top reach 1.2 m up to the west horn.
  • 11The west horn. New. The third collar leaves you on the west side of the column and the upper shelf is on the east. So a second horn grows from the root of the great horn on that shelf, the other way: a rough pale shaft 7.7 m long and 0.7 m thick, level, reaching west over the front of the column to a point. You take its underside from the last spike, climb onto it, and walk east to the shelf. All of it can be held.
  • 12The chimney's five nubs. Rough pale lilac, six-sided, a hand's width proud: the same crystal as the mirror's nubs.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13Two dark points in the bottom corner. Rising from the bottom right corner of the frame, in front of the landing's own rock and the well's wall, with their tips below the landing's floor. They cover nothing you stand on or hold. (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A spike's shake before it goes in, and a click and a little ring of glitter when it comes out (note 34).
  • A glint that travels along each collar's rim as it creeps, so you can read which way it goes.
  • A slow glassy grind from the three waists, each at its own pitch.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Parker, 11-13: the three rings become collars that wrap right round the column, and the spikes are harder: one can go in under your hand. This page is the plan for how that plays. Every number in it is a first try for the course's author to tune with a climber.
  • Why askew: a level collar's spikes would only carry you sideways, and the 16.6 m between the two shelves would have to be nine or ten reaches. A collar that hangs askew carries you up as well as across, as today's rings do, and three askew collars fill the height with four reaches.
  • Why a spike is only out at the front: a collar goes right round the column, 5 m behind you at its far side. A spike that stayed out all the way round would look like a hold where you can't reach it. Sliding in and out (note 34) is what keeps every spike you can see a spike you can hold.
  • Course change: the three face-on rings and their eleven studs go.
  • Course change: the column moves from 1.6 m to 5 m behind the play plane, and its foot goes down to La Rampa. The three collars are added round it. Column and collars are scenery and touch nothing.
  • Course change: new moving holds. On each collar, four pairs of spike tips travel a straight slanted line in the play plane: from (-325.0, 250.7) to (-331.0, 254.5), from (-331.0, 255.5) to (-325.0, 259.3), and from (-325.0, 260.3) to (-331.0, 264.1). 10 seconds a crossing, a pair every 12.7 seconds, 1.3 m between the two of a pair. Each spike is out for 3 seconds and in for 1.5, the two of a pair never in together, and both out at the two ends of a crossing.
  • Course change: the arriving pair on the next collar must be out 1.5 seconds before the leaving pair goes in, at each of the two corners. The collars turn at the same pace in opposite ways, so this happens at the same place every time.
  • Course change: a new piece to hold, the west horn, from the upper shelf's west end at x -324.3 to a point at x -332.0, its top at 266.0 m.
  • Course change: the camera for this challenge needs to see 1.5 m further west.
  • Could not be checked here: whether a climber can make the two changes and the reach to the horn at these numbers. It needs a run in the game.
