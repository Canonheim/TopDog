La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.9: La Columna

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The cave's tallest single crystal: a clear six-sided column that stands on La Rampa behind the glass ceiling and goes up 44 m to the roof. The temple's people made it the heart of their shrine. They fixed three crystal wheels to its face, one above another, each 6 m across and each turning on its own bronze pin in a stone mount. The wheels turn against each other like gears. Their rims are glass. The only holds are the pale spikes that stand out of the rims, eleven in all, and each spike slides into its rim and out again (note 34). You go up the east side of the first wheel, the west side of the second, the east side of the third, and off onto a shelf of crystal at the top. If you fall you land on the glass ceiling's top face, and it slides you back to the pendulum's far rest. Below the wheels is a landing of the hall's own rock, and from its east end five nubs lead up the corner beside the well's wall to the shelf where the column starts.

What must not change

  • Three rings, each 6 m across, where they are. The first and third turn one way at three turns a minute and the second the other way at four. A spike on one ring meets a spike on the next every 5 seconds.
  • Eleven spikes: four, three and four. They are the only holds on the rings.
  • After note 34 each spike slides in and out on a rhythm, with a shake first. The note's plan keeps the two spikes that meet at a crossing out at that moment, so the climb can always be done.
  • The landing, the five nubs up to the lower shelf, and the two shelves.
  • A fall lands on the glass ceiling's top face and slides back to the pendulum's far rest.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The column. One clear six-sided crystal 2.6 m across, floor to roof, with a dim light running up inside it. It does not turn. It is natural: nobody cut it. The game has it today. (1.3 to 2.9 m behind you)
  • 2The column's cap. Where the column meets the roof the temple's people set a collar of carved pale stone 4 m wide, with a pug's face on its front and jade eyes. It is the largest piece of temple work in the cave, and the camera shows it as you reach the third wheel. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3Three clear wheel backs. Each ring is the rim of a crystal wheel. Behind the rim the wheel has a back of clear glass 5 m across, like a plate, turning with it. The back is what joins the rim to its pin, so the ring is not a hoop hanging in the air. It is behind you, mirror-smooth, and shows you a small reflection as you go round. (1.2 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 4Three stone mounts on the column's face. At the middle of each wheel: two carved stone cheeks fixed to the column, 1.4 m across, with a bronze pin between them and a jade ring round the pin. You see each one through the clear back of its wheel. They are the same piece that holds the geode and the pendulum. (1.5 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 5An offering niche at the lower shelf. A carved hollow with a small crystal and a jade ring, in the back wall at head height where you stand before the first wheel. (2.6 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The roots of the two shelves. Each shelf is one wide flat crystal that grew out of the wall like a balcony, frosted on top and clear below. Under each, where it leaves the wall, there is a fan of smaller points. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 7The chimney's crack. The crack again, climbing the corner beside the well's wall. Each of the five nubs is the tip of a thin rough crystal growing out of it, and the crack's zigzag is why the nubs go left and right. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 8The bright cluster on the landing. A cluster 3 m tall on the back edge of the landing, bright white. It lights the landing and the foot of the chimney, and from below it lights the first wheel. It is there today. (1.4 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 9A mirror face east of the column. A clear face 5 m wide on the back wall between the two shelves. It shows the wheels turning, and you on them, from the side you can't see. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The eleven spikes and their sockets. Each spike is a point of rough pale lilac crystal 0.65 m long, sitting in a socket in the rim with a bronze lip. It trembles for half a second, slides in until it is flush, and later snaps out again with a click and a ring of glitter (note 34). When it is in, only the bronze lip shows, and there is nothing to hold.
  • 11The three rims. Twelve flat faces of clear indigo glass, as today, with a white glint that goes round with the wheel so you can read the turn. Glass can't be held, inside or out.
  • 12The chimney's five nubs. Rough pale lilac, six-sided, a hand's width proud: the same crystal as the mirror's nubs.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 13Two dark points in the bottom corner. Rising from the bottom right corner of the frame, in front of the landing's own rock and the well's wall, with their tips below the landing's floor. They cover nothing you stand on or hold. (1.3 to 2.2 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • A spike's shake before it goes in, and a click and a ring of glitter when it comes out (note 34).
  • A glint that travels round each rim with the wheel.
  • A slow grind of stone from the three mounts, each at its own pitch.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 34 (not built yet): the spikes on the rings pop in and out. Shown here as they will be: each spike has a socket, and the socket's bronze lip stays in sight when the spike is in.
  • Note 34's open question, whether a spike may go in under you or only where you are not, is not decided here.
  • The biome sheet lists spikes under things that hurt you. In the course these spikes are the holds, and the design treats them as holds.
