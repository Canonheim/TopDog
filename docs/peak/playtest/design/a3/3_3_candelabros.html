La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.3: Candelabros

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The top of the start hall, under its sloping roof. The floor you walked in on is 23 to 30 m straight down. Water still seeps through the roof here along one seam, and where it drips it has grown threads of crystal: each as thick as an arm, hanging straight down, knuckled every half metre like a spine. At the end of a thread, where the drop hangs before it falls, the crystal has flowered into a cluster of bright points. Three of these have grown 8.7 m long, in a row across the hall, and they are your way over. Nobody hung them. A thread bends at its knuckles, so it swings when you swing it, and its top knuckle sits in a cup it has worn in the roof.

What must not change

  • Three chandeliers, 6 m apart, each hung 3.2 m higher than the last, on threads 8.7 m long from the roof. They swing as they do today.
  • The threads and the chandeliers can be held. The roof and the knobs the threads hang from can't.
  • The rest at each end: the crystal above the mirror, and the one before the geode with its frosted west face.
  • The glass crystal hanging from the roof east of the last chandelier, which stops you flying over the geode.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The drip seam, going back. The seam the water comes through runs back along the roof, and younger threads hang from it behind the three you use. They are short, 1 to 2 m, as thick as the big ones, and two have small clusters that glow. Nothing long or thin hangs behind the climb: a far thread the length of yours would read as one more thing to catch. (3.5 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 2Roof points behind the threads. The roof behind the climb is crowded with short thick points, clear and dim, 0.7 to 1.5 m long. The three long threads are plainly the longest of many. None of the short points comes near a chandelier's swing. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3A mirror face that doubles the lights. A clear face 5 m wide on the back wall, below the second and third chandeliers. When a chandelier swings, its light swings in the face, and so do you. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 4The roots of the two rests. Each rest is the broken end of a fat pale shaft, with its fan of smaller points on a rock collar where it leaves the wall, like the shafts of the stair. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The points of two giants, below. The top of the mirror crystal, and one more further east. You are level with their points now. They are dim, blue and turned away from you. (4.0 to 8.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The three sockets. Today each thread hangs from a glass knob under the roof. Each knob becomes a worn socket: a knob of clear crystal with a round cup in it, polished bright where the thread's top knuckle turns. It is glass, glossy and dark, and can't be held, as today.
  • 7The threads and their chandeliers. What is a chain today becomes a grown thread: rough pale lilac crystal as thick as an arm, in knuckles half a metre long, each grown into the next. It bends at the knuckles and nowhere else, so it hangs and swings exactly as the chain does. At its end is the chandelier: a burst of crystal points 1.2 m across, bright white, the brightest thing in the hall. Thread and cluster are one crystal, and you can hold all of it.
  • 8The far rest's frosted face. The west face of the rest you land on is rough and pale under its lip, so a low catch still holds. It is the same rough lilac as every hold.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A drop of water runs down each thread and falls from the chandelier's lowest point, about once every ten seconds.
  • Each chandelier's light moves with it: the shadows of the roof points swing when it swings.
  • A soft ring of glass notes when a chandelier is caught, and a creak from the knuckles as the thread bends.
  • The start floor far below stays lit, so the drop can be read.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Parker, 11-13: the cave is purely natural. The bronze chains, the carved bosses and the offering niche of the first design are gone. The chandeliers hang on grown threads in worn sockets.
  • Course change, look only: the three chains become knuckled threads of the same length that swing the same way, and the three glass knobs become sockets of the same size. Nothing you touch moves.
  • The five far chandeliers that hang behind this line today go. Short young threads take their place, so nothing that looks like a rope hangs behind the climb.
  • Note 40 (done): nothing stands in front of the chandeliers.
