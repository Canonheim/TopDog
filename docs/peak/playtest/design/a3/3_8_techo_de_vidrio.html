La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.8: Techo de Vidrio

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

One great flat crystal, 21 m long and 1 m thick, grew out of the back wall here and leans across the hall like a lean-to roof, from just above the pendulum's far rest up to a landing of rock. You cross underneath it. Its underside is clear glass. Six crusts of rough pale crystal have grown across the underside, each a band that runs back to the wall, like the mirror's rungs turned upside down, and they are the only things you can hang from (note 33). The first is reached from a broken tooth of crystal that hangs under the slab's low edge. La Rampa is 7 to 8 m below your feet the whole way. The slab's top face is polished smooth: it is where anyone who falls off the column's collars above slides back down.

What must not change

  • The glass slab: its place, its tilt of 32 degrees, and that neither of its faces can be held.
  • Six patches of grip on its underside, each where it is: the long first one, then five that are 1 m long with 2.1 m of glass between one and the next.
  • The broken tooth under its low edge, which is the first hold.
  • The landing's frosted face and lip at the top.
  • The slab's top face slides whatever lands on it back down to the pendulum's far rest.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The slab's root on the wall. The slab carries straight back to the wall at the same thickness. Where it enters the rock there is a seam of rough dark rock and small points along both its faces, for its whole length. It reads as one crystal growing out of the wall, not a sheet of glass hung in the air. (1.2 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The six crusts, running back. Each patch you hang from is the near end of a band of rough crystal 2.5 m long that lies across the underside of the slab and joins the seam at the wall. From the side you see each band's end, a little wider and deeper than the patch in front of it. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The column, standing behind the slab. The great clear column of the next challenge stands on La Rampa 5 m behind the slab and goes on up past it. Today it is nearer, and its foot hangs in the air at 240 m. Here it goes down to the slope, so it reads as standing on something. (3.7 to 6.3 m behind you)
  • 4A mirror face under the slab. A clear face 5 m wide on the back wall, below the middle of the crossing. It shows you hanging from the ceiling, small. One is there today, near the start. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 5A bright cluster on La Rampa. The one bright white crystal of this area stands on the glass slope under the slab, well back. It lights the underside from below, so each crust throws a long shadow up the glass and is easy to find, and it lights the slope, so you see what a miss costs. (3.5 to 5.0 m behind you)
  • 6The roots of the rest, and the landing's back. The rest you start from is the end of a fat pale crystal with its fan of points. The landing at the top is the hall's own rock, frosted over, and it runs back to the wall with a few small clusters standing on its back edge. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The six crusts. Each flat pale plate becomes a low crust of small rough points in the cave's pale lilac, clinging to the underside of the glass, lighter at its edges (note 33). Same size and place. Pale lilac on dark glass: they still stand out at a glance, because finding them is the challenge.
  • 8The broken tooth. A short rough point of the same pale crystal, snapped off, hanging under the slab's low edge. It is the first thing you take.
  • 9The slab's two faces. The underside is clear indigo glass with a white glint, and it shows a dim reflection of the slope below. The top face is the same glass, scored with long straight scratches that all run downhill, so you can read where it will send you if you land on it.
  • 10The landing's frosted face and lip. The last catch is the landing's west face and the lip above it. Both are rough pale lilac for 2 m, where the rest of that wall is glass.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
