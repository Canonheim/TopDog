La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.7: Péndulo de Cuarzo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

A great quartz hangs here on a thread of clear crystal 13 m long, and swings: 38 degrees each way, six seconds a swing, from the lip of one rest to the lip of the next, 16 m apart. Nobody hung it. The thread grew down from the tip of a roof crystal, and the quartz grew crosswise on its end, like the head of a hammer. The thread has cracked through at the top and now hangs by a ball in a cup it has worn there. What swings it is the cave's breath. Air moves up this hall from west to east and out by the well, not steadily but in slow pushes, three seconds on and three seconds off, and each push sends the quartz east. You can see the breath in the glitter in the air. Under the quartz there are 17 m of air and then La Rampa. On the far rest, where there is a small white block today, stands a tiny crystal tree (note 32).

What must not change

  • The pendulum: 13 m long, 38 degrees each way, 6 seconds a swing. Only the quartz at its end can be held. The thread can't.
  • The two rests, 16 m apart. The near one's east lip is glass.
  • The small upright thing on the far rest stays where it is and the size it is, and can be held. It stops whatever rolls along the ledge.
  • The glass fin that hangs from the roof above it.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The swept band. On the back wall behind the swing, a curved band 1.2 m wide follows the quartz's path from one rest to the other. Inside the band the wall's small points are short and all lie over the same way, like grass the wind has combed, and the rock between them is polished. Nothing taller has been able to grow in the wind the quartz makes. The band shows you how far the swing reaches before the quartz gets there. (2.8 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The roof crystals, carrying back. The crystal the pendulum hangs from, and the long fin east of it, are thick roof points of clear glass that carry on back to the wall. Each is wider behind than the part that crosses the play space, so it reads as a root and not a blade. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3A mirror face under the swing. A clear face 5.5 m wide on the back wall below the lowest point of the swing. It shows the quartz going over, and you on it. It is there today, a little higher. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 4The roots of the two rests. Each rest is the broken end of a fat pale shaft, with its fan of smaller points on a collar of rock. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The bright crystal by the socket. One bright white cluster hanging from the roof just west of the pivot. It lights the socket and the top of the thread, and throws the quartz's shadow across the back wall as it swings. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 6A spire on La Rampa, far below. The point of one far crystal standing on the glass slope, 6 m under the east rest. It tells you how high you are. It is there today. (5.0 to 7.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The quartz. A block of rough pale quartz 1.4 m long with a blunt point at each end, grown crosswise on the end of the thread. It is frosted and matte, the palest thing in the frame, and all of it can be held. The two dashed outlines are the ends of its swing.
  • 8The thread. One straight thread of clear indigo glass, as thick as an arm, smooth, with a white glint. It has no knuckles and does not bend. The chandeliers' threads are rough and pale and you hold them. This one is glass and you can't.
  • 9The socket at the roof crystal's tip. The tip of the roof crystal is worn into a cup, and the thread's top end is a ball that sits in it. Both are polished mirror-bright where they rub, the brightest spot on the roof. It is drawn onto the tip that is there today, 10 m above anything you can reach.
  • 10The crystal bonsai. Where the white block stands today (note 32): a tiny tree of crystal, 0.6 m wide and 1.2 m tall. It has a short twisted trunk of rough pale lilac and three flat pads of small points for a crown. It is the size of the block, it still stops what rolls along the ledge, and you can still hold it. What you touch is the trunk and the pads you see.
  • 11The near rest's wet lip. The east lip of the rest you leave from is glass under a drip from the roof crystal above. You take the quartz from the frosted top, not from the lip.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The breath: the glitter in the air drifts east for three seconds and hangs for three, in time with the swing.
  • A low hum from the thread, loudest at the bottom of the swing, and a soft knock from the socket at each end.
  • The quartz's shadow sweeping the back wall along the swept band.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 32 (not built yet): the little white block on the far rest becomes a tiny crystal bonsai tree. Shown here as it will be.
  • Parker, 11-13: the cave is purely natural. The stone cheeks, the bronze pin, the bronze stirrup and the curve of carved niches of the first design are gone. The quartz hangs by a grown thread in a worn socket, and the cave's breath swings it.
  • Course change: the block's shape is cut to the tree's trunk and crown, as note 32 planned. Nothing else you touch moves.
