La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.7: Péndulo de Cuarzo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

A great block of quartz hangs here on a clear crystal rod 13 m long, and swings: 38 degrees each way, six seconds a swing, from the lip of one rest to the lip of the next, 16 m apart. The temple's people hung it. Its pivot is a bronze pin through the tip of a roof crystal, held between two carved stone cheeks. On the back wall behind it they cut a curve of seven small niches that follows the swing, each with a crystal that brightens as the quartz passes over it. It is a clock, or it counts something. Under it there are 17 m of air and then La Rampa. On the far rest, where there is a small white block today, stands a tiny crystal tree (note 32).

What must not change

  • The pendulum: 13 m long, 38 degrees each way, 6 seconds a swing. Only the quartz at its end can be held. The rod can't.
  • The two rests, 16 m apart. The near one's east lip is glass.
  • The small upright thing on the far rest stays where it is and the size it is, and can be held. It stops whatever rolls along the ledge.
  • The glass fin that hangs from the roof above it.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The curve of niches. Seven offering niches cut in the back wall on a curve just under the path of the quartz, from one rest to the other. Each holds a small crystal with a jade ring. A niche glows as the quartz passes over it and fades after, so you can read how far the swing reaches and where it is now without watching the quartz itself. (2.8 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 2The stone mount at the pivot. Two carved stone cheeks stand out from the back wall either side of the roof crystal's tip, with a bronze pin through all three. A pug's face is carved on each cheek. This is the temple's way of holding anything that swings, and the same piece holds the geode. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 3The roof crystals, carrying back. The crystal the pendulum hangs from, and the long fin east of it, are thick roof points of clear glass that carry on back to the wall. Each is wider behind than the part that crosses the play space, so it reads as a root and not a blade. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4A mirror face under the swing. A clear face 5.5 m wide on the back wall below the lowest point of the swing. It shows the quartz going over, and you on it. It is there today, a little higher. (4.3 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 5The roots of the two rests. Each rest is the end of a fat pale crystal, with its fan of smaller points on a collar of rock. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The bright crystal by the mount. One bright white cluster hanging from the roof just west of the pivot. It lights the mount and the top of the rod, and throws the quartz's shadow across the back wall as it swings. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 7A spire on La Rampa, far below. The point of one far crystal standing on the glass slope, 6 m under the east rest. It tells you how high you are. It is there today. (5.0 to 7.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The quartz. A block of rough pale quartz 1.4 m long, hung crosswise like the head of a hammer in a bronze stirrup with one jade stud. It is frosted and matte, the palest thing in the frame. The two dashed outlines are the ends of its swing.
  • 9The rod. One long clear needle of indigo glass, not a chain. Chain in this cave means you can hold it, and you can't hold this.
  • 10The crystal bonsai. Where the white block stands today (note 32): a tiny tree of crystal, 1.2 m tall. It has a short twisted trunk of rough pale lilac and three flat pads of small points for a crown. It is the size of the block, it still stops what rolls along the ledge, and you can still hold it. What you touch is the trunk and the pads you see.
  • 11The near rest's wet lip. The east lip of the rest you leave from is glass under a drip from the roof crystal above. You take the quartz from the frosted top, not from the lip.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
