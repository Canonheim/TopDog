La Gruta de Cristal · Area and background design

Challenge 3.2: El Espejo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

A giant clear crystal stands on the hall floor behind the stair and leans west, and out towards you, as it rises. By the top of the stair its upper face has come forward into the climb. That face is the mirror: 9 m of indigo glass at 67 degrees, smooth enough to show you your own face. Glass can't be held. But five ribs of rough pale crystal have grown across the face, one above another like the rungs of a ladder laid on it, and their near ends are the five nubs you climb. The lower third of the face has sheared off, which is why its foot hangs out over nothing, 2.4 m from the last shaft of the stair. The piece that broke lies in shards on the hall floor 14 m below, round the feet of the climber who was on it.

What must not change

  • The mirror slab: its place, its lean of 67 degrees, its length of 9.1 m, and that its face can't be held.
  • The five nubs on the face and the loose nub before its foot, each exactly where it is. Every reach stays near the limit.
  • The foot hangs 2.4 m out from the sixth shaft of the stair, over a 14 m drop to the hall floor.
  • The two rests: the sixth shaft below, and the crystal at the top with its glass west lip. The mirror's broken top edge can be held.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The mirror crystal. The whole of the giant: clear, six-sided, 6 m thick and 30 m long, rooted where the back wall meets the hall floor and ending in a point above the top rest. Far back and dim at its foot, close behind you at its top. The slab you climb is the one face of it that reaches the play space. (1.3 to 6.0 m behind you)
  • 2Its broad face, which shows you. Beside the slab the giant turns a flat face 2.6 m wide towards the camera, just behind your back as you climb. It is a mirror too. Your reflection climbs beside you, a little smaller than you, the whole way up. (1.3 to 1.5 m behind you)
  • 3The five ribs, running back. Each nub is the near end of a rib of rough crystal 0.4 m thick that lies across the glass and runs 2.5 m back along the face, like a rung. From the side you see only its end. They are the mirror's version of the roots: the thing you hold carries on behind. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The roots of the two rests and the loose nub. The top rest is the broken end of a fat pale shaft, and the sixth shaft of the stair is below it. Each has its fan of smaller points on a rock collar where it leaves the wall. The loose nub is the tip of one thin point with a small collar of its own. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The bright crystal that lights the face. One bright white cluster on the back wall, level with the top of the mirror and east of it. Its light rakes along the glass, so each rib throws a short shadow and stands out, and the glass itself shows one hard white glint. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 6The hall floor, 14 m down. Straight under the foot: the frozen climber, his coil of rope, and the shards of the piece that broke (all described in 3.1). The camera shows them at the bottom of the screen while you are on the lowest nubs, so you see what a slip costs and who paid it. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 7The far hall. One more giant, far back and blue, on the west side. It is there today. (5.0 to 8.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The glass face. Deep indigo, mirror-smooth, with a white glint down its length. It is the cave's plainest picture of what can't be held, and it is 9 m of your route.
  • 9The five rungs and the loose nub. Each nub is drawn as the worn end of its rib: rough, frosted, pale lilac, six-sided, standing a hand's width proud of the glass. The loose nub before the foot is the same crystal, by itself.
  • 10The sheared foot. The slab's lower end is a fresh break: flat, bright at the edge, a little stepped. Below it there is only air where the rest of the face used to be.
  • 11The sixth shaft, snapped. The rest you start from, drawn as it will be (it is designed in 3.1): a thick pale shaft, nearly level, in place of today's six-sided pod. Its west end is the flat glassy break where the mirror's falling foot took its point off. The west end of its top is where it is today, so the reach to the loose nub does not change.
  • 12The top rest's wet lip. Water drips onto the west lip of the top rest from the roof crystal above, and the lip has a skin of new clear crystal: glass, where the rest of the top is frosted. It is why this rest is not quite safe.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
