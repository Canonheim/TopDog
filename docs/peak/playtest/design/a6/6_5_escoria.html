La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.5: Escoria

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The slag pan. Above the loft the smiths smelted their iron in a round-bellied furnace on an iron tapping floor, and the waste, the slag, ran out of it down a long tilted iron pan to cool. The pan hangs from the hall's roof in two cradles. To keep the slag running they raked it: three iron rakes, their handles worked to and fro by three cranks on one steam wheel at the head of the pan, like oars. The rafts you ride are the heads of the rakes. At the pan's low end the slag runs off a lip and falls to the quench pipe, and the smiths' second hoist reaches over the lip. The light: bright orange, the brightest in the forge, and the slag itself is the lamp. It lights everything from underneath and throws the rakes' shadows up the wall. It is the last place where the light comes from under your feet.

What must not change

  • The pan, its tilt, and the slag's throw, exactly as they are.
  • The three rafts: their tracks, speeds and waits.
  • The two dead crusts, the chain at the lip, and the ledge beyond it.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The hall's back wall. The same black wall, lit bright orange from below, with the roof above it left in black. (3.6 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 2The smelter and its spout. A round-bellied furnace of temple stone with the iron pug on its front, standing on the tapping floor behind the iron shore. A stone spout runs from its tap hole to the head of the pan. The slag comes from here, and you can see it pour. (1.3 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 3The pan's back half, its cradles and chains. The pan is 3 m from front to back, and you ride its front edge. It lies in two iron cradles, and each cradle hangs from the hall's roof on a pair of chains with links as thick as an arm. The chains rise behind the two dead crusts, where nobody stands, and well back, so they can't be taken for the chain you want. Today the pan hangs in the air. (1.2 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The rake wheel and the three rake handles. What moves the rafts. Each raft is the head of a slag rake. Its long iron handle runs back up the pan to a crank on one wheel at the pan's head, beside the smelter, and a steam nozzle kicks the wheel round. Three cranks, three handles, three rafts going to and fro. The handles pass through eyes on the cradles' hangers. (1.5 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The lip and the falling slag. At the pan's low east end the slag runs off a lip and falls, in separate bright drops, 22 m into the hopper of Géiseres Gemelos. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 6The second hoist. The same hoist as on the loft's lip, standing on the next ledge with its arm reaching back over the pan's lip. The ring is the eye at the end of its arm and the chain is its chain. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 7The foot of the hammer pier. The ledge at the far side is the first landing of the stepped stone pier that carries the three anvils of La Fragua. The pier comes up from the forge floor, 44 m below. (1.3 to 3.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The pan's front plate. The front of the pan is black riveted plate with slag frozen in runs over its rim. The slag's surface is bright yellow-orange in the middle of each gap and skinned dark red at its edges, so you can see that it is live.
  • 9The rafts' eyes. Each raft is a thick iron plate with a turned-up edge and an eye at its west end, where the rake's handle hooks on. Its top is rust orange. Its sides are black and crusted where they sit in the slag.
  • 10The two dead crusts. Black glassy teeth of slag that has spat up and frozen round the cradles' hangers. They are in the play space and can't be held, so they are plainly dark and wet: they shine, and nothing about them says stand here.

Foreground (sparse)

Small effects, no models

  • The slag pours from the spout in a slow bright rope and spreads down the pan.
  • A burst of sparks wherever the slag throws something back.
  • Embers thick over the pan, a few of them close to the camera. The rakes' shadows move on the wall.
  • A puff at the rake wheel's nozzle each time it kicks.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Scenery moved out of the play space: the pan's hanging chains stand about 1 m behind you today, inside the play space. They become two pairs of heavy chains at the back of the pan, 2 to 3 m behind.
  • The slag and the two dead crusts stay in the play space and can't be held. Neither looks like a hold: the slag glows, and the crusts are black, glassy and shining.
  • Nothing you touch moves or is added.
