La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.7: Tiro de Chimenea

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The updraught shaft: the forge's own chimney. The great hood over the hammers gathers the whole forge's heat and pours it in here, under the foot of an iron baffle, and it goes up between the baffle and the flue's east wall. That is the wind that carries you. Half way up the baffle has a window with an iron flap, and every few seconds the draught snatches the flap open and sucks sideways through it. The rings on the wall are where the smiths hung their chains to sweep the shaft. An iron grille once capped it: one bar is left, and the last rings are on that bar. This is where the made things stop. The ledge you start on is the last cut stone. Above it the wall is the mountain's own basalt, and the baffle is the last big piece of iron. The light: dusty gold. The red still comes from below, on the underside of everything, and for the first time a pale light comes down from above, down the pipe over the shaft's head.

What must not change

  • The steady draught, the cross gust, its window and its timing, exactly as they are.
  • Note 61: seven rings become four. Two stay on the east wall, just under and just over the gust band. Two stay across the top, the first and the last.
  • The baffle and the east wall can't be climbed.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The flue's far wall. Above the pier's head the temple stone stops. From here up the wall is basalt: grey-brown, in rough blocky steps, split by heat. Low in the shaft the cracks still show a dull red. (3.2 to 4.4 m behind you)
  • 2The pier's head, the last door and the last pug. The ledge you start on is the head of the hammer pier: the last cut stone on the climb. Behind it is the last grilled stair door, and over the door the last iron pug. Above this nothing has a face. (1.3 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 3The hood's top end and the steam pipe. The top end of the forge's hood, and the steam pipe that runs up under it. The pipe comes through the hood, goes up the wall and turns in under the next ledge, where it feeds the first vent of El Último Aliento. The hood is what pours the forge's heat into this shaft. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The flap. The window in the baffle has an iron flap, hinged at the top on the far side. It hangs slack while the air is quiet. It starts to rattle and lift 0.9 s before the gust, and it is flung wide open while the gust blows. It is the warning you can see, to go with the whistle. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 5Two chain stubs. The bare flue's only dressing: two short thick stubs of chain on pins in the far wall, three links each. The lower one stands straight up in the steady draught, so you see the wind before you step into it. The upper one is in the gust band, and it lies over to the west when the gust comes. (2.8 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 6The cap grille's last bars. An iron grille once capped the shaft. One bar of it is left whole, slanting from the east wall up to the top ledge, and two more are snapped off behind it. The rings across the shaft's head are eyes on short pins from the whole bar. Today they hang in the air. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 7The pale light down the pipe. Straight above the shaft is the slick pipe that comes down from under the chockstone of La Luz, and daylight comes down it. It is the first daylight on the climb: a pale dusty column in the top of the shaft that fades before it reaches the window. (3.0 to 3.2 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The sockets of the three rings that go. Note 61 takes three rings away: the lowest on the wall, the one inside the gust band, and the middle one across the top. Each of the two on the wall leaves an empty square socket with a rust streak under it: dark, flat, and plainly nothing to hold. The one across the top leaves a snapped pin on the bar. The sketch draws the three over today's rings.
  • 9The four rings that stay. The kit's wall ring: a round iron ring 0.5 m across on a pin, rust orange, worn bright inside where hands have been. With half of them gone, the four are the only orange things in the shaft.
  • 10The baffle's window. The baffle is a wall of black riveted iron plate, greased with soot. The edges of its window are scraped bright by everything the gust has pulled through.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11A heavy chain's last links. The last three links of a heavy chain hang into the top corner of the frame, in front of the solid east wall. They lift a little in the draught. They are in front of rock, never in front of the shaft. (1.3 to 1.6 m in front)
