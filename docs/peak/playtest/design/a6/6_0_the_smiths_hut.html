La Chimenea · Area and background design

Rest 6.0: The smith's hut

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The east end of the forge floor, at the very bottom of the chimney. Every fall in the chimney ends on this floor, and the floor slopes this way, so this corner is where you pick yourself up. Today it is bare: one small anvil, one frozen climber sitting on nothing, a torch on the wall. After note 59 it is the smith's hut. The last smith built a lean-to against the flue's east wall, out of the forge's own black stone and iron sheet, round his anvil and a small hearth. Only the frog lives in it now, and it has taken over the anvil. The frozen climber in his dog mask stays where the course has him, and the hut is built round him: he looks as if he came in to have a ring mended and sat down to wait. The hut is cut away at the front: floor, back wall and roof, no front wall, and you walk in along the floor. The light: this is the bottom of the journey. It is furnace red, low along the floor, from two banked furnace mouths in the back wall, with black shadow above. The hut's coals and its lamp are the one small warm spot in it.

What must not change

  • The floor, its slope, and the slick east wall, exactly as they are.
  • The frozen climber, sitting exactly where he sits today, facing the well.
  • The walk back west along the floor to the first challenge stays clear. The hut is scenery, behind the play plane: nothing of it can be stood on or held.
  • A falling climber is not caught by the hut: its roof is behind the play plane.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The forge's back wall, with two banked furnace mouths. Soot-black temple blocks in bays 4 m wide, with the temple's carved band worn nearly flat. This wall is the foot of the stepped pier that carries the three anvils of La Fragua, 44 m overhead. Two furnace mouths are banked low in it, each under a ribbed hood. Their red glow along the floor is the light of this place. (3.4 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 2The hut's back wall. Soot-black temple blocks, 4 m wide and 3 m tall, the same blocks as the forge wall. The hut is black stone and iron sheet, with no timber in it (Parker, 11-13). A rack of tongs hangs on it, and three wall rings on pegs, finished and half finished: the same rings you hold in Tiro de Chimenea, made here. They are black, not rust orange, and out of reach. (2.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 3The roof and its two back posts. A lean-to of riveted iron sheet, sloping down from the east wall, on two iron posts. The hut is cut along the line just behind the climber. The roof stops 1.2 m behind the play plane, so somebody who falls here passes in front of it and lands on the floor. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 4The hearth. A block of temple stone against the east wall with a bed of coals on it, a small iron hood over it, and a ribbed pipe that goes up the wall and out of sight. The coals are orange. With the lamp they are the only light on the floor that is not red. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 5The little bellows. Beside the hearth, on a stand: two boards, leather pleats and a nozzle, worked by a foot treadle and a rod. It is the great bellows of El Fuelle made small, and like every machine in the forge it shows what works it. (1.5 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 6The small anvil. The anvil that stands here today, moved 0.6 m further back so that you walk in front of it. It stands on a stone footing with the iron pug's face on it, as every machine in the forge does. A wall ring lies on it, bent open: the job the frozen climber is waiting for. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 7The frog. 0.3 m tall, sitting on the heel of the anvil in a leather apron, with a tiny hammer. It taps the anvil now and then and looks up when you come in (notes 59 and 60). It is friendly and nothing more. (1.4 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 8The lamp. The torch that is on the east wall today, 5 m up, comes down under the roof and becomes the hut's lamp, on a bracket on the back wall. It is the last torch on the climb. There are none above the forge floor. (2.1 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 9Quenching trough and coal heap. Outside the hut's west post: a stone trough of black water with a pair of tongs left in it. Further along, between the two furnace mouths, a heap of coal. (1.4 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The frozen climber's seat. He sits where the course has him, in his dog mask, facing the well. Today he sits on nothing. He gets an upturned iron bucket under him, 0.7 m wide and 0.42 m tall. It is in the play space, so it can be held and stood on like any sound iron, and its rim is rust orange. He does not live here: only the frog does (Parker, 11-13).
  • 11The swept floor. The forge's floor of temple slabs carries on into the hut. Inside it is swept paler, with a ring of scale and soot round the anvil. It is drawn onto the floor that is there: nothing is added to stand on.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 12The hut's front sill. A low sill of iron plate on the floor's front edge, with the stumps of the two front posts cut off level with the floor. It shows that the front of the hut was taken away. It sits below the top of the floor and covers nothing. (1.2 to 1.4 m in front)
  • 13The floor's kerb. A low lip of soot-black blocks along the very bottom of the frame, below the floor. The same kerb runs the whole length of the forge floor. It covers nothing you can stand on. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The red in the two furnace mouths swells and sinks slowly, like breathing.
  • A thread of smoke from the hearth's pipe, and a spark each time the frog taps the anvil.
  • The frog croaks when you are within a screen of it (note 60).
  • Look straight up from here and the slit of sky is a pale thread, 100 m overhead.
