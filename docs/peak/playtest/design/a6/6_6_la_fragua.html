La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.6: La Fragua

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The hammer shop. A stepped pier of temple stone rises from the forge floor through the whole hall, each step 4.2 m wide and 4 m tall, like a stair for a giant. One great anvil stands on each of three steps, and over each anvil hangs a drop hammer. Behind each hammer is the wheel that lifts it: a peg on the wheel's rim hooks under the hammer's tail, carries it up and lets it go. A steam pipe under the forge's hood turns the wheels. The chain you climb hangs from the hammer's own lug, so it is only up there while the hammer is. This is the last of the temple's stone and the heaviest of its iron. The light: orange going to gold. The slag's light now comes from behind you and below, and every hammer blow throws white-gold sparks. The hood is a lid over this room, so there is still no daylight in it.

What must not change

  • The three anvils, the three hammers and their timing, and the three chains, exactly as they are.
  • To cross an anvil you walk under its hammer.
  • The slick hood above, which sheds fallers from the flue down into the hall.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The hall's back wall. The same black wall, here lit orange from the left and below, with sparks on it. (3.6 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 2The stepped pier. Soot-black temple blocks in three giant steps and a head, with the iron pug on each riser. One anvil stands on each step. The ledge you start on is the pier's first landing, and the ledge at the top is its head. Today the anvils hang in the air. (1.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The slotted towers. Behind each hammer a narrow stone tower rises from the step to the hood, with a dark slot down its middle. The hammer's tail runs in the slot. It is the temple's rule, kept in the forge: every block that moves has a slot it slides in. (1.3 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 4The three lifting wheels. What lifts the hammers. On each tower's axle turns an iron wheel 4.4 m across, with paddles on its rim and one peg. The peg hooks under the hammer's tail, carries it up 4.2 m and lets it go at the top. You can read each hammer's time from its wheel: when the peg comes round to the bottom, that hammer is about to rise. (1.4 to 2.0 m behind you)
  • 5The steam pipe under the hood. The slanting black slab over the hammers is the forge's great hood, ribbed with iron underneath. A fat steam pipe runs up under it, and a nozzle drops from the pipe towards each wheel and kicks it round. A puff at the nozzle comes just before each hammer starts up. The same pipe goes on up into the flue and feeds the three vents of El Último Aliento. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The scorched bunting. High in the corner where the hall's roof meets the flue: one scorched string of the town's cut paper, with three flags left on it. It hangs there today, nearer. It moves to the far back, where a hanging thing can't be taken for a rope. (4.0 to 4.2 m behind you)
  • 7The second hoist. From Escoria: the hoist on the pier's first landing, reaching back over the slag pan's lip. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 8The last stair door. In the wall behind the top ledge: the last of the grilled stair doors. Above it nobody built in stone. (3.2 to 3.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The anvils' bright faces. Each anvil's face is polished bright in the middle, where the hammer lands, and sooty at its two ends. The bright patch is exactly as wide as the hammer. The anvil's foot carries the iron pug.
  • 10The hammers' striking faces. Each hammer is a block of black greased iron with a peaked top and the pug's face cast on its front. Its striking face is a band of bright worn steel, the only pale thing on it, so you can see which edge will hit you. The lug on its east side, where the chain hangs, is rust orange.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
