La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.9: La Luz

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The crack to daylight, and the way out of the mountain. At its top the flue narrows to a crack where the basalt of the inside meets the pale chalk of the outside. Nothing here is made: no iron, no cut stone, no soot. Two shelves of basalt step up to a gap, and a block of chalk that fell from the roof is jammed across the gap. Chalk is weak. The block is cracked through and it gives way under you. A band of flint knobs in the chalk is the way over when it has gone. Beyond it is a sill of white chalk, and the opening. One frozen climber stands on the doorstep with his hand up toward the light. The light: this is the top of the journey. It is blue-white daylight, coming level through the opening on the right, and there is no red left in it. The design stops at the opening.

What must not change

  • The doorstep, the two steps, the three nubs and the sill, exactly as they are.
  • The chockstone's size, wedge shape and timing: it gives way after 1.2 s and is back in 8 s.
  • The gap under it drops to the updraught shaft, two challenges back.
  • The frozen climber, exactly where he stands today.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack's back wall. The crack is narrow. Its back wall is only 1.2 m behind you: the first time on this climb that a back wall is within reach. West of a slanting line it is the flue's basalt, grey-brown and dry. East of the line, for the last few metres to the opening, it is pale chalk: the mountain's outside. (1.2 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 2The gap's black wall. Under the chockstone the gap between the two walls drops away to the updraught shaft, 26 m below. Its back and its sides are black and glassy, worn smooth by the draught, with nothing on them to catch. It reads as a drop, and it is one. (1.2 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 3The flint band. A band of flint runs up through the chalk: a line of knobs in their white rind, slanting from the second step to the opening. The three nubs over the gap are three knobs of this band that stand out far enough to hold. The band goes on past them, so the way out is drawn on the wall before the chockstone ever drops. (1.2 to 1.4 m behind you)
  • 4The split in the roof. Right above the chockstone the roof has a split, packed with blocks of chalk that have not fallen yet. The chockstone came out of it. When the stone gives way under you, the next block drops out of the split with a thud and a puff of white dust, and wedges where the last one was (note 64). You can see the next one waiting. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 5The edge of daylight. The daylight comes in under the roof, and its lowest edge reaches the frozen climber's raised hand and the top of his mask. The rest of him is in the pillar's shadow. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 6The sky through the opening. What you see through the opening, and all you see of the next place. Sky first: a hard clear blue, the first since the station. Under it the far side of the valley, pale and hazy, and at its foot the town, tiny, with the ring a dot of colour in its plaza. One vulture turns a long way off. The opening is 6 m tall and you walk out along the sill. (6.0 to 8.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The cracked chockstone. Note 64. The chockstone is a real stone: a rough block of chalk jammed between the two walls, the same size and wedge shape as the stand-in, so the step across plays the same. It wears the mountain's one breakable look: cracked right through, with a corner gone. Grit trickles from under it, and it shifts when you first touch it. It breaks into rough chunks that drop down the gap with white dust.
  • 8The three flint nubs. Three knobs of flint in their white rind, each the size of two fists, standing 0.4 m out of the chalk. Dry, pale and matte. They are natural, and they lie on the flint band's line.
  • 9The one who nearly had it. The second of the chimney's frozen climbers stands on the doorstep exactly where he is today, 1.35 m from the last ring, with one arm raised toward the light. His raised hand and the top of his mask are in daylight. Nothing is put beside him, because nothing was ever made up here: he is the only thing in the frame that is not rock. He got out of the forge, and stopped one easy challenge short of the sky.
  • 10The sill. The two steps are the last of the basalt. The sill is the first of the chalk: white, dry and gritty, with wind-blown dust on it. You change rock as you climb out.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11A fin of basalt. The same rough fin as in El Último Aliento, in front of the pillar under the steps. It covers only solid rock. (1.2 to 1.8 m in front)
  • 12A chalk overhang. A dark overhang in front of the roof at the top right, over the opening. With the fin below, it makes the opening the brightest shape in the frame. It covers only solid rock. (1.3 to 2.0 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • White dust blowing in through the opening on the wind, lit.
  • Grit trickling from under the chockstone, and a puff of white dust when the next block lands.
  • The frozen climber's long shadow, pointing back down the flue.
  • The forge's rumble fades here and the wind takes over.
