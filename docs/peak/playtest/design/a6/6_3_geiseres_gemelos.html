La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.3: Géiseres Gemelos

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The quench pipe. The slag pan of Escoria hangs 26 m above this spot, and its waste runs off its east lip and falls. The smiths stood a great iron standpipe on the forge floor to catch it, with a hopper on top. The slag drops into the water in the pipe and the pipe blows steam: that is the second vent. A branch of the same pipe comes up through the furnace's charging step: that is the first. They are twins because they are one pipe with two mouths, and they take turns because the pressure can only be in one place at a time. Above them is the lip of the bellows loft, with the smiths' hoist standing on it. The light: red going to orange. It comes from the furnace's open crown, below and to the left, and from the falling slag, which is the first bright orange thing on the climb.

What must not change

  • Both vents, the draught between them, the two slick plates, the chain and the top ledge, exactly as they are.
  • Nothing to stand on at the second vent.
  • Note 63: both vents are about 10% stronger. Nothing in the design depends on how high they throw.
  • Nothing stands in front of either vent.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The forge's back wall. The forge's back wall goes on up, black, with one worn band. It is lit from the left and below. (3.6 to 4.6 m behind you)
  • 2The furnace's crown. The top of the furnace of Piedra Caliente. The ledge you stand on is its charging step. Its crown is open and glowing, and the air pipe from the bellows loft comes out of the wall and down into it. (1.3 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 3The quench pipe. A standpipe 2.2 m across that comes up from the forge floor, 21 m below, strapped to the wall. It stands here today as scenery. It stays, and it gets its straps and its reason. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The hopper's back half. The two slick plates you can't stand on are the front edge of a round iron hopper on top of the pipe. Its back half stands behind, lopsided like the plates: short on the west, long on the east, to catch the falling slag. Inside it is black and glassy with splashed slag. (1.2 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 5The branch pipe. A thinner pipe leaves the standpipe and runs under the ledge to the first grate. The two vents are one pipe with two mouths, which is why they take turns. (1.3 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The falling slag. Slag falls from the lip of the pan above into the hopper. Today it is drawn as one thin bright strip, 1.5 m from the chain you have to catch, and it could be taken for a second chain. It becomes separate bright drops, set well back. A bigger gob falls 1 s before the second vent blows: the warning you can see. (3.0 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 7The hoist. An iron post, arm and brace standing on the east end of the loft's lip: the smiths' hoist for lowering things to the hopper. The ring is the eye at the end of its arm and the chain is its chain. Today the ring hangs in the air. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 8The stair door. In the back wall behind the top ledge: a stepped arch shut with an iron grille. The smiths had a stair inside the wall. You can see how they came up, and you can't use it. (3.2 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 9The stepping bellows' cam wheels. From El Fuelle, at the top left: the first two of the three stepping bellows, each on its iron brackets with its cam wheel under it, and the coupling rod that joins the wheels. Today the bellows hang in the air. (1.6 to 2.4 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10The first grate's rim. The same grate as the first one, set in the charging step.
  • 11The slick plates. Black greased iron, with runs of glassy slag frozen down them. They are the darkest and shiniest things in the frame, plainly not to be stood on.
  • 12The loft's lip. The ledge that ends the challenge is the lip of the bellows loft: a temple slab, sooted on its face and worn pale on top.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.
