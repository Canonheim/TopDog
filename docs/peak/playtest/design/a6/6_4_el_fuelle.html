La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.4: El Fuelle

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The bellows loft, the upper half of the forge hall. The furnace below needs air, and this is where the air is made. The great bellows stands on a stone shelf on the west wall. Three small box bellows step up towards it on iron brackets, each one's pipe going back into the wall and down to the furnace. One steam wheel under the shelf works all of it: a crank and a rod open and shut the great bellows, and a long rod turns three cam wheels that push the small bellows up and let them down. You climb the small bellows as steps, into the great bellows' blast, onto its deck, and up four iron rungs in a stone pier to the slag pan's shore. One of the rungs is not a rung. The light: orange. It comes from above and to the right, off the slag pan, so the tops of the bellows are lit and the furnace's red is left on their undersides.

What must not change

  • The three stepping bellows: where they are, how far their tops travel (1.8 m), and their timing.
  • The gust: where it blows, how hard, and its 1.4 s on and 2.6 s off.
  • The deck you land on does not move, so the last reach is unchanged.
  • The four rungs' places. Note 58 makes the second from the top a stick of dynamite, the same size and in the same place.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The loft's back wall. The same black temple wall, here lit orange from above. It fills the frame. (3.6 to 4.5 m behind you)
  • 2The stone shelf. A shelf of temple blocks built out from the west wall on stepped corbels. The great bellows stands on it. Today the bellows hangs on the wall with nothing under it. (1.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 3The great bellows. Two boards, leather pleats and an iron nozzle box with the pug's face on it, 6 m long, hinged at the nozzle like a real bellows and opening at the west end. After note 57 it moves. Through the 2.6 s of quiet its top board lifts and the pleats spread, so you can read how long you have. Then a stone weight on the top board slams it shut over the 1.4 s of the gust, and the jet comes out of the nozzle along the line the wind blows. It is drawn open. The dashed line is the top board when shut. The deck you land on is a fixed timber deck on the bellows' iron frame, above the moving board. (1.3 to 3.4 m behind you)
  • 4The steam wheel, its crank and its rod. What works the great bellows. Under the shelf an iron paddle wheel 3 m across sits in stone cheeks. A pipe comes up the west wall and ends in a nozzle that kicks the wheel round with steam. A crank on the wheel's axle pulls a rod, and the rod goes up through the shelf to the free end of the bellows' top board. Wheel, crank and rod turn once for every gust. (1.6 to 2.8 m behind you)
  • 5The long rod and the three cam wheels. What works the three stepping bellows. Each one sits on a pair of iron brackets from the wall, and under it turns an iron cam wheel 2 m across. As the wheel turns, its cam pushes the bellows' top board up 1.8 m and lets it down again. One coupling rod joins the three wheels, each a third of a turn behind the last, which is why the three bellows swell one after another. A long rod runs from the third wheel down to the steam wheel. One wheel works everything in the loft. Today the three bellows hang in the air. (1.6 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The rung pier. A pier of temple blocks from the deck up to the iron shore. The rungs are iron staples driven into it. Today they hang in the air. (1.3 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 7The shore's brackets. The iron shore at the top is the front edge of the slag pan's tapping floor: a riveted iron gallery on brackets, which carries on behind you to the smelter of Escoria. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 8A miner's box of dynamite. A small open box on the deck behind the rungs, with the mine's skull pug stencilled on it and three more red sticks showing. It tells you what the red rung is before you touch it, and how it got here. It is a question for you, below. (1.4 to 1.9 m behind you)
  • 9The loft's lip, with the hoist and the stair door. From Géiseres Gemelos: the ledge you start on is the lip of the loft, with the smiths' hoist on its east end and the grilled stair door in the wall behind it. (1.3 to 3.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10Pleats on the three stepping bellows. Each stepping bellows is a small box bellows: a timber top board with a pug's face on its edge, and leather pleats under it that open as it swells and close as it folds (note 57). Its base stays still on its brackets. Only the top board and the pleats move, and the top board's travel is exactly today's.
  • 11The stick of dynamite. Note 58. The second rung from the top is a stick of dynamite: a fat red stick with a fuse curling off its east end, lying in the two bent stubs of a rung that has snapped off. The pier behind it is scorched in a star from the last time.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The jet: steam and sparks out of the nozzle along the line the wind blows, for the 1.4 s of the gust.
  • The pug on the nozzle box puffs its cheeks. Soot shakes off the frame. A creak on the draw and a thump on the blow.
  • A puff at the steam wheel's nozzle each time it kicks the wheel.
  • The fuse: a hiss and a bright spitting spark that runs along it.
