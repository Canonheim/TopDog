La Chimenea · Area and background design

Challenge 6.8: El Último Aliento

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The bare flue, and the forge's last three breaths. Above the shaft the flue opens out into a tall leaning cleft of basalt with nothing built in it. One thing of the forge's comes this high: the steam pipe that ran up under the hood. It climbs the far wall in three flights, and at the end of each flight an elbow comes forward and ends in a grate. The steam climbs the pipe, so the three vents blow one after another from the bottom up. The rings are the eyes of the pipe's clamps. Above the third grate there is one ring on a spike, and then only rock. The light: gold going pale. The forge's red reaches about as high as the second grate. A shaft of daylight comes in at the crack and slants down across the flue, and the third grate sits in it. Each breath is lit paler than the one before.

What must not change

  • The three vents and their timing, the three rings, and both ledges, exactly as they are.
  • Note 63: the vents are about 10% stronger, so the top of each flight comes out just above its ring.
  • Note 62: the two high grates can be held and stood on, and must look it.
  • Nothing stands in front of a vent.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The flue's far wall. Bare basalt. It gets paler and drier as it climbs: grey-brown and cracked at the bottom ledge, with a dull red still in the lowest cracks, and a dry pale grey by the top ledge. Nothing is built on it. (3.2 to 4.4 m behind you)
  • 2The steam pipe, in three flights. The forge's steam pipe climbs the far wall: up, across, up, across, and a capped stub. Each grate sits on the end of one flight, where an elbow comes forward to the play plane. After note 62 the two high grates are plainly held up, and plainly part of one machine. The first ring is the eye of a clamp on the second riser. The second ring is on the capped stub. (1.3 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The shaft of daylight. Daylight comes in at the crack and slants down across the flue to the west wall. It is painted there today. Low down it turns dusty gold and dies into the red. The third grate and the second ring sit inside it: they are the first things on the climb that daylight touches, and the brightest things above you. (3.8 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 4Two old chain stubs. Two short stubs of chain on pins, both below the second grate. Above it the wall has no iron on it but the pipe. (2.8 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 5The last ring's spike. The last ring is on a single iron spike driven into the far wall beside the top ledge. It is the highest made thing inside the mountain. (1.2 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The two high grates, made plain. Note 62. The second and third grates wear the same raised rim and red grille as the one in the ledge. Each sits on the end of the pipe's elbow, and the elbow stops just behind the play plane, so nothing new is put in the play space under a grate. Steam seeps from them between blasts. The second grate, which daylight does not reach, has a dull red glow in its bars from the steam below, so it is never a dark box in the dark.
  • 7The top ledge. The crack's doorstep: natural basalt, dry and pale, with daylight on it. The frozen climber of La Luz stands on it with one arm raised. From below he is the last thing on the skyline, with light on his hand.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 8A fin of basalt. A rough fin of basalt standing in front of the foot of the pillar on the right. It covers only solid rock. (1.2 to 1.8 m in front)

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 62 (not built yet): the high grates sit on the pipe, seep steam and are lit, so they read as the next step.
  • Note 63 (not built yet): all three vents are about 10% stronger.
  • Nothing you touch moves or is added. The pipe, its elbows and its clamps stay at least 1.2 m behind you. Only the grates and the rings come forward into the play space.
  • The pillar on the right is in the play space and can't be held. It is plainly dark and wet: black, soot-wet basalt.
