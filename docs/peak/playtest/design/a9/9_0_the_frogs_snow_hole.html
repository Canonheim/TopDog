El Glaciar · Area and background design

Rest 9.0: The frog's snow hole

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The east end of the ice cave's floor, 20 m under the long flight from the slide to the rope. Snow that comes in at the window far above has drifted against the east wall into a bank, and somebody dug a round hole in the bank: 3 m wide and 2 m high, the kind of hole a climber digs to sit out a night. A lantern is lit in it and a kettle is on. The glacier's frog lives here, wrapped in a blanket. From the slide you see its light far below you. If you miss the rope, this is where you land, and the frog watches you get up. The way back up is at the other end of the floor: the two at the door.

What must not change

  • The cave's floor: firm snow from the door to the east wall, flat, 21 m long.
  • The rope overhead ends 9.3 m above the floor. It cannot be reached from here.
  • The one icicle that falls into the cave, at x -293, and its timing.
  • The frog's hole is behind the floor. Nothing new can be stood on or held.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cave's far wall. The same deep blue banded ice. It never stops inside the frame, and it gets darker toward the east wall, away from the door. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The snow bank and the hole. A bank of drifted snow against the east wall, 5.3 m long and 2.7 m high, standing behind the floor you walk on. The hole in it is round, 3 m wide and 2 m high, with a smooth domed roof and a step of packed snow at its mouth. Its front is open to you. Its mouth is at the back edge of the play space, so you stand at its door on the cave's own floor. (1.2 to 3.6 m behind you)
  • 3The frog, and what is in the hole. The frog sits on a folded bedroll, wrapped in a striped blanket with only its face showing. Beside it a kettle steams on a tiny spirit stove. A candle lantern hangs from an axe driven into the roof. Its amber fills the hole and lies on the snow in front of it. A short string of the town's paper flags is pinned along the back. (1.6 to 3.2 m behind you)
  • 4The way back up. At the west end of the floor: the door, pale, and beside it the first of the ones who tried, with his lantern. They belong to La Cueva de Hielo. From the frog's door they are the only other light in the cave. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 5The fallen block and the glove. A block of ice 2 m across, off the roof, and a frozen glove beside it. Behind where you walk. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 6One in the wall. Deep in the far wall, 8 m up, leaning: one of the ones who tried, a flat blue shape. He is level with the end of the rope. (2.4 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7Where people have landed. Drawn on the snow: two shallow dents the size of a body, old ones, half snowed over. They say what this floor is before you have fallen onto it. They have no depth and change nothing you stand on.
  • 8Boot prints. Two lines of prints on the snow. One comes in at the door and goes to the first frozen climber. The other goes from each dent back west to him. A third, very small, with webbed toes, goes from the hole to the nearer dent and back.
  • 9Splinters. A ring of ice splinters on the snow under the one icicle that falls into the cave. It grows at a dark crack in the roof, 30 m up, and bursts here (note 86).
  • 10The rope's tail. The frayed end of the rope you slide to, 9.3 m above the floor and 2 m in from the east wall. It is there today. Looking up from the frog's door you see the whole rope, the window's light above it, and how far you fell.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The frog's croak, heard from about a screen away, and its amber light on the snow.
  • Steam from the kettle, rising straight: there is no wind down here.
  • The frog turns its head to follow you. When you land near it, it blinks.
  • A few snowflakes coming down the whole height of the cave from the window.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 60: this biome's frog, in the snow hole the sheet proposes. Finding it is never needed: it is at the dead end of the floor, where a miss on the slide puts you.
  • Note 86: the icicle that falls into the cave is seen growing and shatters on the floor, so it cannot pin anybody who has just landed.
  • The kettle is the same one as in the drivers' cave on the cliffs and in the keeper's cabin on the storm ridge.
  • The cave's floor is 21 m of snow. So that no 4 m of it is blank: the boot prints, the two dents, the splinters, blue hollows, and the lantern's light.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
