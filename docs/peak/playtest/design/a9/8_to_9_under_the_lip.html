El Glaciar · Area and background design

Join 8 to 9: Under the lip: from the cableway to the glacier

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The cableway's last deck stands against the face at 786 m, and the glacier has come down on top of it. The ice's lip hangs over the west half of the deck, 4.8 m above the planks, with a fringe of icicles under it, and the ice lies on the station house's roof. Whatever comes off the glacier's skin slides down the top of the lip and is thrown off its tip, over your head and clear of the deck. So nobody goes up the outside from here. Under the lip there is an alcove, and from the back of the alcove a tunnel runs west along the glacier's bed. It is the way the melt water left in warmer years. It is dry now, 21 m long and 4.8 m high, with a floor of firm snow. The ones who came to climb marked it with a cane and a paper flag and hung a lantern inside its mouth. You walk out of gold light into blue shadow, and at the far end the tunnel opens into the lower crack.

What must not change

  • The deck, as the cableway's file has it: its ice, its three dry patches, its length.
  • The lip over the deck: 4.8 m of head room, its two ramps on top, and the tip that throws a falling body clear of the deck.
  • The tunnel: 21 m long, 4.8 m high, firm snow all the way, nothing to climb and nothing that falls.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The valley and the sky. East of the lip's tip the picture is the one that is given: the painted valley far below with the tiny town, the two painted ranges, the cloud banks above. Nothing of this biome stands in it here. It is the last you see of it until the window at 830 m. (7.8 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The station house, with the ice on it. The cableway's piece, 2.5 m back, as that biome's sheet has it. It is drawn here for the one thing this biome adds: the glacier lies on its roof, has pushed in its west end, and fills its west window with blue ice. (2.5 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 3The alcove's back wall. Where the house ends, the ice begins: the glacier's own body, mid blue and dull, in bands that tilt down to the east. It comes down over the house's west end like a poured thing that set. This is the first of the back wall that runs behind every room from here to the window. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The icicle fringe. A row of small icicles under the lip, 12 m long, hanging thickest over the deck's two western patches of ice: the ice on the planks is what dripped from them. None of them falls and none can be reached. Today the row hangs inside the play space. It moves 1.3 m back. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 5The tunnel's back wall. Banded ice, scooped by the old water into shallow dishes a metre across, with stones standing out of the bands. It never stops inside the frame. It is the same mid blue from end to end, so the tunnel is the darkest part of the walk in. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The first cane and its flag. A bamboo cane 3 m tall, stuck in the snow at the back of the alcove, with a paper flag in the town's colours. It is the first of the canes that mark the route. In here, out of the wind, its flag hangs almost slack and leans toward the tunnel. (1.4 to 1.7 m behind you)
  • 7A lantern inside the tunnel's mouth. A candle lantern on a hook cut from an axe shaft, 1.5 m inside the mouth and above head height. Every lamp on the cableway is cold. This is the first one alight since the frog's car, and it hangs on the side you go through. (1.4 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 8The last pennant line. The cableway's last line of pennants comes in from the east, 5 m above the deck, and its end is frozen into the lip's tip. Its last four pennants are stiff with ice and do not move. It is the town's paper handing over to the canes. (2.0 to 2.2 m behind you)
  • 9The lower crack, ahead. At the tunnel's west end the roof goes up out of sight and the wall is paler: the lower crack. From inside the tunnel it is a tall pale slot at the end of a dark passage. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10Boot prints. The tunnel's snow floor has a line of old boot prints going west, a blue hollow every few metres, and low ridges where the draught has combed it. All of it is drawn on the snow and changes nothing you stand on.
  • 11Where the planks end. The deck's west end and the glacier's snow meet at one line. East of it: steel, cream paint, planks, the deck's ice. West of it nothing is built. The plank ends are drawn broken off under the snow's edge.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 12The snow fringe on the lip. A strip of snow along the top edge of the lip, in front of the lip's own ice. Today's strip sits just outside the ice, over the ramp that a sliding body comes down. It moves 0.5 m in, so it covers ice and nothing else. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • Gold light on the deck's east end, blue shadow from the lip's edge west. The line between them lies across the planks.
  • One slow drip from the fringe onto each patch of ice on the deck.
  • The lantern's amber on the snow just inside the tunnel, and nowhere else.
  • A long way off and overhead, now and then: the rumble of something coming down the slide and going over the tip.
