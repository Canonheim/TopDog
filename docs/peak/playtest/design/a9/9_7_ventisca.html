El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.7: Ventisca

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Out of the window, and the glacier's outer skin is over you: steep, dark and smooth, with a brow of ice jutting over the window like an eyebrow. This is the side of the tongue that the slide runs down, and from here on every miss is the slide. The way goes out under the brow by a blunt tusk of blue ice, over the brow on three plates, and 9 m straight up a wall on nubs of blue ice with one stone among them, to a snow niche under the mouth of the avalanche chute. The wall stands in the wind. It blows from the west for two seconds and peels you off the ice. It rests. Then it blows from the east and pins you. The ones who came this way stuck canes with paper flags into the snow shelves of the ice behind the route, and the flags tell you which wind is coming.

What must not change

  • The tusk under the brow, the three plates on the brow, and the six holds up the wall: five nubs of blue ice and one stone, each where it is.
  • The two winds, their strength and their six-second clock, and the still air below the brow and in the niche.
  • The window's sill and the niche at 846 m.
  • The fall: the skin, the lip, the tip, the whole slide.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The valley, the ranges, the cloud. What is given, and already made: the painted valley far below with the tiny town, the two painted ranges, the cloud banks close above. This is the first place you see it since the cableway, and the town is very small. (7.8 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The glacier's far side. One flat painted card, 45 m back: the rest of the glacier going away along the face. It is 90 m wide and 100 m tall, in three rows of broken ice, the back row hazed to the sky, with snow in drifts along the foot of each row. Its edge shows 4 to 10 m east of the wall you climb, cut into four or five big leaning towers. Mid blues and greys, nothing near white, so the wind's white lines show against it. (7.9 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 3The room you left. Through the window: the ice cave's far wall, deep blue. From outside the window is a dark hole in the skin with the town's paper streaming out of it. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The flank of the tongue. The glacier's own side, 1.5 to 3 m behind the climb: dark ice in flat planes, reaching 2 to 3 m further east than the wall you are on. It has two small snow shelves, each sloping and no wider than 1.5 m. It is why the wall is not a blade standing in the air, and it is dark, so the pale holds show in front of it. One of its bands carries stones, at the height of the one stone you hold. The niche's back is the same ice. (1.5 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5Three canes with paper flags. Bamboo canes 3 m tall with paper flags in the town's colours: one on each snow shelf of the flank, 1.5 m behind the route, and one at the back of the niche. They are the wind's flags (note 89). Before a gust they drop slack and then lift the new way. In the gust they stream flat. (1.5 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 6The town's paper and the window's lantern. Along the top of the window: 3 m of the town's torn paper, another flag. Under the brow on the outer side: the lit lantern that marks this as the way through. They are the same two as on the page before. (1.4 to 1.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The tusk. The first hold outside: a blunt hanging point of blue ice 3 m long, frosted, with no rings and no collar. It is blue ice and not an icicle. It lets go after two seconds like any blue ice, and it never snaps off.
  • 8The one stone. The third hold up the wall is a grey stone, ringed here. It is the only place on this climb where you can wait out a gust. The three plates on the brow and the five nubs are pale blue ice with frost on their top edges.
  • 9The wind. Long wavy white lines, each with a dark inked edge, across the whole of the wind's reach: from the brow's top to just under the niche, and 7 m out from the wall (note 89). They run the way the wind runs. Before the wind changes sides a front of lines crosses from the side it is coming from. Where the lines stop, the wind stops: they curl and die under the niche and at the brow.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 10The snow fringe on the brow. A thin strip of snow along the brow, in front of the brow's own ice and under its top edge. Today's strip lies along the top edge itself, in front of the three plates you hold. It drops 0.4 m, so it covers ice and no hold. (1.2 to 1.4 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The wind's wavy lines, and snow carried in them.
  • Snow smoking off the brow's edge and the niche's edge, the way the wind is going.
  • Before each gust: the flags drop, a whistle rises, a front of lines crosses.
  • The climber's own loose limbs trail downwind.
