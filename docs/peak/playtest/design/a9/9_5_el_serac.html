El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.5: El Serac

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Above the chimney is the upper level: a long low room under a dark roof. Its floor stops after 4.7 m at a pit 10 m across, and on the brink stands a tower of ice 7.1 m tall and 1.9 m thick, with firm snow up its west side and on its top. It is a slab that a crack has cut off the wall. The glacier is still pushing behind it, and it stands only until something touches it. Then it tips east over the pit, slowly and then fast, goes on through level and down into the pit, and breaks to pieces there. You go up its snow side as it turns, and jump from its top to a snow-topped pillar on the far side before it passes level. Where it stood, the push squeezes the next slab up out of the same crack: bare and dark first, then its snow, and then it can be climbed.

What must not change

  • The tower's size and where it stands, its snow side 0.6 m wide, its snow cap, and the stone at its top corner.
  • It starts to tip when it is touched, and it tips on today's curve: slowly, then fast.
  • The jump to the pillar, and the pillar: snow, 2.5 m wide, at 834.5 m.
  • The fall: the pit, its funnel, the slot, and out onto the balcony's snow at the foot of the chimney.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The upper level's far wall. Banded ice, 2.4 m back, from the pit's floor to the roof. The roof is dark, glossy and unbroken from wall to wall: nothing up there is a way out. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The wall the tower was cut from. Behind the tower: its twin, a slab the same size still standing in the far wall, with the crack that will free it drawn as a white line beside it. The tower you climb is the nearest of a row. (1.6 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 3Two that have started to lean. In the pit's far wall: two more slabs of bluer ice, both leaning east, the way this one will fall. They are flush in the wall and come nowhere near the play space. They show which way the tower goes before you touch it. (2.0 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4Old towers, in pieces. Jammed in the back of the pit's funnel: blocks of old dark ice, snow still on some of them. The towers that stood here before. They are behind the funnel and block nothing: a body still falls through the slot. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The way on, paler. East of the pit, behind the pillar and beyond, the far wall is two steps paler: the window's daylight coming back along the roof. From the brink it is the palest thing in the room, and the pillar stands against it. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6An axe and a coil of rope. At the back of the brink's snow, where the chimney comes up: an ice axe stuck in upright, and a coil of rope frozen flat. The party in the chimney left them here. Today they lie inside the play space. They move 0.8 m back. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 7The fall, all the way. The path of the tower's top corner. It does not stop at 75 degrees as it does today. It goes on through level, swings down into the pit and strikes the far slope, and there the tower shatters (note 88). The jump is made from the top before level.
  • 8The stump, and the next one growing. What is left when the tower has gone: a broken stump 0.6 m high. The next tower rises out of it over a few seconds, bare and blue-black with no snow, and cannot be touched while it grows. Its snow side and cap come last. Only then is it the tower again (note 88).
  • 9Bent layers under the foot. Drawn on the face of the block the tower stands on: the ice's bands, bent upward into a peak under the tower's foot. It is the push that raises the next one, shown where you can see it. A white crack runs along the foot itself.
  • 10The pillar's snow. Firm snow with old boot prints on it, all pointing east. It is the head of the slide.
  • 11Where the pieces go. The tower's pieces fall through the slot with whoever was still on it, and are gone in a moment. They hurt nobody.

Small effects, no models

  • A creak when you first touch it, then a long groan that rises as it goes.
  • Snow sliding off its cap as it tips, and a crack opening white along its foot.
  • The shatter in the pit: one crash, a cloud of ice dust up out of the pit, pieces down the slot.
  • The next one growing: a grinding from under the brink, and the bent layers creep.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 88: the next one grows into place and counts only when it is full height. It is the same law as the icicles: ice that comes is seen coming.

  • In the play space today, and moved out of it: the ice axe and the rope coil on the brink (0.8 m back, item 6).
