El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.1: Hielo Azul

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The lower crack is one tall slot in the ice: 25.5 m wide and 23.5 m high, under a dark roof. You come into it at the foot of its east side, and the way out is a lit door at the top of the same side, 18 m straight above you. The slot is climbed as one zigzag: west up the far wall, then back east along the top. This page is the first leg. A seam of old blue ice crosses the wall from the floor by the tunnel to a snow ledge in the west corner, 19.5 m along and 10.7 m up. It is a crack that healed, and it is harder than the ice round it, so it stands proud of the wall in knuckles. The glacier's own layers cross it on a gentler slant, and three of those layers carry stones. Where a layer of stones meets the seam there is a grey stone to hold. Ice lets go of you after two seconds. A stone never does.

What must not change

  • All fifteen holds exactly where they are: twelve knuckles of blue ice and three stones, in runs of two, three, four and three.
  • Blue ice holds for 2 seconds and then that hand cannot grip for a moment. The stones hold for good.
  • The corner ledge at 798.5 m, the snow floor, and the fall: a miss lands on the floor, at the foot of this climb.
  • The four places where icicles fall onto the top third of the line, and their timing.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack's far wall. Mid blue and dull, 2.4 m behind you, in bands that tilt ten degrees down to the east. It fills the frame from the floor to the roof. Its closed corners are the deepest blue: the top west corner over the shelf, and the foot of the west wall. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The blue seam. A band of pale old ice half a metre wide, flush in the far wall, on the same line as the holds. Each knuckle is the near end of a tooth of this seam, and the seam is what you read the route from. At the corner it turns and climbs straight up to the underside of the shelf. Today a pale card 3 m wide lies here and looks like a ramp. It goes (note 84). (2.4 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 3Three layers of stones. Grey-brown stones riding in three of the wall's bands. Each line of stones runs through one of the three stones you hold. So before you start you can see where the seam will let you stop: where a line of stones crosses it. (2.4 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 4The door, lit. The palest thing in the slot, high on the east side: the door of the ice cave, with frost round its edge and the wall round it two steps paler than anywhere else. You see it from the floor the moment you step out of the tunnel. A lantern burns just inside it. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The light down the gap. A paler stripe on the far wall, straight above the shelf's broken end. It is the same light as the door's, coming along the top of the slot and down past the end of the shelf. It marks the one place where you can get up past the shelf. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The shelf's other half. Two blocks of ice on the floor, under the shelf's broken end and behind where you walk. The bigger is 3 m long. They are the piece of the shelf that broke off, and they are why the shelf reads as a thing with an end. (1.3 to 2.3 m behind you)
  • 7Two blue ribs. Leaning slabs of bluer ice in the far wall. Today two stand right behind the line of holds and look like part of it. They move: one to the foot of the west wall under the corner ledge, one to the east wall under the door. (2.0 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 8One who fell. One of the ones who tried, deep in the far wall, head down, a flat blue shape with no warm colour on him. He is under the middle snow bridge, 5 m from any hold. Today he stands 1.5 m from the line of holds. (2.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 9The pug arrow sign. On a post at the back of the corner ledge, pointing east (note 85). It is the only sign on the glacier. You see it from the floor. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 10Knuckles and stones. Each knuckle is pale blue ice, frosted and matte, 0.66 m long, with white frost along its top edge. The three stones are grey-brown and dry with no frost on them: they are ringed here. Nothing else in the slot is either colour.
  • 11Boot prints and splinters. Drawn on the snow floor: boot prints from the tunnel's mouth to the first knuckle, and a ring of ice splinters under each place where an icicle falls. The splinters tell you where not to stand before anything has fallen.
  • 12The bridges, redone. The three bridges and the two wedged stones as they are redone, in yellow, drawn over a black strip that blanks out where they are today. Nine pieces, each 1.1 m long, and 1.2 m of open air between the shelf's broken end and the first piece. The page for Puente de Nieve says what moves.

Small effects, no models

  • The button mark on the limb that holds ice shivers, harder and harder, for the two seconds (note 84).
  • A falling icicle ticks, drops, and bursts into splinters on whatever it lands on: a knuckle, the floor, you (note 86).
  • Snow sifting down from the bridges, 18 m up, all the time.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 86: this room is redone. It is as hard as it was. What changes is that the whole zigzag can be read from the floor: the seam, the corner, the shelf's broken end with light coming down past it, the bridges, the door.
  • Course change on this leg: none to anything you hold. All fifteen holds, the corner ledge and the floor are where they are.
  • Scenery that moves: the pale card behind the holds (3 m wide, from the floor to the corner) goes, and a seam 0.5 m wide takes its place (note 84). The two ribs at x -316.5 and x -311.2 move to x -330.6 and x -308.2. The frozen climber in the wall moves from (-318.5, 795.0) to (-312.5, 798.6).
  • Scenery that is new: the two fallen blocks on the floor, 1.3 m and more behind you; the arrow sign on the corner ledge (note 85); the paler wall round the door.
  • Note 86: the falling icicles grow before they fall and shatter where they land. Their four places over this line do not move.
  • Nothing stands in front of this room.
