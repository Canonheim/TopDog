El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.9: Cornisa de Hielo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The glacier ends at the top in a tower shaped like a mushroom: a stem on the east, and a cornice that curls 12 m west over the gap. This is the cornice's underside: a flat roof of dark ice, 9.4 m from the niche to its lip, with 8 m of air under it, then the chute, then the slide. The cornice has cracked through in five places, and each crack has healed with blue ice. Where each seam comes out underneath there is a nub. The five nubs, 1.7 m apart, are the only holds, and each lets go after two seconds, so you cannot stop. Under you the snow slabs come down the chute and burst in their hollow. At the far end you go round the lip and into a second niche, under the tower's tip.

What must not change

  • The tusk at the niche's mouth, the five nubs under the roof, and the one on the lip, each where it is.
  • Every one of them is blue ice and lets go after 2 seconds. There is no stone on this roof.
  • The two niches, at 867.8 m and 873.3 m.
  • The fall: 8 m of air, the chute, its mouth, the slide.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The cloud. What is given: cloud close overhead, grey, and under it the valley very far down. The light here is flat, with almost no shadow. (7.8 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The glacier's far side. The painted card 45 m back, east of the stem. From here you see only its top row of towers, hazed to the sky. (7.9 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 3The ridge's rock, across the gap. Behind the air under the cornice: the storm ridge's wet slate in leaning slabs, dull blue-grey, 2.4 m back. It is darker than the cloud and duller than the ice, so the five pale nubs and the climber show against it. (2.4 to 4.0 m behind you)
  • 4The ice behind the niches. Behind each niche and behind the roofed part of the chute: banded ice, dark. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5Five seams through the cornice. Five pale bands, half a metre wide, seen through the dark ice of the cornice: the healed cracks. Each comes straight down to one of the nubs. From the niche you can count the holds by counting the bands, and you can see that the cornice is six blocks stuck together. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6The hollow, and the slabs bursting. Below and behind: the hollow in the slate that the snow slabs are thrown into, 6 m under the roof. A slab bursts in it every five and a half seconds while you cross. It cannot reach you. It is La Avalancha's, drawn here because it is what is under your feet. (2.4 to 5.4 m behind you)
  • 7A cane in each niche. A cane with a paper flag at the back of each niche. You leave one flag and cross to the other. (1.5 to 1.8 m behind you)

Play space

  • 8The tusk at the niche's mouth. A blunt hanging point of blue ice 2.4 m long: the way from the niche's floor up to the roof. The same piece as at the window.
  • 9The five nubs. Pale blue ice, frosted, half a metre across, each standing 0.3 m down out of the roof at the foot of its seam. They are the palest things under the cornice.
  • 10The lip. One more nub on the cornice's west edge, 1.1 m above the roof. It turns you round the lip and onto the snow of the second niche.

Foreground (sparse)

  • 11The snow fringe on the tower's top. A strip of snow along the tower's sloping top, in front of the tower's own ice and under its top edge. Today's strip runs all the way up to the tip, in front of the last frozen climber's boots, and the snow cornice model sits inside the play space. The strip is cut back to end 4 m short of the tip, and both move to sit in front of ice only. (1.2 to 1.5 m in front)

Small effects, no models

  • The button mark shivers on each hand in turn: you watch two of them at once.
  • Frost falling from a nub as it lets go.
  • Under you: a slab's hiss, thump and burst, and its dust blowing down the chute.
  • Fine snow streaming off the cornice's lip.
