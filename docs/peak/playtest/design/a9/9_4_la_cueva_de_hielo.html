El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.4: La Cueva de Hielo

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

Through the door is the ice cave: a hollow 18 m wide and 30 m high, the biggest room in the glacier, floored with snow that has drifted down from a window high in its east wall. Nobody could climb its walls. The ones who tried are the way. Two of them are frozen one above the other on the face of ice over the door, and their boots, belts and mittens take you 8 m up to a snow balcony. The balcony runs 28 m west under a low roof to the foot of a chimney: a round shaft that melt water bored down through the ice, 3.5 m wide and 20 m tall. Four more of them are in the chimney, with four knuckles of blue ice and one stone between them. The last one's mitten is a hand's width under the lip he almost climbed out over.

What must not change

  • The six frozen climbers, each exactly where he is, and the three grips on each: boots, belt, mitten.
  • The four knuckles of blue ice and the one stone in the chimney.
  • The balcony, the chimney's width, and the lip at 832 m.
  • The fall: from the door's two onto the cave's floor, from the chimney onto the balcony.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The far wall of the cave and the balcony. Banded ice, mid blue, deeper blue than the lower crack because there is more ice between it and the day. It runs behind the cave, the balcony and the chimney without a break. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The door you came by. Seen from inside: a pale doorway with frost round it, and the lower crack beyond. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The ice at their backs. Behind each of the six: a slab of dull mid-blue ice 1.7 m wide and 3 m tall that runs back to the far wall. He is frozen to its front and stands whole out of it, in the play space, with nothing in front of him (note 87). The slab is why he is there: the crack opened right beside where he was held. Today it is a pale card inside the play space. (1.2 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 4Two lanterns. One beside the first climber at the door, and one beside the first climber at the foot of the chimney. Each hangs on a hook 1.4 m back and lights him, not the wall. Wherever a group of them starts, there is a light on the first. (1.4 to 1.6 m behind you)
  • 5The balcony's lip, lit. A paler patch on the far wall above the two at the door, where the balcony begins. Seen from the door, it is the palest thing in reach, and they are between you and it. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 6The chimney. The melt shaft's far wall: fluted in upright grooves by the water that bored it, and paler in its upper half, where the ice is thin. From the balcony's east end it is a tall pale stripe at the far end of a dark gallery. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 7The heap under the pit. Against the far wall, under the slot that comes down through the balcony's roof: a heap of old broken ice. The slot is the foot of the pit under El Serac. The heap says that things come down here. (1.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 8One lying in the wall. Half way along the balcony, deep in the far wall and lying on his side: a flat blue shape, with no warm colour on him. (2.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 9The tally marks. Scratched in the far wall at the chimney's foot, in fives. Somebody counted his tries. Today they are 0.9 m back, inside the play space. They move to the wall. (2.4 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 10On the cave's floor. A block of ice 2 m across that came off the roof, and a frozen glove beside it. Both are behind where you walk. (1.3 to 2.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 11The ones who tried. Each is frost grey, body and mask. His boots, belt and mitten are warm: honey leather, hemp rope, bone-white wool, each a lump 0.35 m across and exactly the size of its grip. One mitten is always raised, and it points at the next hold: the yellow lines show where each one points. The first at the door has his boots at hand height.
  • 12Four knuckles and a stone. Between the four in the chimney: four knuckles of pale blue ice, which let go after two seconds, and one grey stone, ringed here, which does not. They are the same pieces as in Hielo Azul.
  • 13The balcony's snow. Firm snow, 28 m of it, with a line of boot prints going west, blue hollows, and a pocked patch under the pit's slot. All drawn on. It tells you which way to walk when the chimney is still off the screen.
  • 14The lip. The snow edge of the upper level, 0.2 m above the last one's mitten. His arm is at full stretch. You use it, and go over.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • Each lantern flickers. Its amber lies on one frozen climber and on the snow under him.
  • The button mark shivers on a limb that holds a knuckle. It does not shiver on a boot, a belt or a mitten.
  • A cold draught along the balcony, going west: a little snow moves along the floor the way you are going.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 87: nothing stands in front of any of them. The ice is at their backs, and it stops at the back edge of the play space.
  • Note 87: they read as the way up at first sight because of three things. Their grips are the only warm, dry colour in the room. The first one at the door has his boots at hand height and a lit lantern beside him. Each one's raised mitten points at the next hold.
  • In the play space today, and moved out of it: the pale card behind each of the six (it becomes the slab, from 1.2 m back to the wall), the dead lantern on the door's lintel (lit, on a hook 1.4 m back), the tally marks (to the far wall), the frozen glove (1.3 m back).
  • The sheet allows no icicle fringe in a room where an icicle falls. One icicle falls into this cave, so the row of small icicles on the cave's roof comes out.
  • Nothing you touch moves.
