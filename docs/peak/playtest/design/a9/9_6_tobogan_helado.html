El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.6: Tobogán Helado

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The pillar you landed on is the head of the glacier's old drain. Melt water ran off the upper level here for years: down a face of ice 6 m tall and nearly sheer, round a bend into a bowl, and out through a crack in the bowl's rim. The water polished all of it, and it is the slickest thing on the mountain. Since then a flake of ice has wedged in the crack and tilted up, and it makes a kicker. So you sit down on purpose. You slide, leave the kicker at 32 degrees, and fly 15 m across the top of the ice cave, 20 m above its floor, to a hemp rope that hangs from the sill of a window in the glacier's skin. The window is the first daylight since the cableway. It is the palest thing in the room from the moment you land on the pillar, and the rope hangs in front of it.

What must not change

  • The slide's whole shape: the sheer face, the bend, the bowl, the 1.1 m crack, the kicker and its 32 degrees.
  • The draught across the bowl, which pushes a body that has stalled into the crack.
  • The rope: 16 m long, 15 m from the kicker's lip, hung from the sill's edge. And the sill.
  • The fall: 20 m to the cave's snow floor, at the foot of La Cueva de Hielo.

The three layers

Background

  • 1Outside. East of the glacier's skin: the far side of the glacier as one painted card, and behind it the valley, the ranges and the cloud that are given. From inside you see only what shows through the window. (7.8 to 8.0 m behind you)
  • 2The cave's far wall. Deep blue banded ice behind the whole flight, from the cave's floor to its roof. It is plain on purpose: against it you see the rope, the window and yourself. It grows paler toward the window. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 3The window, the town's paper and a lantern. A gap in the skin 4.5 m wide and 4.5 m tall, full of white daylight, with frost round its edge. The town's torn paper hangs along its top and blows outward. A lit lantern hangs on its outer side. Today the paper is inside the play space. It moves 0.6 m back. (1.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 4The water's grooves. Two long grooves scoured in the far wall beside the slide, following its curve down the face and round into the bowl. They are flush marks, not ledges. They show the whole line of the slide from the pillar before you sit down. (2.4 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 5Two blue ribs. Leaning slabs of bluer ice in the far wall, as tall as the cave. Today one stands 2 m from the rope. Both move west, so that the only upright thing near the rope is the rope. (2.0 to 2.4 m behind you)
  • 6Two in the wall. Deep in the far wall under the flight, flat blue shapes: one leaning, one with his arms out. The one near the rope moves 2 m west of where he is today, away from the rope's line. (2.4 to 2.6 m behind you)
  • 7The rope's anchor. At the back of the sill: an ice axe driven in to its head, with the rope's upper end knotted round it and two turns of spare rope frozen flat. Somebody hung this rope from the window. They came in the way you will go out. (1.3 to 1.8 m behind you)
  • 8The frog's light, far below. At the east end of the cave's floor, straight under the rope: the snow bank and the round hole with a lantern in it. It is the only warm colour under the flight, and you see it as you fly. It has its own page. (1.2 to 3.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 9The kicker. A flake of the glacier's dark ice wedged in the crack's mouth, its face 1.8 m long. It is glossy and slick like the slide, with a white edge along its lip where it is chipped. Snow smokes off the lip in the draught.
  • 10The draught. Long wavy white lines with an inked edge across the bowl, going east (note 89). It is the cave's air being pulled toward the window. The lines curl down into the crack at the bowl's rim, which is where a stalled body goes.
  • 11The flight. The line a clean slide flies, from the course's own numbers: off the lip at 6.1 m a second, 15 m across, meeting the rope 2.3 m below the lip's height. It is drawn here only to show the place. Nothing marks it in the game.
  • 12The one icicle that falls. A dark wet crack in the cave's roof, with one clear icicle that grows, shivers and drops 30 m to the floor, where it bursts (note 86). It is the only icicle in the cave. Its line crosses the flight.
  • 13The rope. Hemp, frozen stiff, pale and dry, with a frayed tail. It is the only thing in the upper half of the cave that is a warm colour, and it hangs against the palest part of the wall.
  • 14The sill. Firm snow with boot prints going out through the window.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • The draught: wavy lines across the bowl, and snow smoking east off the kicker's lip.
  • A long hiss as you slide, and no sound at all while you fly.
  • The rope creaks and swings when you take it, and frost falls off it.
  • The town's paper at the window streaming outward.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 89: the bowl's draught is drawn the loud way, as wavy lines, and the snow off the lip shows it too.
  • Note 86: the cave's one falling icicle grows first and shatters on the floor.
  • The sheet allows no icicle fringe in a room where an icicle falls. The row of small icicles on the cave's roof, which hangs inside the play space today, comes out.
