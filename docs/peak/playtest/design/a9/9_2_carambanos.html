El Glaciar · Area and background design

Challenge 9.2: Carámbanos

Design only. Nothing here is built, and nothing is modelled until the whole mountain has been playtested.

The place, in one paragraph

The second leg of the zigzag. A shelf of hard ice sticks out of the west wall over the corner ledge, 12 m long and 1.4 m thick, and its east end is broken off. The rest of it lies in blocks on the floor of the slot. The blue seam you climbed carries on up the corner and along the underside of the shelf, and from it hang five big icicles of the same old ice: ringed like a stack of cups, each with a white collar of frost. They are the way. You go east from one to the next, with one stone tooth in the middle where you can stop, to a stone on the shelf's broken end. There the roof over you ends, light comes down the gap, and you climb up through the gap onto the shelf's top. Between the big icicles the shelf drips. A clear icicle grows from each of four dark wet cracks, shivers, and falls.

What must not change

  • The five big icicles, the stone tooth and the stone on the shelf's nose, each where it is, 1.4 m apart.
  • A big icicle snaps off after you take it and grows back. How long it holds is note 85's to change, and nothing else about it.
  • The four places under the shelf where icicles fall, the one over the corner ledge, and the one over the shelf's top.
  • The corner ledge and the shelf, as they are.

The three layers

Background

  • 1The crack's far wall. The same banded wall. Above the shelf, in the closed west corner, it is the deepest blue in the room: nothing is up there. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 2The seam, turning the corner. The pale seam from Hielo Azul comes up to the corner ledge and climbs straight on to the underside of the shelf, beside the first big icicle. It is flush in the far wall. It shows that the icicles are the next piece of the same line. (2.4 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 3The scar, and the stones in it. East of the shelf's end the far wall has a pale scar at the shelf's height, as long as the piece that fell, with stones standing out of it. The stone tooth, the stone on the nose and the two stones wedged between the bridges all came out of this one layer. (2.4 to 2.5 m behind you)
  • 4The light down the gap. A paler stripe on the far wall above the shelf's broken end, 1.2 m wide, with frost on the broken edge. Everything else over your head is dark. This is the answer to the question the room used to leave open: you do not go through the shelf, you go round its end, here. (2.4 to 3.0 m behind you)
  • 5The pug arrow sign. On a post at the back of the corner ledge, 2.2 m tall, pointing east along the row of big icicles (note 85). It is the kit's sign, turned round. (1.3 to 1.6 m behind you)

Play space

  • 6The five that hold. Each big icicle is thick, ringed, pale blue and matte, with a white frost collar where it joins the shelf, and the seam runs from collar to collar along the shelf's underside. They hang in a row in the play space, and nothing else here does.
  • 7The four that fall. Each hangs from a dark wet crack in the shelf, 0.8 m long, with no collar. It is smooth, clear and sharp, and darker than the ones that hold. You watch it grow from a nub to full length, then it shivers, ticks and drops (note 86). A fifth crack is over the corner ledge.
  • 8The broken end and its stone. The shelf's east end is a rough broken face, frosted white, with a grey stone standing out of it at 803.1 m. The stone is the last hold of this leg. Today the first snow bridge lies over it. Now there is open air above it.
  • 9The bridges, redone. The three bridges and the two wedged stones as they are redone, in yellow, drawn over a black strip that blanks out where they are today. Nine pieces, each 1.1 m long, and 1.2 m of open air between the shelf's broken end and the first piece. The page for Puente de Nieve says what moves.
  • 10The crack in the roof. One dark wet crack in the roof over the shelf's top, with one falling icicle. The rest of the roof is dark, glossy and unbroken from wall to wall, with nothing hanging from it.

Foreground (sparse)

Nothing.

Small effects, no models

  • A big icicle creaks while you hang on it and snaps off clean at the collar. A new one grows down from the collar in plain view.
  • A falling one: a drip, a nub, full length, a shiver, a tick, the drop, the burst.
  • Frost dust falling through the lit gap.

Tonight's notes folded in

  • Note 85: the big icicles hold longer than today's 0.7 seconds. The note's first try is 1.5 seconds, to be set by play. They look different from the ones that fall: thick, ringed and collared against smooth, clear and hanging from a wet crack.
  • Note 85: the arrow sign on the corner ledge, pointing east.
  • Note 86: the icicles that fall are seen growing first, and shatter where they land.
  • Note 86, the redesign. Course change on this leg: the shelf, its five icicles, the stone tooth and the stone on its nose do not move. What changes at the nose is above it. Today the first snow bridge piece lies over the nose stone, 0.23 m above it, so the way up onto the shelf goes through the end of a bridge. The bridges move east and leave 1.2 m of open air there (see Puente de Nieve).
  • The seam under the shelf and the frost collars are drawn on things that are there. Nothing new can be held.
