## Parker's calls

**11-14 (the brief).** Redo the summit completely: too short and not interesting. Make it longer. Make the old ring
more obviously a ring, with the bell tower climb and the belt above it. Theme: an ancient, run-down mountain monastery,
a sky temple. Keep the frogs' hot spring after the belt, and keep the tea rest somewhere.

**11-19 (on v1).**
- The falls weren't dangerous: every miss landed at the tea house. Move the tea house **inside the mountain**, so the
  mountain's slope pushes you off.
- A miss should throw you into the storm ridge's weather-balloon ropes, as the old summit did ("that was fine").
- Number 6 should be like the mine's shaft (1.10, El Derrumbe): things falling down it, going back and forth, tucking
  into the walls. It is **a library all the way through**, and the things falling are **books and scrolls**.
- The frogs' hot spring goes **inside the mountain**, not floating near the top.
- The summit can be bigger, and things can be rearranged as much as needed.

**11-19 (on v2).**
- A fall inside the library stays inside (to a lower gallery or the floor): that's fine.
- The terraces stay, and are to be made pretty.
- (Asked whether the climb is long enough: v2 is about 105 m, against today's 45 m. Parker to say if it should grow;
  the library is the easiest place to stretch.)

## The layout, bottom to top

The mountain is one big slick skin, x -330 .. -496, from the entry at 964 m to the belt at 1,069 m. Every miss
outside slides down the east face, off the prow and into the weather balloon's rope on the storm ridge (A10).

1. **La Puerta del Cielo:** the gate cut into the mountain's foot, right behind A10's last ledge.
2. **La Casa de Té (the rest):** inside, in the monks' old kitchen behind the gate: the stove's glow, the tea frog's
   counter. A long stair inside climbs to a door high on the east face (about 980 m).
3. **El Claustro Colgante:** three broken cloister arches hanging on chains from a slanting beam off the face.
4. **Los Molinos de Oración:** three prayer wheels on iron brackets out from the face; each turns and throws you up.
5. **Las Terrazas:** two narrow terrace lips with a prayer-flag line in the wind, up to the library's door
   (about 1,013 m). To be made pretty: carved lips, stone lanterns, flags, frost-killed shrubs, a worn pug guardian.
6. **La Biblioteca:** one tall hall inside the mountain (about 29 x 19 m). Four shelf-top galleries zig-zag up it.
   Books and scrolls fall in waves from broken top shelves down three gaps; lamp-lit reading niches are cut into
   the walls at each gallery's end to tuck into. A hatch at the top opens onto the high court. A fall inside stays
   inside.
7. **El Ring:** on the high court, the library's roof and the summit's top: canvas, red apron, corner posts with
   ropes that throw you, stone seats round it.
8. **El Último Salto:** from an iron ring on a broken arch at the court's west edge, the 20 m leap west and 2.7 m down
   over a cleft to the bell rope. A miss drops into the cleft; a slick chute inside carries you out onto the east
   face, and on to the balloon.
9. **El Campanario and the belt:** the bell tower on the west spur: up the rope to the old summit bell, inside on its
   beams, out over its little roof to the belt.

**After the belt:** a cable slide from the tower's roof down into a cave mouth on the west shoulder, into the frogs'
hot spring: a warm grotto inside the mountain, lit through cracks in its roof. Its slide leaves through the west face,
on to the great falls.
